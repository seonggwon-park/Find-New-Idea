## KQ1. Quantized fine-tuning (QLoRA family) and the "LoRA-merge-into-quantized-base" problem

### Takeaway
The QLoRA line (NF4 base + fp16 adapters) is mature, and better initializations (LoftQ, LQ-LoRA, ApiQ, IR-QLoRA, CLoQ) give small-to-moderate gains that shrink toward zero at 4-bit on strong models but matter at 2–3 bits — where all methods are still close to chance on MMLU for 7B models. The merge problem (fold ΔW = BA into a quantized W without re-quantization loss) has exactly one principled closed-form fix in the literature, QA-LoRA's "merge into the zero-points" trick, which works only because the adapter is constrained to be group-constant along the input dimension; otherwise re-quantizing W + BA destroys most of the delta because |ΔW| is typically far below the 4-bit group step. I found no theory bounding the merge error or the accuracy cost of the QA-LoRA constraint.

### Cited Findings
- **QLoRA** (Dettmers et al., 2023; arXiv 2305.14314 **[ID from memory, not verified in session]**) is the baseline every paper below compares against; it keeps the base in 4-bit NF4 and dequantizes during the forward pass, so adapters live in 16-bit and cannot be folded back without re-quantization. A practitioner review states that "merging QLoRA adapters while preserving quantization causes a significant performance drop" and that QLoRA "dequantizes the parameters of the base model during the forward pass" — [Kaitchup QA-LoRA review](https://kaitchup.substack.com/p/qa-lora-quantization-aware-fine-tuning?open=false) **[snippet]**; same author's "Don't merge your LoRA adapter into a 4-bit LLM" — [Kaitchup](https://kaitchup.substack.com/p/dont-merge-your-lora-adapter-into?open=false) **[snippet]**. HF forum threads report the same recommendation (dequantize → merge → requantize) and PEFT/bitsandbytes merge errors — [HF forum 1](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/error-model-cannot-be-quantized-if-a-lora-adapter-has-been-applied-to-it-via-merge-and-unload/86318), [HF forum 2](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/do-i-need-to-dequantization-before-merging-the-qlora/110175).
- **Quantitative evidence that naive re-quantization erases the adapter**: a 2026 systems paper reports that folding a LoRA delta into dequantized weights and re-quantizing to 4-bit "mostly erases it" because the deltas "sit below the 4-bit group step"; only ~34% of the effect survives on an attention projection and ~2% on a dense projection; the authors keep the adapter unmerged beside a read-only base and conclude the merge "fails on arithmetic rather than implementation" — [arXiv 2609.18063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18063) **[abstract/snippet]**.
- **QA-LoRA** (Xu et al., ICLR 2024; [arXiv 2309.14717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.14717); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e6c2e85db1f1039177c4495ccd399ac4-Abstract-Conference.html)) rebalances "degrees of freedom between quantization and adaptation" via group-wise operators (average-pool the input over each quantization group of size L), so after fine-tuning "the adapter weights can be merged into the quantized weights by updating only the zero-point matrix" (Appendix B.3) **[snippet]**. The official repo's merge code is the explicit formula: `qzeros -= (lora_B @ lora_A).T * scale / group_size / scales` (avg-pool variant) and claims the adapters "can be perfectly merged into the quantized model"; it uses GPTQ with `bits=4, group-size=32, act-order=False`, and lists an open TODO "Fix the conflict with the newest Auto-gptq version" — [QA-LoRA README](https://github.com/yuhuixu1993/qa-lora) **[primary]**.
- **LoftQ** (Li et al., 2023; [arXiv 2310.08659](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08659v2)) jointly finds a quantized backbone Q and low-rank (A,B) by alternating quantization/SVD so Q + AB ≈ W; gains are largest at 2-bit "where the quantized pre-trained model obtained by QLoRA exhibits severe degradation below the 3-bit level" **[snippet]**. Official README numbers **[primary]**: LLaMA-2-7B GSM8K after LoftQ fine-tune: 16-bit 36.9, 4-bit 35.0, 3-bit 32.9, 2.5-bit 31.1, 2.25-bit 26.5, 2-bit 20.9 (13B: 43.1 / 45.0 / 44.4 / 41.1 / 38.1 / 25.4); Phi-2 GSM8K: full FT 66.8±1.2, LoRA 64.8±0.5, QLoRA 60.2±0.6, LoftQ 64.1±0.7; LLaMA-3-8B GSM8K: full FT 70.4±0.7, LoRA 69.3±1.5, QLoRA 67.4±1.0, LoftQ 68.0±0.6 — [LoftQ README](https://github.com/yxli2123/LoftQ). Note the LLaMA-3-8B 4-bit gain over QLoRA (0.6 pt) is within one std.
- **LQ-LoRA** (Guo et al., 2023; [arXiv 2311.12023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12023)) decomposes W ≈ Q + L1L2 with an ILP allocating mixed bit-widths under a budget (e.g., 2.75 bits) and optional Fisher-weighted objective; released 7B and 70B 2.75-bit/rank-64 checkpoints. Repo notes that because LQ-LoRA "additionally changes the base model parameters", PEFT's adapter-only saving is insufficient and the full state dict must be saved — [LQ-LoRA README](https://github.com/HanGuo97/lq-lora) **[primary]**.
- **ApiQ** (Liao & Monz, 2024; [arXiv 2402.05147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05147)) "activation-preserving" initialization for 2/3/4-bit Llama-2 and Mistral-7B; evaluated on WikiText-2, GSM8K and four arithmetic datasets; repo warns of an AutoGPTQ asymmetric-quantization bug so real-quantized fine-tuning must use the symmetric variant (paper results are asymmetric) — [ApiQ README](https://github.com/BaohaoLiao/ApiQ) **[primary]**. CLoQ's runtime table reports LoftQ 0.6 h / 14 GB vs ApiQ-lw 4.1 h / 6 GB on Llama-2-7B — [CLoQ, arXiv 2501.18475](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18475) **[snippet]**.
- **IR-QLoRA** (Qin et al., ICML 2024 oral; [arXiv 2402.05445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05445); PMLR 235:41498–41516) adds Information Calibration Quantization (entropy-maximizing calibration of NF quantizer) and Information Elastic Connection in the adapter. Repo-transcribed paper numbers **[primary]**: 4-bit LLaMA-7B/Alpaca MMLU: QLoRA 38.4, QA-LoRA 39.4, IR-QLoRA 40.8; LLaMA-65B/Alpaca: 63.1 / 62.5 / 63.1 (tie with QLoRA); **2-bit LLaMA-7B/Alpaca: 26.2 / 27.5 / 27.8** and 3-bit: 37.8 / 37.4 / 38.4; training-time overhead 0.31–0.46% — [IR-QLoRA README](https://github.com/htqin/IR-QLoRA). (2-bit numbers are essentially chance on 4-way MMLU — see Inferences.)
- **PEQA** (Kim et al., NeurIPS 2023; [arXiv 2305.14152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14152v2)) fine-tunes only per-channel quantization scales of an already sub-4-bit model, up to 65B; claims capabilities "resiliently restored to (or even improved over)" full-precision — a merge-free design by construction **[abstract]**.
- **Newer initializations/recipes**: QuAILoRA ([arXiv 2410.14713](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14713v1)) groups LQ-LoRA/LoftQ/ApiQ as the same family; AQLoRA ([arXiv 2608.23816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23816)) is a "zero-search" recipe that reports LoftQ "ran on some models but failed on others in their environment" and is "level with" LoftQ wherever both run; a 2026 survey "Low-Rank Adaptation Redux" ([arXiv 2604.21905](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.21905)) summarizes IR-QLoRA as information-loss-minimizing calibration **[all snippets]**.
- **Merge-problem fixes beyond QA-LoRA**: QER (IEEE CloudCom 2025) "aligns the bitwidth of its low-rank parameters with the quantized base parameters" so the merge is lossless with no dequantize/requantize step — [HIT publication page](https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/qer-quantized-low-rank-error-reconstructor-for-llm-low-bitwidth-q/) **[snippet]**; FALQON (NeurIPS 2025) "merges the LoRA adapter into the quantized backbone" for up to 3× speedup over quantized-LoRA methods — [NeurIPS 2025 slides](https://neurips.cc/media/neurips-2025/Slides/115144.pdf) **[snippet]**. Neither provides a bound I could verify.
- **Zeroth-order quantized fine-tuning**: QuZO ([arXiv 2502.12346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12346v1), Feb 2025) fine-tunes with only 4-/8-bit forward passes using quantized randomized gradient estimation and "optimized stochastic rounding", avoiding the low-precision straight-through estimator; matches MeZO on GLUE/multiple-choice/generation and reports 2.94× memory reduction on LLaMA2-7B vs quantized first-order methods **[abstract]**. QZO ([arXiv 2505.13430](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13430v3)) instead perturbs the continuous quantization scale parameters rather than discrete weights **[abstract]**.

### Inferences
- The merge problem is a *lattice* problem: ΔW must move W across a rounding boundary to register; with 4-bit group-wise scales the step is ~Δ = (max−min)/15 per group, so any adapter whose per-weight delta is ≪ Δ is rounded away (consistent with 2609.18063's 2–34% survival). QA-LoRA sidesteps this by making ΔW constant along each input group so it can be absorbed into the (integer or fp16) zero-point, i.e., it trades adapter expressivity (rank r × D_in/L parameters instead of r × D_in) for exact mergeability. No paper quantifies this expressivity cost analytically.
- At 4-bit on modern bases (LLaMA-3-8B, Phi-2), initialization tricks recover ≤1 point over QLoRA with overlapping error bars (LoftQ README); their value is almost entirely in the 2–3-bit regime, where absolute accuracy is still poor (2-bit LLaMA-7B MMLU ≈ 27% for *all* methods, i.e., ~chance). Papers rarely state this plainly.
- Evaluation hygiene is weak across this family: GSM8K and MMLU at 7B–13B dominate; no paper in this set evaluates on long-CoT reasoning models or on RL-post-trained checkpoints; "fake quantization" is common and AutoGPTQ asymmetric bugs (ApiQ, EfficientQAT notes) mean real-kernel results may differ.
- Reproducibility flags: ApiQ's paper used asymmetric quantization but real deployment requires symmetric; AQLoRA could not run LoftQ on some models; QA-LoRA's repo has an unresolved AutoGPTQ compatibility TODO.

### Gaps
- No theoretical bound on merge error (e.g., as a function of ‖ΔW‖/Δ_group, rank, and group size) was found; no analysis of *stochastic* re-quantization or "merge-aware" training that keeps ΔW on the lattice (beyond QA-LoRA's constraint).
- I could not verify details of QER or FALQON (no abstract text beyond snippets); whether they are exact or approximate merges is unknown.
- QLoRA's own paper (NF4, double quantization, paged optimizers, "99.3% of ChatGPT" Guanaco claim) was not retrieved in-session; cite from memory with caution.
- No study found on QLoRA/LoftQ-style fine-tuning *of reasoning models* (R1-distills, Qwen3) or on how merge error interacts with long-CoT error accumulation.

## KQ2. Quantization-aware training (QAT) for LLMs: data/compute needs vs. bits and model size; the "bit-width transition"

### Takeaway
The empirical picture is now fairly consistent: (i) QAT is cheap relative to pretraining (EfficientQAT: 2-bit Llama-2-70B in 41 A100-hours; ParetoQ: ~10% of pretraining budget), (ii) there is a qualitative transition between 2 and 3 bits — ≥3-bit QAT "compensates" (weights stay near pretrained values) while ≤2-bit QAT "reconstructs" (representations change substantially) — and (iii) two 2025–2026 scaling laws (HKU/ByteDance W4A4 law in N, D, group size G; Apple law over QAT/FP compute split and bit width) formalize how error grows with tokens and falls with size. No paper offers a mechanistic *theory* of the 2↔3-bit transition; it remains an empirical observation.

### Cited Findings
- **LLM-QAT** (Liu et al., 2023; [arXiv 2305.17888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17888); Findings of ACL 2024 pp. 467–484): data-free QAT distilling from the model's own generations; quantizes weights, activations and KV cache; LLaMA 7/13/30B; "up to ~20 points improvement over training-free methods" at W4A8KV4 — [LLM-QAT README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/LLM-QAT) **[primary]**. Reproduced LLaMA-7B zero-shot averages **[primary]**: W4A8KV4 62.0, W4A8KV8 63.6, W4A16KV16 64.0, W8A8KV8 65.6, W8A8KV16 65.9 (README notes paper results used Meta's internal codebase; open-source numbers "close").
- **EfficientQAT** (Chen et al., 2024; [arXiv 2407.11062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11062); ACL 2025 main): two phases — Block-AP (block-wise training of *all* parameters) then E2E-QP (end-to-end training of only quantization step sizes). Claims 2-bit Llama-2-70B on one A100-80GB in 41 h with <3% degradation (69.48 vs 72.41 avg) **[abstract]**. Model-zoo table **[primary]**: Llama-2-7B fp16 PPL 5.47 / avg 64.86 → w4g128 5.53/64.27, w3g128 5.81/64.02, w2g64 6.86/60.14, w2g128 7.17/59.50; Llama-3-8B fp16 6.14/68.58 → w2g64 9.41/60.76 (Llama-3 loses ~8 pts at 2-bit vs ~4.7 for Llama-2-7B); Llama-3-70B 2.85/75.33 → w2g64 6.08/67.89; Mistral-Large-Instruct-2407 (123B) w2g64 → 35.5 GB, 77.76 → 73.54. Training defaults: 4096 calibration samples for Block-AP; LR 2e-5 (2-bit) / 1e-5 (3–4-bit); E2E-QP on RedPajama or Alpaca — [EfficientQAT README](https://github.com/OpenGVLab/EfficientQAT).
- **BitDistiller** (Du et al., ACL 2024, pp. 102–116, DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.7; arXiv 2402.10631 per README): asymmetric quantization + clipping (matches/surpasses GPTQ/AWQ PTQ before training), then KD-based QAT with a Confidence-Aware KL (CAKLD) objective in self-distillation; 3-bit (nf3) and 2-bit (int); teacher-generated data of only 3000 WikiText + 5000 Alpaca samples for Llama-2-7B — [BitDistiller README](https://github.com/DD-DuDa/BitDistiller) **[primary]**; [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.7) **[snippet]**.
- **ParetoQ** (Liu et al., Meta; [arXiv 2502.02631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02631); NeurIPS 2025 [proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/83b17fb3369b1effa97ca5409526b02e-Abstract-Conference.html)): unified QAT framework across 1/1.58/2/3/4 bits with bit-specific quantizers; key claims **[abstract/snippet]**: "a shift between 2 and 3 bits: at 3 bits and above, fine-tuned models stay near their pre-trained behavior, while at 2 bits and below their internal representations change substantially"; optimal split is most of the budget in full-precision training and ~10% in QAT fine-tuning; a 600M ternary model beats the prior SOTA 3B ternary model; 2-bit and ternary lie on the Pareto frontier and 2-bit is recommended for hardware reasons. Repo tables **[primary]**: MobileLLM-ParetoQ-1B avg (8 zero-shot tasks): BF16 62.1, 4-bit 62.5, 3-bit 61.3, 2-bit 61.0, 1.58-bit 58.5, 1-bit 53.8; 600M: BF16 58.2, 4-bit 59.2, 3-bit 58.5, 2-bit 56.6, 1.58-bit 55.5, 1-bit 50.3; ternary comparison vs "1-bit era" baselines (600M: 55.5 vs 48.1; 3B: 61.9 vs 54.5) — [ParetoQ README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ParetoQ). README caveats: paper numbers from Meta-internal code; the released BF16 reference models were "trained on a more advanced data and achieve higher scores" (confound for FP-vs-quantized gaps).
- **Scaling Law for QAT** (Chen et al., HKU/ByteDance Seed; [arXiv 2505.14302](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14302); ICML 2026 spotlight, [PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/chen26gl.html)): models W4A4 quantization error as a function of model size N, training tokens D and quantization group size G from 268 experiments; error "falls as models grow, and rises with more training tokens and coarser groups"; decomposes weight vs activation error and identifies FC2-input outliers as dominant, proposing 8-bit for the FC2 input; the INT4-fitted law "reportedly also predicts FP4 behavior" **[abstract/snippets]**.
- **Apple QAT scaling law** (Dremov et al., Sept 2025; [arXiv 2509.22935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22935v1)): a loss scaling law across QAT/full-precision compute splits and QAT bit widths, used to predict the optimal QAT bit width under a memory constraint; proposes fusing the LR cooldown with QAT to save compute **[abstract]**.
- **"Low-bit quantization favors undertrained LLMs"** (Ouyang et al., Nov 2024; [arXiv 2411.17691](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17691v1)): larger models or fewer training tokens suffer less quantization degradation; extrapolates that low-bit PTQ "may not be desirable" for models trained on ~100T tokens **[abstract]**. ParetoQ explicitly notes prior low-bit scaling-law conclusions "conflict, with some favoring 4–6 bits and others ~1.58 bits" and argues quantizer/training design changes the answer — [ParetoQ HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02631v2) **[snippet]**.
- **LR-QAT** ([arXiv 2406.06385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06385v3)) is a memory-lean QAT variant with low-rank auxiliary weights (not examined further) **[snippet]**.

### Inferences
- Data/compute as a function of bits: all evidence says QAT cost is dominated by *bits*, not size — 4-bit needs essentially PTQ-level calibration (thousands of samples; EfficientQAT 4096), 2-bit needs end-to-end token-level training on the order of 10% of pretraining tokens (ParetoQ) or tens of GPU-hours with ~100k–1M tokens (EfficientQAT, BitDistiller's 8k samples). No paper reports a fitted "QAT tokens needed to reach x% of FP" curve as a joint function of (bits, N); the two scaling laws each hold one axis fixed (W4A4 only; or compute split only).
- The 2↔3-bit transition is consistent with a simple capacity argument (3 bits give 8 levels — enough that nearest-level rounding preserves most pretrained directions; 2 bits/ternary require re-learning), but nobody has formalized it (e.g., via representational similarity bounds or via the fraction of weights whose quantization cell changes under QAT). This is a clear theory gap.
- Contradiction to note: Ouyang et al. (more tokens → more fragile) vs. ParetoQ/EfficientQAT (strong post-hoc QAT recovers even heavily trained LLaMA-3). Chen et al.'s law partially reconciles this (error rises with D but can be offset by N and finer G), but it is fitted only on W4A4 at ≤~1B scale (scale limits not confirmed in-session).
- Evidence quality: the ParetoQ "frontier" is drawn on MobileLLM-sized models (125M–1.5B) plus one LLaMA-3-8B point; the LLM-QAT/ParetoQ READMEs both admit paper numbers came from internal code. Llama-3's much larger 2-bit drop (EfficientQAT table) supports the "over-trained models are harder to quantize" thesis at least for PTQ-initialized QAT.

### Gaps
- No 2026 paper framing a *theory* of the bit-width transition was found (search returned only Chen et al. and Dremov et al.); searches for "QAT scaling law 2026" produced no newer law beyond these.
- The exact functional form and fitted coefficients of Chen et al. (2505.14302) and Dremov et al. (2509.22935) could not be read (arXiv blocked).
- No study found on QAT applied to *RL-post-trained reasoning models* (e.g., QAT on Qwen3/R1-distills) or on whether QAT degrades long-CoT behavior differently than PTQ.
- Whether ParetoQ's ~10% QAT budget rule depends on bit width (lower bits needing a larger share) was asserted in snippets but not quantified.

## KQ3. Quantization × RL post-training (quantized rollouts, rollout–trainer precision mismatch, importance-sampling corrections, quantization noise as exploration)

### Takeaway
Two threads converged in 2025–2026. (a) *Systems*: FP8/INT8 (and now FP4/NVFP4) rollout engines make GRPO/DAPO/PPO off-policy; truncated or masked importance sampling (TIS/MIS, C≈2) restores BF16-level accuracy, and verl ships a three-policy "decoupled PPO" framework with token/sequence/geometric estimators plus KL/χ² diagnostics. (b) *Algorithmic*: QeRL (NVIDIA, ICLR 2026) argues NVFP4 quantization noise *helps* by raising policy entropy, and adds an adaptive noise schedule; AIS (2026) shows the quantized-vs-FP KL grows during training so the mismatch is a non-stationary mix of useful exploration early and harmful bias late. Theory is limited to classical IS bias/variance bookkeeping (token-level bias O(T²Δ_max), TV/χ² framing, the "length trap"); there is no policy-gradient analysis specific to quantization noise, and the two threads (noise-as-exploration vs. mismatch-as-bias) have not been reconciled on common benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
**Mismatch and TIS (FlashRL line)**
- "Your Efficient RL Framework Secretly Brings You Off-Policy RL Training" (Yao, Liu, Zhang, Dong, Shang, Gao; Aug 2025) — [Notion blog](https://fengyao.notion.site/off-policy-rl); conference version "On the Rollout-Training Mismatch in Modern RL Systems" (NeurIPS 2025) — [neurips.cc](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/124259). Claim: rollout and training backends "can produce significantly different token probabilities, implicitly turning on-policy RL into off-policy"; TIS "improves training quality in standard BF16 RL with hybrid engines and preserves downstream performance relative to BF16 rollouts when using 8-bit rollouts" **[snippet]**. An alphaXiv listing [2510.11345](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.11345) appeared in the same results but I could not confirm it is this paper **[ID unverified]**.
- **FlashRL** (Liu, Yao et al., Aug 2025) — [blog](https://fengyao.notion.site/flash-rl); [repo](https://github.com/yaof20/Flash-RL) **[primary README]**: patches vLLM to generate rollouts in INT8/FP8 "with accurate rollout logprob" and supports online weight updates into a quantized engine; Figure 1 compares AIME accuracy of Qwen2.5-32B DAPO with INT8 vs BF16 rollouts and training throughput (updates/hour). Blog claim (snippet): quantized rollout *without* TIS incurs a significant drop vs BF16; *with* TIS it matches BF16+TIS. README caveats: quantized rollout "is only recommended… for large models (14B+, preferably 32B+) and long CoT generation (DAPO rather than GSM8K)" because of online-quantization overhead; INT8/fp8_channel need an offline profiling step comparing BF16 vs a quantized checkpoint; a hybrid mode runs BF16 on some DP workers and 8-bit on others "to reduce the gap"; only verl + vLLM supported; kernels for online quantization are still a roadmap item; recipe labelled "Early Alpha". Example run names `flash-int8-TIS-2` (GSM8K, 0.5B) and `flash-int8-TIS-8` (DAPO-32B) suggest truncation thresholds 2 and 8 respectively (my reading of script arguments, not stated prose). The FP8 32B DAPO run is listed "In Progress".
- **verl rollout-correction math doc** (Yingru Li, updated 2025-11-04) — [rollout_corr_math.md](https://github.com/verl-project/verl/blob/main/docs/algo/rollout_corr_math.md) **[primary]**: formalizes three policies (π_rollout = behavior, π_old = proximal anchor, π_θ) following Decoupled PPO (Hilton et al. 2021); Drift 1 (rollout→old, corrected by IS weight ρ_t = π_old/π_rollout) vs Drift 2 (old→θ, PPO clipping). Token-TIS: w_t = min(ρ_t, C_IS), C_IS e.g. 2.0; stated bias "O(T² Δ_max) where T is sequence length and Δ_max is maximum per-token policy divergence"; sequence-level IS is unbiased but high-variance; Seq-MIS rejects sequences with ρ(τ) > C to filter "toxic tails"; the **length trap**: multiplicative ratios make ρ ≈ 1.1^100 ≈ 13,780 for a 100-token sequence, so sequence-level rejection "preferentially learns from short, shallow answers and rejects long chains of thought" (termed "Context Collapse"); geometric-mean RS normalizes per token; K3 estimator ρ − log ρ − 1 equals KL(π_rollout‖π_old) in expectation; diagnostics: KL, PPL ratio = (∏ρ_t)^{-1/|T|}, χ² = E[ρ²]−1. Usage guide **[primary]**: default `rollout_is_threshold: 2.0`; typical token-level 1.5–5.0, sequence-level 2.0–10.0; IcePop-style two-sided thresholds "0.5_5.0"; presets "validated with tens of thousands of GPU hours" — [rollout_corr.md](https://github.com/verl-project/verl/blob/main/docs/algo/rollout_corr.md). Cites the blog series "When Speed Kills Stability: Demystifying RL Collapse from the Training-Inference Mismatch" (Liu, Li et al., Sept 2025; [richardli.xyz/rl-collapse](https://richardli.xyz/rl-collapse)) with "TV distance for bias and χ²-divergence for variance", and "Trust Region Masking for Long-Horizon LLM RL" ([arXiv 2512.23075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23075)); also references Marek & Ryabinin 2026 ([arXiv 2609.20807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20807)) for an IS batch-normalization option **[content unverified]**.
- **FP8-RL** (NVIDIA, Jan 2026; [arXiv 2601.18150](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18150)): veRL-based FP8 rollout stack with blockwise W8A8 linears and FP8 KV cache, corrected with token-level TIS/MIS; "up to 44% rollout throughput gains while preserving learning behavior comparable to BF16 baselines" **[abstract]**.
- **Jet-RL** ([arXiv 2601.14243](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2601.14243)): argues for a *single* FP8 precision flow for both training and rollout; says the common "BF16-train, FP8-rollout" setup "suffers from severe instability and accuracy degradation, particularly for long sequences and challenging tasks"; up to 1.16× end-to-end throughput on 8B with near-BF16 accuracy **[abstract]**.
- **QuRL** ([arXiv 2602.13953](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2602.13953)): quantized actor for rollouts plus an Adaptive Clipping Range that adjusts the PPO clip based on the FP-vs-quantized policy ratio to prevent collapse **[abstract]**.
- **HiFloat4 / Rollout-ResQ** ([arXiv 2607.26515](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26515)): end-to-end FP4 RL; finds "rollout activation quantization, not training-side error, drives most of the degradation", that *raising* training precision while keeping FP4 rollouts makes accuracy *worse*, and that outliers cause activation underflow to zero under FP4; a sparse residual correction on the rollout matmul narrows the BF16 gap on Qwen2.5 from 4.9% to 1.1% **[abstract]**.
- **AIS: Adaptive Importance Sampling for Quantized RL** ([arXiv 2605.13907](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13907), 2026): the KL between quantized and full-precision policies "grows monotonically during training as the policy sharpens", so a fixed IS correction has limits; the mismatch is non-stationary — "it can help exploration early in training and then become a source of bias as the policy sharpens" **[abstract]**.
- LMSYS/SGLang "MXFP8/NVFP4 RL" post (2026-07-29) extends FP8 and INT4 QAT recipes to end-to-end MXFP8 RL and per-token NVFP4 MoE experts — [lmsys.org](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-07-29-mxfp8-nvfp4-rl) **[snippet]**.

**Quantization noise as exploration (QeRL line)**
- **QeRL** (Huang et al., NVIDIA/MIT/HKU/Tsinghua; [arXiv 2510.11696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11696), 13 Oct 2025; ICLR 2026 [poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006405); [NVIDIA page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/eai/publication/qerl); [repo](https://github.com/NVlabs/QeRL)). Method **[primary README + abstract]**: NVFP4 base weights + LoRA, rollouts through vLLM with a Marlin NVFP4×BF16 kernel, built on TRL/open-r1; "Quantization noise increases policy entropy, enhancing exploration" and "brings higher initialized entropy… accelerating the increase of reward"; Adaptive Quantization Noise (AQN) with a schedule (`sigma_start`, `sigma_end`, `num_stages`) that the README says must be tuned per dataset; optimal LR "roughly 10× higher than vanilla LoRA". Results (abstract/landing pages): first RL training of a 32B LLM on one H100-80GB; rollout speedup >1.5× vs QLoRA and ~1.3× vs BF16 LoRA (v1), restated as 1.2–1.5× end-to-end vs BF16 LoRA and 1.5–2.0× vs QLoRA on the NVIDIA page (the numbers differ between versions — [alphaXiv note](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.11696v1)); Qwen2.5-7B reaches GSM8K 90.8% and MATH-500 77.4%, described as matching full-parameter fine-tuning; "in GRPO, quantized model delivers better converged reward score and faster reward growth" (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct reward curve). Hardware requirement: NVFP4-capable GPUs (RTX 5090/H100/B100). Evaluation scripts cover GSM8K, AMC23, AIME24/25, MATH-500 only.

### Inferences
- The two threads make opposite-sign claims about the same quantity (rollout policy ≠ trainer policy): FlashRL/verl/Jet-RL/HiFloat4 treat it as bias to be corrected or eliminated; QeRL treats it as beneficial entropy. AIS is the first to say both are true at different training phases, which implies the optimal correction strength should be *scheduled* — exactly what QeRL's AQN does heuristically, but QeRL (as far as I could verify) does not use importance weights at all, and the IS papers do not inject noise deliberately. A unified treatment (noise schedule + IS correction + entropy target) has not been published.
- The theory available is generic off-policy IS theory (bias O(T²Δ_max) for token-level truncation; unbiased-but-exploding sequence-level weights; length trap). None of it models *quantization-specific* structure: the mismatch is deterministic given weights (not sampling noise), grows with policy sharpness (AIS), concentrates at high-entropy tokens (Meta 2606.00206, see KQ4), and scales with sequence length — a natural target for a token-position-aware or entropy-aware correction.
- Evidence quality: QeRL's "matches full FT" is on GSM8K/MATH-500 for 7B with LoRA baselines; no AIME/GPQA/code comparisons, no comparison against TIS-corrected BF16 rollouts, and speedup numbers were revised between versions. FlashRL's headline figure is a single 32B DAPO run, and its own README says quantized rollouts are not worth it below 14B. HiFloat4's "raising training precision makes FP4 rollouts worse" is a striking claim that directly contradicts the "trainer-side fix" philosophy and should be reproduced.
- Scheduler/kernel reality: online re-quantization after each update is the bottleneck (FlashRL profiling step; QeRL NVFP4 requirement; FP8-RL FP8 KV cache). This is why FP4 rollouts with LoRA (QeRL) are attractive — the base never changes, only the adapter.

### Gaps
- No paper found that *jointly* measures entropy, KL(π_quant‖π_FP), and reward over training to settle whether quantization noise improves final accuracy or only speeds early reward (QeRL shows reward curves; AIS shows KL curves; nobody shows both on the same run).
- No policy-gradient bias/variance bound specialized to quantized rollouts (e.g., in terms of per-token KL induced by weight perturbation, or bit-width) was found; the O(T²Δ_max) statement in verl docs is uncited in the doc.
- Blocked follow-ups: "Defeating the Training-Inference Mismatch via FP16" (2025) and the IcePop/Ring-team masking work were not retrievable; FlashRL's AIME numbers and TIS constant were not readable (blog blocked).
- QeRL at >7B on hard benchmarks, QeRL + TIS, and any 2026 follow-up to QeRL were not found.

## KQ4. Quantization × reasoning / long chain-of-thought / test-time compute

### Takeaway
Three systematic studies (Liu et al. COLM 2025; "Quantization Meets Reasoning"; "When Reasoning Meets Compression" ICLR 2026) agree that W8A8 and W4A16 are near-lossless for R1-distilled reasoners, that 4-bit activation/KV or ≤3-bit weights hurt, and that harder tasks, smaller models and *RL-trained* (vs distilled) reasoners degrade most. The 2026 Meta FAIR paper supplies the first mechanism: quantized reasoners get *longer* CoTs because token-level KL spikes at high-entropy positions where hesitation tokens ("wait", "but", "alternatively") are over-sampled, and up to 52% of failures contain the right intermediate answer that is never committed; a logit penalty on those tokens cuts length 12–23% and overthinking errors up to 58%. No paper offers a quantitative error-accumulation-per-token model or a perplexity→accuracy mapping for long CoT; "longer CoT amplifies error" is asserted as a hypothesis, while Liu et al. show quantized models still *benefit* from more test-time compute up to a point.

### Cited Findings
- **"Quantization Hurts Reasoning? An Empirical Study on Quantized Reasoning Models"** (Liu, Sun, Zhang, Bai, Yu, Yu, Yuan, Hou; [arXiv 2504.04823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04823), Apr 2025, rev. Aug 2025; COLM 2025 per repo) — [repo](https://github.com/ruikangliu/Quantized-Reasoning-Models) **[primary README]**: models DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/14B/32B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B/70B, QwQ-32B; quantizers AWQ & GPTQ (W3/W4 A16), KVQuant* & QuaRot-KV (KV3/KV4), SmoothQuant (W8A8KV8), QuaRot & FlatQuant (W4A4KV4, W8A8KV8); benchmarks AIME-120 (= AIME-90 + AIME-2025), MATH-500, GSM8K, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench; three seeds; reports both accuracy and *response length (number of reasoning steps)*; uses self-generated reasoning data from NuminaMath-1.5 as GPTQ calibration; includes visualizations of the "dataset domain gap" (calibration vs reasoning traces) and of a *bias term in the K cache*; real-quantized AWQ/GPTQ W4G128 checkpoints released. Abstract-level findings **[snippet]**: W8A8 and W4A16 are "near-lossless"; "more difficult tasks and RL-based reasoning models incur more severe performance degradation"; under test-time scaling "quantized models follow the same pattern as BF16 models: accuracy improves as test-time compute grows over a range, but can decline beyond it"; a secondary summary adds that 4-bit "causes significant degradation, especially on harder tasks and smaller models" and that quantization errors "might accumulate over long chain-of-thought sequences" (stated as hypothesis) — [HF/aggregator summary](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.00206.md).
- **"Quantized Reasoning Models Think They Need to Think Longer, but They Do Not"** (Meta FAIR; [arXiv 2606.00206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00206), submitted 29 May 2026) **[abstract/snippets]**: aggressive PTQ lowers accuracy *and lengthens* CoT across math, coding and science QA; "in up to 52% of the quantized models' failures, models reach the right answer in intermediate reasoning steps but do not output it as a final answer"; token-level KL between quantized and FP distributions is highest at high next-token-entropy positions, where quantized models over-sample hesitation markers; a training-free logit penalty on those markers cuts CoT length 12–23% across 5 models (1.5B–32B), 3 quantization methods and 5 benchmarks, reducing overthinking errors by up to 58%.
- **"Quantization Meets Reasoning"** (Li, Su, Yang et al.; v1 [arXiv 2501.03035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03035v3), Jan 2025; expanded [arXiv 2505.11574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11574v4), May 2025, rev. Jan 2026, "under review at ICLR 2026") **[abstracts]**: AWQ/GPTQ on Llama-3 cause large losses concentrated in "numerical computation and reasoning planning"; accuracy drops "up to 69.81% in harder settings"; "a few hundred curated examples and minutes of single-GPU compute recover much of the 4-bit weight performance".
- **"When Reasoning Meets Compression"** (retitled "Understanding the Effects of LLMs Compression on Large Reasoning Models"; [arXiv 2504.02010](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02010v3), v3 Mar 2026; ICLR 2026 [poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011689)) **[abstract/snippets]**: evaluates 2.51/1.73/1.58-bit dynamically quantized DeepSeek-R1, LLaMA/Qwen distills, and SparseGPT pruning on AIME-2024, FOLIO, Temporal Sequences, MuSiQue; "parameter count has a much greater impact on LRMs' knowledge memorization than on their reasoning capability"; "shorter outputs generally scored higher than longer ones" for both R1 and compressed variants; v3 adds mechanistic interpretation.
- **Trustworthiness under compression** (background): "Decoding Compressed Trust" (Hong et al., ICML 2024, PMLR 235:18611–18633; [arXiv 2403.15447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15447v1)): 4-bit quantization retains trustworthiness of the original across 8 dimensions, pruning hurts even at 50% sparsity, 3-bit "significantly reduce[s] trustworthiness"; moderate quantization sometimes *improved* ethics/fairness scores **[abstract]**.
- Self-consistency/test-time-scaling literature found (Certified Self-Consistency [arXiv 2510.17472](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17472v2); CGES [arXiv 2511.02603](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02603v1); PETS [arXiv 2602.16745](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16745)) does **not** address quantization — i.e., no paper couples quantization with majority-vote or best-of-N theory **[snippets]**.

### Inferences
- "Which capabilities degrade first": numerical computation and planning (2505.11574), hardest math (AIME) before GSM8K (Liu et al.), and *commitment/termination* behavior (2606.00206) rather than knowledge; knowledge is more parameter-count-sensitive than reasoning (2504.02010). RL-trained reasoners (QwQ) are more fragile than SFT-distilled ones (Liu et al.) — consistent with RL sharpening the policy (AIS, KQ3) so small logit perturbations flip more decisions.
- "Is degradation a function of output length": the data say quantized models *produce* longer outputs and longer outputs correlate with failure, but causality is entangled: length is itself a symptom (hesitation-token over-sampling at high-entropy positions). The simplest consistent model is a per-step branching process: quantization adds noise mostly at high-entropy "decision" tokens; a longer trace has more such tokens, so the probability of at least one derailment grows roughly like 1 − (1 − p_q)^k where k is the number of high-entropy forks — but no paper fits such a model.
- Test-time compute is not a free fix: Liu et al. report the same rise-then-decline as BF16, and 2606.00206 shows the extra tokens are often wasted hesitation, so the right intervention is decoding-side (termination penalties, entropy-aware sampling) or quantization-side (protect high-entropy positions), not more budget.
- Evidence-quality flags: most studies use *fake* quantization (Liu et al. provide real W4 kernels only for AWQ/GPTQ); AIME-level benchmarks have ≤120 items so 3-seed averages still have multi-point noise; results are on R1-distills/QwQ (early-2025 reasoners) — no Qwen3/gpt-oss-class results seen; the 2606.00206 fix is a decoding heuristic whose effect on *non-overthinking* failures is unreported.

### Gaps
- No quantitative "error-accumulation-per-token" model or perplexity→accuracy mapping for long CoT exists in the retrieved literature; no paper reports accuracy vs. *position of first divergence* from the FP trace.
- No study of self-consistency/majority voting under quantization (does quantization decorrelate samples and thereby help voting, or just add noise?).
- KV-cache quantization specifically for 10k+ token reasoning traces: Liu et al. include KV3/KV4 but the detailed length-dependence was not readable; no 2026 paper on this was found before the search budget ran out.
- No results on RL-trained reasoners *after* quantization-aware recovery (e.g., does QAT/QAD restore the lost "commitment" behavior?).

## KQ5. Quantization-aware distillation and post-quantization recovery

### Takeaway
Recovery after low-bit quantization is overwhelmingly done by (self-)distillation from the FP teacher on small, often self-generated datasets: LLM-QAT (data-free), BitDistiller (8k teacher samples, confidence-aware KL), EfficientQAT (4k calibration samples then E2E step-size training), ParetoQ (~10% budget), and "a few hundred curated examples" for reasoning recovery (2505.11574). NVIDIA's ModelOpt now ships quantization-aware distillation (QAD: frozen BF16 teacher, logit-KL to quantized student) and explicitly recommends it for models that went through multi-stage post-training (SFT/RL/merging) — i.e., exactly the reasoning models that degrade most. I found no paper doing QAT/QAD *on R1-distilled or RL-trained reasoners* with long-CoT evaluation, and no work on quantization-aware draft/speculative training.

### Cited Findings
- **LLM-QAT** (data-free): trains on text generated by the FP model itself; "does not need the original training data" — [arXiv 2305.17888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17888) **[abstract]**, [README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/LLM-QAT) **[primary]**.
- **BitDistiller**: CAKLD self-distillation objective; teacher data = 3000 WikiText + 5000 Alpaca generations for Llama-2-7B (code: 3000 samples for WizardCoder); claims to "surpass existing methods in both 3-bit and 2-bit… on general language understanding and complex reasoning benchmarks" with "fewer data and training resources" — [README](https://github.com/DD-DuDa/BitDistiller) **[primary]**, [ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.7) **[snippet]**.
- **EfficientQAT**: Block-AP uses 4096 training / 64 validation samples by default; E2E-QP "can adapt to different scenarios by changing the training datasets" (RedPajama or Alpaca) — [README](https://github.com/OpenGVLab/EfficientQAT) **[primary]**.
- **Self-Distilled Quantization** (2023; [arXiv 2307.05972](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.05972)): combines self-attention and output distillation with quantization for high compression of transformer LMs (pre-R1 era) **[abstract]**.
- **Small-data reasoning recovery**: "a few hundred curated examples and minutes of single-GPU compute recover much of the 4-bit weight performance" on math reasoning — [arXiv 2505.11574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11574v4) **[abstract]**; v1 reported that fine-tuning "on a small set of task-specific examples recovers much of that performance" — [arXiv 2501.03035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03035v3) **[abstract]**.
- **Reasoning-aware calibration** rather than training: Liu et al. generate GPTQ calibration data from the reasoning model itself (NuminaMath-1.5 prompts) and visualize the domain gap between generic calibration text and reasoning traces — [repo](https://github.com/ruikangliu/Quantized-Reasoning-Models) **[primary]**.
- **NVIDIA ModelOpt QAD**: "a frozen full-precision teacher supervises a quantized student through a logit-level KL-divergence loss… usually used after PTQ, with the original BF16 model as the teacher, to recover quality lost specifically to quantization"; recommended "especially for models that have passed through multi-stage post-training such as SFT, RL, or model merging"; costs more memory/compute than plain QAT because the teacher is resident — [ModelOpt QAT/QAD guide](https://nvidia.github.io/Model-Optimizer/pr-preview/pr-2255/guides/quantization_aware_training.html) **[snippet]**. The guide cites a paper "Quantization-Aware Distillation for NVFP4 Inference Accuracy Recovery" **[arXiv ID not found; unverified]**.
- **PEQA**-style scale-only recovery (NeurIPS 2023; [arXiv 2305.14152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14152v2)) restores sub-4-bit models by tuning scales only **[abstract]**.

### Inferences
- The field converged on "teacher = your own FP model" because any external data risks the domain-gap problem Liu et al. visualize; the open question is *what to distill on* for reasoning models (self-generated long CoTs are expensive; short generic text misses high-entropy decision points that 2606.00206 identifies as where quantization hurts).
- A natural but unexplored design is *position-weighted* QAD: upweight KL at high-entropy tokens (where quantized/FP KL is largest) — none of the retrieved papers do this.
- Recovery via LoRA (QLoRA-style) vs. via QAT of the quantized weights is never compared on reasoning models; the merge problem (KQ1) makes LoRA recovery awkward for deployment unless QA-LoRA-style constraints are used.
- Evidence quality: BitDistiller/EfficientQAT recovery claims are on 2023–2024 bases and zero-shot commonsense suites; 2505.11574's "few hundred examples" result is on Llama-3 base models with GPTQ/AWQ, not on long-CoT reasoners.

### Gaps
- No paper found applying QAT/QAD/self-distillation to R1-distills, QwQ, or Qwen3 with AIME/LiveCodeBench evaluation.
- No "quantization-aware speculative decoding / draft training" work surfaced in the searches run.
- The NVIDIA QAD-for-NVFP4 paper could not be located by ID.
- No study on whether recovery data should be *reasoning traces* vs *answers only* vs *generic text*, or how many tokens are needed as a function of bits for reasoners.

## KQ6. Safety, robustness and security consequences of quantization

### Takeaway
Quantization is now an established attack surface and an established *un-doer* of alignment interventions. (1) Attack: a benign-looking FP model can be constructed (via fine-tune → compute quantization-preimage box → PGD repair inside the box) so that its quantized version is malicious; this works for LLM.int8/NF4/FP4 (NeurIPS 2024), for all nine GGUF k-quant types (ICML 2025: 88.7% insecure-code gap), and — with outlier injection — even for data-dependent AWQ/GPTQ and GGUF I-quants, defeating noise-based defenses (ICML 2026). The theory is simply that the preimage of a quantized model is a product of intervals (a box) with nonzero volume; width is the attack budget. (2) Un-doing: utility-constrained unlearning leaves weights inside their original quantization cells, so 4-bit quantization restores forgotten knowledge from 21% to 83% (ICLR 2025); analogous dormant misalignment (Q-Misalign, ICLR 2025) and QLoRA-induced safety loss (AAAI 2026) exist. Evidence that quantization *reduces* risk is thinner: FP8/INT8 models are harder to jailbreak by bit-flip fault injection, 4-bit roughly preserves trustworthiness (ICML 2024), and quantization removes verbatim memorization faster than capability but not enough to be a privacy defense.

### Cited Findings
**Quantization-conditioned attacks**
- **Exploiting LLM Quantization** (Egashira, Vero, Staab, He, Vechev, ETH; NeurIPS 2024; [arXiv 2405.18137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18137v2), v2 Nov 2024; [NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/496720b3c860111b95ac8634349dcc88-Abstract-Conference.html), DOI 10.52202/079017-1319): three stages — fine-tune a malicious model, compute the constraints (intervals) characterizing all FP models that quantize to the same low-bit model, then PGD "removes the poisoned behavior from the full-precision weights while keeping them within the quantization constraints" **[snippet]**. The repo exposes `compute_box_4bit(method="nf4")` and `compute_box_int8` (exact boxes for NF4 and LLM.int8) and a GGUF emulator that uses "the range between original weights and dequantized weights" as a proxy interval because exact GGUF intervals are hard to derive; attack scenarios: vulnerable code generation (SafeCoder), content injection and over-refusal (AutoPoison), jailbreak — [eth-sri repo](https://github.com/eth-sri/llm-quantization-attack) **[primary]**.
- **Mind the Gap: A Practical Attack on GGUF Quantization** (Egashira et al., ICML 2025, PMLR 267:15038–15065; [arXiv 2505.23786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23786v1)): attacks nine GGUF data types used by llama.cpp/ollama; reported benign-vs-quantized gaps of 88.7% (insecure code generation), 85.0% (targeted content injection), 30.1% (benign instruction refusal); concludes "quantization complexity alone is not a sufficient defense" **[abstract]**.
- **Widening the Gap: Exploiting LLM Quantization via Outlier Injection** ([arXiv 2605.15152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15152); ICML 2026 [page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/67885)): outlier injection extends the attack to data-dependent AWQ and GPTQ and GGUF I-quants and "renders existing noise-based defenses ineffective" **[abstract]**.
- **Durable Quantization Conditioned Misalignment (Q-Misalign)** (Dong, Li, Guo; ICLR 2025; [poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/31042); [proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/376b1b131609e764f687afca832e62b3-Paper-Conference.pdf); no arXiv ID found): misalignment "remains dormant in a full-precision LLM but becomes exploitable post-quantization"; a contrastive task-vector method makes it survive downstream fine-tuning; "significantly increases jailbreak success rates in quantized models, while preserving model utility and safety alignment in full precision" **[abstract]**.
- **AGENTQ** ([arXiv 2609.14060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14060)): quantization-conditioned backdoors on LLM *agents* **[title/snippet only]**. **Adversarial Contrastive Learning for LLM Quantization Attacks** ([arXiv 2601.02680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02680)) **[title only]**.
- **Defenses**: the original paper's Gaussian-noise-before-quantization defense is referenced by "Widening the Gap" as defeated **[snippet]**; QuantGuard ("Breaking the Rounding Trap": differentiable rounding control variables + output-consistency, small calibration set, tested on six LLMs with INT8/FP4/NF4) and QVec ("Quantization as a Malicious Task": treat FP→quantized weight difference as a task vector and correct it; Yang, Tsai, Yu, 2026) appear only in aggregator listings — [Wizwand 1](https://www.wizwand.com/paper/6a434d90ba73165dbb743b53), [Wizwand 2](https://www.wizwand.com/paper/6a34cd18206542251d7c5f38) **[IDs unverified]**. Q-resafe ([arXiv 2506.20251](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2506.20251), June 2025) proposes quantization-aware safety patching that "re-aligns the safety of quantized LLMs with their pre-quantization counterparts" **[abstract]**.

**Quantization undoing alignment / unlearning**
- **Catastrophic Failure of LLM Unlearning via Quantization** (Zhang, Wang et al.; ICLR 2025; [arXiv 2410.16454](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16454v3); [poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28528)): "for unlearning methods with utility constraints, the unlearned model retains an average of 21% of the intended forgotten knowledge in full precision, which significantly increases to 83% after 4-bit quantization" **[abstract]**. Repo **[primary]**: MUSE News/Books targets (Llama-2-7B tokenizer), methods GA, GA+GDR, GA+KLR, NPO, NPO+GDR, NPO+KLR, RMU and the proposed SURE variants (`*_sure`, saliency-threshold e.g. 90 + larger LR); evaluation flags for 4-bit and 8-bit; metrics VerbMem-F, PrivLeak, KnowMem-F, KnowMem-R — [FailureLLMUnlearning](https://github.com/zzwjames/FailureLLMUnlearning). Follow-up: QUAIL, quantization-aware unlearning for misinformation ([arXiv 2601.15538](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15538v1)) **[title/snippet]**.
- **Unveiling AI Safety in Fine-tuning Quantized Model** (Hai Le; AAAI 2026; [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/42317)): "aggressive schemes such as 4-bit QLoRA can substantially degrade safety alignment"; mitigates by projecting quantized parameters back into safety-aligned subspaces **[abstract]**.

**Robustness, trust, privacy, watermarks**
- **Fault-injection jailbreaks** (Zahran et al., July 2025; [arXiv 2507.03236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03236v2)): bit-flip attacks reach >80% ASR on FP16, but within a 25-perturbation budget FP8 and INT8 show ASR <20% and <50% respectively; vulnerabilities "can still persist, especially through post-attack quantization" **[abstract]**.
- **Decoding Compressed Trust** (ICML 2024; [arXiv 2403.15447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15447v1)): 4-bit quantization ≈ original trustworthiness; 3-bit significantly worse; pruning worse than quantization **[abstract]**.
- **Memorization/privacy**: "Bits and Memories: Measuring Verbatim Extraction Across LLM Quantization" (Sasi, 2026; [arXiv 2607.25451](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25451v1)): on Pythia, "quantization removes verbatim memorization faster than it removes general capability", yet the largest 4-bit model "still reproduces most memorized sequences", so quantization "is not a privacy defense" **[abstract]**; Haque et al. ([arXiv 2508.00128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00128), July 2025) study membership-inference risk of quantized *code* LLMs **[abstract]**.
- **Watermarks**: EmMark embeds watermarks into already-quantized weights and reports 100% extraction on OPT/LLaMA-2 ([arXiv 2402.17938](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17938)); EditMark reports 100% extraction after Int-8 (Int-4 not in snippet; [arXiv 2510.16367](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16367)); "quantization watermarking" hides the mark in the FP-vs-INT8 gap ([arXiv 2404.05188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.05188)); knowledge-injection watermarks survive quantization while "quantization attacks have a greater impact on backdoor-based watermarking" ([arXiv 2311.09535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09535)); invariant-based weight watermark claims robustness to quantization on Llama3/Phi3/Gemma ([arXiv 2507.08288](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08288v1)) **[all abstracts]**.

### Inferences
- A single geometric fact underlies all of KQ6: for rounding-based quantizers the preimage Q⁻¹(Q(W)) is an axis-aligned box of nonzero volume; its *width* is simultaneously (a) the adversary's budget for hiding behavior (Egashira), (b) the region in which gradient-based unlearning/alignment can move without changing the deployed model (Zhang et al.), (c) a place to hide watermarks (2404.05188), and (d) the reason Gaussian-noise defenses work only if the noise exceeds the box width — which outlier injection circumvents by *widening* the box (2605.15152). Nobody has written this down as a unified theorem (e.g., a bound relating box volume / scale magnitude to attackable behavior change or to unlearning durability).
- Opposite-direction effects coexist: quantization can *remove* safety fine-tuning that lives in small deltas (QLoRA safety loss; unlearning reversal) and can *add* adversary-planted behavior (Q-attacks); both stem from alignment interventions being low-magnitude. Interventions with large, salient weight changes (SURE) are the natural countermeasure — but SURE's cost to utility and its interaction with 8-bit were not readable.
- Data-dependent quantizers (GPTQ/AWQ) were believed safer because the attacker cannot know the calibration set; 2605.15152 shows that planting outliers makes the scale (and hence the box) attacker-controlled regardless of calibration data. Defenders now need *calibration-data-independent* detection (e.g., checking FP-vs-quantized behavioral divergence), which is what Q-resafe/QVec attempt.
- Evidence quality: attack papers use small/mid models (StarCoder/Phi-2/Gemma-2b/Qwen2.5-1.5B per repo configs) and narrow behaviors; unlearning results are on MUSE with Llama-2-7B only; the AAAI 2026 QLoRA-safety claim and fault-injection numbers are abstract-only. No study evaluates these attacks on reasoning models or under QAT/QAD (which changes the box).

### Gaps
- No formal theory of "quantization-induced behavior switch" beyond the box construction; no bound on achievable behavior change as a function of bit width/group size, and no result on whether QAT/QAD (which trains the quantized model directly) closes or widens the attack surface.
- Whether 8-bit also reverses unlearning (the repo has an 8-bit flag; the abstract only quotes 4-bit) could not be confirmed.
- Jailbreak/ASR numbers for standard (non-fault-injection) adversarial prompts across bit widths were not retrieved (searches returned attacks/defenses, not a clean "ASR vs bits" study); the Kumar et al. "Fine-tuning, quantization and LLMs: navigating unintended outcomes" paper was not reachable.
- Calibration-induced bias (safety behavior shifting with calibration domain) was not found as a dedicated study (see KQ8).

## KQ7. Quantized models in long-horizon agentic / tool-use / instruction-following settings

### Takeaway
Evidence is sparse and partly contradictory: a 2025 benchmark (T-Eval function calling) finds GPTQ/AWQ preserve agentic ability far better than pruning, while a 2026 τ²-bench study finds *no statistically significant* aggregate change from quantization — but shows this is an artifact of the benchmark's ten-error budget: shrinking the budget to two errors re-exposes a 17-point gap, and quantization acts as an "amplifier of existing failure modes" rather than a new failure class. There is no multi-turn, long-horizon theory, and the many blog-level reports of "silent tool-call failures" at INT4 are unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Flat Score, Amplified Failures: How the Error Budget Masks Damage in Quantized LLM Agents** ([arXiv 2607.27275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27275), 2026) **[abstract]**: τ²-bench, two model families (dense and MoE), two domains; "no score change survives multiple-comparison correction, and the largest paired decline, 2.0 points, sits within 1.3× the run-to-run noise floor"; "shrinking the budget to two errors re-exposes a score gap of 17 points, and only in the one cell where quantization added error volume"; "the failure set is identical across precisions, so quantization is not a new failure class: it is an amplifier of existing ones".
- **Can Compressed LLMs Truly Act? An Empirical Evaluation of Agentic Capabilities in LLM Compression** ([arXiv 2505.19433](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19433), 2025) **[abstract]**: on T-Eval function calling/tool use, "quantization approaches, specifically GPTQ and AWQ, maintain model performance more effectively than sparsification techniques such as SparseGPT and Wanda".
- **AGENTQ** ([arXiv 2609.14060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14060)) studies quantization-conditioned backdoors specifically in LLM agents **[title/snippet]** — the security and capability threads meet here.
- Non-peer-reviewed reports (flagged, unverified): a dev.to write-up measuring BFCL on 0.6B–1.7B models with 3 seeds/bootstrap CIs — [dev.to](https://dev.to/happynood/does-quantization-break-tool-calling-i-measured-it-on-a-4gb-laptop-gpu-bfcl-3-seeds-bootstrap-185l); a news post claiming INT4 *KV-cache* quantization caused non-recovering tool-call divergence while INT8 recovered — [mer.vin](https://mer.vin/news/local-llm-quantization-benchmarks-reveal-silent-tool-call-failures/); a blog claiming 4-bit retains ~97% of FP16 on agentic tasks while 1-bit collapses tool-call validity to 36% — [dailyaiworld](https://dailyaiworld.com/blogs/qwen38-27b-quantization-benchmarks-bit-holds-up-bit). None have primary sources.

### Inferences
- The "error budget masks damage" finding is methodologically important beyond agents: any pass@k-style or tolerance-based metric (τ-bench, SWE-bench with retries, self-consistency) can hide quantization damage that compounds over turns; the right metric is per-step error rate or failure-count distributions, not aggregate pass rates.
- "Amplifier, not new failure class" matches the reasoning findings (KQ4: quantization sharpens existing hesitation/termination weaknesses at high-entropy forks) and suggests a shared per-decision noise model across reasoning and agentic settings.
- Evidence quality is low: two preprints, abstract-only, with no overlap in benchmarks; the 2025 paper's positive result is relative to pruning, not to FP16.

### Gaps
- No study of quantized models in *long-horizon* (dozens of tool calls) or multi-agent settings; no analysis of per-turn error compounding (e.g., failure probability vs. number of tool calls) was found.
- IFEval-style instruction-following vs. bit width: the targeted search was blocked by the budget limit; no source retrieved.
- No results on quantization + RL-trained agentic models (e.g., tool-use RL) or on KV-cache quantization over long agent contexts beyond blog anecdotes.

## KQ8. Calibration-data effects on behavior (domain shift, language bias)

### Takeaway
Calibration data materially changes *what* a quantized model loses: English-only calibration disproportionately hurts low-resource and non-Latin-script languages (55-language MT study; up to 3.52-PPL gains from multilingual calibration), generic text misses the activation statistics of reasoning traces (Liu et al. use self-generated reasoning calibration and visualize the domain gap), and some language–quantizer pairs get *worse* with matched calibration because activation-range distributions differ across languages. Theory is absent; everything is empirical and PTQ-centric.

### Cited Findings
- **Calibrating Beyond English** ([arXiv 2601.18306](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18306), Jan 2026): eight calibration settings with GPTQ and AWQ across 10 languages; non-English and multilingual calibration sets lowered perplexity vs English-only, multilingual mixes giving the largest reductions "up to 3.52 points"; some language–quantizer pairs degraded, attributed to "differences in activation range distributions across languages" **[abstract]**.
- **The Uneven Impact of Post-Training Quantization in Machine Translation** (Marie & Fujita, Aug 2025; [arXiv 2508.20893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20893v1)): 55 languages, five LLMs 1.7B–70B; 4-bit mostly preserves high-resource languages; low-resource/typologically diverse languages "degraded significantly, especially at 2-bit"; GGUF variants most consistent; language-matched calibration helps "mainly in low-bit settings"; models <10B degrade noticeably; quantization "can, in some cases, remove a model's ability to translate Indic languages into English" **[abstract]**.
- **How Does Quantization Affect Multilingual LLMs?** (Marchisio et al., 2024; arXiv 2407.03211): cited by 2601.18306 as showing quantization "disproportionately hurts multilingual models, with the worst effects on low-resource and non-Latin-script languages" — [secondary review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/how-does-quantization-affect-multilingual-llms) **[secondary only; ID as cited]**.
- **Reasoning-domain calibration**: Liu et al. generate GPTQ calibration sets from the reasoning model on NuminaMath-1.5 prompts and provide a "dataset domain gap" visualization (Figs. 8–9) plus a K-cache bias-term analysis (Figs. 10–11) — [repo](https://github.com/ruikangliu/Quantized-Reasoning-Models) **[primary]**.
- **Security angle**: data-dependent quantizers were assumed robust to the box attack because calibration data is unknown to the attacker; outlier injection removes that protection ([arXiv 2605.15152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15152)) **[abstract]** (see KQ6).

### Inferences
- Calibration selects which activation outliers/scales are protected; since reasoning traces, tool-call JSON, and low-resource scripts all have distinct token/activation statistics, "generic WikiText/C4 calibration" is a hidden source of the behavioral degradations reported in KQ4/KQ7. A plausible unifying hypothesis (untested): degradation on a target distribution scales with the mismatch between calibration and target *activation-range* statistics, which would also predict the language–quantizer pairs that got worse.
- GGUF's consistency across languages (Marie & Fujita) despite being data-free suggests that simple, data-free quantizers can be *more* robust to distribution shift than calibrated ones — a trade-off worth formalizing.

### Gaps
- The Williams & Aletras (ACL 2024) calibration-data study and any work on *safety/behavioral* bias induced by calibration domain were not retrievable (search budget exhausted).
- No study connects calibration data to RL-post-trained reasoners' long-CoT behavior beyond Liu et al.'s qualitative visualization.
- No theoretical model linking calibration-set statistics to downstream task loss.

## KQ9. Cross-paper contradictions and open theoretical gaps (synthesis for idea generation)

### Takeaway
The literature contains several unreconciled tensions and essentially no theory specific to quantization in post-training; each tension below is a candidate research question.

### Cited Findings
- **Noise helps vs noise hurts in RL**: QeRL claims quantization noise raises entropy and improves exploration/reward ([arXiv 2510.11696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11696)); FlashRL/verl/Jet-RL/HiFloat4 treat the same mismatch as bias causing collapse ([Flash-RL](https://github.com/yaof20/Flash-RL); [verl doc](https://github.com/verl-project/verl/blob/main/docs/algo/rollout_corr_math.md); [arXiv 2601.14243](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2601.14243); [arXiv 2607.26515](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26515)); AIS says both, phase-dependently ([arXiv 2605.13907](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13907)).
- **Fix on trainer side vs rollout side**: Jet-RL (unify precision), FP8-RL/FlashRL (correct via IS), HiFloat4 (fix rollout activations; raising trainer precision *worsens* FP4 rollouts) — three incompatible prescriptions ([arXiv 2601.14243](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2601.14243); [arXiv 2601.18150](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18150); [arXiv 2607.26515](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26515)).
- **Longer CoT = better vs worse under quantization**: quantized models still gain from more test-time compute up to a point (Liu et al., [arXiv 2504.04823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04823)) yet produce longer, worse CoTs and shorter outputs score higher ([arXiv 2606.00206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00206); [arXiv 2504.02010](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02010v3)).
- **Over-training makes quantization harder vs QAT recovers anyway**: Ouyang et al. ([arXiv 2411.17691](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17691v1)) vs ParetoQ/EfficientQAT ([arXiv 2502.02631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02631); [EfficientQAT README](https://github.com/OpenGVLab/EfficientQAT) shows Llama-3-8B losing ~8 pts at 2-bit vs ~4.7 for Llama-2-7B, partially supporting Ouyang).
- **Quantization preserves trust (4-bit) vs quantization destroys alignment/unlearning (4-bit)**: Decoding Compressed Trust ([arXiv 2403.15447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15447v1)) vs Zhang et al. ([arXiv 2410.16454](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16454v3)) and Hai Le AAAI 2026 ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/42317)) — the difference is whether the safety property was *pretrained-in* (robust) or *added by small-delta post-training* (fragile).
- **Quantization as privacy defense?** Removes verbatim memorization faster than capability but insufficient ([arXiv 2607.25451](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25451v1)); and it *restores* unlearned knowledge ([arXiv 2410.16454](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16454v3)) — the two findings are about different kinds of memorization (pretraining vs. post-hoc suppression) and have not been jointly studied.
- **Data-dependent quantizers as a defense**: assumed safe, broken by outlier injection ([arXiv 2605.15152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15152)).

### Inferences
- A single geometric object — the preimage box of a quantized model, whose width is set by scales/outliers — explains the attack surface (KQ6), the unlearning reversal (KQ6), the LoRA-merge failure (KQ1), and why small-delta post-training (safety SFT, unlearning, LoRA) is fragile under quantization while pretrained capabilities are robust. A theory paper formalizing "post-training deltas vs quantization cell width" (with bounds in terms of group size, scale distribution, and delta norm) would unify KQ1 and KQ6 and is absent from the literature.
- A second shared object — per-token KL(π_quant‖π_FP) concentrated at high-entropy positions and growing with policy sharpness — links the RL mismatch (KQ3), the overthinking mechanism (KQ4), and the agentic "amplifier" finding (KQ7). An entropy-/position-aware importance correction, QAD weighting, or decoding fix is an obvious unexplored design.
- Evaluation methodology is a systematic weakness: fake quantization, ≤3 seeds on ≤120-item benchmarks, tolerance-based metrics that hide compounding errors, and reliance on 7B–8B 2023–2024 bases.

### Gaps
- No unified theory (bounds) for any of: merge error, bit-width transition, quantized-rollout policy-gradient bias/variance, CoT error accumulation, or quantization-induced behavior switching.
- No experimental work combining the threads: QAT/QAD on RL-trained reasoners; QeRL-style noise + IS correction; quantization-conditioned attacks on QAT'd or RL-trained models; calibration-domain effects on safety.
- Several 2026 items (AIS, QuRL, Jet-RL, HiFloat4, Flat Score, Widening the Gap, AGENTQ, QuantGuard/QVec, Bits and Memories) were seen only as abstracts/listings; their experimental scale and exact numbers remain unverified.
