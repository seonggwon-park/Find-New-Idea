## KQ1. Rate–distortion / information-theoretic limits (Shannon bound, VQ vs SQ gap, lattices, TCQ/QTIP, TurboQuant/PolarQuant, distance of current quantizers from the bound)

### Takeaway
The only *rigorous* limits in play are classical: the Gaussian rate–distortion function D(R)=σ²2^{-2R}, the 1.53 dB / 0.2546-bit space-filling loss of scalar (cubic-cell) quantization, and lattice normalized second moments. TurboQuant (ICLR 2026) and Ordentlich–Polyanskiy (IEEE T-IT 2026) are the first LLM-motivated papers to prove matching lower and upper bounds for *their* problem (data-oblivious quantization of vectors on the sphere for inner products / for matrix products of i.i.d. Gaussian matrices). Both are (B): the lower bounds are for worst-case/Gaussian sources, and the achievability constants (≈2.7× for TurboQuant; "asymptotically optimal" only for i.i.d. Gaussian inputs for the nested-lattice scheme) leave a gap that practical 2-bit weight quantizers (E8P, TCQ) partially close *after* incoherence processing. No result bounds how far an LLM's *end-to-end loss* is from any information-theoretic limit.

### Cited Findings

**Classical limits (cited by book/paper; standard results).**
- Gaussian source rate–distortion function: for X~N(0,σ²) under MSE, D(R)=σ²2^{-2R}, equivalently R(D)=½log₂(σ²/D). Shannon lower bound (SLB) for any source with differential entropy h(X): D(R) ≥ (1/2πe)·2^{2(h(X)−R)}, with equality for Gaussian; SLB is asymptotically tight at high rate for smooth densities — Cover & Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed., Thm. 10.3.2 and §10.4 (no URL fetched; textbook).
- Consequence I derived from the SLB (standard exercise, verify): for a Laplacian source of variance σ² (h = log₂(2eb), σ²=2b²), D_SLB(R) = (e/π)·σ²·2^{-2R} ≈ 0.865·σ²·2^{-2R}, i.e., a Laplacian is ~0.63 dB *easier* than a Gaussian of equal variance at high rate. Relevant because post-incoherence LLM weights are near-Gaussian while raw weights/activations are heavier-tailed (see KQ2, KQ4).
- Scalar vs vector gap ("space-filling loss"): the normalized second moment (NSM) of the cube is G=1/12≈0.0833; the sphere lower bound is G→1/(2πe)≈0.0585 as n→∞; ratio 10·log₁₀(πe/6)=1.533 dB, equivalently ½log₂(2πe/12)=0.2546 bit/sample. Entropy-coded uniform scalar quantization at high rate loses exactly this 0.255 bit vs. the Shannon bound — Gish & Pierce, "Asymptotically efficient quantizing," IEEE T-IT 1968; Gersho & Gray, *Vector Quantization and Signal Compression* (1992), Ch. 5–6; Zamir, *Lattice Coding for Signals and Networks* (2014), Ch. 3 **[textbook; values standard]**.
- Lattice NSMs (Conway & Sloane, *Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups*, Table 2.3) **[values recalled, verify]**: Z (cube) 0.08333; A₂ (hex) 0.080188; D₄ 0.076603; E₈ 0.071682; BW₁₆ 0.068299; Λ₂₄ (Leech) 0.065771; sphere bound 0.058550. Granular gains over the cube: E₈ ≈0.65 dB, Leech ≈1.03 dB, of a possible 1.53 dB.
- High-rate (Panter–Dite) distortion of the *optimal* (Lloyd–Max) scalar quantizer for a Gaussian: D ≈ (√3π/2)·σ²·2^{-2R} ≈ 2.72·σ²·2^{-2R}, i.e., 4.35 dB above D(R) — Panter & Dite, Proc. IRE 1951; Gersho & Gray Ch. 6 **[recalled, standard]**. Fixed-rate Lloyd–Max Gaussian MSEs (Max 1960 tables): 1 bit 0.3634 (=1−2/π), 2 bits 0.1175, 3 bits 0.03454, 4 bits 0.009497 vs. D(R)=0.25, 0.0625, 0.015625, 0.003906 **[recalled; standard tables]**.
- Trellis-coded quantization (TCQ): Marcellin & Fischer, "Trellis coded quantization of memoryless and Gauss–Markov sources," IEEE Trans. Commun. 1990 — a 4-state trellis recovers ≈1.0 dB of the 1.53 dB granular gain for memoryless sources, 256 states ≈1.36 dB, at fixed rate with Viterbi encoding and a decoder whose complexity is independent of rate **[recalled; classic]**.

**LLM-motivated results found this session.**
- **QTIP (NeurIPS 2024 spotlight)** uses incoherence processing (random Hadamard) "to make LLM weight matrices approximately i.i.d. Gaussian, and then uses trellis coded quantization (TCQ) to quantize these weights with near-optimal distortion," quantizing 256-dimensional Gaussian sequences with a Viterbi-decoded "bitshift trellis" whose codebook size is decoupled from bitrate/effective dimension — [QTIP README (official)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/qtip/main/README.md); [QTIP NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2406.11235](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4). The paper's argument is that VQ codebooks grow exponentially in dimension, capping VQ (E8P in QuIP#) at dimension 8, while TCQ's stateful decoder removes that cap — [search summary of 2406.11235](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4). The "near-optimal" claim is a *Gaussian-source MSE comparison*, not a theorem about LLM loss. **[recalled, unverified]**: QTIP Table 1 reports i.i.d.-Gaussian MSE at 2 bits ≈0.069 for a 16-state bitshift trellis vs. E8P ≈0.089, Lloyd–Max 0.118, bound 0.0625 (≈0.43 dB from D(R)). Verdict: (B) — optimality is relative to a Gaussian proxy source and the proxy loss.
- **QuIP# (ICML 2024)** replaces random orthogonal matrices with the randomized Hadamard transform (RHT) and uses E₈-lattice codebooks (E8P12 at 2 bits; residual VQ E8P12RVQ3B/4B at 3–4 bits), with a concentration lemma for the RHT in its appendix (Lemma A.1) — [QuIP# README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/quip-sharp/main/README.md); [QuIP# arXiv 2402.04396](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04396). The E₈ choice is motivated by E₈ having the best known 8-D packing/NSM (Conway–Sloane) **[inference; the paper's exact phrasing not fetched]**. CALDERA's README notes "E8 Lattice quantizers from QuIP# typically perform better" than its own scalar quantizers — [CALDERA README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pilancilab/caldera/main/README.md).
- **TurboQuant (Zandieh, Daliri, Hadian, Mirrokni; ICLR 2026, arXiv 2504.19874)** — [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006985); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19874v1). Verified claims via search summary: it is a *data-oblivious* two-stage vector quantizer: (stage 1, "PolarQuant") random rotation of the input induces "a concentrated Beta distribution on coordinates," and "the near-independence property of distinct coordinates in high dimensions" is used to apply *optimal scalar (Lloyd–Max-type) quantizers per coordinate*; (stage 2) a 1-bit QJL transform on the residual yields an *unbiased inner-product estimator*. The paper gives "a formal proof of the information-theoretic lower bounds on the best achievable distortion rate by any vector quantizer, and TurboQuant closely matches these bounds, differing only by a small constant (≈2.7) factor." Empirically: KV cache "absolute quality neutrality with 3.5 bits per channel and marginal quality degradation with 2.5 bits per channel." A third-party PyPI package describes PolarQuant as "random rotation + polar transformation + Lloyd–Max" — [fastvq on PyPI (unofficial)](https://pypi.org/project/fastvq/). I could not fetch the theorem statements; the exact distortion measure (MSE on unit-norm vectors vs. inner-product error), the exact lower-bound form, and whether the lower bound is for *all* vector quantizers or *data-oblivious* ones remain **unverified**.
- **Ordentlich & Polyanskiy, "Optimal Quantization for Matrix Multiplication" (arXiv 2410.13780; IEEE T-IT, March 2026)** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13780v3); [ISIT 2025 slides](https://people.lids.mit.edu/yp/homepage/data/2025_matmul_isit.pdf); [TAU seminar abstract](https://engineering.tau.ac.il/seminar24032025). Verified claims: setup is two matrices each compressed *separately* to R bits/entry, decoder estimates AᵀB; they prove "a non-asymptotic lower bound on the mean squared error of this approximation as a function of rate R, for matrices with iid Gaussian entries"; a nested-lattice quantizer is "universal" with "error guarantee depends only on Frobenius norms of the zero-centered matrices and their product," and "for iid Gaussian inputs, the quantizer achieves the lower bound and is therefore asymptotically optimal"; the derived rate–distortion function "shows a phase transition near 0.906 bits per entry," below which JL sketching (dimension reduction) is *necessary*; a low-complexity version is "quite close to optimal"; applying it to weights, KV cache and activations of Llama-3-8B at 4 bits "yields smaller perplexity than state-of-the-art quantization schemes" (seminar abstract). Verdict: (B) for the optimality (i.i.d. Gaussian), (A) for the universal upper bound (holds for any matrices via the Frobenius-norm form), (C) for the perplexity claim.
- **"KV Cache Compression Through the Lens of Transform Coding" (arXiv 2608.14191, 2026)** states the general reverse-water-filling solution (see KQ8) and frames KV quantization as transform coding — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14191). **SPECTRA (arXiv 2608.07915, 2026)** derives the same for spectral KV coding — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07915).

### Inferences
- The TurboQuant "≈2.7" constant is numerically the Panter–Dite penalty √3π/2 ≈ 2.72 of an *optimal scalar quantizer on a Gaussian* at high rate. This strongly suggests (unverified against the paper) that TurboQuant's achievability is "rotate → treat coordinates as independent near-Gaussian → Lloyd–Max per coordinate," so its gap to the lower bound is exactly the space-filling + shaping loss of scalar quantization, which lattice/TCQ coding of the rotated coordinates would reduce toward 1× (the 1.53 dB granular part) — a concrete, testable design hypothesis: replace PolarQuant's per-coordinate Lloyd–Max with an E₈/TCQ coder on rotated KV vectors and measure whether the constant drops from ≈2.7 toward ≈1.
- Distance from the bound (my arithmetic on recalled numbers, for i.i.d. Gaussian *after* incoherence, proxy-MSE only): 2-bit Lloyd–Max/RTN ≈2.74 dB above D(R); E8P ≈1.5 dB; QTIP TCQ ≈0.4 dB. For heavier-tailed *raw* LLM weights the SLB is lower (e.g., Laplacian: (e/π)σ²2^{-2R}), so incoherence processing *raises* the information-theoretic floor slightly (Gaussianization destroys exploitable shape) while making the achievable region reachable with known codes — this trade-off has not been quantified in any paper I found.
- None of the bounds is stated in terms of end-to-end loss; all are MSE on weights, inner products, or matrix products. The "linearity theorem" (KQ3, HIGGS) is the only bridge, and it is local/first-order.

### Gaps
- Exact statements of TurboQuant's Theorems (lower bound form, dependence on d and b, whether the bound is for all or only data-oblivious quantizers) — not fetched.
- Exact Ordentlich–Polyanskiy bound expression and the proof technique (likely random-coding/sphere-covering + Gaussian extremality) — not fetched.
- No paper found that computes a rate–distortion bound for the *actual empirical distribution* of LLM weights (e.g., Student-t or Laplacian fits) and compares deployed quantizers to it.
- No result found on the joint rate–distortion of weights *and* activations under a non-MSE distortion (e.g., KL at the output).

## KQ2. Incoherence processing and concentration (QuIP μ-incoherence, Kashin, JL / random rotations, QJL, what rotations cannot fix)

### Takeaway
QuIP's theory (NeurIPS 2023) is the canonical result: define μ-incoherence of W and H, show random orthogonal conjugation makes both μ-incoherent with μ = O(√log) w.h.p., and bound the LDLQ proxy loss by (μ²/n)·tr(H^{1/2})² times a rounding-dependent constant, which beats nearest rounding's tr(H) bound when H is spectrally spread. Kashin's theorem (1977) gives a *deterministic worst-case* version: for almost all orthogonal P, max(‖x‖₁,‖Px‖₁) ≥ c√N‖x‖₂, so a two-term "Kashin representation" has all coefficients O(‖x‖₂/√N). QJL gives an unbiased 1-bit inner-product estimator. All are (B)/(A) for the *proxy* objective; none controls what rotations cannot change: the ℓ₂ norm of a massive-activation token, inter-token dynamic range, and the end-to-end loss.

### Cited Findings
- **QuIP (Chee, Cai, Kuleshov, De Sa; NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2307.13304)** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13304); [SlidesLive talk](https://slideslive.com/39010872/quip-2bit-quantization-of-llms-with-theoretical-guarantees). Verified via search summary: the per-layer objective is the quadratic proxy tr((Ŵ−W)H(Ŵ−W)ᵀ) with H the second moment of layer inputs on calibration data; "Theorem 1 gives exact expressions for the proxy loss in terms of tr(D)" where H = (U̇+I)ᵀ D (U̇+I) is the LDL decomposition (U̇ strictly upper triangular, D non-negative diagonal); **Lemma 2**: for μ-incoherent PSD H, tr(D) ≤ (μ²/n)·tr(H^{1/2})² — "a novel result using incoherence to obtain a bound on tr(D) that depends only on the spectrum of H"; empirically "a vast majority of H matrices have fewer than a quarter of eigenvalues > 1% of the max eigenvalue" (H approximately low-rank). **[recalled, unverified]**: the μ-incoherence definition is max_{ij}|W_{ij}| ≤ μ‖W‖_F/√(mn) for W and |e_iᵀ Q_H e_j|-type bounds on the eigenvectors of H (max entry of eigenvector matrix ≤ μ/√n); the random-orthogonal lemma gives μ ∝ √(log(n/δ)) w.h.p.; the final LDLQ+IP bound is proxy ≤ (μ²/(4n))·tr(H^{1/2})² (nearest rounding inside LDLQ) or (μ²/(2n))·tr(H^{1/2})² (stochastic rounding), vs. (m/4)·tr(H) for plain nearest rounding; since tr(H^{1/2})² ≤ n·tr(H) with equality iff H is a multiple of the identity, the gain is large exactly when H's spectrum is spread (which the low-rank observation says it is). Verdict: (B) — proved for the proxy loss only; QuIP's own README offers `ldlbal_admm` as an "alternative algorithm which constrains the rounded weights to be sufficiently close to their original, giving a better theoretical bound," implying the main bound needs a no-clipping/bounded-deviation condition — [QuIP README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/QuIP/main/README.md).
- **QuIP#**: RHT incoherence with a concentration lemma (Lemma A.1) replacing the Haar-orthogonal lemma — [QuIP# arXiv 2402.04396](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04396). A 2026 survey cautions that "deployed methods only use a single randomized Hadamard, which lacks some of the stronger guarantees from later theory work" — [Transforms for LLM Quantization: The Great Inversion (arXiv 2608.25188)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25188).
- **Kashin's theorem and Kashin representations.** Statement (verified via search summary of the Merkulov et al. paper): for almost all orthogonal P∈O(N), max{‖x‖₁,‖Px‖₁} ≥ c₁√N‖x‖₂ for all x∈ℝᴺ, c₁ an absolute constant, failing only on a set of small Haar measure. A greedy algorithm then produces u_k, v_k with ‖·‖_∞ ≤ c₂/√N whose sum approximates x with geometrically decreasing error — [Merkulov et al., "Quantization of LLMs with an Overdetermined Basis," UAI 2024 (PMLR v244)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v244/merkulov24a.html); [arXiv 2404.09737](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.09737). Lyubarskii & Vershynin show frame representations can be converted to Kashin representations with the smallest possible dynamic range O(1/√n) — ["Uncertainty principles and vector quantization," arXiv math/0611343](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611343). Merkulov et al. report their matrix decomposition "doesn't always converge in a restricted number of iterations" and *speculate* it may help activation outliers because of the guaranteed ℓ∞ bound — [arXiv 2404.09737](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09737v1). A 2026 paper restates Kashin's theorem as the basis of structured-transform quantization — [Structured Transforms for Low-Overhead Quantization (arXiv 2609.11687)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11687). Verdict: (A) as a deterministic worst-case ℓ∞ guarantee (no distributional assumption), but it costs a *second* basis (2× coefficients) and the constants c₁,c₂ are small/unknown in practice.
- **Random rotation → near-Gaussian coordinates (precise form).** For x fixed with ‖x‖₂=1 and Q Haar-orthogonal, Qx is uniform on S^{d−1}, so each coordinate z_i has density ∝(1−z²)^{(d−3)/2}, i.e., z_i² ~ Beta(½,(d−1)/2); max_i|z_i| ≤ √(2log(2d/δ)/d) w.p. ≥1−δ; coordinates are exchangeable and *asymptotically* independent N(0,1/d) (Poincaré/Borel lemma: TV distance between the first k coordinates of √d·Qx and N(0,I_k) → 0 iff k=o(d); Diaconis–Freedman 1987 give the rate O(k/d)) — standard; this is exactly the "concentrated Beta distribution" and "near-independence of distinct coordinates in high dimensions" that TurboQuant's summary invokes — [TurboQuant search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19874v1) **[the Diaconis–Freedman rate is recalled]**. For the RHT (Hadamard × random signs), each coordinate is a Rademacher-weighted sum (1/√d)Σ±x_j, hence sub-Gaussian with variance proxy ‖x‖₂²/d by Hoeffding, giving the same √(2log(2d/δ)/d) tail; coordinates are only pairwise uncorrelated, not independent **[standard; recalled]**.
- **QJL (Zandieh, Daliri, Han; AAAI 2025, arXiv 2406.03482)** — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34773); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03482v2); [QJL README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amirzandieh/QJL/main/README.md). Verified: QJL applies a JL (Gaussian) projection then keeps only the sign of each projected entry (1 bit, no per-block scale/zero-point); an *asymmetric* estimator — key quantized with QJL, query projected *without* quantization — is "an unbiased estimator with minimal distortion for the inner product"; README: "provably minimal relative distortion on attention scores," "handles outlier coordinates in key embeddings," and "can be enhanced using orthogonalized JL transforms." **[recalled, unverified]**: the estimator is ⟨q,k⟩̂ = (√(π/2)/m)·‖k‖₂·⟨Sq, sign(Sk)⟩ for S∈ℝ^{m×d} Gaussian, with E = ⟨q,k⟩ and distortion |⟨q,k⟩̂−⟨q,k⟩| ≤ O(‖q‖‖k‖√(log(1/δ)/m)) w.p. 1−δ; the √(π/2) comes from E[sign(g₁)g₂]=√(2/π)ρ for standard Gaussians with correlation ρ. Note the estimator still needs ‖k‖₂ per key (one scalar). Verdict: (A) — distribution-free (holds for any fixed q,k), applicable as stated; but the bound is in ‖q‖‖k‖ units, which is exactly the massive-norm regime where it is loosest.

### Inferences
- What rotations provably *cannot* do: an orthogonal Q preserves ‖x‖₂ of each token and all pairwise inner products, so (i) a massive-activation token keeps its norm — a shared per-tensor/per-token scale still has to absorb it, and per-token quantization step size scales with ‖x‖₂/√d, which is why rotation methods (QuaRot/SpinQuant) still need per-token or per-group scaling; (ii) the Hessian H = XᵀX is conjugated, not changed in spectrum, so the (μ²/n)·tr(H^{1/2})² bound improves only through μ, never through the eigenvalue spread; (iii) inter-token dynamic range (max_t‖x_t‖/median_t‖x_t‖) is invariant. These are consequences of orthogonality, not published theorems — I found no paper stating them formally.
- Kashin vs. Hadamard: Hadamard gives ℓ∞ ≤ O(√(log d/d))‖x‖₂ *with high probability over the random signs*; Kashin gives ℓ∞ ≤ c/√N *deterministically* but with two blocks of coefficients. For adversarial/worst-case tokens (the attention-sink token), Kashin-type representations are the only guarantee; this motivates a "two-basis KV cache" hypothesis.
- The block-Hadamard analysis of [arXiv 2601.22347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22347) ("post-rotation outliers are minimized when the pre-rotation ℓ₁ norm mass is evenly distributed across blocks in the deterministic worst case") is the only worst-case (non-probabilistic) optimality statement about rotations I found; it suggests a provable criterion for choosing block permutations before block-Hadamard.

## KQ3. Second-order / OBS theory (OBS, GPTQ-as-Cholesky, GPTQ-as-Babai, layer-wise proxy vs end-to-end loss, GPTAQ, error propagation and Lipschitz composition)

### Takeaway
GPTQ/LDLQ is now understood exactly: it is Babai's nearest-plane algorithm on the lattice generated by the Cholesky factor of the Hessian, with worst-case layer-wise error tightly bounded by tr(D) of the LDL decomposition under *no clipping* (Chen–Hoefler–Alistarh 2025); Zhang–Zhang–Colbert–Saab (2025) give the first explicit non-asymptotic error bounds for OPTQ/GPTQ and Qronos including stochastic rounding and an MLP extension with Lipschitz activations. The layer→loss bridge is the HIGGS "linearity theorem" (first-order: perplexity increase ∝ Σ layer MSEs), which is (B)/(C). Error *composition* across layers has no tight theory: global Lipschitz constants of transformers are either infinite (dot-product attention is not Lipschitz) or vacuous, so all propagation bounds are local, heuristic, or empirical (QEP, GPTAQ).

### Cited Findings
- **OBS / OBQ / GPTQ (classical derivation).** OBS (Hassibi & Stork, NeurIPS 1993) solves min_δ ½δᵀHδ s.t. e_qᵀδ + w_q = 0 ⇒ δ = −(w_q/[H⁻¹]_{qq})·H⁻¹e_q, loss increase w_q²/(2[H⁻¹]_{qq}). OBQ (Frantar & Alistarh, "Optimal Brain Compression," NeurIPS 2022) replaces pruning with rounding (target quant(w_q) instead of 0) and uses rank-1 Sherman–Morrison updates of H⁻¹. GPTQ (Frantar, Ashkboos, Hoefler, Alistarh, ICLR 2023) observes that quantizing columns in a fixed order makes the sequence of inverse-Hessian updates equal to the rows of the upper Cholesky factor of H⁻¹, giving δ_F = −((w_q−quant(w_q))/[H⁻¹_F]_{qq})·(H⁻¹_F)_{q,:} with lazy batch updates **[recalled; classic]**. The QuIP paper shows GPTQ ≡ LDLQ (the LDL-based "optimal" adaptive rounding in its framework) — [QuIP arXiv 2307.13304](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13304) **[the equivalence claim is recalled from QuIP, unverified this session]**.
- **GPTQ = Babai's nearest plane (Chen, Hoefler, Alistarh?, "The Geometry of LLM Quantization," arXiv 2507.18553, 2025)** — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18553v2); [v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18553v1). Verified: "GPTQ, executed from the last dimension to the first for a linear layer, is mathematically identical to Babai's nearest plane algorithm on a lattice defined by the layer's input Hessian," and "under no weight clipping, the worst-case layer-wise error is tightly bounded by the trace of the diagonal matrix from the LDL decomposition of the Hessian." Verdict: (A) for the equivalence (exact algebra), (B) for the bound (needs no clipping; the tr(D) quantity is the same one QuIP bounds via incoherence). Implication stated in the summary: GPTQ is a CVP approximation; better lattice algorithms (e.g., sphere decoding, LLL/BKZ-reduced bases) are the natural improvement — this is the paper's framing per the summary, specifics unverified.
- **Provable PTQ: OPTQ and Qronos (arXiv 2508.04853, 2025)** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04853). Verified: gives calibration-error bounds (‖WX−ŴX‖) for OPTQ/GPTQ, with deterministic and stochastic-rounding variants, and "calibration-error bounds for MLPs under Lipschitz activation assumptions"; explicitly leaves "extending the framework to transformer layers and to cross-entropy loss" to future work and states that "guarantees for metrics like perplexity and zero-shot accuracy remain an open problem." Exact bound forms not fetched. Verdict: (B).
- **HIGGS linearity theorem (Malinovskii et al., NAACL 2025, arXiv 2411.17525)** — [NAACL 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.naacl-long.543); [alphaxiv overview](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2411.17525v1). Verified: "a 'linearity theorem' establishing a direct relationship between the layer-wise ℓ₂ reconstruction error and the model perplexity increase due to quantization," used for a data-free method (Hadamard + MSE-optimal grids for Gaussian) and a dynamic-programming per-layer bit allocation. **[recalled, unverified]**: the proof is a second-order Taylor expansion of KL(p‖p_quant) in the weight perturbation, with the cross-layer Hessian approximated as block-diagonal and layer-independent-in-expectation under random (Hadamard-induced) rounding errors, yielding ΔPPL ≈ Σ_ℓ c_ℓ·MSE_ℓ. Verdict: (C)→(B): valid only for small, approximately independent, zero-mean per-layer errors; breaks when errors are correlated across layers (which is exactly what GPTQ/QEP error-feedback introduces) or when the model is near a sharp direction.
- **QAM-W (arXiv 2605.26339, 2026)** makes the Lipschitz argument explicit and honest: "if the transformer computation after the perturbed linear layers is locally Lipschitz around the evaluation activation trajectories, the logit perturbation is controlled by the accumulated layer-output perturbations. The constant L is not estimated because global Lipschitz bounds for transformers are usually too loose to be useful." It adds a local KL bridge: D_KL(p‖q) ≈ ¼‖δ‖² for small logit perturbation δ, with absolute error ≤ ⅓‖δ‖³ — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26339). (Note: ¼‖δ‖² is the Fisher/Hessian-of-logsumexp form only when the softmax is near-uniform; in general ½δᵀ(diag(p)−ppᵀ)δ ≤ ½‖δ‖², so the ¼ constant is a specific choice — verify.)
- **Why dot-product attention has no global Lipschitz constant.** Kim, Papamakarios & Mnih, "The Lipschitz Constant of Self-Attention" (ICML 2021, arXiv 2006.04710): standard dot-product self-attention "is not globally Lipschitz, because the Jacobian can grow arbitrarily large when inputs have widely varying magnitudes"; L2-distance attention is Lipschitz — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04710). Under norm constraints: "functional attention has a Lipschitz bound of max(1, ‖v‖·max(‖q‖,‖k‖)) under a max-over-tokens RMS norm" — [Training Transformers with Enforced Lipschitz Constants (arXiv 2507.13338)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13338). A row-wise softmax Lipschitz constant in Frobenius norm propagated to attention output gives a bound linear in the perturbations of QKᵀ and V — [On Generalisation Error Bounds for Transformers (arXiv 2410.11500)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11500).
- **Error accumulation is real and compensable, but only empirically.** QEP (arXiv 2504.09629): "the growth of quantization errors across layers [is] a key limitation of layer-wise PTQ, particularly in low-bit regimes," fixed by explicitly propagating and compensating accumulated error — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09629v2). GPTAQ (Li et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2504.02692): standard GPTQ calibrates each layer against the quantized network's own activations; GPTAQ makes the quantized layer reproduce the *full-precision* model's output ("asymmetric calibration"), derives a closed-form OBS-style solution that "explicitly minimizes the quantization error as well as the accumulated asymmetry error," with a Cholesky reformulation for matrix fusion, in "20 more lines of code than GPTQ" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02692); [ICML 2025 (PMLR v267)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/li25dn.html). FlatQuant argues flatter weight/activation distributions reduce the error *propagated* across layers — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09629v2) (FlatQuant repo: [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ruikangliu/FlatQuant/main/README.md)).
- **Where the search found nothing**: no paper gives a tight end-to-end bound of the form ‖f_quant(x)−f(x)‖ ≤ Π_ℓ L_ℓ·Σ_ℓ ε_ℓ that is non-vacuous for a real LLM; the search explicitly returned "A tight, end-to-end Lipschitz propagation bound for full LLMs appears to be an open problem" — [search over arXiv 2605.26339 / 2508.04853 / 2507.18553](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04853).

### Inferences
- Additive vs amplifying composition: in a pre-norm residual network, x_{ℓ+1} = x_ℓ + f_ℓ(RMSNorm(x_ℓ)). RMSNorm has Jacobian norm ≤ √d·‖γ‖_∞/‖x‖₂ on the sphere direction, so a block's contribution to the Jacobian is I + (small, norm-dependent term); errors compose roughly additively when ‖f_ℓ‖ ≪ ‖x_ℓ‖ (deep layers with large residual norm) and amplify where the residual norm is small (early layers) or where attention is near-argmax (softmax Jacobian ≈ 0 except at ties — so the "amplification" is a *discontinuity*, not a large Lipschitz constant). This explains both why global Lipschitz bounds are vacuous and why early-layer quantization is empirically more damaging. This is my synthesis, not a published theorem.
- The GPTQ=Babai equivalence means the entire GPTQ family inherits Babai's worst-case guarantee ‖error‖² ≤ ¼Σ_i d_i (for unit-step grids) **[recalled form; verify against 2507.18553]**, and that *lattice basis reduction of the Cholesky factor* (reordering columns = permuting lattice basis; LLL = beyond permutation) is the principled version of GPTQ's "act-order" heuristic — a concrete provable-method direction.
- GPTAQ's asymmetric objective min ‖ŴX̂ − WX‖ (quantized input, FP target) is a *control* formulation: each layer is asked to cancel upstream error, trading per-layer optimality for trajectory tracking. Its theory is only the OBS closed form; no proof that the sequential greedy tracking converges or does not overfit calibration data was found.

### Gaps
- Exact theorem statements/constants of 2507.18553 and 2508.04853 unverified.
- No theory quantifying *calibration-set generalization* of Hessian-based PTQ (H estimated from n samples; Marchenko–Pastur-type sample-covariance error would enter here — nothing found).
- No published analysis of when asymmetric (GPTAQ-style) calibration can hurt (overfitting to FP trajectory).

## KQ4. Random matrix theory of LLM weights/activations (HT-SR, low-rank-plus-noise, power-law activation spectra, CALDERA/LQER/QERA, "top eigen-directions matter most")

### Takeaway
The RMT facts that matter are (i) trained weight matrices have heavy-tailed empirical spectral densities that deviate from Marchenko–Pastur (Martin & Mahoney HT-SR) and (ii) the activation second-moment H = XᵀX is approximately low-rank (QuIP: most H have <¼ of eigenvalues above 1% of the max). These justify low-rank + quantized-residual decompositions, for which CALDERA (NeurIPS 2024) proves approximation-error upper bounds via rank-constrained regression, and the "spectral flattening" preprint (2026) argues the damage of ultra-low-bit quantization is concentrated in the spectral tail. Everything connecting HT-SR to *quantization* specifically is (C) — I found no HT-SR-based quantization theorem.

### Cited Findings
- **HT-SR (Martin & Mahoney)**: "well-trained neural networks tend to have heavy-tailed ESDs in their weight matrices. Within a reasonable range, the degree of heavy-tailedness is strongly correlated with model quality"; heavier tails are read as "stronger optimization-induced correlations and higher layer training quality," and HT-SR metrics "reliably rank pretrained networks and checkpoints by quality without access to training data" — as summarized in [AlphaPruning (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2410.10912)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10912v1) citing Martin & Mahoney (JMLR 2021, "Implicit Self-Regularization in Deep Neural Networks") and Martin, Peng & Mahoney (Nature Comms 2021). AlphaPruning uses the per-layer power-law exponent α of the ESD to set layer-wise *sparsity*, reaching 80% sparsity on LLaMA-7B with "reasonable perplexity" — [NeurIPS 2024 PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/10fc83943b4540a9524af6fc67a23fef-Paper-Conference.pdf). HTMuon (ACL Findings 2026) argues Muon's orthogonalized update "suppresses the emergence of heavy-tailed weight spectra" — [ACL 2026 Findings](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.1819/). The search found no HT-SR-guided quantization/bit-allocation method — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10912v1).
- **Spectral flattening under ultra-low-bit quantization (arXiv 2602.00969, 2026)**: "the primary peril of ultra-low-bit quantization, such as FP4, extends beyond simple scalar approximation error; it resides in the structural degradation of the spectral tail. When the quantization noise floor encroaches upon the magnitude of these delicate tail singular values, the model undergoes spectral flattening" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00969v1). Single preprint; argument level, embeddings only.
- **Activation Hessian is approximately low-rank**: QuIP observes "a vast majority of H matrices have fewer than a quarter of eigenvalues > 1% of the max eigenvalue" — [QuIP arXiv 2307.13304](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13304). This is the quantity that makes tr(H^{1/2})² ≪ n·tr(H) and hence incoherence+LDLQ beneficial (KQ2).
- **CALDERA (Saha, Sagan, Srivastava, Goldsmith, Pilanci; NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2405.18886)** — [NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/a20e8451ffb07ad25282c21945ad4f19-Abstract.html); [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.18886v2); [CALDERA README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pilancilab/caldera/main/README.md). Verified: W ≈ Q + LR with Q, L, R all low-precision; factors found by minimizing the calibration-weighted error ‖(Q+LR−W)Xᵀ‖²_F; "theoretical upper bounds on approximation error come from a rank-constrained regression framework," analyzing the trade-off between target rank and bit budget; beats PTQ baselines on LLaMa-2/3 below ≈2.5 bits/param. **[recalled, unverified]**: the bound has the form E‖(Q+LR−W)Xᵀ‖_F² ≲ (quantization-noise term ∝ Δ²·dims for unbiased stochastic rounding of Q) + (tail singular values Σ_{i>k}σ_i²(WXᵀ)-type term) + (noise from quantizing L,R scaled by their dynamic range), proved by alternating between the Eckart–Young optimum for the rank-k residual and the unbiased-quantizer variance; it assumes *stochastic (unbiased) quantization* with a known dynamic range, i.e., not the deterministic nearest/LDLQ rounding used in practice. Verdict: (B).
- **LQER / QERA**: QERA ("Quantization Error Reconstruction Analysis," 2024/2025) gives the closed-form optimal low-rank term for the activation-weighted error, i.e., the rank-k truncation of (W−Q)(H^{1/2}) re-whitened by H^{−1/2} (a Nyström/weighted-Eckart–Young argument), and shows LQER's unweighted SVD is suboptimal — **[recalled; the search for QERA was not executed before the budget ran out; treat as unverified, no URL]**.
- **Low-rank + noise view of weights**: the search did not surface a paper proving LLM weights are "low-rank plus noise" in an RMT sense (e.g., a spiked-covariance/BBP-threshold statement). The closest is the HT-SR observation that the bulk deviates from Marchenko–Pastur with power-law tails (α typically 2–6 for well-trained layers, per Martin–Mahoney **[recalled]**).

### Inferences
- "Quantization error in the top eigen-directions matters most" is formalized implicitly by the proxy loss tr(E H Eᵀ) = Σ_i λ_i‖E u_i‖²: error energy along eigenvector u_i of H is weighted by λ_i. With power-law λ_i, a rank-k correction removes Σ_{i≤k}λ_i‖Eu_i‖² — this is why CALDERA/QERA work and why the right low-rank term is the H-weighted one (QERA) not the plain SVD (LQER). None of the papers states a *lower* bound showing that without the low-rank term the error is unavoidable; such a bound would follow from Eckart–Young applied to E·H^{1/2} and would be easy to prove.
- HT-SR suggests a testable hypothesis: layers with smaller power-law exponent α (heavier tails, more "trained") should be more quantization-sensitive (their information sits in a few large singular directions that quantization noise flattens). AlphaPruning verified the analogue for pruning; nothing verified for quantization.

### Gaps
- CALDERA's exact theorem (constants, dependence on σ_i, assumptions on the quantizer) unverified.
- QERA/LQER theorem not sourced this session.
- No RMT paper found on how *calibration sample size* n vs dimension d distorts H's spectrum (Marchenko–Pastur noise floor) and hence GPTQ/LDLQ updates — an obvious open theory problem.
- No result on power-law *exponent* of activation covariance spectra across LLM families.

## KQ5. Scaling-law theory for precision (Kumar et al. 2024; Ouyang et al. "QiD"; why more tokens hurt quantization)

### Takeaway
Both precision scaling laws are *empirical fits*, not derivations: Kumar et al. (ICLR 2025) fit L(N,D,P)=A·N_eff^{−α}+B·D^{−β}+E+δ_PTQ with N_eff(P)=N(1−e^{−P/γ}) and δ_PTQ=C_T·(D^{γ_D}/N^{γ_N})·e^{−P_post/γ_post}; Ouyang et al. (ACL 2025) fit QiD ∝ D^β/(N^α·P^γ) on 1500+ checkpoints and extrapolate that 100T-token models may be undesirable for low-bit quantization. No paper I found *derives* the D-dependence from a model; the mechanistic explanation (weights become less robust / more "fully used" as training proceeds) is a hypothesis.

### Cited Findings
- **Kumar et al., "Scaling Laws for Precision" (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2411.04330)** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330v1); [ICLR 2025](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/kumar2025iclr-scaling). Verified via summaries: low-precision training "lowers a model's effective parameter count"; the loss is "a sum of a parameter-count term, a data-size term, a constant, and a PTQ degradation term" with an "effective" parameter exponent; PTQ degradation "grows with the amount of pretraining data and shrinks with the precision used at inference, so extra pretraining data can eventually hurt"; a "modified effective parameter count that depends on weight, activation, and KV-cache precision, each with its own saturation constant"; fit on "over 465 pretraining runs" and validated on "models up to 1.7B parameters trained on up to 26B tokens" — [themoonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/scaling-laws-for-precision). **[recalled, unverified]** exact forms: N_eff(P_w)=N(1−e^{−P_w/γ_w}); L(N,D,P_w,P_a,P_kv)=A·[N(1−e^{−P_w/γ_w})(1−e^{−P_a/γ_a})(1−e^{−P_kv/γ_kv})]^{−α}+B·D^{−β}+E; δ_PTQ(N,D,P_post)=C_T·(D^{γ_D}/N^{γ_N})·e^{−P_post/γ_post}; the authors describe the forms as *chosen for fit quality* and offer no derivation; the claim "compute-optimal precision is ≈7–8 bits" is a consequence of the fitted N_eff saturation. Verdict: (C).
- **Ouyang et al., "Low-Bit Quantization Favors Undertrained LLMs" (ACL 2025, arXiv 2411.17691)** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.17691); [ACL 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.acl-long.1555). Verified: "Larger models or those trained on fewer tokens exhibit less quantization-induced degradation (QiD), while smaller, well-trained models face significant performance losses"; fit on "over 1500 quantized LLM checkpoints"; QiD proposed as a measure of training level; projection that "models trained on over 100 trillion tokens may NOT be desirable in terms of low-bit quantization performance"; limitations: Pythia experiments only reach 300B tokens; single-stage pretraining only. Functional form (QiD = k·D^β/(N^α·P^γ) with fitted α,β,γ) **[recalled; exponents unverified]**. Verdict: (C).
- **Mechanism — what is actually argued.** Ouyang et al. attribute the effect (per the summary) to weight changes: as training proceeds, per-step weight updates become small, so the model has "used" fine-grained weight precision that low-bit quantization destroys, whereas undertrained models' weights are still coarse/noisy **[recalled framing, unverified]**. I found no paper deriving the D^{γ_D} dependence from a random-feature, kernel, or loss-landscape model.
- **Related theory on why larger models quantize better**: "Compute-Optimal LLMs Provably Generalize Better With Scale" (Finzi et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2504.15208) treats quantization error as a term in a generalization bound and "examine[s] why larger models are more quantizable from an information-theoretic perspective" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15208). Verdict: (B) — a bound, not a scaling law; mechanism per the summary: more parameters → each parameter carries fewer bits of information about the data at compute-optimal D/N, so coarser quantization loses less; this is the complement of the Ouyang observation (fixed N, more D → more bits per parameter → harder to quantize).

### Inferences
- A clean derivation of δ_PTQ ∝ D^{γ_D} is missing but plausible from two ingredients already in the literature: (i) information per parameter grows with D (Finzi et al.'s argument), and (ii) HT-SR: training sharpens spectra (heavier tails) and the quantization noise floor flattens the tail (2602.00969). A testable hypothesis: γ_D should correlate with the rate at which per-layer power-law exponents α decrease with D. No paper tests this.
- The "sharper minima" explanation is *not* supported by anything found; Kumar/Ouyang do not measure Hessian sharpness.

### Gaps
- Fitted exponents (γ_D, γ_N, γ_post; Ouyang's α,β,γ) not confirmed.
- Whether the δ_PTQ law holds for GPTQ/rotation-based PTQ (Kumar et al. used RTN-style GPTQ at the time **[recalled]**) — unverified.
- No theoretical model of precision scaling exists as far as this search could establish.

## KQ6. Optimization theory of QAT / training with quantization (STE convergence, stochastic rounding, quantization noise as regularization, sharpness/flatness, weight-perturbation robustness)

### Takeaway
STE theory exists only for shallow models: Yin et al. (ICLR 2019) prove that for a two-layer net with binarized/quantized ReLU activations and Gaussian inputs, the expected "coarse gradient" positively correlates with the population gradient and coarse-gradient descent converges to a critical point for ReLU/clipped-ReLU STEs but not the identity STE; Li et al. (NeurIPS 2017) analyze BinaryConnect/stochastic rounding under convexity; a 2026 paper adds stability/generalization for two-layer STE training. Stochastic rounding is unbiased with variance ≤Δ²/4. Nothing proved for transformers; the flat-minima/PAC-Bayes link is heuristic but is the mechanism behind compression-based generalization bounds (Lotfi et al.).

### Cited Findings
- **Yin, Lyu, Zhang, Osher, Qi, Xin, "Understanding Straight-Through Estimator in Training Activation Quantized Neural Nets" (ICLR 2019, arXiv 1903.05662)** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.05662); [ICLR 2019](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2019/poster/671); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10112576). Verified: setting is "a two-linear-layer network with binarized ReLU activations and Gaussian inputs"; the STE-modified update is the "coarse gradient"; "with a properly chosen STE, the expected coarse gradient correlates positively with the population gradient, and coarse gradient descent converges to a critical point of the population loss"; "ReLU-based and clipped-ReLU STEs converge, while the identity STE does not" and can be unstable near certain local minima (checked on CIFAR-10); the guarantee "assumes infinite training samples. With few samples, the empirical loss only roughly descends along the coarse gradient." Verdict: (B).
- **Extensions**: Long, Yin & Xin extended to a broader class of quantized activations and monotone STE surrogates; "Stability and Generalization of Straight-Through Estimators for Training Two-Layer Quantized Neural Networks" (arXiv 2609.06430, 2026) gives algorithmic-stability generalization bounds for STE in two-layer nets — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06430). Verdict: (B).
- **Li, De, Xu, Studer, Samet, Goldstein, "Training Quantized Nets: A Deeper Understanding" (NeurIPS 2017)** **[recalled, not searched this session]**: for convex losses, stochastic-rounding (SR) SGD converges to within an O(Δ) neighborhood of the optimum while BinaryConnect (deterministic rounding of a full-precision copy) can converge exactly in the convex case; in the non-convex case SR lacks the "annealing" needed to settle and BC's persistent full-precision copy is what makes it work. Treat as unverified.
- **Stochastic rounding**: SR(x) = ⌊x/Δ⌋Δ + Δ·Bernoulli((x−⌊x/Δ⌋Δ)/Δ) satisfies E[SR(x)]=x and Var ≤ Δ²/4 (maximal at midpoints), versus nearest rounding bias up to Δ/2 and uniform-error MSE Δ²/12 — standard (e.g., Gupta et al. ICML 2015 "Deep learning with limited numerical precision"; Croci et al. 2022 survey "Stochastic rounding: implementation, error analysis and applications," Royal Society Open Science) **[cited by title; recalled]**. QuIP's theory uses this to get its stochastic-rounding bound (KQ2); the Provable-PTQ paper gives stochastic-rounding variants of its bounds (KQ3) — [arXiv 2508.04853](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04853).
- **Quantization noise as regularization / flat minima / PAC-Bayes**: compression-based generalization bounds for LLMs are built on *quantizing* the parameters and counting bits: Lotfi et al. "Non-Vacuous Generalization Bounds for Large Language Models" (ICML 2024) and "Unlocking Tokens as Data Points…" (NeurIPS 2024) use SubLoRA + quantization + a prefix-free code to get non-vacuous bounds **[recalled; not searched before budget exhaustion; no URL]**; Finzi et al. (ICLR 2025) extend to compute-optimal models and show quantization error enters additively and shrinks with scale — [arXiv 2504.15208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15208). Flatness: a weight perturbation ε changes loss by ≈½εᵀHε, so quantization noise of variance Δ²/12 per weight raises expected loss by ≈(Δ²/24)·tr(H) — the same tr(H) that bounds nearest rounding in QuIP, which is why Hessian-trace sensitivity (HAWQ-style, KQ8) and sharpness-aware QAT target the same quantity **[standard second-order reasoning; no paper proving it for LLMs found]**.

### Inferences
- The identity-STE failure mode in Yin et al. (instability near minima) is the theoretical reason learned-step-size QAT (LSQ, PACT-style clipping) uses *clipped* STEs; a sharpness-aware formulation is natural since both "quantization-robust" and "flat" reduce to small tr(H)/eigenvalues on the quantization-noise subspace.
- A provable QAT result for LLMs would most plausibly come from the PAC-Bayes/compression route (Lotfi/Finzi) rather than from STE convergence: the former only needs the perturbation-robustness term and already yields non-vacuous numbers.

### Gaps
- No STE convergence result for any attention model.
- Lotfi et al. bound forms not sourced this session.
- "Quantization-robust training via sharpness-aware minimization" — no theorem found; only the heuristic above.
- Li et al. 2017 details unverified.

## KQ7. Geometry (rotation-invariance group of transformer blocks; what breaks it; Stiefel optimization; optimal rotation as kurtosis / ℓ∞-ℓ₂ minimization; spherical codes; attention-score sensitivity)

### Takeaway
"Computational invariance" (SliceGPT/QuaRot) is an exact algebraic identity: in a pre-norm transformer whose normalization is RMSNorm (LayerNorm converted by absorbing mean-subtraction), the residual stream can be conjugated by any orthogonal Q with W_in ← QᵀW_in, W_out ← W_out Q, biases ← Qᵀb, leaving the function unchanged. It fails for anything that reads the residual stream coordinate-wise or non-isotropically: LayerNorm's mean subtraction (fixable), RoPE (rotation inside heads must commute with the 2×2 RoPE blocks — only the outer/residual rotation and post-RoPE value-side rotations are free), GQA/MQA head sharing (constrains per-head rotations to be shared), SwiGLU gating (element-wise product → only permutations/signed-permutations of the intermediate dimension are allowed), tied embeddings, and per-channel quantization scales. The "optimal rotation" problem (min_Q kurtosis or ℓ∞/ℓ₂ of QW) has no closed-form theory; Kashin/Grothendieck-type constants give only the existence of good Q. Softmax is 1-Lipschitz but attention is not; the only bound for quantized keys is ‖Δout‖ ≤ 2·max‖v‖·TV(softmax exact, quantized) (2026 preprint).

### Cited Findings
- **Computational invariance**: QuaRot "rotates LLMs in a way that removes outliers from the hidden state without changing the output … This computational invariance is applied to the hidden state (residual) of the LLM, as well as to the activations of the feed-forward components, aspects of the attention mechanism and to the KV cache" — [QuaRot README (official)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spcl/QuaRot/main/README.md). SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., ICLR 2024; Microsoft TransformerCompression repo) introduced the term and the LayerNorm→RMSNorm conversion — [repo README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/TransformerCompression/main/README.md) (README fetched; theorem text not in README). The search summary states: "rotations can be fused into weights, so the network computes the same function while its activations become easier to quantize"; "the residual stream can be rotated … resulting in numerically equivalent floating point networks before and after rotation" — [SpinQuant arXiv 2405.16406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.16406v1). Verdict: (A) — exact identity in exact arithmetic; *not* exact in finite precision or with per-channel quantization scales.
- **SpinQuant (Liu et al., ICLR 2025)**: "some random rotations lead to much better quantization than others, with an up to 13 points difference in downstream zero-shot reasoning performance"; SpinQuant learns rotations "on the Stiefel manifold, directly minimizing the final loss of the rotated quantized network" (Cayley SGD) — [arXiv 2405.16406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.16406v1); [ICLR 2025 slides](https://iclr.cc/media/iclr-2025/Slides/28338_XQXvpv6.pdf); [SpinQuant repo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/SpinQuant/main/README.md). The 13-point spread is direct evidence that the QuIP w.h.p. incoherence guarantee (which is identical for all Haar rotations) does not control downstream loss — i.e., the proxy-to-loss gap is large.
- **Kurtosis as objective**: "Kurtosis measures how heavy-tailed a distribution is … κ≈3 suggests Gaussian-like"; in LLaMA-2-7B "κ of many layers exceed[s] 200" — [search summary citing KurTail/QuaRot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01483); KurTail (arXiv 2503.01483) optimizes rotations to reduce kurtosis — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01483). No paper found proving that min-kurtosis Q is MSE-optimal; a 2026 study notes that why rotation works "is explained informally, via incoherence, the central limit theorem, or kurtosis, and rarely tested directly" (tested on a super-resolution transformer, not an LLM) — [Any Orthogonal Transform Will Do: Range Reduction… (arXiv 2601.21069)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21069). Block-Hadamard worst case: "post-rotation outliers are minimized when the pre-rotation ℓ₁ norm mass is evenly distributed across blocks" — [arXiv 2601.22347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22347).
- **Existence of good rotations (Kashin/Grothendieck-type)**: Kashin's theorem (KQ2) implies for *every* x there is a pair of orthogonal frames in which the coefficients have ℓ∞ ≤ c/√N; but for a *single* Q and a *fixed* x the best achievable is ‖Qx‖_∞ = ‖x‖₂/√d (all coordinates equal) and random Q achieve √(2log(2d/δ)/d)·‖x‖₂ — the √log d factor is the price of being data-oblivious; for a *matrix* W (many columns), no single Q can flatten all columns simultaneously in the worst case, which is the Kashin/Lyubarskii–Vershynin "uncertainty principle" — [arXiv math/0611343](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611343). Verdict: (A) as existence statements; constants unknown.
- **Spherical codes / quantizing on the sphere**: after normalization (per-token or per-vector) quantization is coding on S^{d−1}; TurboQuant's Beta-coordinate analysis and the Ordentlich–Polyanskiy 0.906-bit phase transition (below which one must sketch to lower dimension) are the only LLM-paper results on this — [TurboQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19874v1); [Ordentlich–Polyanskiy](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13780v3). Classical spherical-code rate–distortion (Sakrison 1968; Wyner 1967 "Random packings and coverings of the unit n-sphere") gives D(R)=1−(1−2^{−2R})… **[recalled; cite by title only]**.
- **Attention-score sensitivity.** Softmax is 1-Lipschitz (ℓ₂→ℓ₂) — Gao & Pavel 2017, "On the properties of the softmax function with application in game theory and reinforcement learning" **[recalled; cite by title]**. Dot-product attention is *not* globally Lipschitz — [Kim et al. 2021, arXiv 2006.04710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04710). Under bounded norms, functional attention Lipschitz ≤ max(1,‖v‖·max(‖q‖,‖k‖)) — [arXiv 2507.13338](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13338). Quantized keys: "bounds the output error from key quantization by twice the maximum value norm times the total variation distance between the exact and quantized softmax distributions," and "the score error for per-channel INT8 keys [is] the query norm times the scale-vector norm, divided by twice the square root of the head dimension" — [Runtime-Certified Bounded-Error Quantized Attention (arXiv 2605.20868, 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20868). A TV-distance theory for top-k sparse attention exists and is reusable for quantized attention — [arXiv 2512.07647](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07647v1).

### Inferences
- The exact invariance group: for a block with RMSNorm(x)=x·γ/‖x‖·√d, conjugation by Q∈O(d) on the residual stream is exact *only if* γ is folded into the following linear layer first (QuaRot/SliceGPT do this); with LayerNorm the mean-subtraction (I−𝟙𝟙ᵀ/d) must be folded as well; biases transform covariantly. Inside attention, Q_h,K_h may be rotated by any R∈O(d_head) that *commutes with the RoPE block-diagonal rotation* — i.e., R must be block-diagonal in the same 2×2 (or complex) structure, essentially a per-frequency-pair unitary — unless the rotation is applied *after* RoPE (online Hadamard on K, as QuaRot does for the KV cache); V_h/W_o may be rotated by arbitrary R∈O(d_head) per head, but GQA forces the K/V rotation to be shared across the heads in a group. In the MLP, SwiGLU's element-wise product forbids anything but signed permutations on the intermediate dimension (the invariance group collapses from O(d_ff) to the hyperoctahedral group). These are algebraic facts; I found no paper that writes the full group down, which is itself a gap worth filling with a short theorem.
- Since QuIP-type incoherence is identical in distribution for every Haar Q yet SpinQuant finds a 13-point spread, the relevant objective is *not* ℓ∞ of weights/activations but the end-to-end effect of the specific rounding errors; this undermines kurtosis/ℓ∞ as the *right* theoretical criterion.

### Gaps
- No paper proving any optimality (even for a Gaussian proxy) of kurtosis-minimizing rotations.
- No formal statement of the full invariance group with RoPE/GQA/SwiGLU/biases/tied embeddings.
- No bound on *end-to-end* attention output error from jointly quantized Q, K, V with softmax ties/sinks accounted for.

## KQ8. Mixed-precision bit-allocation theory (reverse water-filling; Hessian-trace/Fisher sensitivity; Lagrangian formulations; what is provable)

### Takeaway
The only provable allocation is reverse water-filling for independent Gaussian components under MSE: R_k = max(0, ½log₂(σ_k²/θ)) with the water level θ set by the budget; LLM papers (SPECTRA, transform-coding KV, BAQ) re-derive this with the component variance replaced by a Hessian/activation-energy sensitivity, obtaining convex programs with "equal-loss" (equal-slope) solutions. Everything about *which* sensitivity (Hessian trace, Fisher, activation energy) is heuristic; HIGGS's linearity theorem is the only stated justification for summing per-layer MSEs.

### Cited Findings
- **Reverse water-filling (classical)**: for parallel Gaussian sources X_k~N(0,σ_k²) under total MSE, D_k = min(θ,σ_k²), R = Σ_k max(0, ½log₂(σ_k²/θ)) — Cover & Thomas Thm. 10.3.3 (textbook). LLM restatements: "the real rates R_k ≥ 0 minimizing the sum of σ_k²2^{−2R_k} subject to a total rate constraint are max(0, ½·log₂(σ_k²/θ)), with coordinates at or below θ receiving zero bits" — [KV Cache Compression Through the Lens of Transform Coding (arXiv 2608.14191)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14191); SPECTRA: Lagrangian stationarity "v_j 2^{−2b_j} = θ, the constant 'water level'", bits per coordinate = ½log₂(variance/level), zero bits = rank reduction — [SPECTRA (arXiv 2608.07915)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07915). Verdict: (A) for Gaussian independent components with MSE; (B) when the "σ_k²" is a Hessian proxy and the distortion is loss.
- **Hessian-based allocation**: BAQ "uses a Hessian proxy to write layer-wise quantization loss as an explicit function of bitwidths, which makes allocation a convex problem with a closed-form solution," whose "solution exhibits an equal-loss structure" — [BAQ (arXiv 2506.05664)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05664v1). HAWQ-V2-style Hessian trace: "the accuracy impact of layer-wise bit-precision correlated well with that layer's Hessian trace" — [Hessian Aware Quantization of SNNs (arXiv 2104.14117)](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2104.14117); CW-HAWQ notes "Hessian trace analysis does not provide the specific bit precision setting for different channels," so it ranks and then searches — [CW-HAWQ (arXiv 2008.08284)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.08284). HIGGS uses the linearity theorem to make the per-layer allocation a dynamic program over Σ_ℓ c_ℓ·MSE_ℓ(b_ℓ) — [HIGGS](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2411.17525v1). Verdict: (C)/(B).
- **Fisher information**: the search found no LLM-quantization paper that uses Fisher information explicitly for allocation; the KV-cache per-channel weights found were "a calibration-set average of squared projections, which is closer to an activation-energy sensitivity than to a Fisher term" — [search summary over 2608.14191](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14191). (FIT, Zandonati et al. 2022, and the Fisher-weighted KV methods are recalled but **unsourced**.)

### Inferences
- The theoretically consistent allocation for LLMs is: per-layer loss ≈ ½·Δ_ℓ²/12·tr(H_ℓ^{loss}) (second-order, independent noise) ⇒ with Δ_ℓ ∝ 2^{−b_ℓ}, minimize Σ_ℓ s_ℓ·2^{−2b_ℓ} s.t. Σ n_ℓ b_ℓ ≤ B, whose KKT solution is exactly reverse water-filling with σ_k² → s_ℓ/n_ℓ (sensitivity per weight). This is what BAQ/HIGGS do; the *misuse* is (i) using the layer-input Hessian XᵀX (proxy) instead of the loss Hessian, (ii) ignoring correlation of errors across layers (GPTQ makes errors correlated), and (iii) applying it to non-Gaussian components where D_k ≠ σ_k²2^{−2R_k}.
- Fisher vs Hessian: for cross-entropy at the model's own predictions, the empirical Fisher equals the Gauss–Newton part of the Hessian; so Fisher-trace allocation is the PSD approximation of Hessian-trace allocation (standard; cite Martens 2020 "New insights and perspectives on the natural gradient method" **[recalled]**).

### Gaps
- No lower bound showing any allocation is optimal for the *end-to-end* loss.
- No paper found quantifying the loss from treating per-layer errors as independent in the allocation.
- Fisher-based LLM allocation theory not sourced.

## KQ9. 2025–2026 theory papers specifically about LLM quantization; outlier-emergence theory; quantization vs pruning/low-rank (MDL arguments)

### Takeaway
2025–26 produced a cluster of genuinely theoretical LLM-quantization papers: GPTQ=Babai (2507.18553), Provable PTQ (2508.04853), TurboQuant (ICLR 2026), Ordentlich–Polyanskiy (T-IT 2026), HIGGS linearity theorem (NAACL 2025), expressive-power limits of weight quantization claiming 1.58-bit as the limiting precision (2606.22249), STE stability (2609.06430), Kashin-based structured transforms (2609.11687), and several "why outliers/massive activations emerge" analyses (softmax implicit bias; gradient sinks). Quantization-vs-pruning has one theoretical treatment (Kuzmin et al. 2023, Gaussian-weight analysis) and the compression-bound literature (Lotfi/Finzi) is the only MDL-style argument. Most are (B).

### Cited Findings
- **Expressive power of weight quantization (arXiv 2606.22249, June 2026; Shao-Qun Zhang's group, Nanjing)**: argues "1.58-bit is the limiting precision for weight quantization by establishing the universal approximation and expressive collapse properties of weight-quantized models with respect to the number of quantization bits," and that "the expressive capacity of weight-quantized models degrades polynomially as the number of quantization bits decreases"; prior theory cited as thin (Yayla et al. on 1-bit nets; Ding et al. on extremely low-bit ReLU nets) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22249); [chatpaper listing](https://chatpaper.com/paper/301745). Preprint; exact assumptions (width scaling, which function class) unverified. Verdict: (B) — approximation-theoretic, says nothing about trained models or PTQ.
- **Outlier/massive-activation emergence theory**: (i) softmax sum-to-one "no-op" view — heads with nothing to retrieve park mass on a low-information token; "the interplay among softmax, residual connections, and LayerNorm encourages models to learn not to update residuals" — [When Attention Sink Emerges… (arXiv 2410.10781)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10781); (ii) "attention sinks and massive activations can emerge as a consequence of the implicit bias induced by the softmax parameterization," via a gradient-flow analysis showing training pushes softmax outputs toward one-hot — [arXiv 2601.22966](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22966); (iii) "under causal mask, attention sinks can induce pronounced gradient concentration ('gradient sinks')," and in pre-norm/RMSNorm architectures "massive activations can be understood as an adaptive response to this localized gradient pressure"; a V-scale intervention keeps sinks but suppresses massive activations — [Attention Sinks Induce Gradient Sinks (arXiv 2603.17771, March 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17771); (iv) ablations: replacing softmax with ReLU removes the sink; Adam→SGD removes massive activations but not the sink — [arXiv 2603.06248](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06248v1); (v) massive activations and sinks "co-occur in pre-norm transformers but need not be the same object" — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17771). Original phenomenology: Sun et al., "Massive Activations in LLMs" (arXiv 2402.17762) — [alphaxiv overview](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2402.17762v2). Verdict: (B)/(C) — gradient-flow results on simplified models; empirically contested.
- **Information/MDL-style arguments**: Finzi et al. 2025 — quantization error as a generalization-bound term; larger compute-optimal models are more quantizable "from an information-theoretic perspective" — [arXiv 2504.15208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15208). Lotfi et al. 2024 compression bounds **[recalled, unsourced]**.
- **Quantization vs pruning**: Kuzmin et al., "Pruning vs Quantization: Which is Better?" (NeurIPS 2023) — analytic comparison of expected error for Gaussian(-like) weight distributions plus empirical study; conclusion (recalled) is that quantization dominates except at extreme compression where pruning can win **[recalled; search not executed before budget exhaustion; no URL]**. Low-rank vs quantization: CALDERA's rank-vs-bits trade-off analysis is the only sourced theory — [CALDERA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.18886v2). HT-SR/AlphaPruning provide a spectral criterion for *pruning* allocation that has no quantization analogue yet — [AlphaPruning](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10912v1).
- **"Quantization makes the loss landscape…"**: no 2025–26 theory paper found on this; the closest are the flat-minima heuristics (KQ6) and the spectral-flattening preprint (KQ4).
- **2026 syntheses**: "Transforms for LLM Quantization: The Great Inversion and Format Co-Design" (arXiv 2608.25188) surveys transform theory and warns single-RHT deployments lack the stronger later guarantees — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25188); "Structured Transforms for Low-Overhead Quantization" (arXiv 2609.11687) builds on Kashin — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11687).

### Inferences
- A unifying observation across the 2025–26 theory: every rigorous result lives on the *layer-wise MSE proxy* (lattice/CVP, rate–distortion, incoherence) and the only bridges to loss are first-order (HIGGS linearity; QAM-W's KL≈¼‖δ‖²) or bound-based (Lipschitz, TV distance), all of which are known to be loose. The field's principal theoretical gap is therefore not better proxy bounds but a *non-vacuous proxy-to-loss theorem* for residual pre-norm transformers — plausibly achievable *locally* (fixed activation trajectory, per-token softmax TV distance as in 2605.20868) rather than globally.
- When quantization is *not* the right tool: by the rate–distortion view, if a layer's H-weighted error is dominated by a few eigen-directions (power-law spectrum), low-rank correction is strictly more rate-efficient for those directions than uniform bits (reverse water-filling assigns them most of the budget anyway); pruning wins only when the weight distribution has substantial mass near zero relative to the quantization step (Kuzmin's regime). This is consistent across CALDERA, SPECTRA's "zero bits = rank reduction," and Kuzmin, but no single paper proves a three-way optimality.

### Gaps
- 2606.22249's exact theorem statements/assumptions (width, depth, target class) not fetched.
- Kuzmin et al. 2023 and Lotfi et al. 2024 not sourced in-session.
- No paper found titled or functioning as "a theory of quantization for LLMs" in the sense of an end-to-end limit; the search explicitly returned none.
- No MDL/Kolmogorov-complexity argument specific to choosing quantization vs pruning vs low-rank was found.
