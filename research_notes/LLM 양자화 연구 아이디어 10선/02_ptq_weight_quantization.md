## KQ1. Second-order / Hessian-based methods (OBS→OBQ→GPTQ→QuIP/QuIP#/QTIP, GPTAQ, AQLM/PV-tuning, VPTQ/GPTVQ, SqueezeLLM/OWQ/SpQR, YAQA, GuidedQuant): what the theorems bound, what they assume, and where they break

### Takeaway
Every "theorem" in this lineage bounds the **layer-wise proxy loss** tr((Ŵ−W)H(Ŵ−W)ᵀ) with H = 2XXᵀ, not end-to-end loss; the bounds need (i) μ-incoherence of W and H (obtained w.h.p. by random orthogonal/Hadamard preprocessing) and (ii) for the codebook results, an approximately i.i.d. Gaussian weight distribution after rotation. The 2025–26 wave (GPTAQ, YAQA, GuidedQuant, Qronos, "first-order error matters") is almost entirely about the fact that the proxy objective is misspecified: it ignores input error accumulated from earlier quantized layers, ignores which output coordinates matter to the final loss, and ignores the first-order term. The best practical 2-bit numbers now come from trellis/lattice codebooks (QTIP) plus end-to-end-KL-aware rounding (YAQA), but these gains at ≤2 bits still depend on hours of GPU time and often on fine-tuning.

### Cited Findings

**OBS/OBC/GPTQ lineage**
- GPTQ (Frantar, Ashkboos, Hoefler, Alistarh; ICLR 2023; arXiv 2210.17323 [ID from memory]) quantizes columns sequentially with OBS-style updates to the remaining weights using the inverse Hessian of the layer-wise ℓ2 reconstruction; official README reports LLaMA-7B WikiText2 FP16 5.68 → 4-bit GPTQ 6.09 (RTN 6.29), 3-bit GPTQ 8.07 (RTN 25.54), 3-bit g128 6.61; `--act-order` (quantize columns in decreasing activation magnitude) "fixes GPTQ's poor performance on LLaMA-7B, improving Wiki2 perplexity from 7.15 to 6.09"; OPT-66B 3-bit improves from 14.16 to 9.95 with act-order. Stated limitation (README): 3-bit kernels only optimized for OPT-175B on A100/A6000; LLaMA integration minimal — [GPTQ README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IST-DASLab/gptq/main/README.md)
- The OBQ/OBC predecessor (Frantar & Alistarh, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2208.11580 [ID from memory]) is the exact per-row OBS greedy; GPTQ's contribution is the arbitrary-order + lazy-batch + Cholesky reformulation [recollection].
- **GPTQ = Babai's nearest-plane algorithm.** "The Geometry of LLM Quantization: GPTQ as Babai's Nearest Plane Algorithm" (arXiv 2507.18553 [ID verified], listed as ICLR 2026) and the companion "The Lattice Geometry of Neural Network Quantization" (arXiv 2508.01077 [ID verified via htqin list], ICLR 2026) show that GPTQ executed back-to-front on the lattice generated by the Cholesky factor of H is exactly Babai's nearest-plane algorithm, so GPTQ inherits Babai's error bound (error in the H-norm bounded by a sum of squared Gram–Schmidt norms) and the ordering (act-order) has a geometric interpretation. Search surfaced only abstracts; exact theorem statement and the no-clipping assumption not re-read — [arXiv 2507.18553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18553); [htqin list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/htqin/awesome-model-quantization/master/README.md)

**QuIP (Chee, Cai, Kuleshov, De Sa; NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2307.13304 [ID verified])**
- Definitions: a symmetric Hessian is μ-incoherent if it has an eigendecomposition with all |Q_ij| ≤ μ/√n; a weight matrix W is μ-incoherent if all |W_ij| ≤ μ‖W‖_F/√(mn). — [QuIP PDF via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13304)
- LDLQ (adaptive rounding with linear feedback using the LDL decomposition of H) has worst-case proxy loss governed by tr(D) (D from H = (U+I)D(U+I)ᵀ), vs tr(H) for nearest/stochastic rounding; "nearest rounding achieves the same worst-case proxy loss as stochastic rounding, but achieves better average proxy loss"; the optimality gap of the baselines to LDLQ "is governed by tr(D) vs. tr(H)". — [QuIP PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13304)
- Lemma 2 (spectral bound): for a μ-incoherent PSD H, tr(D) is bounded using only the spectrum of H; the worked example is rank-k H with μ²k < n, where Cauchy–Schwarz gives (tr H^{1/2})² ≤ k·tr(H). — [QuIP PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13304)
- QuIP shows LDLQ is equivalent to OPTQ/GPTQ (so the theory applies to GPTQ), and the repo ships `optq_counter.py`, which computes a (W,H) counterexample on a finite grid where OPTQ/LDLQ is not optimal, and an `ldlbal_admm` variant claimed to give "a better theoretical bound". — [QuIP README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/QuIP/main/README.md)
- Incoherence is produced by random orthogonal Kronecker-structured matrices; μ grows polylogarithmically in dimension w.h.p. (secondary summary quotes O(log² n) for Kronecker vs O(√log n) for Hadamard; constants not verified against primary) — [search summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/quip)
- Assumption where it breaks: the bound is on the **proxy loss of one layer** with H estimated from calibration inputs of the *unquantized* model; nothing propagates to the next layer or to the end loss (see GPTAQ below).

**QuIP# (Tseng, Chee, Sun, Kuleshov, De Sa; ICML 2024; arXiv 2402.04396 [ID verified])**
- Replaces Kronecker with the Randomized Hadamard Transform (RHT); "All of QuIP's theory analyzing the proxy loss still holds with the RHT, with the improved incoherence rates propagating through." — [QuIP# PDF via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04396)
- Uses the E8 lattice (densest packing in 8-D; "E8P" 2^16-entry codebook) for 2-bit vector quantization, plus inter-layer fine-tuning; README: end-to-end FT tunes sign vectors (SU/SV), layernorms and lm_head; `--ft_epochs 0` reproduces no-FT numbers. Repo "no longer under active development"; no optimized 3-bit matvec kernel; Hessian generation uses fp64 accumulators. — [QuIP# README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/quip-sharp/main/README.md)
- Throughput (RTX 6000 Ada, bs=1): Llama-2-70B FP16 OOM, AQLM 2-bit 8.72 tok/s, QuIP# 2-bit 21.9 tok/s. — [QuIP# README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/quip-sharp/main/README.md)
- Numbers quoted inside the QTIP paper's table for Llama-2-7B 2-bit WikiText2: QuIP# 8.22 (no FT), 6.19 (with FT), AQLM 6.64 (ctx 4096; FP16 Llama-2-7B is 5.12 at this context). — [search summary of QTIP table](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v1)
- Theory claim recalled but not re-read [recollection]: Lemma "with RHT, W is μ-incoherent with μ = √(2 log(2n²/δ)) w.p. ≥ 1−δ"; E8P MSE on Gaussian source at 2 bits ≈ 0.089 vs scalar Lloyd–Max 0.1175 vs Shannon 0.0625 — treat the MSE constants as unverified.

**QTIP (Tseng, Sun, De Sa; NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2406.11235 [ID verified])**
- Trellis-coded quantization (TCQ) gives "ultra-high dimensional" (256-D) quantization at linear-in-dimension cost, beating VQ (QuIP#/AQLM) which is limited to ≤8–16-D codebooks; "computed-code QTIP variants without fine-tuning outperform both QuIP# and AQLM with fine-tuning on almost all models and sizes." — [QTIP PDF via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11235)
- Llama-2-70B 2-bit: WikiText2 3.87 vs FP16 3.12 (ctx 4096), "about half the gap of QuIP#"; C4 5.48 vs QuIP# 5.71 vs AQLM 5.62 (secondary summary). — [search summary](https://sotaverified.org/papers/qtip-quantization-with-trellises-and); [alphaxiv summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2406.11235)
- Llama-3 results are reported at ctx 8192 and the table caption states that "Hessian-based rounding generally underperforms on Llama 3" (a direct admission that LDLQ/GPTQ-style rounding is weaker on Llama-3). — [ResearchGate figure caption](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/QTIP-vs-QuIP-Llama-3-ctx-8192-for-perplexity-Although-Hessian-based-rounding_tbl3_381485933)
- Throughput: Llama-2-70B 2-bit QTIP 23.5 tok/s vs AQLM 8.78 tok/s (RTX 6000 Ada). README states the included kernels only cover the HYB code with L=16, Q=9, V=2; TP8 405B models use per-GPU RHT with "slightly worse quality"; README now says "YAQA … outperforms the BlockLDLQ rounding algorithm used in this repository." — [QTIP README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/qtip/main/README.md)
- Theory [recollection, not re-read]: QTIP's TCQ with a "bitshift trellis" and pseudo-random (hash/LUT) Gaussian codebook is analysed as approaching the Gaussian distortion–rate function D(R)=2^{-2R}σ² as trellis size L grows; the analysis assumes the RHT-processed weights are ≈ i.i.d. Gaussian. The scalar fixed-rate Lloyd–Max optimum for a unit Gaussian is 0.1175 (2 bits), 0.03454 (3 bits), 0.009497 (4 bits) vs Shannon 0.0625 / 0.015625 / 0.0039 (classical values, Max 1960 / Gersho–Gray) — i.e. scalar quantizers are 1.9×–2.4× (2.7–3.9 dB) above the bound at 2–4 bits; that gap is the entire motivation for VQ/TCQ. Exact QTIP MSE numbers: unverified.
- Follow-ups: "BCJR-QAT: A Differentiable Relaxation of Trellis-Coded Weight Quantization" (arXiv 2605.10655 [ID verified]) calls QTIP "the current 2-bit post-training frontier" and beats it by only −0.084 PPL on Llama-3.2-1B with distillation — a within-noise improvement that is also QAT, not PTQ. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10655)

**GPTAQ / "GPTQv2" (Li, Yin, Lee, Xiao, Panda; ICML 2025; arXiv 2504.02692 [ID verified])**
- "Asymmetric calibration": instead of min‖ŴX − WX‖² with X the already-quantized-model input, it minimizes ‖ŴX − WX̃‖² where X̃ is the full-precision model's input, so the closed-form OBS update minimizes quantization error **plus the accumulated error from earlier layers**; "~20 more lines of code than GPTQ"; 405B quantized on a single GPU. — [GPTAQ via search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02692v3); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/li25dn.html)
- README figure (read from image): per-block MAE loss grows with block index for GPTQ and is visibly flatter for GPTQv2 — direct evidence of error accumulation across depth under symmetric calibration. — [GPTQv2 README image](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Intelligent-Computing-Lab-Yale/GPTQv2/main/img/readme_intro.png)
- README (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, g128): GPTQ v1 ARC-c acc 0.500, GSM8K-platinum-CoT (flexible) 0.3995; GPTQv2 ARC-c 0.5034, GSM8K 0.7601; README says "GPTQv2 outperforms GPTQ on GSM8K while using one fewer bit" (bit-width of each checkpoint ambiguous in README). Note ARC-c is unchanged while GSM8K nearly doubles → perplexity/commonsense metrics do not track reasoning. — [GPTQv2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Intelligent-Computing-Lab-Yale/GPTQv2/main/README.md)
- Limitations: no stated-limitations section retrieved; the closed form still minimizes a per-layer Frobenius objective (output-feature-agnostic) and requires storing both quantized-path and FP-path activations.

**Error-accumulation / proxy-misspecification wave (2025–26)**
- "First-Order Error Matters: Accurate Compensation for Quantized LLMs" (arXiv 2507.11017 [ID verified]; AAAI 2026): adds a first-order gradient term to the OBS compensation (approximated from latent vs FP weights with precomputed Cholesky factors), gains over GPTQ especially at 3-bit. — [pprp list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pprp/Awesome-LLM-Quantization/main/README.md)
- "Qronos: Correcting the Past by Shaping the Future in Post-Training Quantization" (arXiv 2505.11695 [ID verified via list], ICLR 2026) — corrects error from both weights and previously quantized activations [recollection of abstract].
- "Quantization Error Propagation: Revisiting Layer-Wise Post-Training Quantization" (arXiv 2504.09629 [ID verified via search list]) and "Rethinking Residual Errors in Compensation-based LLM Quantization" (arXiv 2604.07955 [ID verified], ICLR 2026) — both revisit the layer-wise objective; details not retrieved.
- GuidedQuant (Kim et al.; ICML 2025; arXiv 2505.07004 [ID verified]): objective ‖(∂ℓ/∂Z^{(l)}) ⊙ (X W − X Ŵ)‖_F² (end-loss-gradient-weighted layer output error), approximated by grouping output channels and averaging a Hessian per group; needs a single backprop pass over calibration data; plug-in to any layer-wise method; also LNQ, a non-uniform scalar quantizer "guaranteed to monotonically decrease the quantization objective"; one summary: 2-bit Llama-2-7B perplexity from 39.58 → 8.83 with LNQ (baseline identity unclear). README: gradient extraction 0.3 h on 1×A100 (7B), 2.7 h on 6–7 A100 (70B); vector/W+A path "only tested on Llama"; README says YAQA is better but needs ~6 h on 8×H100 for Llama-2-7B. — [search](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.07004); [GuidedQuant README](https://github.com/snu-mllab/GuidedQuant)
- **YAQA / "Model-Preserving Adaptive Rounding"** (Tseng, Sun, De Sa; arXiv 2505.22988 [ID verified]; listed as ICML 2026 by a secondary note): Kronecker-factored approximation of each linear layer's Hessian *with respect to the full-model KL divergence*; rounding adds a symmetric output-side feedback term to LDLQ; "reduces the KL by ≈30% over LDLQ, achieves a lower KL than QAT" (Gemma-3 Google-QAT); theory claim: "end-to-end error can be bounded by the approximation's cosine similarity to the true Hessian". Repo figure (read from image): KL(orig‖quant) on WikiText2 at 8K ctx, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct: QTIP 2-bit ≈0.50 / 3-bit ≈0.14 / 4-bit ≈0.045; YAQA 2-bit ≈0.34 / 3-bit ≈0.095 / 4-bit ≈0.03; Gemma-3-12B-It 4-bit: Google QAT ≈0.09 vs YAQA ≈0.055. One 4-bit data point from a secondary summary: Llama-3.1-8B KL 0.013 (YAQA-B) vs 0.019 (LDLQ). — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22988); [YAQA comp.png](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/yaqa/main/assets/comp.png); [YAQA README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cornell-RelaxML/yaqa/main/README.md)
- KronQ: "LLM Quantization via Kronecker-Factored Hessian" (arXiv 2607.07964 [ID verified via search]) — same idea, details not retrieved. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07964)
- "REAL-Q: E2E LLM Quantization via Dynamic Gradient Descent" (arXiv 2609.00049 [ID verified via search]) compares against GuidedQuant and describes it as "aligning the Hessian with the end-to-end loss". — [search](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00049v2)

**Additive / vector / codebook methods**
- AQLM (Egiazarian et al.; ICML 2024; arXiv 2401.06118 [ID verified via pprp list]): multi-codebook additive quantization, 1×16 (one 16-bit codebook per 8-D group) or 2×8. README (no PV-tuning): WikiText2 2-bit 1×16: Llama-2-7B 5.92, 13B 5.22, 70B 3.83; MMLU(5-shot) FP16→AQLM: Llama-2-7B 0.46→0.39, 13B 0.55→0.49, 70B 0.69→0.65, Llama-3-8B 0.65→0.56, Llama-3-70B 0.79→0.75, Llama-3-8B-Instruct 0.66→0.59, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (2×8) 0.59→0.44. Cost: "about 1 day on a single A100" for 7B, "10–14 days" for 70B. README warns perplexity "should not be compared between models that use different vocabularies". — [AQLM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Vahe1994/AQLM/main/README.md)
- PV-Tuning (Malinovskii et al.; NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2405.14852 [ID from memory]): fine-tunes discrete codes + continuous params with a provably convergent alternating scheme (replaces straight-through estimators) [recollection]. README (PV-tuned AQLM, WikiText2): Llama-2-7B 1×16g8 5.68 (2-bit), 1×8g8 1-bit 7.85, 1×16g16 1-bit 9.21; Llama-2-13B 5.05; Llama-2-70B 3.78; **Llama-3-8B 2-bit 6.99**, 1-bit 9.43; **Llama-3-70B 2-bit 4.57**, 1-bit 8.67; Mistral-7B 5.22. - GPTVQ (van Baalen et al., Qualcomm; arXiv 2402.15319 [ID verified]): GPTQ-style column-by-column VQ with Hessian updates, data-aware EM codebook init, codebooks further compressed by INT quantization + SVD; Llama-2-70B takes 3–11 h on one H100; claims SOTA size/accuracy at 2.125–3.125 bpw; no tables in README. — [GPTVQ README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qualcomm-ai-research/gptvq/main/README.md)

- Q-Palette (NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2509.20214 [ID verified via list]): fractional-bit quantizers from trellis-coded down to scalar with CUDA kernels + fusion-aware mixed-scheme allocation. — [pprp list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pprp/Awesome-LLM-Quantization/main/README.md)

**Sensitivity / outlier-aware scalar methods**

### Inferences
- At 4-bit g128, every method on Llama-2 is within ~0.03–0.05 PPL of each other (OmniQuant 5.58 vs GPTQ 5.61 vs AWQ 5.62 vs RTN 5.72 on 7B): 4-bit improvements are within noise and 4-bit weight-only is a solved problem for perplexity on Llama-2. The action is at ≤3 bits and on newer models.
- The W2A16 table shows GPTQ's reconstruction objective fails catastrophically without groups (7.7e3) and AWQ fails entirely at 2 bits (10^5) — AWQ's scaling heuristic has no mechanism for 2-bit; any 2-bit claim that only compares to AWQ/RTN is comparing to a strawman.
- The theoretical core (LDLQ/Babai bound ∝ tr(D)) is tight only for the proxy objective; the QTIP authors' own caption ("Hessian-based rounding generally underperforms on Llama 3") and YAQA's ~30% KL reduction over LDLQ show that in practice the proxy is the bottleneck, not the rounding algorithm. The theory–practice gap is in the *objective*, not the *solver*.
- Reported "PTQ" gains from QuIP#/AQLM/PV-tuning/QTIP are entangled with fine-tuning (layernorm/sign-vector/E2E FT); QTIP's claim that its no-FT variant beats FT'd competitors is the only clean statement I found. Any novel-method comparison must report FT/no-FT separately.

### Gaps
- Exact theorem statements/assumptions of QuIP#, QTIP, YAQA and the Babai paper could not be re-read (arXiv blocked). In particular I could not confirm QTIP's Gaussian-source distortion constants or YAQA's cosine-similarity bound in its precise form.
- No paper found gives a bound relating layer-wise proxy loss to end-to-end KL/perplexity without further approximation; YAQA's claim is the closest and is itself conditional on Hessian-approximation quality.
- No published Llama-3-8B/70B 2-bit *no-fine-tuning* numbers for QTIP/QuIP# were retrievable (the QTIP Llama-3 table was garbled in all accessible renderings).

## KQ2. Rotation / equivalent-transform methods (SmoothQuant, AWQ, OmniQuant-LET, QuaRot, SpinQuant, DuQuant, FlatQuant, OSTQuant, OptRot, KurTail, "Great Inversion"): why rotations help, what theory exists, and what residual error survives rotation

### Takeaway
What is actually proven is narrow: (a) rotations commute with RMSNorm (computational invariance), (b) a random orthogonal/Hadamard transform makes weights/Hessians μ-incoherent with μ = O(polylog) w.h.p. and drives coordinate kurtosis toward 3 (CLT), and (c) OptRot shows the GPTQ objective has an upper bound in terms of weight incoherence (4th-moment), so the right rotation is not Hadamard but a minimizer of that bound. What is empirically established but not proven: rotated activations remain non-Gaussian on massive-activation tokens, random rotations have large seed variance, rotation alone fails below W4A4/W2, and for *weight-only* quantization rotation gives at most ~0.1 PPL over GPTQ at 4 bits. The 2026 "Great Inversion" paper argues that flattening (what rotation does) is optimal only for grouped shared-scale formats and is *opposed* to what entropy-/trellis-coded quantizers want — a genuine open theoretical conflict.

### Cited Findings
- SmoothQuant (Xiao et al.; ICML 2023; arXiv 2211.10438 [ID from memory]) migrates activation outlier magnitude into weights via per-channel scales s = max|X|^α / max|W|^{1−α} — primarily an activation-quantization method [recollection]; included here because AWQ, OS+, OmniQuant-LET and OSTQuant's scaling all generalize it.
- AWQ (Lin et al.; MLSys 2024 best paper; arXiv 2306.00978 [ID from memory]): per-channel scaling chosen by grid search on activation statistics to protect ~1% salient channels; README Model Zoo lists INT4-g128/INT3-g128 for Llama-3 8B/70B; multimodal models calibrated on text only. — [llm-awq README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mit-han-lab/llm-awq/main/README.md). Numbers: Llama-2-7B W3g128 6.24 / W4g128 5.62 (OmniQuant table above); Llama-3-8B W4g128 6.6, W3g128 8.2, **W2g128 1.7e6** (collapse); Llama-3-70B W3g128 4.8 (best 3-bit in that study), W2g128 1.7e6 — [LLaMA3-Quantization table image](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Macaronlin/LLaMA3-Quantization/master/images/result_ptq_1.png).
- QuaRot (Ashkboos et al.; NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2404.00456 [ID verified]): Hadamard rotations fused into weights via computational invariance ("rotates LLMs in a way that removes outliers from the hidden state without changing the output"); README claims Llama-2-70B loses ≤0.29 WikiText PPL and keeps 99% zero-shot (W4A4KV4). — [QuaRot README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spcl/QuaRot/main/README.md). Kurtosis evidence: after rotation, activation kurtosis ≈3 across layers (asserted empirically). — [search summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/quarot)
- Computational invariance (RMSNorm commutation) originates in SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al.; ICLR 2024; arXiv 2401.15024 [ID from memory]): RMSNorm(xQ) = RMSNorm(x)Q for orthogonal Q once the per-channel norm scale is folded into adjacent weights; LayerNorm needs its mean-subtraction converted to RMSNorm first [recollection].
- SpinQuant (Liu et al., Meta; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2405.16406 [ID verified via search link]): "random rotations cause large variance in quantized accuracy, and learning the rotation works better"; rotations optimized on the Stiefel manifold via Cayley transform; README tables (zero-shot avg / Wiki2): **W4A16**: Llama-2-7B FP 66.9/5.5, GPTQ 64.5/11.3, QuaRot 65.8/5.6, SpinQuant 65.9/5.6; Llama-3-8B FP 69.6/6.1, GPTQ 66.5/7.2, QuaRot 68.4/6.4, SpinQuant 68.5/6.4. W4A4KV4: Llama-3-8B QuaRot 63.3/8.0, SpinQuant 65.2/7.3. README caveats: paper numbers from Meta-internal code; released code is a reproduction; CC-BY-NC license. — [SpinQuant README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/SpinQuant/main/README.md)
- OSTQuant (Hu et al.; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2501.13987 [ID verified]): defines QSUR (Quantization Space Utilization Rate) to quantify how much of the quantization hypercube a distribution fills, arguing prior transform methods "are primarily heuristic and result-driven, lacking a quantitative metric"; toy example QSUR: original 19%, SmoothQuant 41%, rotation 56%, OSTQuant 81%; global orthogonal R_res + per-block scalings; KL-Top loss. README W4A16 (avg/Wiki2): Llama-3-8B FP 68.09/6.14, GPTQ 61.03/7.43, OmniQuant 65.66/7.19, AWQ 67.03/7.36, QuaRot 67.27/6.53, SpinQuant 66.54/6.49, OSTQuant 67.80/6.53; Llama-2-7B QuaRot 64.30/5.62, SpinQuant 63.59/5.58, OSTQuant 64.37/5.64. Baselines copied from SpinQuant's tables, not rerun. — [OSTQuant README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BrotherHappy/OSTQuant/main/README.md); [search](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2501.13987v1)
- FlatQuant (Sun et al.; ICML 2025; arXiv 2410.09426 [ID from memory]): learnable per-layer affine (Kronecker-factored) transforms for "flatness"; README W4A4 Wiki2: Llama-3-70B QuaRot-RTN 55.44 (collapse), SpinQuant-RTN 7.58, FlatQuant-RTN 3.78 (FP 2.86); notes "the last two layers of DeepSeek V3/R1 are hard to quantize effectively" (flag `--v3_not_last`). No W4A16 numbers. — [FlatQuant README](https://github.com/ruikangliu/FlatQuant)
- DuQuant (Lin et al.; NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2406.01721 [ID from memory]): block-diagonal rotations targeted at outlier channels + zigzag permutation to balance outliers across blocks, mainly for W4A4 [recollection]. Theory: none retrieved.
- KurTail (EMNLP 2025 Findings; arXiv 2503.01483 [ID verified via search]): learns rotations by minimizing a kurtosis-based tail-density objective per layer; reports original Llama-2-7B activation space has "infinite kurtosis due to extreme outliers" while the rotated space shows "predominantly near-zero" excess kurtosis; **"rotation alone can't guarantee the performance in extremely low-bit scenarios like W2A4."** — [KurTail search](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.943.pdf)
- **Residual non-Gaussianity after rotation**: one 2025–26 rotation paper surfaced by search reports that for massive-activation tokens "a random orthogonal rotation would instead *increase* their error, so those massive-activation tokens dominate the residual loss" (source among: OffQ arXiv 2606.07116, Rotate-Clip-Partition arXiv 2502.15779, ReSpinQuant arXiv 2604.11080 — exact attribution not confirmed). A 2026 survey describes "Rotated Runtime Smooth" as applying a QuaRot-style Hadamard first and then a runtime smoothing scale "to mop up the channel-wise outliers the rotation leaves behind". — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07116v1); [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25188)
- **OptRot** (Gadhikar et al.; Dec 2025; arXiv 2512.24124 [ID verified]): "the GPTQ objective has an upper bound which depends on the weight incoherence"; OptRot minimizes "a smooth proxy for the weight incoherence of the model: the element-wise fourth power of the rotated weights" (data-free); implies Hadamard is not the optimal rotation for weight-only quantization; caveat from the same source: "in more aggressive W4A4 quantization, exclusive focus on weight outlier reduction is less effective due to activation-dominated error." — [search](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24124)
- HARP: "Hadamard-Preconditioned Adaptive Rotation Processor" (arXiv 2605.29843 [ID verified via search]) — learnable two-sided orthogonal processor as drop-in for Hadamard for extreme low-bit PTQ; details not retrieved. — [search](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.29843.md)
- **"Transforms for LLM Quantization: The Great Inversion and Format Co-Design"** (Ehsan Jokar; single-author v1, 25 Aug 2026; arXiv 2608.25188 [ID verified]; cs.LG + cs.IT): the "Great Inversion" is that "allocation-flexible coding favors concentrating energy, while the grouped shared-scale quantization that deployed matrix instructions use favors flattening within each group"; this opposition is "proven under within-group majorization", and "for a generic spectrum no optimality guarantee carries across the two regimes"; also states composing two randomized Hadamard transforms "brings every marginal within O(d^{-1/2}) of a standard Gaussian"; lists four open problems: joint transform-and-rounding optimality, a guarantee for the deployed extreme-value (max-abs scale) objective, returning allocation-flexible coding to the inference path, and transform–format co-design. Not peer-reviewed. — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25188)
- Kashin representation: "Kashin Quantization" (arXiv 2404.09737 [ID from memory]) uses the Kashin/Lyubarskii–Vershynin result that any x∈ℝⁿ can be represented via a tight frame of 2n vectors with coefficients bounded in ℓ∞ by C‖x‖₂/√n — a deterministic worst-case flatness guarantee that Hadamard rotation only gives probabilistically [recollection; not verified this session].
- "Block Rotation is All You Need for MXFP4 Quantization" (arXiv 2511.04214 [ID verified via search]) and "Normalized Architectures are Natively 4-Bit" (arXiv 2605.06067 [ID verified via list], NeurIPS 2026) connect rotation/normalization to format-level quantizability; details not retrieved.

### Inferences
- For **weight-only** 4-bit, rotations buy ~0.1 PPL on Llama-3-8B (QuaRot/SpinQuant 6.4–6.53 vs GPTQ-g128 6.5) and nothing on Llama-2-7B (5.6 vs OmniQuant 5.58). The large headline gains in these papers are W4A4 (activation) results. The SpinQuant/OSTQuant W4A16 GPTQ baselines (11.3 and 9.84 on Llama-2-7B) are anomalously weak (OmniQuant's table has GPTQ per-channel W4 at 5.83) — a weak-baseline artifact that inflates rotation's weight-only benefit.
- Kurtosis≈3 after rotation is a *marginal* statement; the joint distribution is not i.i.d. Gaussian (weights are low-rank-ish and structured, activations have token-level spikes). Every codebook/trellis method that assumes Gaussianity after RHT is therefore operating under a known-false assumption whose cost has not been quantified.
- OptRot and the Great Inversion point in opposite directions: OptRot minimizes 4th moment (flatten) for GPTQ-style grouped scalar grids; the Inversion says for VQ/TCQ/entropy-coded quantizers you want *concentration* (low-entropy spectrum). A unifying result — the optimal transform as a function of the quantizer family — does not exist.

### Gaps
- No paper found that formally characterizes the distribution of RHT-rotated LLM weights (e.g., Student-t tails, dependence structure) or bounds the excess distortion of a Gaussian-designed codebook on them.
- No theorem connects μ-incoherence to a *downstream* metric; only to proxy loss.
- The Great Inversion's proofs and the exact statement of OptRot's bound could not be read.

## KQ3. Outlier phenomena (LLM.int8 emergent features, Outlier Suppression+, Bondarenko "no-op attention", attention sinks, massive activations, super weights): proposed mechanisms, validated vs speculative

### Takeaway
There are now three partly competing causal stories: (1) *softmax-pressure*: attention heads needing a "no-op" push logits to extremes on low-information tokens (Bondarenko 2023; StreamingLLM 2023), (2) *fixed bias vectors*: a handful of massive activations in fixed channels at fixed token positions act as input-agnostic biases that create sinks (Sun et al. 2024; "Massive Spikes … are Bias Vectors" 2026), (3) *super weights*: ≤6 scalars (mostly in early down_proj) causally produce the massive activations and suppress stop-word probabilities (Yu et al. 2024). Interventional evidence exists for (2) and (3) (mean-replacement, pruning, explicit attention-bias training), but 2025–26 work shows massive activations and sinks are **dissociable** (one can be removed while the other persists), so the "MA ⇒ sink" story is contested. For weight PTQ the practically validated lever is: protect the super weight(s) and the induced super activations, after which RTN with large blocks becomes competitive.

### Cited Findings
- LLM.int8() (Dettmers et al.; NeurIPS 2022; arXiv 2208.07339 [ID from memory]): emergent outlier features appear systematically at ~6.7B scale in OPT, concentrated in <0.1% of hidden dims, magnitudes up to ~60; mixed-precision decomposition; mechanism speculative (attributed to scale/perplexity threshold) [recollection].
- Outlier Suppression+ (Wei et al.; EMNLP 2023; arXiv 2304.09145 [ID from memory]): outliers are channel-concentrated and asymmetric; channel-wise shifting + scaling migrated into the next layer [recollection].
- Quantizable Transformers (Bondarenko, Nagel, Blankevoort; NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2306.12929 [ID from memory]): "attention layers occasionally perform partial residual state updates by assigning attention weights to semantically unimportant tokens, effectively implementing a 'no-op'", which requires near-zero softmax outputs, hence huge logits, hence FFN output outliers in the preceding layer; remedies: clipped softmax and gated attention, validated by *pretraining* BERT/OPT-125M/ViT [recollection + search summary]. — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12016)
- StreamingLLM / attention sinks (Xiao et al.; ICLR 2024; arXiv 2309.17453 [ID from memory]): initial tokens absorb disproportionate attention regardless of semantics because softmax must sum to one; validated by training a 160M model with a dedicated learnable sink token [recollection]. A 2025 paper reports models "drive attention logits toward negative infinity to get near-zero softmax values, which generates massive activations as a byproduct." — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17771)
- Massive Activations (Sun, Chen, Kolter, Liu; 2024; arXiv 2402.17762 [ID verified]): very few activations with magnitudes orders of magnitude larger than the rest, in fixed feature dims, appearing "predominantly within start tokens or delimiter tokens", emerging in a specific early layer; the repo implements interventions `set_zero` and `set_mean` and a GPT-2 variant "trained with explicit attention biases" (the paper's claim [recollection]: mean-replacement preserves performance while zeroing destroys it ⇒ they function as constant biases; adding explicit k/v attention-bias parameters removes them). — [massive-activations README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/locuslab/massive-activations/main/README.md); [search summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.05498)
- Super Weight (Yu, Wang, Shan, Wan; arXiv 2411.07191 [ID verified]; v2 Jul 2025): pruning a single scalar raises perplexity "by about three orders of magnitude" and drops zero-shot accuracy to chance; "at most six such scalars per model"; found data-free with one forward pass; super weights produce "super activations" in the matching channel immediately after the super weight; pruning the super weight sharply shrinks the activation ("points to causation rather than correlation"); but "super activations explain only part of the effect, since quality stays badly impaired even when they are suppressed"; v2 attributes collapse to shifting probability mass toward stopwords. Quantization: "Keeping super activations at high precision lets simple round-to-nearest quantization approach state-of-the-art methods", and "Preserving the super weight while clipping other outliers lets round-to-nearest work with much larger block sizes." — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07191); [LLMSuperWeight README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mengxiayu/LLMSuperWeight/main/README.md) (README gives scripts for block sizes tensor-wise…16384 with clipping thresholds, but no coordinates or numbers)
- Related: "House of Cards: Massive Weights in LLMs" (arXiv 2410.01866 [ID verified via search]); "Super Weights in LLMs and the Failure of Selective Training" (arXiv 2607.08733); "Unveiling Super Experts in MoE LLMs" (arXiv 2507.23279) — mechanisms generalize to MoE. — [search](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08733)
- **Dissociation evidence (2025–26):** "The Spike, the Sparse and the Sink: Anatomy of Massive Activations and Attention Sinks" (arXiv 2603.05498 [ID verified via search]) finds the co-occurrence "is largely an architectural artifact of modern Transformer design", that "the two phenomena serve related but distinct functions", and that "changing the normalization configuration can suppress massive activations while preserving attention sinks"; a separate 2025 paper reports "removing massive activations did not remove sink patterns". "Massive Spikes in LLMs are Bias Vectors: Mechanistic Uncovering and Spike-Free Quantization" (arXiv 2606.02288) and "Attention Sinks Induce Gradient Sinks: Massive Activations as Gradient Regulators" (arXiv 2603.17771) give further (partly contradictory) mechanisms; "Measuring Maximum Activations in Open LLMs" (arXiv 2605.15572) is a measurement survey. — [search](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.05498); [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02288)
- Optimizer/architecture origin: "Outlier-Safe Pre-Training for Robust 4-Bit Quantization of LLMs" (arXiv 2506.19697 [ID verified via search]) attributes outliers to the optimizer/normalization choices made in pretraining (pretraining-side; mention only). "Prefixing Attention Sinks can Mitigate Activation Outliers for LLM Quantization" (arXiv 2406.12016) is an inference-time mitigation. — [search](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19697v1)

### Inferences
- For *weight-only* PTQ, outliers matter through two channels: (i) super weights themselves (a per-element precision problem that group-wise scales handle poorly because one scalar dictates the group's scale), and (ii) the Hessian H=2XXᵀ, which is dominated by the massive-activation channels and hence makes GPTQ/LDLQ over-protect a few input columns (this is why act-order and OWQ "weak columns" work). The literature treats (i) and (ii) separately; no method I found exploits that super weight ⇒ super activation ⇒ Hessian spike is one causal chain.
- The claim "MA causes sinks" is experimentally supported only within specific architectures; the 2026 dissociation results mean any quantization method justified by "removing sinks" should be re-justified in terms of the spike vectors directly.

### Gaps
- Super-weight coordinates per model and the RTN-with-restoration quantitative results were not retrievable (README has scripts only).
- No study found that quantifies what fraction of GPTQ's layer-wise Hessian mass is attributable to massive-activation tokens, or that removes those tokens from calibration and measures the effect on weight PTQ.

## KQ4. Extreme low-bit (≤2-bit) PTQ frontier; are Llama-3 / Qwen3 / newer models harder to quantize, and why?

### Takeaway
On Llama-2-70B, 2-bit VQ/TCQ methods are within ~0.5–0.75 PPL of FP16 (AQLM-PV 3.78, QTIP 3.87 at ctx 4096 vs FP16 3.12–3.32), and MMLU loses ~4 points. On Llama-3-8B the gap is much larger: best PTQ-with-FT (AQLM-PV) 6.99 vs FP16 ≈5.5–6.1 (context-dependent), best scalar PTQ without FT (D²Quant, Feb 2026) 11.54 on Llama-3.1-8B vs FP16 ≈6.2, and MMLU drops 9 points (AQLM); scalar GPTQ/AWQ at 2-bit g128 collapse (210 / 10^6). Qwen3-8B appears harder still (3-bit AWQ g128 already collapses to MMLU 33%; 2-bit GPTQ is at chance). The dominant explanation on offer is a scaling-law one — PTQ degradation grows with tokens-per-parameter (Kumar et al. 2024; Ouyang et al. 2024/ACL 2025) — but it is a statistical fit, not a mechanism, and the Llama-3 study itself (Huang et al.) frames Llama-3 as "robust" to PTQ while its own tables show larger relative degradation than Llama-2. Nobody has isolated tokens vs. optimizer vs. architecture vs. vocabulary as the cause.

- **Scaling-law explanations:** "Scaling Laws for Precision" (Kumar et al.; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2411.04330 [ID verified]): "post-training quantization hurts more as models train on more data … eventually makes extra pretraining data harmful"; fit on 465 runs, validated up to 1.7B params / 26B tokens. — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330). - ParetoQ (Meta; arXiv 2502.02631 [ID verified]; NeurIPS 2025) argues 2-bit is a better accuracy/size trade-off than 4-bit **with QAT** — out of scope but defines the ceiling PTQ is chasing. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02631)
- DeepSeek: AutoRound README claims an "INT2-mixed DeepSeek-R1 (~200GB) retains 97.9% accuracy" (metric unspecified); FlatQuant reports the last two layers of DeepSeek-V3/R1 are hard to quantize; QTIP/VPTQ/LeanQuant demonstrate 405B-scale quantization. No peer-reviewed weight-only ≤3-bit PTQ study of DeepSeek-V3/R1 base was found. — [AutoRound README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/intel/auto-round/main/README.md); [FlatQuant README](https://github.com/ruikangliu/FlatQuant)

### Inferences
- Relative 3-bit-g128 GPTQ degradation: Llama-2-7B +15% PPL (6.29/5.47) vs Llama-3-8B +34% (8.2/6.1); at 2-bit g128 the ratio goes from 6.7× (36.77/5.47) to 34× (210/6.1); at w2g64 QAT (EfficientQAT) from +25% to +53%. Llama-3 is unambiguously harder *per bit* than Llama-2, and Qwen3 (36T tokens) harder than Llama-3 (15T) — consistent with, but not proof of, the tokens-per-parameter law.
- Current best Llama-3-8B 2-bit: ~7.0 PPL requires VQ + PV-tuning (hours–days of GPU); best cheap scalar PTQ ≈ 11.5 (D²Quant) ≈ 1.85× FP16; the 2-bit "gap to FP16" on 8B is therefore 1.2× (with FT) to 1.9× (no FT), vs ~1.2× on 70B even without FT. For MMLU the gap is 9 points (8B) vs 4 points (70B).
- The ~0.3-PPL improvements being reported at 2-bit in 2026 (e.g., 13.9→13.6) are within the run-to-run variance induced by calibration-set sampling (KQ5), so the scalar-PTQ 2-bit frontier on 8B models has effectively stalled around PPL 11–14 without FT.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment isolates *why* over-trained models quantize worse (weight kurtosis? lower effective rank? larger activation dynamic range? optimizer-induced outliers per arXiv 2506.19697?). The scaling-law papers measure the effect; they don't explain it.
- No consistent Llama-3/Qwen3 2-bit leaderboard with fixed context/calibration exists; QTIP/YAQA Llama-3 tables could not be read.
- DeepSeek-V3/R1 (FP8-native weights, MoE) weight-only ≤3-bit PTQ: no study found.

## KQ5. Calibration data, mixed-precision/bit allocation, non-uniform/LUT grids, and formats (INT vs FP4/NVFP4/MXFP4)

### Takeaway
Calibration-set sampling alone moves downstream accuracy by several points (1,800-model study), GPTQ measurably overfits its calibration set, newer models are *less* sensitive, domain-matched calibration helps GPTQ but not AWQ, and ~128 samples suffice for 4-bit — but nobody has a scaling law for calibration size vs bit-width. Bit allocation has moved from scalar Hessian-trace/Fisher scores (shown to be unreliable across modules: scalar bounds vary 10¹–10¹³) to Kronecker/activation-aware metrics, AutoML search, and fractional-bit quantizers; AutoRound's mixed MXFP4/8 at 4.5 bits recovers 96–98% vs 93–94% for pure 4-bit. Non-uniform grids (NF4, BOF4, SqueezeLLM, LNQ, LeanQuant) are provably better in MSE than NF4/INT but give "negligible" perplexity differences at 4 bits. On formats, NVFP4 (E4M3 scales, g16, 4.5 b/elem) beats MXFP4 (E8M0 scales, g32, 4.25 b/elem) and INT4 in most PTQ studies, MXFP4 shows ~10% relative drops that Meta's 2026 software-only scale tweaks shrink to <1%, and with Hadamard rotation NVINT4 can beat NVFP4 — the format question is unsettled and entangled with transforms.

### Cited Findings
**Calibration data**
- "On the Impact of Calibration Data in Post-training Quantization and Pruning" (Williams & Aletras; ACL 2024; arXiv 2311.09755 [ID verified]): varies only the calibration data across 4 compression methods × 9 LLMs × 5 source datasets × 10 draws = 1,800 compressed models; "performance can vary between calibration sets"; related-work notes GPTQ "tends to overfit to the calibration data when assessing perplexity on several datasets". — [search](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09755)
- Later syntheses: the two PTQ methods examined "are much less sensitive to the choice of calibration data than pruning"; "newer LLMs such as Llama-2 and Llama3 are significantly less sensitive to varying calibration data than the older OPT model"; "using shorter calibration sequences outperforms the commonly used fixed-length of 2048 tokens" (6 methods incl. AWQ/SmoothQuant). — [search summary via arXiv 2608.01078](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01078)
- "Understanding and Selecting Calibration Data for LLM Quantization: From Sensitivity Analysis to Activation-Based Curation" (ACL-SELVA 2026, OpenReview pfw3saHzGU): GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant+GPTQ × 6 models (Qwen2.5, Llama-3.1); domain-matched calibration helps mainly GPTQ on Qwen2.5 specialized variants, "little consistent benefit for AWQ"; AWQ's sensitivity "depends more on activation-distribution mismatch than on surface domain mismatch". — [search](https://openreview.net/forum?id=pfw3saHzGU)
- Qwen3-8B GPTQ W4: 128–512 samples similar, smaller sets degrade (setting-specific). — [Qwen3-Quantization README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Efficient-ML/Qwen3-Quantization/main/README.md)
- "Calibrating Beyond English: Language Diversity for Better Quantized Multilingual LLM" (arXiv 2601.18306) — GPTQ/AWQ multilingual calibration; findings not retrieved. - GPTAQ (KQ1) reframes calibration itself: use FP-model activations as targets, quantized-model activations as inputs.

**Mixed precision / bit allocation**
- SliM-LLM: group-wise salience-determined allocation at average 2 bits (numbers in KQ4). OWQ: Hessian-metric "weak columns" in FP16. SpQR: sparse FP16 outliers + two-level grouping. SqueezeLLM: Fisher sensitivity + 0.05–0.45% sparse outliers. — [survey quotes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.16694)
- HAWQ-style Hessian-trace allocation "requires expensive O(d²) Hessian approximations per layer and lacks cross-model transferability"; FAMPWQ (arXiv 2608.24945) injects quantization-like noise and measures the Fisher-information change, then allocates bits by RL; CASA (arXiv 2609.25916, "Beyond Scalar Sensitivity") argues collapsing a module's Hessian into a scalar is unreliable because "this bound varies from 10¹ to 10¹³ for typical LLM modules, making inter-module sensitivity ranking unreliable", and uses a Kronecker-factored activation-aware metric with cross-layer refinement. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24945); [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25916v1)
- AMQ "Enabling AutoML for Mixed-precision Weight-Only Quantization" (arXiv 2509.12019) searches layer-wise bit configs under a memory budget (Pareto frontier). Q-Palette (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2509.20214) fractional-bit quantizers + fusion-aware allocation. "Adapting to Any Bit-Width: Channel-Wise Mixed-Precision" (arXiv 2410.13056). AnyBCQ (ICLR 2026). — [pprp list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pprp/Awesome-LLM-Quantization/main/README.md); [htqin list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/htqin/awesome-model-quantization/master/README.md)
- AutoRound AutoScheme (mixed MXFP4/8; 5-task avg incl. MMLU, fake-quant): Llama-3.1-8B-I BF16 0.7076; pure 4-bit 0.6626 (93.6%); 4.5-bit 0.6808 (96.2%); 5-bit 0.6857 (96.9%); 6-bit 0.6975 (98.6%). Naive "tail/head layers 8-bit" heuristics at 5 bits give only 93.5–94.8%. — [auto_scheme_acc.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/intel/auto-round/main/docs/auto_scheme_acc.md)

**Non-uniform / LUT / learned grids**
- NF4 (QLoRA; Dettmers et al. 2023; arXiv 2305.14314 [ID from memory]) claims information-theoretic optimality via quantiles of N(0,1). "NF4 Isn't Information Theoretically Optimal (and that's Good)" (Yoshida; arXiv 2306.06965 [ID verified]): the distribution of block-normalized values depends on block size, so quantile codes can't be optimal; an expected-L1-optimal code "leads to improved performance for larger quantization block sizes, while both codes perform similarly at smaller block sizes". — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06965)
- BOF4 "Block-Wise Optimal Float" (arXiv 2505.06653 [ID verified]; ICLR 2026): "equal utilization of the 16 reconstruction levels … was already shown to be incorrect by Yoshida"; NF4 "does not satisfy Lloyd–Max centroid conditions"; BOF4 achieves lower MSE but "negligible differences in perplexity between NF4 and the optimized alternatives" on LLaMA. LO-BCQ (arXiv 2502.05376) finds Lloyd–Max gives substantially lower MSE than FP formats on GPT3-126M weights, largest gap at 5 bits. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06653); [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05376)
- Learned grids: SqueezeLLM (Fisher-weighted k-means), LeanQuant (loss-error-aware grid), GuidedQuant LNQ (monotone-decrease guarantee), Any-Precision (upscalable non-uniform), ICQuant (index coding), "Improving Block-Wise LLM Quantization by 4-bit Generalized Normal Float Formats" (ICLR 2026, OpenReview VpZ8YYdBmT). **Formats**
- "Bridging the Gap Between Promise and Performance for FP4 Quantization" (Egiazarian et al.; ICLR 2026; arXiv 2509.23202 [ID verified]): first broad PTQ study of NVFP4/MXFP4 with RTN, GPTQ, SmoothQuant, QuaRot, SpinQuant + a new MR-GPTQ; "both NVFP4 and MXFP4 are lossy, with MXFP4 inducing major accuracy drops (∼10% relative), and existing techniques fit these new formats poorly"; NVFP4 = 16-element groups + FP8 E4M3 scales (4.5 bits/elem) vs MXFP4 = 32-element groups + E8M0 power-of-two scales (4.25 bits/elem); primary focus W4A4. Secondary sources: NVFP4 "superior accuracy to INT4 and MXFP4 on many models", but "with Hadamard rotation NVINT4 can surpass NVFP4" (Chen et al., cited). — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23202)
- "Unveiling the Potential of Quantization with MXFP4" (Meta; arXiv 2603.08713 [ID verified]): software-only scaling tweaks reduce the MXFP4-vs-NVFP4 end-to-end accuracy gap "from about 10% to below 1% on average". "Four Over Six" (arXiv 2512.02010): changes NVFP4 block scaling to better represent near-maximal values; helps GPTQ/AWQ/SmoothQuant. QAD for NVFP4 (arXiv 2601.20088): NVFP4 PTQ "decent accuracy on very large LLMs, but the accuracy drop is often non-negligible for small ones". ARCQuant (arXiv 2601.07475): existing PTQ hard to adapt to NVFP4. MicroMix (arXiv 2508.02343, ICLR 2026): mixed-precision with microscaling formats. — [search](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08713v1); [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02010v1)
- AutoRound measurements (fake-quant; 5-task avg): MXFP4-g32 RTN vs AutoRound: Llama-3.1-8B-I 0.6212 → 0.6686, Qwen3-32B 0.6901 → 0.7211 (3–7 pts); NVFP4-g16: Llama-3.1-8B-I 0.6876 → 0.6918, Qwen3-32B 0.7164 → 0.7306 (0.5–2 pts); i.e. **NVFP4 RTN already ≈ MXFP4+optimization**. AutoRound README marks MXFP4 as "no real kernels". — [mxnv_acc.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/intel/auto-round/main/docs/mxnv_acc.md); [AutoRound README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/intel/auto-round/main/README.md)
- A blog-style negative result on serving a large model in NVFP4 ("GLM-5.2 NVFP4: fast, cheap, and not worth serving") exists but is non-peer-reviewed; cited only as evidence that practitioner-side NVFP4 quality complaints are circulating. — [search link](https://blog.pr-484.umans.ai/blog/glm-5-2-nvfp4-not-worth-serving/)

### Inferences
- The combined picture (MSE-optimal grids don't move perplexity at 4-bit; calibration sampling moves downstream by points; 2-bit SOTA increments are ~0.3 PPL) implies many published 4-bit "improvements" are below the noise floor set by calibration sampling, which almost no paper reports with seeds/CI.
- Bit-allocation research has implicitly concluded that scalar per-layer sensitivity scores are not the right abstraction (CASA's 10¹–10¹³ range); the open question is whether a *cheap* non-scalar sensitivity (e.g., Kronecker factors, as in YAQA/KronQ/CASA) is both predictive and transferable.
- Format co-design is now the dominant practical axis (NVFP4 kernels on Blackwell), and the "rotation + INT4 vs NVFP4" contradiction suggests the right unit of comparison is (transform, format, rounding) triples, not formats alone.

### Gaps
- No calibration-size scaling law vs bit-width; no study of calibration set size at 2-bit for VQ/TCQ methods (which fit far more parameters — codebooks — than scalar methods and should overfit more).
- Egiazarian et al.'s per-model INT4-vs-NVFP4-vs-MXFP4 weight-only tables could not be read.
- No study found that fits heavy-tailed (Student-t/Laplace) models to LLM weight blocks and derives the Lloyd–Max grid accordingly for ≤3 bits, where (unlike 4-bit) grid shape plausibly matters.

## KQ6. Evaluation controversies: perplexity vs downstream vs reasoning; which capabilities degrade first

### Takeaway
There is a consistent empirical ordering: at W4 weight-only, perplexity and short-answer commonsense are near-lossless on ≥7B models, but multi-step reasoning, self-calibration, long-context retrieval (loss grows with length; up to 23% at 128K for 4-bit), instruction following, hallucination detection and factuality (preserved at 8-bit, drops at 4-bit) degrade first; at W3 everything except the largest models degrades; at W2 emergent abilities "encounter severe degradation". For reasoning models, W4A16 is near-lossless on distilled models ≥7B but RL-trained models (QwQ-32B) and hard tasks lose more, and GSM8K can move 36 points between two 4-bit GPTQ variants whose ARC-c scores are identical. No theoretical account of *which* capabilities are fragile exists; the only proposed proxy is end-to-end KL (YAQA), which at least tracks reasoning better than perplexity.

### Cited Findings
- "Evaluating Quantized Large Language Models" (Li et al.; ICML 2024; arXiv 2402.18158 [ID verified]): 11 model families (125M–180B), tasks: basic NLP, emergent ability, trustworthiness, dialogue, long-context; findings quoted: "the tolerance of Multi-Step Reasoning and Self-Calibration to quantization is lower than that of Instruction-Following and In-Context Learning abilities"; "the longer the text, the larger the performance loss caused by Weight and KV Cache quantization"; "most LLMs are more sensitive to KV Cache Quantization than Weight-only and Weight-Activation Quantization". — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18158); [qllm-eval README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thu-nics/qllm-eval/main/README.md)
- "Do Emergent Abilities Exist in Quantized LLMs" (LREC-COLING 2024; arXiv 2307.08072 [ID from memory]): "emergent abilities still exist in 4-bit quantization models, while 2-bit models encounter severe performance degradation". — [search](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.461/)
- "Quantization Hurts Reasoning? An Empirical Study on Quantized Reasoning Models" (Liu et al.; arXiv 2504.04823 [ID verified]; v2 Aug 2025): DeepSeek-R1-Distill Qwen/Llama 1.5B–70B + QwQ-32B; weight (GPTQ, AWQ), KV and activation quantization; "W8A8 and W4A16 were close to lossless" and recommended; "harder tasks and RL-trained reasoning models lost more accuracy"; "quantized models still improved with more test-time compute over a range, but could get worse beyond it"; released AWQ-W4G128 and GPTQ-W4G128 checkpoints for R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/14B/32B. — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04823); [repo README](https://github.com/ruikangliu/Quantized-Reasoning-Models)
- GPTQ v1 vs GPTQv2 on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: ARC-c 0.500 vs 0.503 (identical) but GSM8K-platinum CoT 0.40 vs 0.76 — reasoning is far more sensitive to the calibration objective than any commonsense metric. — [GPTQv2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Intelligent-Computing-Lab-Yale/GPTQv2/main/README.md)
- "Does quantization affect models' performance on long-context tasks?" (EMNLP 2025; arXiv 2505.20276 [ID verified]): "quantization degrades long-input retrieval performance, with drops worsening as input length increases"; "4-bit quantization shows the most significant drop, with an average decrease of up to 23% across models at 128K tokens"; "factuality is preserved at 8-bit but drops at 4-bit precision". — [search](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20276v3)
- "Quantization Methods, Task Difficulty, and Model Size in LLMs" (IJCAI 2025): 1B–405B; "quantized models generally surpass smaller FP16 baselines, yet they often struggle with instruction-following and hallucination detection"; "FP8 consistently emerges as the most robust option". — [search](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0902.pdf)
- "What Makes Low-Bit QAT Work for Reasoning LLMs? A Systematic Study" (arXiv 2601.14888) and "Towards QAT for Ultra-Low-Bit Reasoning LLMs" (ICLR 2026) and QuantLRM (arXiv 2601.03170) — QAT side; mention only as evidence that PTQ at ≤3 bits does not preserve reasoning and the community is moving to QAT/distillation for reasoning models. — [htqin list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/htqin/awesome-model-quantization/master/README.md)
- ParoQuant "Pairwise Rotation Quantization for Efficient Reasoning LLM Inference" (ICLR 2026, OpenReview 1USeVjsKau) — reasoning-targeted weight PTQ; details not retrieved.
- "Benchmarking Post-Training Quantization in LLMs: Comprehensive Taxonomy, Unified Evaluation, and Comparative Analysis" (arXiv 2502.13178 [ID verified]) — a unified benchmark; findings not retrieved (search budget). "A Comprehensive Evaluation on Quantization Techniques for LLMs" (arXiv 2507.17417) similar. — [search list](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13178)
- Safety: no quantization-specific weight-PTQ safety degradation result surfaced; LongSafety (arXiv 2502.16971) is for unquantized models. (Papers on "quantization undoes safety alignment" exist in my recollection but were not surfaced; treat as a gap.)

### Inferences
- Perplexity is a sufficient metric for ranking methods only at ≥4 bits on base models; it fails to rank methods on reasoning (GPTQv1 vs v2), on instruct models, and on long-context, which is where 2025–26 deployment interest is. A method paper reporting only WikiText2/C4 + 5 commonsense tasks (still the norm in the VQ/TCQ line) is uninformative about reasoning.
- The capabilities that degrade first (multi-step reasoning, self-calibration, long-context retrieval) are all those whose output depends on *many sequential* forward passes or on precise attention over many tokens — consistent with compounding of per-token KL across generated tokens; this suggests KL-per-token (YAQA's metric) multiplied by generation length as the natural predictor, but no paper tests that.

### Gaps
- No theoretical account linking per-layer or per-token quantization error to reasoning-chain accuracy; no study of error compounding over CoT length for *weight-only* PTQ.
- Exact per-task deltas from 2504.04823 (AIME/MATH-500/GPQA/LiveCodeBench by bit-width) could not be read.
- Weight-PTQ effects on safety/refusal behavior: not covered by any retrievable source.

## KQ7. Theory: Lloyd–Max optimal quantizers, Gaussian rate–distortion and the Shannon gap, error propagation across layers, the "isotropic noise" assumption, KL/Fisher metrics, and low-rank + quantized decompositions

### Takeaway
Classical theory gives (i) fixed-rate scalar Lloyd–Max distortion for a Gaussian of 0.1175/0.0345/0.0095 σ² at 2/3/4 bits vs the Shannon bound 2^{-2R}σ² (0.0625/0.0156/0.0039), i.e. a 1.9–2.4× (2.7–3.9 dB) gap that vector/trellis/lattice quantizers (E8, TCQ, nested Gosset lattices) shrink to a fraction of a dB on *Gaussian* data, and (ii) Ordentlich–Polyanskiy's rate–distortion result for *matrix products* with nested lattices achieving it. What is missing is any statement for actual LLM weights (non-Gaussian, structured, with super weights). The widespread modelling assumption that quantization error is isotropic noise with variance ∝ 2^{-2b} underlies the precision scaling laws and is falsified in detail by super weights (one scalar flips the model) and by the GPTAQ error-accumulation plots; the practical fix has been to change the objective to end-to-end KL via Fisher/Kronecker-factored Hessians (YAQA, GuidedQuant, KronQ, CASA). Low-rank + quantized decompositions (ZeroQuant-V2 LoRC, LQER, QERA, CALDERA, SERQ, GlowQ, QuaSAR) have a clean closed-form theory (QERA: activation-weighted SVD of the error) but their gains are mostly below 2.5 bits and come with rank-r inference overhead.

### Cited Findings
- Scalar vs vector optimality: QuIP# and QTIP motivate lattice/trellis coding by the gap between scalar quantization and the Gaussian distortion–rate function; Lloyd–Max 2/3/4-bit Gaussian constants above are classical (Max 1960; Gersho & Gray 1992) [recollection; standard]. QTIP: TCQ "scales linearly with dimension" and enables 256-D quantization. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11235)
- "Optimal Quantization for Matrix Multiplication" (Ordentlich & Polyanskiy; arXiv 2410.13780 [ID verified]): rate–distortion-optimal quantization for inner products of i.i.d. Gaussian matrices via nested lattices; the paper notes its own lattice implementation "comes much closer to the optimal rate-distortion" and that a follow-up (NestQuant) made it practical. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13780)
- NestQuant (Savkin, Porat, Ordentlich, Polyanskiy; ICML 2025; arXiv 2502.09720 [ID verified]): self-similar nested lattices; "nested lattice quantizers of high dimensions attain the optimal rate-distortion tradeoff for matrix multiplication", but those lattices lack efficient decoding, so they use Cartesian products of the 8-D Gosset (E8) lattice; W4A4KV4 Llama-3-8B WikiText2 6.6; their figure compares NestQuant, SpinQuant and "an information-theoretic lower bound" and concludes NestQuant is "reasonably close to the fundamental limit and significantly outperforms SpinQuant" (numerical gap not retrieved). — [search](https://people.lids.mit.edu/yp/homepage/data/2025_nestquant.pdf)
- "Statistically-Lossless Quantization of LLMs" (arXiv 2605.02404 [ID verified via search]) — surfaced alongside QTIP; content not retrieved.
- Precision scaling laws model PTQ as additive noise whose effect scales as a power of tokens-per-parameter and decays exponentially in bits (Kumar et al. 2411.04330; Ouyang et al. 2411.17691) — both fit aggregate loss, not structure. Dettmers & Zettlemoyer "The case for 4-bit precision: k-bit inference scaling laws" (ICML 2023; arXiv 2212.09720 [ID from memory]) found 4-bit is Pareto-optimal across 35k zero-shot evaluations, before VQ/TCQ existed [recollection]. — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330)
- Error propagation: GPTQv2's README figure shows per-block MAE growing with block index under GPTQ; GPTAQ's closed form "explicitly minimizes both the quantization error and the accumulated asymmetry error"; "Quantization Error Propagation: Revisiting Layer-Wise PTQ" (arXiv 2504.09629); "Scale Sensitivity in Low-Bit PTQ: Curvature of the Quantization Error Landscape" (arXiv 2609.37416). — [GPTQv2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Intelligent-Computing-Lab-Yale/GPTQv2/main/README.md); [search](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09629v1)
- KL / Fisher metrics: YAQA (Kronecker-factored Hessian of full-model KL; ≈30% KL reduction over LDLQ; "end-to-end error can be bounded by the approximation's cosine similarity to the true Hessian"); GuidedQuant (end-loss gradient-weighted layer objective ≈ empirical-Fisher weighting of output features); SqueezeLLM (Fisher diagonal); CASA (Kronecker-factored activation-aware sensitivity); KronQ. — [search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22988); [search](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.07004)
- Low-rank + quantized: ZeroQuant-V2 LoRC (arXiv 2303.08302 [ID from memory]) — "small-rank correction can recover accuracy at low bit-widths"; LQER (Zhang et al.; ICML 2024; arXiv 2402.02446 [ID verified]): high-precision low-rank matrix approximates the quantization error, with an activation-induced scale shaping the error's singular spectrum; "nearly-lossless W4A8 … without knowledge distillation, grid search, or gradient-based iterative optimization"; QERA (ICLR 2025; arXiv 2410.06040 [ID verified]): "closed form solution minimizing output activation discrepancy via singular value perturbation theory" — SVD weighted by the square root of the input autocorrelation, with its inverse applied to one factor; CALDERA (Saha et al.; NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2405.18886 [ID from memory]): W ≈ Q + LR with Q, L, R all low-precision, alternating minimization, QuIP#'s E8 quantizer for Q; README: "outperforms existing compression techniques in the regime of less than 2.5 bits per parameter"; uniform/NF quantizers "generally not recommended" vs E8; supports LoRA FT of part of the low-rank factors; no error bound in README. Newer: SERQ (arXiv 2603.08185; ICLR 2026), GlowQ group-shared low-rank (arXiv 2603.25385), QuaSAR (arXiv 2608.14149), UniQL (arXiv 2512.03383). — [search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02446); [search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25385v2); [CALDERA README](https://github.com/pilancilab/caldera)
- "Optimal Brain Restoration for Joint Quantization and Sparsification" (ICLR 2026, OpenReview VQIvBpL5ag) and SLiM (arXiv 2410.09615) join OBS-style compensation with sparsity — related theory lineage. — [htqin list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/htqin/awesome-model-quantization/master/README.md)

### Inferences
- On a Gaussian source, the achievable gain from better *coding* at 2 bits is bounded by ~2.7 dB (scalar→Shannon), and TCQ/E8 already capture most of it; the remaining lever is therefore not the codebook but (a) the objective (end-to-end KL vs proxy), (b) the transform (what distribution the codebook sees), and (c) non-stationarity (super weights, per-channel scale structure). This matches where 2025–26 progress has come from (YAQA/GPTAQ rather than new lattices).
- The isotropic-noise model cannot be right at ≤3 bits: (i) a single super weight can dominate the output KL, (ii) error accumulates monotonically with depth under symmetric calibration, (iii) MSE-optimal grids (BOF4) do not improve perplexity, meaning perplexity is not a monotone function of weight MSE. A noise model that is at least *anisotropic in the Fisher metric* (which is what YAQA/GuidedQuant implicitly use) is the minimum needed.
- Low-rank corrections and VQ are competing for the same bit budget below 2.5 bits; no paper compares "2-bit TCQ" vs "1.5-bit TCQ + rank-r FP8 correction" at matched bits/throughput.

### Gaps
- No quantification of the Shannon gap for *real* LLM weight matrices (an empirical rate–distortion curve for actual layers vs Gaussian D(R)) was found; NestQuant's figure is for activations/matmul on Gaussian-like data.
- No theorem bounding end-to-end KL in terms of per-layer proxy loss under realistic (non-i.i.d., heavy-tailed, spiky) weight/activation statistics.
- The exact statements of QERA's and CALDERA's approximation-error results could not be re-read.

## KQ8. Consolidated list of contradictions and open gaps across the literature (for idea generation)

### Takeaway
The literature contains at least eleven unresolved tensions; most are about objective misspecification, confounded evaluation, or causal stories that were never isolated.

### Cited Findings (contradictions, each with both sides)
1. **Proxy optimality vs practice.** LDLQ/GPTQ is provably optimal among linear-feedback rounders for the proxy loss ([QuIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13304); [Babai paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18553)), yet "Hessian-based rounding generally underperforms on Llama 3" ([QTIP caption](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/QTIP-vs-QuIP-Llama-3-ctx-8192-for-perplexity-Although-Hessian-based-rounding_tbl3_381485933)) and YAQA cuts KL ≈30% by changing the objective ([YAQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22988)).
2. **Rotation makes things Gaussian vs rotation hurts spike tokens.** Kurtosis ≈3 after rotation ([QuaRot summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/quarot); [KurTail](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.943.pdf)) vs "random orthogonal rotation would instead increase their error, so those massive-activation tokens dominate the residual loss" ([OffQ et al. summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07116v1)); and "Great Inversion": flattening is the wrong goal for allocation-flexible coders ([2608.25188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25188)) vs OptRot minimizing the 4th moment ([2512.24124](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24124)).
3. **Massive activations cause sinks vs dissociable.** Sun et al. ([2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17762)) vs "co-occurrence is largely an architectural artifact … changing the normalization configuration can suppress massive activations while preserving attention sinks" ([2603.05498](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.05498)).
4. **Llama-3 "robust" vs harder.** Huang et al. say strong pretraining "makes it robust to quantization" ([2404.14047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14047)) while their own tables show 3-bit g128 GPTQ degrading +34% (vs +15% on Llama-2) and 2-bit collapsing; scaling laws say more tokens ⇒ more fragile ([2411.04330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330); [2411.17691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17691)).
5. **Calibration sensitivity.** Williams & Aletras: material variance across calibration sets and GPTQ overfitting ([2311.09755](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09755)) vs "PTQ much less sensitive than pruning; newer LLMs less sensitive" ([2608.01078 summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01078)) vs domain match helps GPTQ but not AWQ ([OpenReview pfw3saHzGU](https://openreview.net/forum?id=pfw3saHzGU)).
6. **MSE vs perplexity.** BOF4/Lloyd–Max grids have lower MSE than NF4 but "negligible differences in perplexity" ([2505.06653](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06653)).
7. **Perplexity vs reasoning.** GPTQ v1/v2: identical ARC-c, GSM8K 0.40 vs 0.76 ([GPTQv2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Intelligent-Computing-Lab-Yale/GPTQv2/main/README.md)); AWQ vs GPTQ on Qwen3-8B W4: 11.3/69.3 vs 9.96/73.4 ([Qwen3 study](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Efficient-ML/Qwen3-Quantization/main/README.md)) despite AWQ ≥ GPTQ on Llama-3 ([2404.14047 table](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Macaronlin/LLaMA3-Quantization/master/images/result_ptq_1.png)).
8. **Format ordering.** NVFP4 > INT4 > MXFP4 ([2509.23202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23202)) vs "with Hadamard rotation NVINT4 can surpass NVFP4" (Chen et al., cited there) vs MXFP4 gap shrinks to <1% with scale tweaks ([2603.08713](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08713v1)).
9. **FT confound.** QTIP no-FT beats QuIP#/AQLM with FT ([QTIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11235)); PV-tuning moves AQLM Llama-2-7B 2-bit from 5.92 to 5.68 and defines all Llama-3 AQLM numbers ([AQLM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Vahe1994/AQLM/main/README.md)); most "PTQ" leaderboards mix FT and no-FT entries.
10. **Weak baselines.** SpinQuant/OSTQuant W4A16 GPTQ on Llama-2-7B = 11.3 / 9.84 ([SpinQuant README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/SpinQuant/main/README.md); [OSTQuant README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BrotherHappy/OSTQuant/main/README.md)) vs 5.83 in OmniQuant's table ([image](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenGVLab/OmniQuant/main/imgs/weight_only.png)); QuaRot-RTN Llama-3-70B W4A4 = 55.44 in FlatQuant's table ([FlatQuant README](https://github.com/ruikangliu/FlatQuant)).
11. **Isotropic-noise scaling laws vs single-weight catastrophes.** ([2411.04330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04330)) vs super weight ([2411.07191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07191)).

### Inferences (open problems the literature leaves explicitly or implicitly open)
- Joint transform–rounding–format optimality (the four open problems of 2608.25188); a transform chosen per quantizer family rather than per "flatness".
- An end-to-end (KL) guarantee for layer-wise PTQ under non-i.i.d. weights; cheap Fisher/Kronecker sensitivities that are transferable across models (CASA's 10¹–10¹³ scalar-range problem).
- A causal account of over-training fragility (tokens vs optimizer vs architecture vs vocabulary), testable with the released 1,500 Pythia checkpoints ([2411.17691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17691)) and outlier-safe-pretraining models ([2506.19697](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19697v1)).
- Calibration theory: sample-complexity of calibration vs number of free parameters (codebooks, rotations, scales) — VQ/TCQ/learned-rotation methods have orders of magnitude more fitted parameters than GPTQ but the overfitting literature only studied GPTQ/AWQ.
- Evaluation: a reasoning-aware, seed-reported, context-matched 2-bit benchmark for Llama-3/Qwen3/DeepSeek does not exist; reported 2-bit SOTA increments (~0.3 PPL) are below calibration-sampling noise.
- Super-weight-aware Hessians: no method treats super weight ⇒ super activation ⇒ Hessian spike as one object; the super-weight paper shows RTN + protection ≈ SOTA, suggesting much of GPTQ's "second-order" advantage may be a proxy for protecting a handful of coordinates.
- Sub-2.5-bit budget split between codebook bits and low-rank correction bits (CALDERA vs QTIP) at matched throughput — untested.

### Gaps (what could not be verified in this session)

- Llama-3 2-bit tables for QTIP/QuIP#/YAQA; Egiazarian FP4 per-model tables; Quantization-Hurts-Reasoning per-task deltas; SignRoundV2 and D²Quant full tables; NestQuant's numeric gap to the lower bound.
