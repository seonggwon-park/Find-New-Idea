## 2311.09755 — Williams & Aletras, "On the Impact of Calibration Data in Post-training Quantization and Pruning"

- **Exists:** yes. v1 2023-11-16, v2 2024-08-12. Authors: Miles Williams, Nikolaos Aletras (Sheffield).
- **Venue:** ACL 2024 Long (comment "ACL 2024"; journal_ref "Proceedings of the 62nd Annual Meeting of the ACL (Volume 1: Long Papers) (2024)"; DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.544). The report's "ACL 2024" tag is correct.
- **Report claim (tag [S]):** "캘리브레이션 데이터만 바꿔 1,800개 모델을 만든 연구는 '성능이 캘리브레이션 세트에 따라 변하고 GPTQ는 과적합한다'고 보고"
- **Verdict: partially_confirmed.**
  - "1,800 models, only calibration data varied" — **confirmed.** §3 Methodology: "we experiment with four compression methods and nine LLMs (i.e. three different sizes from three model families). We vary only the calibration data, trialing five source datasets, each comprising ten distinct calibration sets. This provides a total of 1,800 compressed models. We then evaluate each model across 11 standard NLP tasks, resulting in a total of 19,800 model evaluations."
  - "performance varies with the calibration set" — **confirmed.** §4: "Performance can vary between calibration sets. ... In the most extreme cases with LLaMA-7B, we observe that the accuracy with SparseGPT ranges from 52.7% to 61.7% for RTE, and from 66.4% to 73.0% for BoolQ."
  - "GPTQ overfits" — **NOT this paper's finding; it is a related-work citation of Wu et al. (2023).** §2.2: "Moreover, Wu et al. (2023) find that GPTQ tends to overfit to the calibration data when assessing perplexity on several datasets." The word "overfit" appears nowhere else in the paper. Williams & Aletras's own GPTQ result goes the other direction: §4 "Quantization methods exhibit lower sensitivity. ... we observe that the quantization methods generally exhibit lower levels of dispersion than the pruning methods." and "In the case of quantization, we note that GPTQ displays a higher range (0.9-1.6%) than SpQR (0.6-1.0%)" (mean zero-shot accuracy range across 50 calibration sets, Figure 4), vs. SparseGPT 2.4-4.8% and Wanda 0.6-2.9%.
- **Correction:** Attribute the overfitting statement to Wu et al. 2023 (as cited in Williams & Aletras §2.2), or drop it. If keeping a Williams & Aletras GPTQ number, use: GPTQ mean-accuracy range across calibration sets 0.9–1.6% (OPT family 1.1–1.6%, LLaMA/Vicuna 0.9–1.3%), lower than pruning (SparseGPT 2.4–4.8%). Suggested Korean: "...1,800개 모델을 만든 연구는 성능이 캘리브레이션 세트에 따라 변한다(특히 프루닝; 양자화는 분산이 더 작음, GPTQ 0.9–1.6%)고 보고 [2311.09755]; GPTQ 과적합은 그 논문이 인용한 Wu et al. 2023의 관찰."
- **Other relevant facts:** Methods = GPTQ, SpQR (4-bit weight), SparseGPT, Wanda; models = OPT, LLaMA(-1), Vicuna at 3 sizes each — all pre-Llama-2 models. Tasks = 10 zero-shot commonsense tasks + WikiText PPL. Recommendations (§5): release calibration data; evaluate across several calibration sets; manually inspect calibration data. Purely empirical, no theorem.

## 2608.01078 — Wang, Li, Kang, Fan, Yao (Intel Labs China), "Attend to Your Own Thoughts: Breaking the Barrier for Post-Training Quantization of Reasoning LLMs through the Lens of 1.58-Bit Quantization"

- **Exists:** yes. Published 2026-08-02. Proposes ScaleQ-1.58 = CAT-Q ternary PTQ + AYOT calibration (self-generated reasoning traces + answers from the FP target model as calibration context).
- **Venue:** preprint/unknown. Comment: "This research work was completed and submitted for publication in early May 2026. The project page: https://github.com/IntelChina-AI/BitTern". No acceptance stated; WebSearch finds only arXiv/alphaxiv/aggregator listings.
- **Report claim (tag [S]):** "후속은 '새 모델은 덜 민감'([2608.01078])" — i.e., a follow-up to Williams & Aletras found that newer models are less sensitive to calibration data.
- **Verdict: incorrect (misattribution).** The sentence exists in the paper only as a related-work summary of someone else's result, reference [69] = Paglieri et al. 2024 (arXiv 2405.20835, ICML 2024 Workshop): "[69] finds that, newer LLMs such as Llama-2 and Llama3 are significantly less sensitive to varying calibration data than the older OPT model, when using GPTQ, AWQ, and SmoothQuant." (Related Work, p.10). The adjacent sentence also summarizes [67] Williams et al. NAACL 2025: "they find that the tested two PTQ methods are much less sensitive to the choice of calibration data."
  - The paper's OWN finding is the opposite in spirit: calibration data is decisive for reasoning LLMs under ternary PTQ. Abstract: "even the latest CAT-Q method ... still leads to performance collapse on challenging mathematics and coding tasks when using conventional calibration schemes that ignore the model's reasoning process." AYOT fixes this: "with only 4M calibration tokens, Qwen3-1.7B ternarized by ScaleQ-1.58 reaches over 90.52% of the performance of the prior best BitNet b1.58 2B4T averaged over 4 mathematics and coding tasks, and our ternary Qwen3-4B shows an absolute gain of 8.97%, while requiring 1,000,000x fewer calibration tokens".
- **Correction:** Replace the citation for "새 모델은 덜 민감" with Paglieri et al. 2024, arXiv 2405.20835 ("Outliers and Calibration Sets have Diminishing Effect on Quantization of Modern LLMs"), which is the actual source (Llama-2/Llama-3 vs OPT; GPTQ/AWQ/SmoothQuant). 2608.01078 can instead be cited in the reasoning-domain calibration bullet (alongside 2504.04823 and 2606.25519) as evidence that self-generated reasoning-trace calibration matters a lot for ternary PTQ of reasoning models.
- **Other relevant facts:** Also claims scaling to dense + MoE up to 235B, performance improving with more calibration tokens, and that AYOT generalizes to other bit-widths. §Experiments: "smaller models are more sensitive to quantization than larger ones, and under comparable parameter counts, MoE models exhibit higher sensitivity than" dense. Empirical only; no theorem. Code "will be available".

## 2601.18306 — Chimoto, Elhoushi, Bassett, "Calibrating Beyond English: Language Diversity for Better Quantized Multilingual LLM"

- **Exists:** yes. Published 2026-01-26.
- **Venue:** EACL 2026 Main Conference (comment: "Accepted to EACL 2026 Main Conference").
- **Report claim (tag [S]):** "영어 캘리브레이션이 저자원 언어를 더 해치고 다국어 캘리브레이션이 PPL을 최대 3.52 낮춤"
- **Verdict: partially_confirmed.**
  - "multilingual calibration lowers PPL by up to 3.52" — **confirmed, with scope.** Abstract: "multilingual mixes achieving the largest overall reductions of up to 3.52 points in perplexity". Conclusion: "achieving up to a 3.52 perplexity reduction on Llama3.1 using GPTQ." Argument 3 (§4): "GPTQ shows large swings across calibration sets (up to 3.52 ppl), while AWQ varies only slightly (max ≈ 0.35 ppl)". So 3.52 is Llama3.1-8B + GPTQ specifically (ΔPPL = PPL_English − PPL_Other on 5,000-sentence Wikipedia validation); AWQ gains are an order of magnitude smaller.
  - "English calibration hurts low-resource languages more" — **not a result of this paper.** The disproportionate-harm-to-low-resource statement is prior work restated in the intro: "prior work has shown that quantization disproportionately degrades performance in multilingual LLMs, with the most severe effects observed for low-resource, and non-Latin script languages ... (Marchisio et al., 2024)." The paper's own claim is that non-English/multilingual calibration beats English-only across languages ("almost all of the non-English calibration sets yield a positive ∆ perplexity compared to the English calibration baseline"), plus "especially for low-resource languages" as a framing phrase (§2 end), with no high- vs low-resource contrast quantified.
- **Correction:** "다국어 캘리브레이션이 Llama3.1-8B GPTQ에서 PPL을 최대 3.52 낮춤(AWQ는 최대 ≈0.35)([2601.18306], EACL 2026); 양자화가 저자원 언어를 더 해친다는 점은 Marchisio et al. 2024의 선행 결과." Drop or re-attribute the "English hurts low-resource more" phrasing.
- **Other relevant facts:** Setup = Llama3.1 8B, Qwen2.5 7B; GPTQ, AWQ (4-bit weight-only); 8 calibration settings (en, fr, sw, zh, xh + 3 multilingual mixes multimix/multi/multi10); 10 eval languages (en, fr, sw, zh, xh + st, zu, yo, ig, ha); Wikipedia and C4 validation PPL. Failure cases: "French calibration set under AWQ and the Swahili calibration set under GPTQ on the Llama model, where performance decreases slightly relative to using the English set" (traced to narrower activation ranges). Language-matched calibration gives the largest per-language gains under AWQ; multi10 best averages for GPTQ. Downstream (GPTQ Llama only): XNLI, XStoryCloze, Global MMLU follow PPL trends "albeit with smaller margins". Limitations section: perplexity-centric, 10 languages, 2 models, 2 quantizers. Empirical only.

## 2606.25519 — Lian, Krichene, Huang, Tanaka, Ruwase, Zhang, Zhang (UIUC/Microsoft/Anyscale/Snowflake), "Quantization Inflates Reasoning: Token Inflation as a Hidden Cost of Low-Bit Reasoning Models"

- **Exists:** yes. v1 2026-06-24, v2 2026-06-29.
- **Venue:** preprint/unknown (no comment/journal_ref; WebSearch finds only arXiv + aggregator coverage).
- **Report claims (tag [S], two places):** "Qwen3-4B INT3에서 WikiText→Math-CoT 캘리브레이션이 정확도 28%→65%" and "28%→65%, CoT 30k→12k 토큰".
- **Verdict: confirmed.** §7.2 "Calibration Data as a Lightweight PTQ Mitigation": "Fig. 5 plots macro-average accuracy against average CoT length for Qwen3-4B under INT3 GPTQ. Calibration data has a large joint effect on both axes: switching from Wikitext to Math-CoT raises accuracy from 28% to 65% while cutting CoT length from 30k to 12k tokens. Reasoning-domain corpora (Math-CoT, Code-CoT) outperform generic text (Ultrachat, C4, Wikitext) on both dimensions."
- **Scope notes the report should carry:** (i) model = Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, INT3 GPTQ, group size 128, temperature 0 via vLLM; (ii) numbers are read off a figure (Fig. 5), not a table, and "accuracy" is a macro-average over the paper's benchmark suite (AIME25, GSM8K, LiveCodeBench, GPQA-Diamond, BBH, plus general tasks); (iii) "Math-CoT" = OpenR1-Math-220k traces "self-generated by the target BF16 model" (same for Code-CoT = codeforces-cots) — so this is self-generated reasoning-trace calibration, consistent with how the report groups it with 2504.04823; (iv) the paper's own ranking: "calibration-data optimization remains strictly below QAT on both axes" and "QAT is the only Pareto-dominant mitigation".
- **Other relevant facts:** Introduces CoT Token Inflation Ratio (CTIR). Models: Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus (14B), Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking. Methods: RTN, GPTQ, AWQ, HQQ, ParoQuant at INT4/INT3 g128. Prompt interventions on MBPP (Table 4): INT3 baseline 88.0% / 9704 CoT tokens; "no restate" 80.0% / 7367. Empirical only; no theorem. A separate, related preprint 2606.00206 ("Quantized Reasoning Models Think They Need to Think Longer, but They Do Not") is a different paper and should not be conflated.
