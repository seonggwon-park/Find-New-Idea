## Q1a. Is any importance-sampling correction used?

**Answer: No additional IS correction.** The only ratio in the method is the standard GRPO/DAPO clipped surrogate, and the paper positions itself explicitly *against* FlashRL-style IS correction by making rollout and trainer share the same 4-bit weights.

- §1 (Introduction): "Another strategy, demonstrated by FlashRL (Liu et al., 2025a), uses quantized rollout models to reduce computational costs. However, precision mismatches between the rollout model and logits model (e.g., 8-bit vs. 16-bit) require importance sampling to correct discrepancies, necessitating both 8-bit and 16-bit models to run simultaneously, which increases memory usage. To overcome these limitations, we focus on lower-bit quantization while avoiding duplicate models in memory."
- §3.1, Eq. (3): J(θ) = E[ (1/G) Σ_i (1/|o_i|) Σ_t ( min( (π_θ/π_θold) A_{i,t}, clip(π_θ/π_θold, 1−α, 1+α) A_{i,t} ) ) − β D_KL(π_θ‖π_ref) ]. "the clipping range (1 − α, 1 + α) stabilized the gradient steps of the policy model." Eq. (4): A_i = (r_i − mean)/std. DAPO variant: "the KL penalty from Eq.3 is removed".
- Appendix E, Table 4 (hyperparameters): "Policy updates per rollout: 4 (GSM8K, off-policy) / 1 (BigMath, on-policy)"; "Clip range ε_low, ε_high: 0.2, 0.28"; "Rollout temperature 1.0"; "we use the hyperparameters in Tab.4, without using entropy or KL losses."
- Appendix F, Algorithm 1 line 9: "Update the old policy model with AQN: π_θold ← π_θ + N(0, σ²)"; line 10: "Sample G outputs ... ∼ π_θold(·|q)"; line 14: "Update the policy model π_θ by maximizing the GRPO objective (Equation 3)". I.e., the behaviour policy is the AQN-noised policy and the learner relies solely on the PPO-style clipped ratio; the paper never states whether π_θold log-probs are taken from vLLM (NVFP4 Marlin) or recomputed in the trainer.
- [OpenReview, authors' reply gkwoLLg6NH] "QeRL (and QLoRA-style methods) avoid this problem: the same quantized representation is used consistently across rollout, forward, and update, with higher-precision LoRA parameters handling the updates. As a result, we do not **suffer from the train–inference mismatch**, while can **benefit from exploration** led by quantization." And [gdqBtX5hvC]: FlashRL/QuaRL "leads to a significant **train–inference mismatch** ... By contrast, QeRL keeps the training and inference models aligned and thus avoids this mismatch".
- [OpenReview gdqBtX5hvC] Only direct QeRL-vs-TIS number (rebuttal table, not in arXiv v1): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GSM8K — "FlashRL (w TIS), 8-bit rollout, > 70 GB, 89.2" vs "QeRL, 4-bit, > 15 GB, 90.8".

**Critical reading.** (i) QeRL's "no IS" is justified by *weight alignment* (same NVFP4 tensor on both sides), not by any measurement that the residual mismatch is benign. (ii) Residual sources of behaviour/learner discrepancy that the paper does **not** measure: kernel-level numerics (vLLM Marlin NVFP4×BF16 vs. the TRL training forward), and the AQN noise itself (Algorithm 1 line 9 deliberately perturbs π_θold). (iii) No KL(μ‖π), no per-token ratio statistics, no clip-fraction is reported anywhere in the paper or rebuttals. (iv) The GSM8K runs are "off-policy" in the ordinary PPO sense (4 updates per rollout), the BigMath runs are 1-update.

## Q1b. Exact entropy claims and the evidence

**Claim (abstract):** "our findings show that quantization noise increases policy entropy, enhancing exploration, and enabling the discovery of better strategies during RL." Camera-ready narrows to "enhancing exploration in LoRA-based RL".

**§3.2 "Quantization Encourages Exploration":**
- "Our central finding is that the noise introduced by quantization serves as an implicit exploration mechanism, similar to explicit noise injection techniques in the parameter and action space".
- "We study 3 different quantization formats of FP4 (NVPF4, MXFP4, and NF4) on GSM8K ... Our empirical study on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct reveals an intriguing finding: when applying PEFT-based RL, models quantized to 4-bit precision consistently outperform their 16-bit counterparts."
- "We discover that quantization inherently increases the sampling entropy, H(π(·|q)) = −Σ_{o_t∈V} π(o_t|q) log π(o_t|q), where V is the vocabulary) of the policy during deployment (shown in Fig.5)."
- Mechanism (verbal, no derivation): "During the forward pass, a quantized model introduces small but systematic errors, which can be modeled as static network noise (Fan et al., 2020). This noise propagates across the network layers, perturbing the final logits before the softmax function is applied. Consequently, the output probability distribution over the vocabulary, denoted as π_θ(·|q), becomes 'flatter,' with less pronounced peaks."
- Eq. (5): "(θ̃ + θ_lora) − (θ + θ_lora) = Q(θ) − θ = Δϵ ... where Q(θ) denotes the de-quantized weight, and Δϵ is the quantization noise."
- "A key limitation of quantization errors is their deterministic nature ... quantization noise remains static throughout the process, lacking the adaptability needed to enhance exploration at critical phases."

**Evidence offered:**
- Fig. 5 "Comparison of RL entropy": Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GSM8K, x-axis 0–200 training steps, y-axis ≈0.1–0.3, four curves NVFP4-LoRA(r32), NF4-LoRA(r32), MXFP4-LoRA(r32), Float16-LoRA(r32); annotated "Relatively High Entropy" for the quantized curves. No numeric values in text.
- Fig. 14 (Appendix H): Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, 0–600 steps, y ≈0.2–0.5: "the entropy of QeRL remains consistently higher than that of LoRA throughout the RL process, particularly during the initial steps."
- Fig. 4 / Fig. 7 reward curves: "QeRL achieves a rapid reward increase within 200 steps, while vanilla LoRA requires over 500 steps (Appendix H)". "Although MXFP4 achieves higher scores in the early stages of training, NVFP4 ultimately converges to better final rewards."
- Appendix I (learning-rate ablation): "QeRL demonstrates remarkable robustness to larger learning rates due to the presence of NVFP4 quantization noise, which helps stabilize updates" — attribution asserted, not isolated.

**What is PROVEN:** nothing about entropy. The only formal derivation in the paper is Appendix G "Proof of Noise Sharing" (Eqs. 11–12): RMSNorm_noise(X) = (Z_noise/w + I) ⊙ RMSNorm(X), and ((Z_noise/w + I) ⊙ RMSNorm(X)) · Ŵ = RMSNorm(X) · ((Z_noise/w + I)ᵀ ⊙ Ŵ), i.e. "additive Gaussian noise, when incorporated into the noise-sharing mechanism of LayerNorm, can be equivalently regarded as multiplicative Gaussian noise ... applied row-wise to the weight matrix Ŵ." This is a broadcasting identity (assumption: RMSNorm scale w is immediately followed by the linear layer Ŵ; Q/K/V share one noise, gate/up share another). It says nothing about exploration or entropy.

**Confounds the reviewers raised and the authors' answers [OpenReview]:**
- Reviewer 9ptM (rating 4, soundness 2): "The evidence currently shows quantized+LoRA > BF16+LoRA (Section 3.2), which may reflect LoRA limitations rather than quantization benefits. Could you isolate the effect of quantization by comparing Quantized Full RL vs BF16 Full RL, and potentially provide analysis on *why* quantization increases sampling entropy?" — no theory or Quantized-Full-vs-BF16-Full comparison was provided in the visible replies.
- Reviewer a31R (rating 8) asked for "the theoretical connection between the specific properties of NVFP4 quantization noise ... and established RL exploration theory" — not provided.
- Reviewer g8xh (rating 6): "Limited statistical rigor: No multi-seed averages or error bars"; "Hyperparameter fairness: Learning-rate differences may partly explain improved stability" (QeRL/QLoRA LR 1e-5 vs BF16 LoRA 5e-6, Appendix E: "due to the fragile of the BF16 model with LoRA, the learning rate can not be larger than 5e-6"). Authors' reply: BF16 LoRA "is most stable around a learning rate of 5e-6; under this setting QeRL still achieves higher reward".
- Authors' AQN-on-other-formats ablation [gkwoLLg6NH], Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct GSM8K: Full 91.2 → Full+AQN 91.4; BF16 LoRA 88.1 → **BF16 LoRA + AQN 89.7**; MXFP4 LoRA 86.4 → 88.1; NF4 LoRA 85.0 → 85.7. "adding AQN consistently gives modest gains, which is consistent with parameter-noise based exploration". Note: BF16 LoRA + AQN (89.7) recovers most of the gap to NVFP4 LoRA + AQN (90.8), so a large part of the "quantization helps" effect is reproducible with plain Gaussian parameter noise.
- Multi-seed std added in rebuttal [gdqBtX5hvC]: 3B GSM8K — base 61.2±1.9, Full 84.4±1.5, BF16 LoRA 76.1±1.6, QeRL 83.7±1.1; 7B GSM8K/MATH500 — base 76.3±1.2 / 78.4±1.0 (NB: Table 2 in the paper lists the 7B BF16 base MATH500 as 74.8), Full 91.2±0.6 / 77.4±0.4, BF16 LoRA 88.1±0.9 / 77.0±0.7, QeRL 90.8±1.2 / 77.4±1.0. On MATH500 the QeRL-vs-BF16-LoRA gap (0.4) is inside one std.

## Q1c. Adaptive Quantization Noise (AQN) schedule — exact details

§3.3:
- NVFP4 dequant, Eq. (6): Ŵ = Dequant(W̃) = S_FP32 · (S_E4M3 ⊙ W̃); quantization noise Δϵ = Ŵ − W.
- Noise vector: "for each quantized linear layer, we sample a stochastic noise vector, Z_noisy ∈ R^{1×d}, where d is the input dimension of the layer. This vector is not fixed but is resampled for each forward pass. We define it as: Z_noisy = ϵ, ϵ ∼ N(0, σ²I)".
- Eq. (7): Δϵ′ = Z_noisy + Δϵ = Z_noisy + Ŵ − W. "we freeze the main branch weight and update the low-rank matrix during RL. The W and Ŵ are consistent values."
- **Schedule, Eq. (8):** σ(k) = σ_start · (σ_end/σ_start)^{(k−1)/(K−1)}, "where σ_start and σ_end represent the initial and final noise levels, k is the current stage, and K is the total interval, which are evenly divided in the training steps". "For instance, our experiments in GSM8K with a total of around 600 training steps, noise is injected at 10 evenly spaced intervals, initialized with quantization noise, then from σ_start to σ_end." Algorithm 1 line 7: σ = 0 if k = 0 (quantization noise only), exponential decay otherwise; stage k = ⌊(step−1)/⌊M/K⌋⌋.
- **Merging into RMSNorm (zero parameter overhead), Eqs. (9)–(10):** X(Z_noisy + Ŵ) = X·Z_noisy + X·Ŵ; RMSNorm_noise(x) = w_noise ⊙ x/√(mean(x²)+δ), w_noise = Z_noise + w. "channel-wise additive noise Z_noisy transfers to row-wise multiplicative noise Z_noise/w + I of weight (proof provided in Appendix G)." Applied to W_q, W_k, W_v (shared RMSNorm) and W_gate, W_up (shared RMSNorm); not to W_o / W_down.
- "Due to the higher sensitivity of RL to multiplicative noise, we initialize the noise level with σ_start = 1e-2 to ensure stability." Appendix G: "we initialize the noise standard deviation (σ) to 1e-2, which is smaller than the typical 1e-1 used in traditional noise-based networks."
- **Inconsistent σ ranges inside the paper:** §3.3 σ_start = 1e-2; §4.1 "we set its range from 5e-2 to 5e-4"; Table 4 "Noise range Z_start, Z_end: 1e-2, 5e-4"; [OpenReview] 32B recipe `sigma_start=1e-2, sigma_end=1e-4`; [OpenReview] sensitivity (3B GSM8K): (1e-1,1e-3) 82.5; (1e-2,1e-3) 83.7; (1e-1,5e-4) 83.1; (1e-2,5e-4) 83.7; "too large noise (above 1e-1) can start to hurt performance".
- Scheduler ablation §4.2 / Fig. 9 / Fig. 15 (3B, linear vs exponential vs cosine vs logarithmic, 10 stages): "their performance differences are negligible in the early training stages, exponential decay achieves more stable improvements later by reducing noise to lower levels."
- AQN ablation §4.2 / Fig. 8: "when we start with the default quantized noise and periodically inject additional noise in later stages, the reward curve grows more steadily." Reviewer dYgm: "the curves with and without AQN are close to each other"; authors: "Figure 8 alone may make the effect of AQN look modest, partly because the curves are smoothed for visualization."
- **AQN effect size from Tables 1–2 (NVFP4 LoRA → +AQN):** GSM8K 3B 83.3→83.7 (+0.4), 7B 88.5→90.8 (+2.3); BigMath average 7B 37.0→**36.4 (−0.6; AMC23 47.5→42.5)**, 14B 40.5→42.0 (+1.5), 32B 41.4→45.6 (+4.2; AIME25 15.0→19.2, AMC23 52.5→63.3). AIME24/25 are 30 problems (3.3 pt/problem); AMC23 is 40 problems (2.5 pt/problem).

## Paper's own limitations / future work (Appendix K "Limitation Analysis", near-verbatim)

"We have demonstrated that our method, QeRL, achieves superior performance in RL training for LLMs compared to 16-bit vanilla LoRA training. Additionally, QeRL matches the accuracy of 16-bit full-parameter reinforcement fine-tuning while delivering over 2× training speedup relative to both vanilla LoRA and QLoRA. However, since RL for LLMs inherently demands significantly greater computational resources than SFT, our experiments, conducted on model sizes ranging from 3B to 32B, do not yet establish whether QeRL can maintain the same level of performance for models exceeding 70B parameters, leaving that investigation for future work. Another limitation is that RL training often requires tens or even hundreds of hours, and while we have provided comprehensive evaluations on reasoning benchmarks such as GSM8K, MATH 500, AIME 24, AIME 25, and AMC 23, we did not extend our evaluations to other benchmarks or data types, such as code, or to general-purpose language tasks unrelated to reasoning. Nevertheless, our technique can be seamlessly adapted to richer and more diverse training datasets. We encourage the community to explore and apply this method to a broader range of tasks in future research."

Also §3.2: "A key limitation of quantization errors is their deterministic nature" (motivates AQN). Rebuttal-acknowledged: NVIDIA Hopper/Blackwell + Marlin dependency ("On other GPU architectures such as Ampere, one can still apply QeRL using slower de-quantization schemes"); code RL left to future work; vector-quantization PTQ (QuIP#, VPTQ) integration left to future work.

## Implications for the report (`reports/LLM 양자화 연구 아이디어 10선.md`, Idea 6, lines 230–247)

1. **Line 234 provenance tag.** Replace "[V: README]" with full-text verification; add "ICLR 2026 Poster, OpenReview zw8zxMJJlm". The "ICLR 2026" attribution is correct.
2. **"IS 보정은 쓰지 않는다" is correct but under-specified.** QeRL uses only the GRPO/DAPO clipped ratio (Eq. 3) and *justifies* the absence of IS by weight alignment: the same NVFP4 tensor is used by the vLLM rollout and the trainer (rebuttal: "the same quantized representation is used consistently across rollout, forward, and update"). It is therefore **not** a FlashRL-type "INT8/FP8 rollout vs BF16 learner" setting. The report's arm "(a) 무보정(QeRL식)" conflates two distinct configurations; split it into (a1) aligned low-bit weights on both sides with the plain clipped ratio (QeRL), and (a2) low-bit rollout + BF16 learner without TIS (the FlashRL failure case). Also note QeRL's GSM8K runs are PPO-off-policy (4 updates/rollout, Table 4) while BigMath runs are 1-update.
3. **Residual mismatch that QeRL leaves unmeasured** is exactly the report's target: (i) kernel numerics vLLM-Marlin vs TRL forward, (ii) the AQN perturbation π_θold ← π_θ + N(0,σ²) (Algorithm 1 line 9). The paper reports no KL(μ‖π), no ratio statistics, no clip fraction. The report's sentence "엔트로피, KL(μ‖π), 보상을 한 런에서 함께 보고한 논문이 없다" is confirmed for QeRL.
