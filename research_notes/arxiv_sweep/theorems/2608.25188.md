## Q1. The inversion result and the majorization assumption

### Setup (Section 2.5)

**Definition 2.8 (Shared-scale uniform vs. allocation-flexible).** "A quantizer is *shared-scale uniform* if every coefficient of a group is stored at the same bit-width on a uniform grid with a single shared scale, so no bits can be re-allocated between coordinates ... It is *allocation-flexible* if the coder may spend a different number of bits on different coordinates under a fixed total-rate budget."

**Definition 2.10 (Majorization).** "For a, b ∈ R^d_{≥0} with equal sums, a majorizes b (a ⪰ b) if Σ_{i=1}^k a_[i] ≥ Σ_{i=1}^k b_[i] for all k, where x_[1] ≥ ··· ≥ x_[d] are the sorted entries. ... Fixing the partition of the coordinates into scale groups, we write a ⪰_G b (a majorizes b *within groups*) when the two agree on every group's sum and the restriction of a to each group majorizes the restriction of b. A block-diagonal doubly stochastic map is doubly stochastic, so a ⪰_G b implies a ⪰ b ... The converse fails, and that failure is what matters below: a sum of per-group maxima depends on the grouping, so it is not a functional of ⪰ at all, and the opposition of Proposition 2.11 is stated along ⪰_G."

**Proposition 2.11 (The two regimes are opposed).** "Fix the total energy Σ_k v_k over the transformed per-coordinate energy vector v = (v_k) ≥ 0. (i) The variable-rate distortion is governed by the geometric mean GM(v) = (Π_k v_k)^{1/d}: exactly proportional to it when every coordinate draws positive rate and the per-coordinate quantizers share one shape constant (as under the Gaussian source of Theorem 3.3), and bounded below by the water-filling floor of Proposition 3.4 when the allocation is not interior; GM is symmetric and Schur-concave, hence minimized at the most concentrated (majorizing) v ... (ii) The uniform-grid fixed-rate distortion is a sum of per-group maxima, D_fr = Σ_g max_{k∈g} v_k, which by the max-vs-mean bound satisfies D_fr ≥ (1/G) Σ_k v_k with equality iff every group is internally uniform; it is therefore minimized by within-group flattening and driven to its maximum by concentration. As functionals on the energy vector v, then, the two surrogate objectives are extremized at opposite ends of the within-group majorization order, at fixed, strictly positive group sums."

The paper itself says: "Neither half of the opposition is new mathematics." (cites Goyal 2001; Bhadane et al. 2021; Marshall–Olkin–Arnold Prop. 3.C.1; Xu et al. 2026 for Schur–Horn flattening). "What Proposition 2.11 contributes is to place both, as opposite ends of one within-group order, at the foundation of the transform taxonomy."

### Assumption 4.1 (the premise that the kernel is fixed-rate — empirical, not a theorem)

"**Assumption 4.1 (The datapath fixes the rate).** A quantized GEMM kernel that attains hardware peak throughput requires (i) every element of the operand tile its matrix instruction consumes to arrive at the same bit-width, and (ii) at most one shared scale per contiguous block ... Neither per-coefficient bit allocation nor entropy-coded indices are expressible in the operand tile ... This is a property of today's tensor cores rather than a theorem about computation (Section 13)."

### Surrogate (Section 4.2)

Eq. (10): D_fr ∝ Σ_g M_g², from Bennett's model with AbsMax step Δ_g = M_g/(2^{b−1}−1) on a *uniform (integer)* grid. "The uniform-grid hypothesis is load-bearing, because on a non-uniform element grid (the E2M1 grid of MXFP4/NVFP4) the element grid's own contribution is governed not by Σ_g M_g² but by a joint energy-concentration measure. ... the measure is itself derived for an ideal FP grid and has not been evaluated against the deployed formats."

**Proposition 4.2:** D_fr = Σ_g M_g² ≥ (1/G) Σ_k v_k² = E/G, equality iff every group internally uniform; D_fr ≤ E, equality when each group's mass sits on one coordinate.

### The theorem (Section 4.3)

"**Theorem 4.3 (Inversion of the surrogate objectives).** Regard both distortions as functionals of the transformed energy vector v at fixed E = Σ_k v_k, the shared-scale one on a uniform grid.
• The allocation-flexible coding surrogate (Section 3) D_vr(v) ∝ (Π_k v_k)^{1/d} is the geometric mean, the form the variable-rate distortion takes while every coordinate draws positive rate (Proposition 2.11(i); once the water level of Proposition 3.4 rises above a coordinate's energy the true distortion is floored by the un-coded coordinates' energy, so the vanishing limit below is an idealization of the surrogate). It is strictly Schur-concave wherever all coordinates are positive and identically zero elsewhere; it decreases under majorization, so it is driven down by concentration, the direction in which the KLT moves the profile, reaching the majorization-maximal reachable one, the spectrum (Theorem 3.3).
• The shared-scale AbsMax surrogate (Section 4.2) D_fr(v) = Σ_g max_{k∈g} v_k obeys the max-vs-mean bound of Proposition 2.11(ii), so among all v of fixed sum it is minimized at the flat v, the direction in which incoherence processing and the Hadamard move the profile (Proposition 2.15), and maximized under within-group concentration.
Over all v of fixed total energy, then, the two surrogates are driven apart. The geometric mean is strictly Schur-concave on the positive orthant, so every majorization step toward concentration strictly lowers D_vr while every coordinate remains positive, and it is maximized at the flat v; while E/G ≤ D_fr ≤ E, the lower bound attained exactly when every group is internally uniform and the upper bound when each group's mass sits on a single coordinate. Concentrating energy within a group therefore strictly lowers the first whenever every coordinate remains positive, and never lowers the second, strictly raising it as soon as the group maximum rises; flattening within groups does the reverse. Along no within-group step of the order do the two objectives move the same way."

Proof: AM–GM + Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya for (i); max ≥ mean per group for (ii). Counterexample that D_fr is not permutation-symmetric: "v = (4,1,3,1) and v′ = (4,3,1,1) give 7 and 5 at G = 2".

**Degeneracy the paper names itself:** "Because D_vr vanishes identically wherever any coordinate is zero, a within-group-flat vector that carries zeros (for instance v = (E/2, E/2, 0, 0) at G = 2) minimizes both surrogates at once. ... The claim is therefore an opposition of directions rather than a separation of minimizers; and because D_fr is grouping-dependent and not permutation-symmetric, it is not monotone along every majorization step."

**Remark 4.4 (What the theorem does and does not say):** "Theorem 4.3 is a statement about two objectives, not a proof that the KLT and the Hadamard are the optimal transforms. ... What transfers rigorously is the pole structure, not a step-by-step monotonicity ... For a generic spectrum, carrying no such aligned degeneracies ... the two minimizer sets are disjoint ... The randomized Hadamard realizes the flat pole only in the high-probability, near-incoherence sense of Proposition 2.15, not as an exact minimizer of Σ_g max_{k∈g} v_k; and WUSH (Theorem 4.9) does not minimize surrogate (ii) either. ... Majorization compares vectors of equal total energy, so this axis prices only what a linear homogeneous map does to a fixed energy budget. It does not price location: a per-channel shift changes Σ_i v_i itself ... Nor does it price clipping."

**Remark 4.5 (Domain):** "Theorem 4.3's shared-scale surrogate is the AbsMax cost of a uniform grid carrying one scale per group, (10), and that is the regime the theorem covers. ... On an ideal floating-point grid the governing quantity is the joint concentration Δ_FP of the operand pair rather than either operand's spread, and the prescription can reverse (row 8; Section 8.2). ... And in the KV-cache the reported wins either bundle the transform with an axis choice, a nonlinear codebook, attention-sink handling and outlier retention, or, where a transform is isolated (OSCAR against a data-agnostic Hadamard), answer to the attention product's distortion rather than to the shared-scale AbsMax cost this ledger indexes, so no flattening theorem accounts for them (Sections 9.1 and 11.4). We therefore state the inversion for the uniform grid and treat the rest as extensions, not corollaries."

**Corollary 4.10 (No transfer), Section 4.7:** "A transform proven optimal for one regime carries no optimality guarantee for the other. In particular, for a generic spectrum the KLT (Theorem 3.3), optimal for allocation-flexible coding, leaves the energy profile bounded away from the within-group flatness that the AbsMax step-size surrogate rewards (Theorem 4.3, Proposition 2.11(ii)). Because that surrogate is not a functional of the global order at all, the change relative to an arbitrary starting basis is not even signed ... The one exception is degenerate: if the spectrum is constant on every scale group in the eigenvalue-sorted order (plateaus of multiplicity G aligned with the group boundaries, Remark 4.4), the sorted KLT sits on the fixed-rate floor E/G and both surrogates are minimized at once." Then: "The corollary is a statement about the surrogate rather than a measured guarantee on every deployed instance: it licenses no transform; it only forbids importing an optimality proof across the divide."

**Cold reading.** The "theorem" is elementary (AM–GM / max ≥ mean / Schur-concavity of log-sum). The paper concedes this. What it proves is about two *surrogates* (geometric mean vs. sum of group maxima of the energy profile), under: fixed total energy, uniform element grid, one AbsMax scale per group, high-resolution Bennett noise model (Lemma 2.6, with the paper's own caveats at §2.3/§3.4), and positivity of group sums. It does not prove any transform optimal; it does not cover FP4 grids (Section 8.2 says the prescription "can reverse"), nor KV cache, nor clipping/centering. Figure 3 is a single-layer illustration on a 1.1B model, not an evaluation.

## Q2. The four open problems (Section 13, "Open Problems and Conclusion")

Summary line in §1 (vii): "It argues that the inversion exposes four open problems: joint transform-and-rounding optimality, a guarantee for the truly deployed (extreme-value, non-surrogate) objective, the return of allocation-flexible coding to the inference path, and transform–format co-design."

Verbatim opening sentences of each in §13:

1. "The first open problem is the joint optimality of transform and rounding. Every optimality result in the survey holds one stage fixed. The KLT and WUSH optima (Theorems 3.3 and 4.9) assume a given quantizer, and LDLQ's optimality (Section 7) is among rounders for a fixed basis. ... What is missing is a theory of the jointly optimal (T, Q) pair: the transform and the rounding (or codebook) chosen together to minimize the deployed error, rather than a good transform followed by a good rounder. The lattice view of Chen et al. (2026b), independently established by Birnick (2026), casts GPTQ as Babai's algorithm on the Hessian lattice and suggests the right language. ... What stays open is the provable optimality of the pair, and the lattice basis reduction both proofs leave for future work (scale-aware in Chen et al.'s formulation, LLL-style in Birnick's), not the fact of joint optimization."

2. "A second and subtler gap, the absence of an optimality guarantee for the deployed objective, runs underneath the first. Almost every optimality theorem is proved for a surrogate: the ℓ2 proxy of (3), an i.i.d.-Gaussian source model, or a distribution-free upper bound on the crest factor. The deployed error, by contrast, is the realized round-to-nearest error of the shared-scale AbsMax kernel. ... The sharpest live instance of the gap is that Σ_g M_g² places a scale-block-confined rotation and a fixed global one at the same ideal floor on MXFP4, and we do not compute it on the two deployed maps, although their accuracies differ by more than seven points (Table 13). ... The missing piece is a matching result: a data-dependent transform that provably minimizes the realized deployed error on heavy-tailed weights and activations, rather than bounding it in the worst case."

3. "A third problem is whether allocation-flexible coding might return to the inference path. The inversion rests on the operand tile being fixed-rate, and that (Assumption 4.1) is an assumption about today's tensor cores closed empirically, not a law of nature. ... Were a future datapath to serve an allocation-flexible representation as cheaply as a shared-scale one, the concentration optimum would return to relevance and the field's recent turn to flattening would read as a transitional episode rather than a permanent regime. Which of the two the hardware ultimately rewards is unsettled, and it decides whether the two poles of the inversion stay separate."

4. "A fourth problem is the co-design of transform and format. Section 8 treated the number format as a design variable, but only downstream of a format the hardware had already frozen. When the element grid, the shared-scale format, and the block size are themselves in play ... the optimal transform is a joint function of all of them. The power-of-two scale calls for a block-level rotation, a mantissa-carrying scale weakens the need, and a normalized architecture removes it entirely. Designing the transform and the format together (choosing how many bits to spend on the scale versus the element grid versus a rotation, under a fixed hardware budget) is an open co-design problem. ... What stays genuinely open is the joint choice, the transform and the format designed against each other rather than one frozen while the other moves. The sharp, already-live instance of that joint choice is the block size itself, which today's block-scaled hardware fixes at 16 or 32 (Section 10.3). ... The open question is how much finer the free per-block scale can profitably go on a tensor core, weighed against the area each halving costs, and which transform is optimal at that granularity."

## Q3. The two-RHT O(d^{-1/2}) Gaussian-marginal claim

**Not the paper's own result.** It is attributed, in Section 2.6 (immediately after Proposition 2.15), to Ben-Basat, Kuszmaul, Mitzenmacher, Portnoy, Vargaftik, "Quantizing with randomized Hadamard transforms: Efficient heuristic now proven", arXiv:2605.06014 (2026). Exact text:

"Note that the invariant this guarantee turns on is the crest factor of Lemma 2.14, an ℓ∞/ℓ2 (extreme-value) quantity, not the kurtosis, which does not control an AbsMax objective. The sub-Gaussian marginal bound of Proposition 2.15 is all an AbsMax scale needs, and it is less than a codebook needs: Ben-Basat et al. (2026) show a single randomized Hadamard can leave individual coordinates far from Gaussian on adversarial inputs, and prove that composing two brings every marginal within O(d^{−1/2}) of a standard Gaussian, and that even two may not suffice for vector quantization, a composition of three being what makes the coordinate covariance decay. The lattice codebooks of Section 7.3 are deployed with a randomized Hadamard applied once per matrix axis, so along the axis a codebook block spans they see a single randomized Hadamard: neither the two-fold marginal guarantee nor the three-fold covariance one is what they rest on."

Table 18 index entry: "Composing two randomized Hadamards puts every marginal within O(d^{−1/2}) of a Gaussian, and three makes the coordinate covariance decay, so Haar-designed codebooks transfer to Hadamards."

Repeated in §7.3: "A random Hadamard rotation makes each weight's marginal approximately Gaussian and the block incoherent (the rotated coordinates are sub-Gaussian, though not strictly independent). ... along the axis a codebook block spans, the deployed methods see a single randomized Hadamard, which carries neither the two-fold marginal guarantee nor the three-fold covariance one of Ben-Basat et al. (2026). The Gaussian source in what follows is therefore the model these codebooks are designed against and validated on, not a theorem about the weights they receive."

Cross-check against the source abstract (2605.06014, fetched): "after composing two RHTs on any d-sized input vector, the marginal distribution of every fixed coordinate of the normalized rotated vector is within O(d^{−1/2}) of a standard Gaussian both in Kolmogorov distance and in 1-Wasserstein distance ... two RHTs may not be sufficient for Vector Quantization (VQ) ... a composition of three RHTs leads to decaying coordinate covariance." The survey's paraphrase is faithful; it omits the metric (Kolmogorov and W1) and the normalization.

What the paper itself proves about one RHT: **Lemma 2.14** (sub-Gaussian max: E max_k|z_k| ≤ τ√(2 log 2G); CF = O(√log G) if realized RMS σ ≥ cτ) and **Proposition 2.15** (for Haar R or R = HD with random signs, entries of Rw are identically distributed, marginally sub-Gaussian with proxy variance ‖w‖²/d, so E[CF] ≤ √(2 ln 2d); a deterministic Hadamard or a signed permutation gives no such guarantee). It also notes the per-group crest-factor premise σ_g ≥ cτ "is not automatic" (cancellation example: Sylvester H at d=2048, w on coordinates 1 and 1025).

## Paper's own limitations (Section 13, "three limits")

"First, we run no benchmarks of our own. Every reported result in Section 11 is transcribed from a cited table or figure ... The exceptions are our own: the mechanism figures of Sections 2, 4 and 6 and the measurement in Table 5, computed on a single layer of one 1.1B model (Figure 2 on a synthetic sixteen-value group) and offered as illustrations of the theory rather than as evaluations. ... Each head-to-head table is also transcribed from a paper proposing one of its own rows, so within-table margins favor that row."

"Second, and most consequential, the organizing inversion and nearly all the optimality results we assemble are proved against surrogate objectives (the ℓ2 bilateral proxy, the AbsMax crest factor, or an i.i.d. unbiased-noise model). They are not proved against the realized extreme-value error of the deployed shared-scale kernel on heavy-tailed data. This gap we can only flag (it is the second open problem above) and not close."

"Third, the corpus carries a June 2026 cutoff in a field that moves in months, so particular method rankings, especially in the still-consolidating FP4/microscaling and learned-affine lines, will date. ... We classify the 43 transform methods proper (Table 2) but carry no table covering all 200 cited works ... We survey function-preserving linear transforms, treating quantization-aware retraining of the base weights, non-transform mixed-precision allocation, and architectural change only where they touch the transform stage."

## Implications for the report

1. **A5 (line 384):** the tag should become [V]. The sentence is accurate but under-specified: "4개 공개문제" should be named (joint (T,Q) optimality; deployed-objective guarantee; return of allocation-flexible coding; transform–format co-design incl. block size). Transform–format co-design is only the *fourth*. Also the paper gives no venue; "단일 저자 프리프린트" is correct. The claim "저자가 후속을 낼 가능성이 높아" is speculation; the paper states it runs no benchmarks and proposes no method, so direct competition on a method is not evidenced.

2. **Idea 3 (line 131, "RHT 후 블록 원소가 근사 Gaussian이라는 표준 가정"):** the paper (§2.6 and §7.3, citing Ben-Basat et al. 2605.06014) states that one RHT does *not* guarantee Gaussian marginals in the worst case; two RHTs give O(d^{-1/2}) Kolmogorov/W1 marginal closeness, three give covariance decay. Idea 3's Gumbel block-max model should (a) state that its Gaussian premise is a model, not a theorem, for the single RHT that deployed pipelines apply; (b) note that the paper's Lemma 2.14/Prop. 2.15 supply only sub-Gaussian *upper bounds* on the block max, E max ≤ τ√(2 log 2G), not the exact extreme-value distribution Idea 3 wants; and (c) cite the paper as naming the exact gap Idea 3 targets: "the measure is itself derived for an ideal FP grid and has not been evaluated against the deployed formats", "MXFP4's power-of-two block scale re-imposes an AbsMax-like penalty one level up, which NVFP4's mantissa-carrying scale weakens" (§4.2), the Σ M_g² surrogate not being computed on the two MXFP4 maps whose accuracies differ by >7 points (§13, second problem), and the block-size question (§13, fourth problem). This strengthens Idea 3's "문헌상 공백" and simultaneously makes 2608.25188 the closest prior framing.

3. **Idea 10 (MLA latent KV "가중 변환 부호화 ... 최적 변환과 할당"):** Corollary 4.10 says a KLT/concentration optimum carries no guarantee on a shared-scale AbsMax path; but Remark 4.5 explicitly excludes KV cache from the theorem ("no flattening theorem accounts for them"), and §9.1 reads OSCAR as "data-aware alignment ... not the energy-concentration-and-bit-allocation that Corollary 4.10 deprives of any guarantee on a shared-scale path". The report must state which regime Idea 10's latent-KV quantizer is in: if per-group AbsMax (fixed-rate), "allocation" can only act across groups or via mixed precision outside the operand tile, and the weighted-KLT must be justified as alignment to the attention product (as OSCAR does), not as transform-coding gain. If Idea 10 proposes bit allocation across latent channels, it is in the allocation-flexible regime and the inversion does not bite, but then the dequantization path (lookup, not GEMM) must be stated.

4. **§0.2 line 39 ("회전은 ℓ₂ 노름, Hessian 스펙트럼, 토큰 간 동적 범위를 바꾸지 못한다"):** consistent with the paper's Remark 2.13 (alignment term, from Federici et al. 2026, is invariant under any orthogonal map) and Remark 4.4 (centering and clipping are "levers beside the axis"). Can be cited as corroboration, with the caveat that the paper's own evidence is a single-layer illustration.

5. **Terminology:** the paper flags (footnote 2) that "concentration" is used in the transform-coding sense (energy on few coordinates), the inverse of some quantization papers' usage (Federici et al. 2026). The report should fix one sense if it uses the word.
