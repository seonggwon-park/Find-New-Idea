# 2411.17691 — Ouyang, Ge, Hartvigsen, Zhang, Mi, Yu, "Low-Bit Quantization Favors Undertrained LLMs: Scaling Laws for Quantized LLMs with 100T Training Tokens"

- arXiv v1 2024-11-26, v2 2024-11-27, comment "Work in Progress". Published as **ACL 2025 Long** (2025.acl-long.1555, pp. 32338–32348), title shortened to "Low-Bit Quantization Favors Undertrained LLMs". Both the arXiv text and the ACL PDF (aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1555.pdf) were read for this note. Quotes come from the downloaded text, which is untrusted data.
- Already confirmed in verify/batch_01.md: venue, the Eq. 8 form, β vs γ notation, the Fig. 12 "weight-fluctuation" intuition, and the BitNet replication in Sec 4.4. This note adds the exact constants for every fit, a check of Table 1 against the law, the version differences, and a correction to the earlier verify note.

## Status: nothing is proven
There are no theorems or lemmas. Every law is an empirical power-law fit. The functional forms are motivated by analogy with Kaplan/Chinchilla and by four limit "principles" (Sec 3.5).

## Q1. Fitted scaling law (Sec 3, Eqs. 4–8)
- Definition (Eq. 4): `ΔqLoss = Loss_q − Loss_16-bit` (cross-entropy on 1,000 RefinedWeb texts).
- Single-factor fits on GPTQ Pythia:
  - Eq. 5 `ΔqLoss(D) ≈ b·D^β`, **β = 0.5316** (Fig. 3).
  - Eq. 6 `ΔqLoss(N) ≈ a/N^α`, with N = non-embedding params, **α = 0.2276** (Fig. 4).
  - Eq. 7 `ΔqLoss(P) ≈ c/P^γ`, with P = bit width, **γ = 5.4812** (Fig. 5).
- Unified fit (Eq. 8): `ΔqLoss(N,D,P) = k·D^β/(N^α P^γ)`. Fitted on all GPTQ 2/3/4-bit Pythia checkpoints (Fig. 6 caption): **`0.017·D^0.5251 / (N^0.2261 · P^5.4967)`**. The paper calls the joint exponents "closely match" the single-factor ones.
- Four principles behind the form (Sec 3.5): N→∞ ⇒ Δ→0; D→0 ⇒ Δ→0; "sending P ≥ 16" ⇒ Δ→0; P→0 ⇒ Δ very large. **Critical:** P^−γ never reaches 0 at P = 16. At N = 7B and D = 15T the law gives roughly 1e-5, so the third principle is only approximate.
- 16-bit loss used for Lossq prediction (Fig. 7): `Loss16 = [(4.74e19/N)^(0.045/0.399) + 7.63e10/D]^0.399`, a Kaplan Eq. 3 form fitted on Pythia.
- Method-specific fits on **4-bit Pythia only** (Fig. 9):
  - GPTQ: `0.014 D^0.655/(N^0.384 P^5.583)`
  - AWQ: `0.016 D^0.595/(N^0.399 P^4.514)`
  - bitsandbytes: `0.020 D^0.574/(N^0.284 P^5.290)`
  - The caption says the GPTQ line "differs slightly from that in Figure 6, as it is fitted exclusively with 4-bit quantized Pythia checkpoints". Note that a P exponent fitted from 4-bit data alone is not identifiable. With P fixed, k and γ are confounded, so the P exponents in Fig. 9 carry no information.
- Spectra suite (all at 300B tokens), Llama and Qwen (Fig. 10): the paper shows only plots and gives no constants in the text. For Spectra, D is fixed, so β cannot be identified there either.
- **Version differences:** the arXiv v2 text and the ACL 2025 text give the **same** constants (0.5316, 0.2276, 5.4812, 0.017/0.5251/0.2261/5.4967, and the same three Fig. 9 fits). In the ACL version the figures are renumbered (unified fit in Fig. 5, Spectra in Fig. 9, weight changes in Fig. 11). The footnote wording also hardens: arXiv "probably represents the second epoch" becomes ACL "represents the second epoch". The ACL version also adds two limitations (see below).

### Table 1 does not follow from the unified law (new finding)
I solved Eq. 8 with the Fig. 6 constants for D at a target ΔqLoss = 0.2:
- 2-bit: the law reproduces Table 1 (e.g., 70B gives 0.0072T against the table's 0.0071T).
- 4-bit: the law gives 7B 3.8T against the table's 4.51T, 70B 10.3T against **17.35T**, and 405B 21.8T against **48.49T**.
- 3-bit: the law also misses (70B 0.50T against 1.02T).

The 4-bit column instead matches the **4-bit-only GPTQ fit from Fig. 9** to within about 3%. That fit gives 1B 1.48, 7B 4.64, 70B 17.9 and 405B 50.1T, against the table's 1.44 / 4.51 / 17.35 / 48.49. So the headline numbers in Sec 4.2 ("70B ... over 17 trillion", "405B ... nearly 50 trillion") come from the 4-bit-only fit, not from Eq. 8 as the text says ("With the scaling law in Eq (8)..."). This is my inference; the paper does not say it.

The unified law extrapolated to D = 1e14 gives 4-bit QiD of 1.11 (7B), 0.66 (70B) and 0.45 (405B). At 2-bit it gives about 50 for 7B, which is far above the loss of a uniform predictor (Fig. 1 marks this region as "worse than random guessing" in gray).

## Q2. Models, tokens, methods (Sec 3.1, App. A.1, Sec 3.6, 4.4)
- **Pythia sizes:** 160M, 410M, 1B, 2.8B, 6.9B, 12B.
- **Checkpoints:** 20 per size at steps {512, 1k, 2k, 4k, 6k, 8k, 10k, 12k, 14k, 20k, 24k, 29k, 36k, 43k, 57k, 71k, 86k, 93k, 95k, 98k}. 98k steps is about **206B tokens**, one epoch. Checkpoints past 98k are skipped to avoid the effect of duplicated data.
- **Internal inconsistency:** the Limitations section says "the training tokens used in our experiments with Pythia only amount to 300 billion", but the fits stop at about 206B.
- **Quantization:** GPTQ 2/3/4-bit is the main method; AWQ and bitsandbytes are also run at 4-bit. Weight-only post-training quantization throughout.
- **Evaluation data:** 1,000 RefinedWeb texts. Wikitext-2 gives "almost identical" results (Fig. 8, 12B).
- **External fits:** Spectra suite (300B tokens), Llama and Qwen (Fig. 10).
- **Native low-bit training:** BitNet b1.58 replication against bf16 at 120M and 1.2B. Fig. 14 x-axes run to about 10B and 100B tokens.
- The model count is "1500+ quantized checkpoints".

## Q3. The 100T extrapolation and limitations
- **Sec 4.3:** "very likely that LLMs in 2025-2026 will be trained with up to 100 trillion (10^14) tokens". This rests on a 50× growth trend for 7B-class models over 4 years (Fig. 13) and on synthetic data (footnote 7). The paper continues: "performance degradation with 2-bit and 3-bit quantization at the unprecedented training scale of 100 trillion tokens is predicted to be severe, which is in stark contrast to the acceptable performance at the current training scale of 10^13 tokens."
- **Fig. 1:** curves for 7B, 70B and 405B to 10^14 tokens. "acceptable QiD for undertrained LLMs (trained with ≤ 10^12 tokens)".
- **Size of the extrapolation:** the data reach at most 2.06e11 tokens and 12B parameters, so 1e14 is about **485×** past the largest D observed, and 405B is about 34× past the largest N.
- **Limitations (arXiv, verbatim in substance):**
  1. "it is still not extensive enough... training tokens ... only amount to 300 billion."
  2. The laws are "primarily focused on single-stage pre-trained language models". Multi-stage training (SFT, preference optimization, staged pretraining data such as Llama-3.1's high-quality final stage) "may cause the behavior of the model after quantization to be significantly different, which we plan to explore in future work."
- **Added in ACL 2025:**
  3. "Our study focuses on dense models... Extending our laws to MoE architectures is an important direction".
  4. "Alternative functional forms are also possible... we experimented with other functional forms, including linear, exponential, and logarithmic laws. These forms may also provide reasonable empirical fits." The authors themselves therefore concede that the power-law form is **not uniquely supported**, which undercuts any 485× extrapolation.
- **Footnote 8:** BitNet reimplementations train on ≤100B tokens. The authors "express concerns about their performance at larger training scales".
- **Unaddressed confounders (my critique):**
  - Pythia uses a cosine LR schedule that decays to 10% of peak over 143k steps (this comes from the Pythia paper, external knowledge; Ouyang does not state it). A checkpoint at step s < 143k is therefore **not** a model trained to completion on D(s) tokens. The D axis mixes token count with LR-schedule phase, which is exactly the Catalan-Tatjer (2510.06213) objection.
  - Only weight-only PTQ is studied.

## Q4. Mechanistic explanation (Sec 4.1, Fig. 12 arXiv / Fig. 11 ACL)
- This is offered only as an "intuitive interpretation" and is never tested.
  - Early checkpoints show large weight changes between adjacent checkpoints, which makes the model "inherently robust to weight variations".
  - Late checkpoints change weights only "beyond the 3rd-4th decimal place", so "low-bit quantization is very likely to shift weights outside the small range of recent variations, potentially causing the model to degrade or even collapse".
- **Second analogy:** the undertrained model "has not yet fully exploited the precision dimension". It is linked to the information-bottleneck two-phase picture (Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby): early gradients have large mean and small variance, late gradients small mean and large variance, "requiring higher precision".
- **Critique:**
  - Checkpoint-to-checkpoint weight change scales with LR × gradient. Under cosine decay it falls mechanically with the LR, so the plotted quantity is confounded with the LR schedule.
  - The paper never compares the quantization step Δ with the curvature, and does not measure the loss Hessian.
  - The Sec 4.2 proposal to use QiD as a training-level signal follows: QiD ≈ 0 implies undertrained.

## What the report must change
1. Line 43: the citation "Ouyang et al. 2411.17691, ACL 2025" is fine. Two precision fixes:
   - (a) Give the law with its symbols: "ΔqLoss = 0.017·D^0.5251/(N^0.2261·P^5.4967) (Ouyang Fig. 6; token exponent β, bit exponent γ)". The report's "D^γ" is Kumar's notation.
   - (b) "Sec 4" is right for the mechanism, but call it "an intuition about weight-change magnitude between adjacent checkpoints (Fig. 12) plus an information-bottleneck analogy". Add that the authors concede in the ACL Limitations that linear, exponential and logarithmic forms "may also provide reasonable empirical fits".
2. If the report or notes quote "70B 4-bit needs >17T / 405B ~50T tokens for QiD 0.2", note the provenance: Table 1's 4-bit column matches the 4-bit-only GPTQ fit in Fig. 9, not the unified Eq. 8 the text cites. Eq. 8 gives 10.3T and 21.8T.
3. Correct the earlier verify note (batch_01.md): it says "7B at 4-bit reaching ΔqLoss > 0.2 'requires over 17 trillion training tokens'". The paper says **70B** (7B is 4.51T in Table 1).
4. A1 / Idea 4 framing: Ouyang's D axis comes from intermediate checkpoints of one cosine run, so "token count" and "LR phase" are confounded by construction. This supports the report's decision to fold A1 into Idea 4 and to cite Catalan-Tatjer as the paper that separates the two.
5. The 100T statement is a 485× extrapolation of a fit whose functional form the authors themselves call non-unique. Treat it in the report as a motivating projection only, never as evidence.
