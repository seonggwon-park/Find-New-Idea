# 2411.17691 — Low-Bit Quantization Favors Undertrained LLMs: Scaling Laws for Quantized LLMs with 100T Training Tokens

- Ouyang, Ge, Hartvigsen, Zhang, Mi, Yu (Tencent AI Lab / UVA). arXiv v1 2024-11-26, v2 2024-11-27; comment "Work in Progress". Venue: ACL 2025 Long (already confirmed in verify/batch_01.md; not repeated here).
- Source: full text at scratchpad/papers/2411.17691/2411.17691.txt (36.9k chars). All quotes are from that text (untrusted data; quoted, not followed).
- Nature of the paper: purely empirical curve fitting. There is **no theorem, lemma or proof** anywhere in the text. Everything called a "scaling law" is a fitted power law; the only "derivation" is a list of four limiting-behaviour desiderata (Sec 3.5).

## Q1. Exact fitted scaling law (all exponents/constants, version differences)

Definition (Sec 3, Eq 4): "∆q Loss = Lossq − Loss16-bit ... where Lossq is the cross-entropy loss of a quantized LLM, and Loss16-bit is the cross-entropy loss of its pre-quantized counterpart with fp16 or bf16 weights."

Single-factor fits (Pythia, GPTQ 2/3/4-bit, RefinedWeb eval):
- Tokens, Sec 3.2 Eq 5: "∆q Loss(D) ≈ b · D^β ... because the more training tokens, the more significant the QiD becomes, according to our observations in Figure 2." Fit: "β = 0.5316" (Fig 3).
- Model size, Sec 3.3 Eq 6: "∆q Loss(N) ≈ a / N^α" with N = "the number of non-embedding parameters". Fit: "α = 0.2276" (Fig 4).
- Bit width, Sec 3.4 Eq 7: "∆q Loss(P) ≈ c / P^γ", justified only by analogy: "Considering that the role of bit width is similar to that of the number of parameters (both aim to increase the model's expressiveness)". Fit: "γ is fitted to be 5.4812" (Fig 5).

Unified law, Sec 3.5 Eq 8: "∆q Loss(N, D, P) = k · D^β / (N^α P^γ) where k is the joint coefficient, and both the coefficient and exponents (α, β, γ) are positive." The four "principles" used to pick the form: "Fixing D and P, sending N → ∞, we expect ∆q Loss → 0. Fixing N and P, sending D → 0, we expect ∆q Loss → 0. Fixing N and D, sending P ≥ 16, we expect ∆q Loss → 0. Fixing N and D, sending P → 0, ∆q Loss should be very large." (Note: the form does NOT actually satisfy the third principle — k·D^β/(N^α 16^γ) is not 0; the law is only ever used for P ∈ {2,3,4}.)

Joint fit, Figure 6 caption (all GPTQ 2/3/4-bit Pythia checkpoints):
  **∆q Loss(N, D, P) = 0.017 · D^0.5251 / (N^0.2261 · P^5.4967)**

Method-specific 4-bit-only fits, Figure 9 (text-extraction order; GPTQ label precedes the first formula, bitsandbytes and AWQ labels precede theirs):
- GPTQ (4 bits) [apparent]: ∆q Loss = 0.014 D^0.655 / (N^0.384 P^5.583)
- bitsandbytes (4 bits): ∆q Loss = 0.020 D^0.574 / (N^0.284 P^5.290)
- AWQ (4 bits): ∆q Loss = 0.016 D^0.595 / (N^0.399 P^4.514)
  Caption: "Note that the GPTQ function here differs slightly from that in Figure 6, as it is fitted exclusively with 4-bit quantized Pythia checkpoints, whereas the function in Figure 6 is fitted using all quantized Pythia checkpoints." So the token exponent moves between 0.5251 (joint, all bits) and 0.574–0.655 (4-bit only), and the N exponent between 0.2261 and 0.284–0.399: the constants are **not stable across fits**, a ±25–75% spread.

Accompanying 16-bit Chinchilla-form fit (Fig 7 caption): "Loss16-bit = [(4.74e19/N)^(0.045/0.399) + 7.63e10/D]^0.399", used so that "Lossq = Loss16-bit + ∆q Loss".

Version differences: v1 (2024-11-26) and v2 (2024-11-27) differ by one day; the fetched text is v2. The ACL 2025 camera-ready uses the shortened title; the numbers above are from the arXiv text and I did not diff against the ACL PDF (skipped — budget).

External validation (Sec 3.6.3, Fig 10): the *functional form* Eq 8 is refit on the Spectra suite ("all trained with 300B tokens") and on Llama/Qwen; the paper claims this "confirms that the scaling laws are not only valid for Pythia but are likely to be broadly applicable." The refit constants for Spectra/Llama/Qwen are only in the figure and were not extractable from the text.

## Q2. Models, tokens, quantization methods

Sec 3.1: "We choose 6 different sizes of Pythia LLMs: 160M, 410M, 1B, 2.8B, 6.9B, and 12B. For each size, we sample 20 checkpoints (see Appendix A.1) up to 98k steps." Footnote 5: "98k steps correspond to approximately 206 billion tokens, which is equivalent to one epoch of Pythia's training data. Although Pythia was trained for 143k steps, we skipped checkpoints beyond 98k steps to avoid the influence of duplicated data".
Appendix A.1: checkpoints at steps "{512, 1k, 2k, 4k, 6k, 8k, 10k, 12k, 14k, 20k, 24k, 29k, 36k, 43k, 57k, 71k, 86k, 93k, 95k, 98k}"; "we use the token counts generated by the Llama-3 8B tokenizer for all QiD calculations".
Quantization: "GPTQ ... to 2-bit, 3-bit and 4-bit levels"; ablation adds "AWQ and bitsandbytes" (4-bit only, Fig 9). Eval: "1,000 randomly sampled texts from RefinedWeb"; Wikitext-2 as ablation (Fig 8, "almost identical").
Count: 6 sizes × 20 ckpts × 3 bits = 360 GPTQ points; "over 1500 quantized LLM checkpoints" includes AWQ/bnb/Spectra/Llama/Qwen.
**Important caveat not in the first-pass note:** all Pythia checkpoints come from ONE training run per size with Pythia's cosine schedule; the token axis is therefore confounded with LR-decay progress (exactly the confound Catalan-Tatjer 2510.06213 later identifies). The paper never controls for the schedule.

## Q3. 100T-token extrapolation and stated limitations

Sec 4.3: "Based on this trend, it is very likely that LLMs in 2025-2026 will be trained with up to 100 trillion (10^14) tokens. Using the scaling laws derived, we predict the performance of quantized LLMs trained on 100 trillion tokens, as illustrated in Figure 1 ... performance degradation with 2-bit and 3-bit quantization at the unprecedented training scale of 100 trillion tokens is predicted to be severe, which is in stark contrast to the acceptable performance at the current training scale of 10^13 tokens." Fig 1 caption: predictions for "7B, 70B, and 405B models trained on up to 100 trillion (10^14) tokens".
This is a ~500× extrapolation in D (fit range ≤ 2.06e11 tokens → 1e14) and ~30× in N (12B → 405B), from a power law with no uncertainty bands reported.

Text: "For a 70B scale model, achieving a QiD greater than 0.2 ... under 4-bit quantization requires over 17 trillion training tokens. In contrast, for a 405B scale LLM, ... nearly 50 trillion training tokens". (Note the first-pass note said "7B ... 17T"; the paper says **70B** — 7B is 4.5T.)

Stated Limitations section (verbatim):
- "Although we have done our best to conduct extensive experiments and derive the scaling laws from over 1500 quantized checkpoints, it is still not extensive enough. For example, the training tokens used in our experiments with Pythia only amount to 300 billion. We expect more observations from a greater number of quantized checkpoints in the future to refine the scaling laws we have derived."
- "The scaling laws derived in this work are primarily focused on single-stage pre-trained language models. However, advanced LLMs today often employ multi-stage training strategies including supervised fine-tuning and preference optimization, and even within pre-training, multiple stages are often involved (e.g., Llama-3.1 ...). Such multi-stage training strategies may cause the behavior of the model after quantization to be significantly different, which we plan to explore in future work."
Footnote 8 (BitNet): "found that their numbers of training tokens were up to 100 billion ... we express concerns about their performance at larger training scales ... We call for results of native low-bit LLMs at larger training scales".

## Q4. Mechanistic explanation for why more tokens hurt

Only an "intuitive interpretation" (Sec 4.1, Fig 12), two strands, neither tested:
1. Weight-step-size story: "the early checkpoints exhibit significant changes in weights. Due to the significant fluctuations in weights during training, the model becomes inherently robust to weight variations ... checkpoints from the later stages of training ... show very small changes in weights (often at a very small scale, even beyond the 3rd-4th decimal place). In such cases, low-bit quantization is very likely to shift weights outside the small range of recent variations, potentially causing the model to degrade or even collapse."
2. Information-bottleneck analogy: "during the undertrained stage, the model's weights ... have not yet fully exploited the precision dimension. In the later, more fully trained stage, ... the model increasingly relies on precision ... This aligns with the two phrases of representation learning in the information bottleneck theory (Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby, 2017): during the early training phase, gradients have a large mean and small variance, making high precision unnecessary. However, in the later training phase, gradients have a small mean and large variance, requiring higher precision".
Conclusion itself calls it "an intuitive interpretation". No curvature/Hessian, no LR-schedule analysis, no causal intervention. The "weight changes between adjacent checkpoints" shrinking late in training is, of course, exactly what a decaying LR produces, so strand 1 is observationally indistinguishable from the LR-schedule confound.

Sec 4.4 (BitNet b1.58 at 120M and 1.2B vs bf16): "in the early stages of training, the training loss curves of BitNet closely match (and even outperform) those of bf16 ... As training continues, the 120M BitNet gradually begins to lag behind ... a noticeable gap starts to appear in the 1.2B models ... native low-bit LLMs such as BitNet may also favor undertrained LLMs, though the gap manifests later compared to post-quantization". Two runs, training loss only, no numbers in text.

## What the report must change (beyond verify/batch_01.md)

Report currently cites 2411.17691 only in Sec 0.3 item 3 ("적합식이다 ... Ouyang Sec 4의 '말기 가중치 변동폭이 작아 양자화가 그 범위를 벗어난다'는 직관뿐").
1. "적합식" is correct and can be sharpened: the fitted constants are unstable across sub-fits (D exponent 0.5251 joint vs 0.574–0.655 per-method 4-bit; N exponent 0.2261 vs 0.284–0.399), and the 100T projection is a ~500× extrapolation in D with no error bars. Worth one clause.
2. Add the confound explicitly: Pythia checkpoints sampled along a single cosine-schedule run, so D and LR-decay progress are collinear; Ouyang's "small late-training weight changes" (Fig 12) is itself an LR-decay artifact. This strengthens Idea 4's framing and ties Ouyang to Catalan-Tatjer's Sec 4.2 critique.
3. Ouyang's two intuitions (small Δ-steps; gradient small-mean/large-variance) are both *first-order/step-size* stories, not curvature stories — the report's Δ²·tr(H) decomposition (Idea 4 수학) distinguishes them from Kumar/Catalan-Tatjer's sharpness story; say so.
4. If the report ever quotes the token threshold: it is 70B@4-bit → >17T (not 7B); 7B@4-bit → 4.5T for ∆q=0.2.
5. Eq 8 does not satisfy the authors' own "P ≥ 16 ⇒ ∆q → 0" principle; the law is only meaningful for P ∈ {2,3,4}. Minor, optional footnote.
