## 0. What the paper proves vs. claims (cold reading)

**QTIP contains no theorem, lemma, or proposition of its own.** The only numbered formal statement is Definition 2.1 (restated from QuIP). Everything quantitative is (a) classical source-coding theory cited to others, (b) a Gaussian-source MSE *measurement* (Table 1, Table 2), or (c) LLM perplexity/zeroshot *measurements* (Tables 3, 5–9, 15).

Proxy objective, Section 2, Eq. (1):
> "Most current state-of-the-art PTQ methods round to minimize the per-layer proxy loss from Nagel et al. [26]. ℓ(Ŵ) = E_x‖(Ŵ − W)x‖² = tr((Ŵ − W)H(Ŵ − W)ᵀ) (1) Here, Ŵ ∈ R^{m×n} is the quantized weight matrix, x ∈ R^n is an input activation, and H = E_x[xxᵀ] ∈ R^{n×n} is interpreted as a proxy Hessian matrix."

Definition 2.1 (Section 2.1, attributed to Chee et al. [5]):
> "A Hessian H ∈ R^{n×n} is µ-incoherent if its eigendecomposition H = QΛQᵀ has max_{i,j}|Q_ij| = max_{i,j}|e_iᵀQe_j| ≤ µ/√n. A weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} is µ-incoherent if max_{i,j}|W_ij| = max_{i,j}|e_iᵀWe_j| ≤ µ‖W‖_F/√(mn)."

The Gaussianity assumption is *heuristic*, cited, not proven (Section 2.1):
> "The RHT achieves, with probability ≥ 1 − δ, µ_W̃ = 2 log(4mn/δ), meaning that W̃'s entries are approximately independently Gaussian distributed, which can aid quantization [34, 3]. We choose to build on incoherence processing here because the independent Gaussian-like weights it produces are suitable inputs for trellis coding [22]."
(The cited guarantee is a max-entry/incoherence bound; "approximately independently Gaussian" is an interpretation, not a theorem. The paper is explicit elsewhere: "Incoherence processing makes W approximatelly i.i.d Gaussian, which reduces quantization to Gaussian source coding." — Section 3.)

The distortion-rate statement is cited, not proven (Section 2.3):
> "For i.i.d sources, as L increases, TCQ efficiently approaches the infinite-length distortion-rate D_R, which lower bounds the attainable distortion of a k-bit quantizer [18]."

The only error bound mentioned is QuIP#'s BlockLDLQ bound, invoked to justify the Tx×Ty block design (Section 4, Experiments intro):
> "Since the proxy error is not an additive distortion metric, we cannot minimize it by quantizing W as one sequence. Instead, for all experiments, we use QTIP as a quantizer in QuIP#'s BlockLDLQ ... Since BlockLDLQ only specifies feedback along the input dimension, this is equivalent to BlockLDLQ with g = Ty but a vector dimension of TxTy ≫ Ty. This has the benefit of limiting the effect of g in BlockLDLQ's error bound gmµ²σ²tr(H^{1/2})²/n while achieving a high dimension for TCQ."

Trellis size: "a trellis – a directed graph G with 2^L nodes, each with 2^k incoming and outgoing edges" (Section 1). So **L = 16 means a 2^16 = 65,536-node trellis, not "16 states"** (Table 10 confirms: L=16 trellis structure = 4.19 Mb, codebook 1.05 Mb). Viterbi cost "O(2^L T)".

Scope statement (Section 3): "The main focus of QTIP is on what to quantize with (i.e. TCQ) and not how to quantize (e.g. adaptive rounding or descent methods). The general construction of QTIP can be used as a drop-in replacement for VQ in any rounding framework."

## 1. Gaussian-source MSE at 2 bits — Table 1 (Section 2.3 / 3), identical in v1 and v4

Text (Section 2.3):
> "As shown in Table 1, when quantizing an i.i.d. Gaussian with k = 2, the scalar Lloyd-Max quantizer attains 0.118 MSE, QuIP#'s 8D E8P codebook 0.089 MSE, our (QTIP) 256D L = 16 TCQ quantizer 0.069 MSE, and D_R = 0.063 [20, 24, 34, 8]."

Table 1 caption:
> "QTIP's compute-based codes (1MAD, 3INST, HYB) achieve similar distortion rates as a pure-lookup random Gaussian trellis code (RPTC) when quantizing an i.i.d Gaussian source to 2 bits. All TCQ methods (L=16) outperform SQ and VQ and are significantly closer to the infinite-length distortion rate D_R, which lower bounds the distortion a k-bit quantizer can attain."

Table 1 rows (from HTML cells):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dim. | 1 | 8 | 256 | 256 | 256 | 256 | 256 | ∞ |
| MSE | 0.118 | 0.089 | 0.069 | 0.069 | 0.068 | 0.071 | 0.069 | 0.063 |

Notes: unit-variance source implied (D_R = 2^{-2·2} = 0.0625, printed as 0.063). "256D" = sequence length T = 256 (Tx = Ty = 16), not codebook dimension; the codebook is 2^L × V. HYB (the code actually used for all headline LLM results) is the *worst* of the TCQ variants at 0.071.

My arithmetic on these values (not in the paper): above D_R: Lloyd-Max 2.73 dB (×1.87), E8P 1.50 dB (×1.41), 1MAD/3INST 0.40 dB (×1.10), 1D-RPTC 0.33 dB, HYB 0.52 dB (×1.13). Gain over Lloyd-Max: E8P 1.22 dB, L=16 TCQ 2.33 dB, HYB 2.21 dB. Note the scalar penalty at R = 2 bits is ×1.87–1.89, *not* the high-rate Panter–Dite constant √3π/2 ≈ 2.72 (×2.72 = 4.35 dB); the high-rate constant is not reached at 2 bits.

Table 2 (tail-biting approximation, Section 3.2): "Quantizing 4K T = 256 i.i.d Gaussian seqs. with a tail-biting (12, k, 1) trellis." Alg. 4 MSE vs optimal MSE for k = 1,2,3,4: 0.2803/0.2798, 0.0733/0.0733, 0.0198/0.0198, 0.0055/0.0055. So at L = 12 the 2-bit MSE is 0.0733 (vs 0.069 at L = 16; D_R 0.0625) and the tail-biting approximation is lossless to 4 decimals.

Table 10 (Appendix, Llama 2 7B 2-bit, K=2, V=1, pure-lookup, no FT) ties trellis size to perplexity: L=8 W2 7.83 (12.29 Kb), L=10 7.49, L=12 6.97, L=16 LUT 6.83 (5.24 Mb), L=16 bitshift+3INST 6.82 (0 Kb), QuIP# 8.22 (8 Kb). The text: "an equal-sized LUT would need > 10× more cache than the latest GPUs have".

## 5. Limitations / future work as stated by the paper

There is no "Limitations" section. NeurIPS checklist item 2: "Answer: [Yes] Justification: The main text describes limitations of the work that the authors were able perceive at submission time." Broader impacts: "[NA] ... This paper concerns foundational research on LLM quantization. There is not a direct path to any negative applications." The main-text statements that function as limitations are:
- "However, TCQ is not free. During inference, vanilla TCQ requires storing both G and the size 2^L × V node value codebook, which can be too large to fit in cache. TCQ-quantized sequences also cannot [be decoded in parallel]" (Section 1) and "These components are too large for fast inference when L ⪆ 12, which is necessary for high quality." (Section 3) — motivates the bitshift trellis and computed codes rather than a residual limitation.
- "Since the proxy error is not an additive distortion metric, we cannot minimize it by quantizing W as one sequence." (Section 4) — QTIP is bound to BlockLDLQ blocks (Tx×Ty = 256) and inherits BlockLDLQ's group-size dependence.
- Llama 3 70B: "quantizing layer 0 v of all Llama 3 70B variants ... resulted in catastrophic collapse of zeroshot performance" (v4) / "Hessian-based adaptive rounding (GPTQ/LDLQ/BlockLDLQ) performs worse than expected on Llama 3" (v1–v3).
- Hybrid code tied to GPU cache: Q = 9 chosen because "Setting Q = 9 gives a 2KiB codebook, which fits in L1 cache even after duplication for bank conflicts (32×)"; the better LUT code of Table 15 "is too large (32KB) for current GPU L1 caches, but could fit on near-future hardware" and "Setting Tx = 32, Ty = 8 corresponds to using a larger MMA tile size than current GPUs allow for."
- "It is hard to measure V's impact on decoding speed since this is highly implementation and hardware dependent, so V is more of a user-chosen hyperparameter." (Appendix, ablations)
- "Although our empirical throughput numbers were timed on NVIDIA GPUs, QTIP can be fast on a broad class of accelerators due to its flexibility." (Section 4.3; the CPU claim is a sketch — NEON vqtbl4q_u8 — with one perplexity number, no timing.)
- 405B Hessians "only used 2048 sequences due to time constraints" (A.3.2).
- "Table 6 shows zeroshot results computed with LM Eval, which are slightly random" (Section 4.2).
Not discussed anywhere: calibration-set sensitivity, fine-tuning cost, non-Gaussianity of RHT-processed weights, any end-to-end (non-proxy) error statement, reasoning/long-context evaluation, or why Llama-3-8B degrades more than Llama-2 at 2 bits.

## 6. What the report / notes must change

1. The report never cites 2406.11235 by id; QTIP appears only by name (report lines 201, 205, 220) and implicitly in "격자/TCQ 율-왜곡" (line 38) and A4 (line 382). Add the id and the version caveat where the Llama-3 remark is used.
2. **Report line 201** ("코드북(AQLM/VPTQ/QTIP, 수백만 파라미터)"): QTIP's codebook is 2^Q = 512 entries × V = 2 (2 KiB), not millions of parameters; AQLM's is 1 MiB. QTIP's extra calibration-fitted degrees of freedom come from QuIP#-style fine-tuning of unquantized weights, not from the codebook. Rewrite as "코드북(AQLM/VPTQ: 수백만 파라미터) 또는 QuIP#식 미세조정(QTIP)". Also note QTIP's Hessians use ≈12.6M (Llama 2) / ≈33.5M (Llama 3) RedPajama tokens, so the "calibration overfitting" hypothesis must account for QTIP being at the opposite end of the calibration-size spectrum from GPTQ's 128×2048.
3. **Report A4 (line 382)**: replace the classical "E8 (0.65 dB) / TCQ (~1 dB)" granular-gain figures with QTIP's measured 2-bit Gaussian numbers: Lloyd-Max 0.118, E8P 0.089, L=16 TCQ 0.069, HYB 0.071, D_R 0.063 ⇒ E8P recovers 1.22 dB and TCQ 2.33 dB of the 2.73 dB scalar gap. And correct the premise: at R = 2 the scalar/Shannon ratio is ×1.87–1.89, not the high-rate ×2.72 = √3π/2, so "TurboQuant 2.7 → 1" cannot be read off the Panter–Dite constant at 2 bits.
4. **Note 02 line 31**: AQLM Llama-2-7B 2-bit W2 inside QTIP's Table 3 is 6.14 (≈2 bit, with FT), not 6.64. QuIP# 8.22 (no FT) / 6.19 (FT) are correct.
5. **Note 02 line 36**: "Llama-2-70B 2-bit W2 3.87 ... C4 5.48" mixes Table 3 (1MAD, no FT: 3.87/5.70) with Table 5 (HYB + FT: 3.70/5.48). Use one row: HYB+FT 3.70/5.48 vs QuIP# 3.91/5.71 vs AQLM 3.83/5.62 vs FP16 3.12/4.97 (ctx 4096). "About half the gap of QuIP#": (3.70−3.12)/(3.91−3.12) = 0.73, not half; the paper's "roughly halves the perplexity gap" refers to 3- and 4-bit.
6. **Note 02 line 37 and line 76**: the caption "Hessian-based rounding generally underperforms on Llama 3" exists verbatim in arXiv v1–v3 (NeurIPS-era) but was **removed in v4 (June 2025)** and replaced by a layer-0-v explanation citing YAQA. Cite as "v1–v3 caption; v4 retracts the general claim". Do not call it "a direct admission that LDLQ is weaker on Llama 3" without the version qualifier; the authors themselves now attribute the 70B anomaly to a single layer and a prefix-token artifact, and the 8B gap is left unexplained.
7. **Note 02 line 82 and line 179** ("No published Llama-3-8B/70B 2-bit numbers for QTIP/QuIP# retrievable"): now retrievable — Table 7 above (ctx 8192): 8B 7.33/8.62 (QTIP) vs 7.84/9.06 (QuIP#) vs 5.54/7.10 (BF16); 70B 4.97/6.80 vs 5.77/7.46 vs 2.59/5.78. 10. Anywhere the report leans on "QTIP proves near-optimality": there is no theorem in the paper. The near-optimality is a Gaussian-source MSE measurement at 2 bits (Table 1) plus a citation to classical TCQ theory; the LLM results rest on the unproven "RHT makes W approximately i.i.d. Gaussian" heuristic and on BlockLDLQ's proxy-loss bound from QuIP#.
