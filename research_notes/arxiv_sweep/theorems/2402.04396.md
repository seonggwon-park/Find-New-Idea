## Q1. RHT incoherence concentration lemma — Lemma 3.1 (Section 3; proof in Appendix A.1 via Lemmas A.3, A.4)

Definition it relies on (Definition 2.1, from Chee et al. 2023, Section 2.3):
> "A Hessian H ∈ R^{n×n} is µ-incoherent if its eigendecomposition H = QΛQ^T has max_{i,j} |Q_ij| = max_{i,j} |e_i^T Q e_j| ≤ µ/√n. A weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} is µ-incoherent if max_{i,j} |W_ij| = max_{i,j} |e_i^T W e_j| ≤ µ‖W‖_F/√(mn)."

Exact statement (Section 3, restated verbatim in Appendix A.1):
> "Lemma 3.1. Let H be any positive semidefinite matrix on R^{n×n} and W any weight matrix on R^{m×n}. Let U ∈ R^{m×m} and V ∈ R^{n×n} be orthogonal scaled Hadamard matrices. Let S_U ∈ R^{m×m} and S_V ∈ R^{n×n} be random diagonal matrices with independent diagonal elements drawn uniformly from {−1, +1}. Then for any δ > 0, V S_V H S_V V^T is µ_H-incoherent with probability at least 1−δ, and U S_U W S_V V^T is µ_W-incoherent with probability at least 1−δ, where
>   µ_H = sqrt( 2 log( 2n² / δ ) )   and   µ_W = 2 log( 4mn / δ )."

(LaTeX source: `\mu_{H}=\sqrt{2\log(2n^{2}/\delta)}` and `\mu_{W}=2\log(4mn/\delta)` — note: NO square root on µ_W; it depends on both m and n.)

Assumptions, precisely: H any PSD matrix, W any fixed matrix (no distributional assumption on weights); randomness is only over the sign vectors; Hadamard matrices of size m and n must exist (powers of 2 or a known Hadamard factorization n = pq, Section 3). Proof: "The incoherence of H follows from the application of Lemma A.3. The incoherence of W follows from the application of Lemma A.4."

Component lemmas (Appendix A.1), exact bounds from LaTeX:
- Lemma A.3 (Hadamard HS or FFT FP times any fixed orthogonal U): `Prob( max_{i,j} |e_i^T H S U e_j| ≥ sqrt( (2/(nd)) log(2n²/ε) ) ) ≤ ε` (the "nd" is a holdover of the (n,d)-block notation of Lemma A.2; for Lemma 3.1 read it as n), i.e. "with probability at least 1 − ε ... µ-incoherent, where µ_H = sqrt(2 log(2n²/ε))". The tail used: `P(max |e_i^T HSU e_j| ≥ a) ≤ 2n² exp(−a² nd/2)`.
- Lemma A.4 (two-sided RHT/RFFT on any fixed W ∈ R^{m×n}): `P( max_{i,j} |e_i^T H_L S_L W S_R^T H_R^T e_j| ≥ ‖W‖_F · sqrt(4/(mn)) · log(4mn/ε) ) ≤ ε`, i.e. "µ_W = 2 log(4mn/ε)". The intermediate one-sided bound is `P(|b^T U S x| ≥ ‖b‖‖x‖ sqrt((2/n) log(4mn/ε))) ≤ ε/(2mn)`.
- Both lemmas also cover the RFFT (orthogonal FFT matrix with random 2×2 SO(2) phase blocks P), "Incoherence processing via the RFFT achieves similar theoretical guarantees as the RHT, see Lemmas A.3 and A.4" (A.2).
- Lemma A.1 is NOT the concentration lemma: "Lemma A.1. For any non-negative real number n, ..." is a scalar inequality used in the proof of Lemma A.2.

Comparison the paper makes (Section 3): "In QuIP (Chee et al., 2023), the 2-factor Kronecker approach achieves µ_W^Kron = A² log(4Cmn/δ)², where A and C are global constants independent of n and the number of factors. QuIP#'s RHT achieves superior incoherence via a log dependence on the matrix size rather that the Kronecker method's log-squared dependence. All of QuIP's theory analyzing the proxy loss in Eq. (1) still holds with the RHT, with the improved incoherence rates propagating through." Three stated advantages: "(1) the theoretical bound on the incoherence parameter µ is improved; (2) the asymptotic cost ... improved from Θ(n√n) to Θ(n log n); (3) ... Hadamard matrix multiply can be performed without any floating-point multiplies".

Related proxy-loss theorem (Section 4.1, Appendix B), the only loss bound in the paper:
> "Theorem 4.1. Suppose that we round W ∈ R^{m×n} using g-block LDLQ with Hessian H, producing Ŵ. Suppose that H is µ-incoherent, and that we use a (possibly stochastic) vector quantizer Q that satisfies E[(Q(x) − x)(Q(x) − x)^T] ⪯ σ² I for any x ∈ R^g. Then
>   E[tr((Ŵ − W)H(Ŵ − W)^T)] ≤ (g m µ² σ² / n) · tr(H^{1/2})²."
> "Observe that under the same conditions, just quantizing all blocks independently would yield E[...] ≤ g m σ² tr(H)."
Proof via Lemma B.1: `tr(D) ≤ tr(H^{1/2})·‖H^{1/2} ⊙ M_D‖_2` and under µ-incoherence `tr(D) ≤ (µ²/n) tr(H^{1/2})²`. Footnote 3: "The original QuIP paper also included multiple other technical guarantees, including a bound that considers more rigorously the 'real' case of finite-sized codebooks. While these results could also be generalized to the block-LDLQ case, we view this as not providing much insight relevant to QuIP# beyond Theorem 4.1, so (if desired) they are left as an exercise for the reader." → The finite-codebook (E8P) case has NO theorem in this paper; the σ² assumption is a per-input error-covariance bound that a deterministic nearest-lattice quantizer satisfies only with σ² = worst-case error.

What is proved vs. motivated: proved — Lemma 3.1 (incoherence w.h.p., any H, W) and Theorem 4.1 (proxy loss, block LDLQ, abstract quantizer). Motivated only — "It follows from the central limit theorem that RHT-transformed weights follow a roughly ball-shaped Gaussian distribution" (Section 4, opening; no theorem, no test of Gaussianity), and Appendix F.5 concedes "incoherence processing does not produce an exact Gaussian".

## Q2. E8P codebook MSE on i.i.d. Gaussian at 2 bits — NOT given as a number

- The only MSE evidence is Figure 3 (Section 4.2), an image. Caption: "Minimum achievable elementwise MSE of quantizing a Gaussian to various codebooks. E_8-based codebooks outperform other presented codebooks due to the underlying packing density and high dimensionality of E_8." Text (end of 4.2): "Figure 3 plots the elementwise MSE of quantizing a standard multivariate Gaussian to various k bit codebooks. Each k-bit codebook consists of a d-dimensional base lattice intersected with a ball to reach 2^{kd} points. The E_8-based codebooks achieve lower MSEs than all other presented codebooks, including those based on the D_4 lattice (the even-parity vectors in Z^4), which achieves the kissing number in R^4. ... Increasing the vector dimension decreases the error for the half integer grid ... Finally, while K-means on the source distribution would achieve lower MSE (Lloyd, 1982), there are a number of practical reasons why a K-means based codebook would be less practical, including worse end-to-end empirical performance."
- No numeric MSE value for E8P, no Lloyd–Max number, no Shannon/rate-distortion bound appears anywhere in the text (grep for "Lloyd", "Shannon", "MSE", "distortion", "0.0x" over the full text: only the Figure 3 caption, the paragraph above, the Lloyd 1982 reference, and Algorithm 5's MSE objective). The HTML version's figure is a raster image with no data alt text.
- Appendix C.3 ("Why not K-Means?") states the opposite ordering for end-to-end quality: "K-means based codebooks offer strong theoretical performance but ... Finally, we observe that empirically, E8P actually outperforms K-means, which is somewhat interesting and suggests that allocating more information to the edge of the distribution, even after incoherence processing, is useful."
- The paper's codebook comparison is end-to-end perplexity, Table 7 (Appendix C.4; Llama-2-70B, no fine-tuning, ctx 4096, Wiki2 / C4): FP16 (dim 1, 16 b) 3.120 / 5.533; E8P (dim 8, 2 b) 4.156 / 6.535; E8 lattice (dim 8, 2.37 b) 3.702 / 6.082; D4 lattice (dim 4, 2 b) 4.408 / 6.797; D4 lattice (dim 4, 2.21 b) 3.970 / 6.332; K-means (dim 8, 2 b) 4.452 / 6.925.
- E8P construction (Section 4.2, C.1): E_8 = (Z^8 ∪ (Z^8 + ½)) ∩ {x : 1^T x even}; D̂_8 = half-integer even-parity vectors; E_8 + ¼ = (D̂_8 − ¼) ∪ (D̂_8 + ¼); S = "the 227 elements of |D̂_8| with norm² ≤ 10 plus 29 'padding' elements from |D̂_8| with norm² 12"; 16-bit codeword = 8 bits (index in S) + 7 bits (sign flips, 8th inferred from parity) + 1 bit (±¼ shift); "2^16-entry lattice codebook"; "E8P requires only 1KiB"; "fits in the L1 cache of any modern GPU, even after duplicating for bank conflicts (32×)". Scale: "we scale W by ρ‖W‖_F ... For E8P, we used ρ = 0.9" chosen by "minimiz[ing] the quantization error of quantizing a Gaussian to the codebook" (F.5). E8 justification: "achieves the highest density unit ball packing in R^8 (Viazovska, 2017)" — a proven optimality, not "best known". Higher bitrates use residual VQ: 4 bit = E8P twice; 3 bit = E8P + 1-bit E8 codebook (Section 4.3).

Context-length note (Section 6): "there are currently 2 methods for evaluating perplexity: using the Llama 1 context length of 2048 or using the model's native context length (e.g. 4096 for Llama 2). OmniQuant and AWQ use 2048 for Llama 2 while AQLM uses 4096; we report both sets of numbers." Perplexity uses "the OPTQ 'Wiktext2' and 'C4' (not 'C4 New') sampling functions" (F.4). Hessians: "6144 sequences of a model's native context length ... from the RedPajama 1T" (F.2). Fine-tuning (Section 5, Algorithm 5, F.6): within-block FT of unquantized layers before quantizing each layer, then end-to-end FT of "the layernorms, all S_U and S_V, and the language model head"; sign vectors optimized "as real vectors instead of binary vectors" and stored in FP16 (< 0.01 bpw); 256 train / 128 validation RedPajama sequences, 5 epochs (160 steps), Adam lr 5e-5 (5e-4 for S_U, S_V at 2 bits); "can be performed in around 50 GPU-hours for a 70B parameter model".

At 2-7B, 2 bit: RHT-only 11.2 → +E8P 8.22 → +FT 6.19 (FP16 5.12), i.e. of the 6.1-ppl gap closed, 3.0 comes from the codebook and 2.0 from fine-tuning; the incoherence theory alone leaves the model at 11.2.

## Paper's own limitations / future-work statements (there is no "Limitations" section; collected verbatim or close)

1. Theory scope: Theorem 4.1 is the only loss bound; the finite-codebook case is "left as an exercise for the reader" (footnote 3). Gaussianity of RHT weights is a CLT heuristic; "incoherence processing does not produce an exact Gaussian" (F.5).
2. Hadamard existence: "While the Hadamard conjecture states that ∃H_k ∀k, 4 | k, finding such Hadamard matrices is still an open problem"; for non-power-of-2 dims they need n = pq with a known H_q (from Sloane's tables, F.3) or fall back to the RFFT, which "performs slightly worse than the RHT" (Table 1).
3. Codebook scaling: "The E_8 lattice works well for low bitrates (e.g. 2 bits), but quickly becomes intractable at higher bitrates due to codebook size"; RVQ used for 3/4 bits; "One could also use more advanced multi-codebook quantization approaches other than RVQ" and "one could potentially achieve better numbers with more advanced multi-codebook quantization approaches" (Sections 4.3, 6.1).
4. Fine-tuning: "Since fine-tuning for PTQ is a very recent development, both the methods presented here and in AQLM are almost certainly not optimal" (2.5); comparison to QAT carries "the caveat that QAT for LLMs is a relatively underexplored area" (2.5).
5. Quality vs. speed: "since these 'fast inference design choices' essentially amount to restrictions on what can be done during quantization, it should be entirely possible to achieve even better quantization quality at the expense of inference speed" (6.3).
6. Evaluation noise: "zeroshot tasks have an element of randomness and even FP16 numbers can disagree by up to 0.5%" (6.1); "at extreme quantization levels, even 0.1 bits can make a significant difference in quantization quality" (footnote 5); two incompatible context-length conventions in the literature (6).
7. Unexplained empirical finding: uniform E8P beats Gaussian-shaped K-means end-to-end although K-means has lower MSE — "somewhat interesting" (C.3, C.4); no explanation offered.
8. Forward-looking claim, not a result: "Our results indicate that, in the near future, 2 bit models are likely to scale better than 3 bit ones" (Conclusion).
9. Impact Statement is boilerplate ("none which we feel must be specifically highlighted here").

## Implications for the report and sweep notes

1. Notes `02_ptq_weight_quantization.md` l.32 and `05_theory.md` l.26/51/63 record the RHT lemma as "W is µ-incoherent with µ = √(2 log(2n²/δ))" and call it "Lemma A.1". Both are wrong: √(2 log(2n²/δ)) is µ_H (Hessian); for W the paper gives µ_W = 2 log(4mn/δ) (no root, depends on m and n); the statement is Lemma 3.1 (main text, Section 3), with Lemmas A.3/A.4 as the components; Lemma A.1 is a scalar inequality. Any report sentence that uses the lemma must use these forms and the correct number.
2. The MSE constants "E8P ≈ 0.089, Lloyd–Max 0.1175, Shannon 0.0625" (notes l.32, `05_theory.md` l.25/33) are not in 2402.04396. The paper gives only the qualitative Figure 3. If the report keeps the "E8P ≈1.5 dB above D(R)" arithmetic (05_theory l.33) or the idea-A4 line "E8(0.65 dB)" (report l.382), it must cite another source (e.g. QTIP 2406.11235's table, after full-text verification, or Conway–Sloane's E8 normalized second moment for the 0.65 dB lattice gain) — not QuIP#.
3. Llama-2-7B 2-bit numbers are confirmed: 6.19 (FT) / 8.22 (no FT) / FP16 5.12 at ctx 4096 (Table 4). Add the RHT-only ablation 11.2 and the ctx-2048 pair 6.66 (FT) / 12.3 (RHT-only) vs FP16 5.47 (Table 2). The "AQLM 6.64" figure in the notes is from QTIP's later table; QuIP#'s own Table 4 lists AQLM at 6.93 (2.02 bits, Jan-2024 AQLM version). Never cite 6.64 to 2402.04396, and always state the context length.
4. Report foundation fact #1 (l.38, "rigorous theory sits on the layer-wise MSE proxy") can now cite QuIP# directly as [V]: Theorem 4.1 bounds E[tr((Ŵ−W)H(Ŵ−W)^T)] only, under µ-incoherent H and an abstract σ²-bounded quantizer; nothing is proved for E8P or for fine-tuning, and the no-FT&no-E8 ablation (11.2 → 6.19) shows most of the 2-bit quality comes from the un-theorized parts. This strengthens, rather than changes, the report's thesis at l.38–39.
5. Report l.131/152 ("RHT 후 블록 원소가 근사 Gaussian이라는 표준 가정") should attribute the assumption to QuIP# Section 4 as a CLT heuristic ("roughly ball-shaped Gaussian") and note the paper's own F.5 caveat that it is not exact (relevant to the OffQ-style token-spike caveat already in l.152).
6. If the report describes E8 as "best known" 8-D packing (05_theory l.26 inference), change to "proven optimal (Viazovska 2017)", which is how the paper phrases it.
7. Methodological: QuIP#'s RHT guarantee holds for arbitrary fixed W and PSD H (randomness over signs only); it does not require Gaussian weights. The Gaussian assumption enters only in codebook design (E8P, ρ = 0.9 scale). Keep these two roles separate in the report's Idea-3/Idea-A4 derivations.
