## Scope (what the paper is about)
Post-RoPE MHA/GQA keys and values (Sec. 2.1: "qt = q̃t Φt and kt = k̃t Φt, while values carry no positional encoding"). Transform coding of the cache with distortion = attention-product error; key transform + fixed-rate VQ (g=4, 256 centroids, 2 bits/coord); values keep OSCAR's SQ. Models: Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, GPT-OSS-20B (only its 12 global-attention layers quantized).

## Q1. Closed-form transform, distortion target, assumptions

### Distortion target: attention-product error, reduced to a query-weighted (M_q) MSE — NOT plain MSE
Sec. 3, Eq. (3)-(5): objective is ‖SV − ŜV̂‖_F. "In the high-resolution regime, second order terms on the quantization errors vanish, and the target error can be upper-bounded by the sum of two terms (Appendix E.3): ‖SV − ŜV̂‖_F ≲ ‖S − Ŝ‖_F‖V‖_2 + ‖S(V − V̂)‖_F. (4)". Keys are designed from the score error, values from the score-weighted value error.

Sec. 3.1: "Since row-wise softmax is 1/2-Lipschitz (Gao and Pavel, 2017), the key-dependent term in (4), ‖S − Ŝ‖_F, can be replaced by the logit error: ‖S − Ŝ‖_F ≤ 1/(2√d)‖QK⊤ − QK̂⊤‖_F. Then, defining M_q = Q⊤Q, our goal is to design a transform and a quantizer that minimize: ‖QK⊤ − QK̂⊤‖²_F = Σ_j (k_j − k̂_j) M_q (k_j − k̂_j)⊤ (5)" — "an input-weighted quadratic distortion with constant sensitivity matrix M_q (Linder et al., 1999)".

Note: M_q = Q⊤Q is the (uncentered) query SECOND MOMENT, not a covariance. For GQA (App. F.5, Eq. 38) the M_q of the shared KV head is the SUM over the H query heads: "This has the same form as (5) with M_q = Σ_h M_q^(h), so we accumulate the query second moment over the heads sharing each KV head."

Exact bound behind (4): Proposition S2 (App. E.3, Eq. 20): "‖O − Ô‖_F ≤ ‖V‖_2/(2√d)‖QK⊤ − QK̂⊤‖_F + ‖S(V − V̂)‖_F + ‖(S − Ŝ)(V − V̂)‖_F", with Proposition S1 (App. E.2): "‖S − Ŝ‖_F ≤ 1/(2√d)‖Z − Ẑ‖_F". The paper explicitly disclaims joint optimality: "the third is a product of the two quantization errors, hence second order. We do not claim joint optimality of the two-step design for the combined objective." (lines 2702-2705)

### Closed-form key transform (Theorem 1, Sec. 3.1; proof App. F.2)
"Theorem 1. Let k̃_j = k_j − k̄, S̃_k = Σ_j k̃_j⊤k̃_j, and let R⋆_{K,p} = argmin_{R∈R^{d×p}} Σ_j ‖k̃_j − k̃_j R R†‖²_{M_q} (6). Then R⋆_{K,p} = M_q^{1/2} E_{1:p}, where EΛE⊤ is the eigendecomposition of M_q^{1/2} S̃_k M_q^{1/2}."
Here R† = (R⊤M_q^{-1}R)^{-1}R⊤M_q^{-1} (M_q-weighted pseudoinverse). Encoding r_j = (k_j − k̄)R_K, reconstruction k̂_j = r_j R_K^{-1} + k̄. "We have R_K R_K⊤ = M_q, as demanded by (Linder et al., 1999)". Remark S2: "For p = d, the columns of R are not orthonormal in general; they satisfy RR⊤ = M_q ≠ I whenever M_q ∝̸ I." Remark S1: "If M_q ∝ I, the procedure reduces to ordinary mean-centered PCA on the keys."
The proof (App. F.2) is more general: it minimizes the logit error (25) directly over codes r_j, synthesis D (rank p), and shared offset c, and lands on the same R via Ky Fan; "Invertibility of M_q requires the calibration queries to span R^d (in particular N ≥ d)". Centering is free by Lemma S1 (softmax offset invariance): "softmax_j q(k_j − c)⊤ = softmax_j qk_j⊤".

### Generalized Parseval (Proposition 1, Sec. 3.1; App. F.3)
"Proposition 1. Let r and r̂ be any two vectors, and k = rR_K^{-1} and k̂ = r̂R_K^{-1}. Then, ‖r − r̂‖²_2 = ‖k − k̂‖²_{M_q}." → "the transform converts the weighted objective into ordinary MSE. Thus, we can run any off-the-shelf MSE-optimal quantizer on the transform coefficients." (full-rank p = d only).

### Why non-orthogonal: high-resolution companding theory (App. E.1, Linder–Zamir–Zeger 1999 Thm. 2)
Eq. (17)-(18): rate lower bound achieved "with equality if and only if J(x)J(x)⊤ = c M(x) a.e." With M ≡ M_q constant: "(18) reads RR⊤ = c M_q. By (C3), orthogonal transforms are suboptimal for the attention-aware criterion unless the queries are isotropic; by (C2), Theorem 1 must resolve the remaining orthogonal factor, which we do by minimizing the rank-p reconstruction error." Note this is the ENTROPY-CODED (variable-rate), D→0 asymptotic statement; the orthogonal factor is fixed by energy compaction, which Theorem 1 does by weighted low-rank fit (a separate, exact finite-sample result).

### Value transform (Corollary 1, Sec. 3.2; App. F.4) — orthogonal
"Corollary 1. Let o_i = s_i V ... EΛE⊤ the eigendecomposition of M_o = V⊤M_sV [M_s = S⊤S]. The minimizer of ‖SV − SV̂‖²_F over low-rank approximations is R_V = E_{1:p}, with codes s_j = v_j R_V and reconstruction v̂_j = s_j R_V⊤." "Unlike R_K, the value transform is orthogonal. The term V⊤M_sV is the covariance used in (Zhou et al., 2026) [OSCAR]." In deployment they keep OSCAR's value SQ path (App. A.3: "For values, we use the default rotation matrix provided in (Zhou et al., 2026)").

### Fixed-rate grouping (Theorem 2, Sec. 4; App. G.1)
Model (7): coefficients independent zero-mean Gaussians, σ_1² ≥ … ≥ σ_d² ("independence is assumed for tractability"), Zador high-resolution: D_ℓ(b_ℓ) = C_g 2^{−2b_ℓ} (Π_{i∈G_ℓ} σ_i²)^{1/g}(1+o(1)), continuous rates.
"Theorem 2. Under the model (7), for any partition π the allocation minimizing D(π,{b_ℓ}) subject to Σ_ℓ b_ℓ = Lb is b*_ℓ(π) = b + 1/(2g) log₂(v_ℓ(π)/Π_m v_m(π)^{1/L}) (8), which spends more bits on groups of larger volume. The optimal distortion, D*(b) = C_g L 2^{−2b}(Π_i σ_i²)^{1/d}, is the same for every π, assuming that rates can be the arbitrary real values from (8)." Consequence: with fixed b_ℓ ≡ b, distortion ∝ 2^{−2b}Σ_ℓ v_ℓ(π)^{1/g} ≥ D*(b), equality iff all group volumes equal → equal-volume grouping makes fixed-rate VQ attain the variable-rate optimum. Balancing Σ log σ_i² is NP-hard (3-Partition); heuristic = sort by log σ_i², deal round-robin. App. G.2 gives a non-asymptotic lower bound (44) via reverse water-filling with the same v_ℓ^{1/g} dependence.

### Assumption honesty in the paper
Sec. 5: "The high-resolution model might not hold at 2 bits, we use the theory to guide our design rather than as a guarantee." App. H: "our results are asymptotic rather than finite-rate guarantees." App. H.3 fit of 2^{−αb} on held-out distortions at b∈{1,1.5,2,2.5}: α = 1.816 (Qwen3-8B), 1.828 (Qwen3-4B-Thinking), 1.878 (Llama-3.1-8B) vs. theory α = 2; adjacent-rate exponents 1.796/1.813/1.840 (Qwen3-8B), "increasing monotonically".

## Q2. MLA / weight absorption / per-token scale heteroscedasticity

### MLA / weight absorption: NOT covered
- The strings "MLA", "latent", "multi-head latent", "absorb(ed/tion)" in the weight-absorption sense do not occur. DeepSeek-V2 is cited once, in Related Work Sec. 2.3 (line 311), as "(ii) low-rank designs, which shrink the head dimension (Liu et al., 2024a)", i.e., as an alternative to quantization, not as a target.
- The only multi-head sharing treated is GQA (App. F.5): M_q summed over query heads sharing a KV head. This is a one-line extension structurally identical to the "head-sum M_K = Σ_h E[a^h a^{h⊤}]" of report 정리 10.1 — but with raw queries q^h, not absorbed queries a^h = W_UK^{h⊤}q^h, and no value-side W_UV term, no RoPE/latent split.
- "Absorbed" appears only for deployment: App. B.5 "The synthesis R_K^{−⊤} is folded into the query once per step: the engine forms the modified query q̃ = q R_K^{−⊤} (a single d×d product, amortized over the whole cache)" and "On the value side the INT2 code is dequantized in the OSCAR rotation basis, whose inverse is absorbed into the output projection, so it adds no online matmul either." So the KEY transform is applied to the query per decode step (post-RoPE keys prevent folding into W_K); it is not folded into weights.

### Per-token scale heteroscedasticity: used empirically, explicitly outside the theory
- Algorithm 3 (App. B.3, line 1884): "ρ_j ← ‖r_j‖_2/√d; r_j ← ρ_j^{−1} r_j ▷ Normalize" (per-token RMS normalization of the TRANSFORMED key, before k-means). Decode kernel "applies the per-token scale ρ_j recovered from metadata (one multiply)".
- Line 1816: "The per-token scale ρ_j in Algorithm 3 normalizes each transformed key before the codebooks are trained. It is not part of the model of Theorem 2, which treats the entries as independent Gaussians with fixed variances σ_i²."
- App. H (line 3428): "Per-token RMS normalization was off for this study; it isolates the unnormalized transform coefficients analyzed in Theorem 2."
- Rate accounting (Sec. 5 "Bit rate"): K = 2 BPE + per-token BF16 scale 16/128 = 0.125; V = INT2 + BF16 scale and offset = 2.25; average 2.1875; BF16 sink(64)+recent(256) band adds 0.034 → 2.22 BPE. OSCAR 2.28 ("the 0.06 gap is the K-side zero-point").
- No theorem, bound, or bias analysis on per-token vs per-channel scales; no softmax-bias/heteroscedasticity argument. The only quantitative evidence on scale granularity is for the SCALAR (OSCAR) codec on GPT-OSS-20B, Table 22 (App. J.3, bit-exact simulation, single greedy rollout, NIAH-8K 200 prompts / MATH-500 100 prompts): INT2 1 scale-group/row (2.5 BPE) NIAH 0.8, MATH 4; INT2 4 groups/row (4.0 BPE) 27.0, 15; INT3 1 group (3.5 BPE) 82.8, 73; INT4 1 group (4.5 BPE) 98.3, 85; BF16 99.0, 92. Paper's reading: "Finer scale granularity at 2 bits does not substitute, despite a higher rate than INT3: the deficit is resolution per coordinate, not outlier range." App. J.2 Table 21: same OSCAR key SNR (calibrated basis 8.8 dB GPT-OSS vs 8.1 dB Llama) yields 3.5–3.9× the relative logit error (0.64 vs 0.17); excess kurtosis in calibrated basis −0.3 / −0.1 (vs 20.8 / 7.2 identity basis). GPT-OSS keys "are not bounded by QK-norm" (Table 20 discussion).

## Paper's own limitations / future work (verbatim or close)
- Sec. 6: "Joint quantization of keys and values is left for future work."
- App. E.3: "We do not claim joint optimality of the two-step design for the combined objective. ... the value objective below treats the scores as computed from full-precision keys; the discrepancy from using Ŝ instead is absorbed by the second-order term."
- Sec. 5: "The high-resolution model might not hold at 2 bits, we use the theory to guide our design rather than as a guarantee."
- App. H: "We emphasize, in any case, that our results are asymptotic rather than finite-rate guarantees." H.3: fitted exponent α ≈ 1.82–1.88 vs 2; "the reported deviation includes both finite-rate effects and any residual suboptimality of the trained vector quantizers; we do not attempt to separate these effects."
- Sec. 4: "independence is assumed for tractability"; balancing log-volumes "is NP-hard (reduces to 3-Partition); we use a heuristic."
- App. B.3: per-token scale ρ_j "is not part of the model of Theorem 2".
- Values: VQ for values "barely changes downstream accuracy" (within 2 points, Table 6); kept SQ "for read-path simplicity".
- Not addressed anywhere: MLA / latent KV / weight absorption; pre-RoPE keys; per-channel vs per-token scale theory; models other than the four listed.

## What the report must change
1. Line 353 (idea 10 gap): "query 공분산 가중 변환" → "query 2차 모멘트 M_q = Q⊤Q 가중, 비직교 변환 R_K = M_q^{1/2}E (Thm. 1, RR⊤ = M_q)". Upgrade [S] → full-text verified. State that the Hadamard/OSCAR win is on RULER NIAH at 2.22 BPE and holds ONLY with VQ (Table 4: NOVA-KV transform + SQ = 0.0 at 64K/128K); cite Qwen3-8B 128K 75.4 vs OSCAR 25.3 vs QuaRot 0.0 (BF16 83.4).
2. Novelty of 정리 10.2 must be re-scoped: Theorem 1 of 2608.04074 is exactly "whitening by M^{1/2} then KLT of M^{1/2}ΣM^{1/2}, rank-p truncation" for MHA/GQA keys, including the generalized Parseval (Prop. 1) and the GQA head-sum of M_q (F.5). What remains MLA-specific in idea 10: (a) M_K built from ABSORBED queries a^h = W_UK^{h⊤}q^h, (b) a value-side term M_V = Σ_h W_UV^{h⊤}W_UV^h on the SAME latent (the paper says "Joint quantization of keys and values is left for future work" and "We do not claim joint optimality"), (c) the RoPE/no-RoPE split of the latent, (d) folding T into W_UK/W_UV/W_O. The report's claim "변환을 가중치에 접을 수 있다는 점은 MHA ... 디코드 비용이 0" needs nuance: NOVA-KV already folds the value inverse into W_O and applies R_K^{−⊤} to the query once per step (one d×d product per query), so MHA decode cost is already near zero; the MLA advantage is that the pre-RoPE latent lets the KEY-side transform also be folded into weights (no per-step product), which should be stated precisely.
3. Idea 10's variable-rate water-filling (b_i = max(0, ½log₂(λ_i/θ))) conflicts with fixed-width serving; the paper's Theorem 2 (equal-volume grouping + fixed-rate VQ attains the variable-rate optimum under indep.-Gaussian high-resolution) is the ready-made fix and should be cited as prior art for the allocation step; rank-p truncation (zero bits on trailing directions) is already in Theorem 1 (R⋆_{K,p}).
4. Idea 1 / per-token scale: the paper gives no heteroscedasticity theory; its per-token RMS scale ρ_j is explicitly outside Theorem 2. So the "이분산 편향" gap claim survives against this paper. But add the GPT-OSS counter-evidence as a caveat: for the scalar codec at 2 bits, 4 scale groups per row (4.0 BPE) only lifts NIAH-8K from 0.8 to 27.0 while INT3 with one scale (3.5 BPE) reaches 82.8 — "the deficit is resolution per coordinate, not outlier range" (Table 22). Any per-channel-scale prediction in idea 1/10 (RoPE branch) must be stated as conditional on the bit width.
5. Idea 10 risk line ("OSCAR/Spend-Bits 저자들이 MLA로 확장하는 데 몇 달이면 충분하다") is supported: the GQA extension in F.5 is one equation; the absorbed-query version is the same form. Also note Spend-Bits is a preprint (Aug 2026), no venue.
6. Idea 9 line 331: "query 에너지 가중(OSCAR/Spend-Bits의 논점)" can cite Eq. (5)/Thm. 1 exactly; note Spend-Bits works on POST-RoPE keys and does not analyze per-frequency-band allocation, so idea 9's band-wise claim is not pre-empted.
7. Also note the paper's own asymptotic-fit numbers (α ≈ 1.82–1.88 vs 2 at b∈[1,2.5]) wherever the report invokes high-resolution theory at ≤2 bits (ideas 9, 10).
