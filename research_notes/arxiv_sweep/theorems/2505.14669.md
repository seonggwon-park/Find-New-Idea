- Castro, Panferov, Tabesh, Sieberling, Chen, Nikdan, Ashkboos, Alistarh (ISTA et al.)
- Venue: **NeurIPS 2025 main track** (proceedings.neurips.cc hash 3e11872d…; poster 117521).

- Text checked: v4 full text from the paper cache, plus v1/v2/v3 PDFs fetched into scratchpad `papers/2505.14669v1/` for comparison.

## IMPORTANT: the backward-pass recipe changed between versions
- v1–v3: backward = **stochastic rounding (SR)** after a block-wise RHT. Algorithm 1 has `Gq ← SR(3/4·Gh); Wq ← SR(3/4·Wh⊤)`. Table 2 caption: "the best performing method is stochastic rounding, correlated with perfect magnitude alignment … combines block-wise QuEST on the forward, with Stochastic Rounding on the backward pass."
- v4: backward = **RTN** after the same RHT, with a 16/9 rescale. Footnote 2 (Sec 4.3): "In the first draft of the paper, an implementation bug caused large performance degradation for RTN backward quantization for longer training runs. This degradation led to our arriving at a different conclusion (SR superiority), which we now reject."
- The NeurIPS camera-ready probably matches v3 (Nov 2025), which still uses SR. This is an inference from dates only and was not checked against the proceedings PDF.
- Consequence: any note saying "Quartet = SR + RHT backward" describes v1–v3 only. The current arXiv version says RTN + RHT, and it explicitly rejects its earlier claim that SR is better.

## Q1. Eq. 1 (Sec 4.1): exact form
L(N, D, P_fwd, P_bwd) = ( A/(N·effN(P_fwd))^α + B/(D·effD(P_bwd))^β )^γ + E

- **Reciprocal (Chinchilla-type) form with an outer exponent γ.** This is the Busbridge et al. [8] form. The other form found in secondary sources, (A(N·effN)^α + …)^γ, is wrong.
- "Both these factors are naturally in the interval (0, 1], where the value 1 is reached for full-precision."
- Assumptions are **postulated, not derived**:
  - "our parametrization postulates that the impact of the forward-pass precision is felt primarily w.r.t. the trainable parameters"
  - "we postulate that lowering backward-pass precision primarily impacts the data term D … This is a novel way to model backward pass quantization that we propose, consistent with optimization theory results [1]"
- effN and effD are per-method constants that do not depend on N or D.
- Fitting (App. A.3):
  - Stage 1 fits A, α, B, β, E, γ on baseline runs, N ∈ {30M, 50M, 100M, 200M}, D/N ∈ {25…800}, Huber loss δ=1e-4 on log L.
  - Inconsistency: the appendix says "baseline BF16 runs", but Sec 5 says "(FP8 forward, FP8 backward)".
  - Table 6 values: A=1.52e5, α=0.589, B=5.25e5, β=0.544, E=1.35, γ=0.274.
  - Stage 2 fits effN and effD with everything else frozen:
    - Sec 4.3 isolated methods: effN fitted on forward-only runs, effD on backward-only runs.
    - Table 3 methods: effN and effD fitted jointly.
- The paper says it compares against Kumar et al. [30] in App. A.3. App. A.3 actually only shows alternative fits with γ=1 or β=1 (Fig. 6), not Kumar's law.
- No theorem. The scaling law is a fitted model, and the effN/effD split is an assumption.

## Q3. Quantizers (v4, Sec 4.4 and Algorithm 1)
- **Forward:**
  - Fixed block Hadamard H_g with g = MXFP4 group size of 32.
  - Then QuEST: "Hadamard normalization followed by RMSE-based clipping". This is an MSE-optimal clipping scale, which gives a clipping mask M.
  - Then an MXFP4 GEMM.
- **Backward (v4):**
  - Identical block-wise **random** Hadamard Ĥ_g(·, ξ) applied to dy and to Wq⊤.
  - `Gq ← RTN(3/4·Gh); Wq ← RTN(3/4·Wh⊤)`, then an MXFP4 GEMM.
  - `dx ← 16/9 · H_g^{-1}(dx_q) ⊙ M_x`, and the same for dW with M_w.
  - The 3/4 range scaling and 16/9 rescale follow [47] (Tseng et al.). The paper says this "rescales to compensate for RTN range matching".
- **Backward (v1–v3):** the same structure with SR in place of RTN.
- The unbiased RTN-after-RHT rescale S of [48, 49] "is incompatible with coarse group-wise scaling of the MXFP4 format, so we cannot use it in practice". It is used only as a diagnostic, 1−E[1/S].
- Status of the main claims:
  - "effN appears to correlate heavily with MSE" is an empirical observation.
  - "data efficiency is linked to the bias in the method's gradient estimator" is a claim (contribution 2) supported by Table 2 and Fig. 2 on a 30M model. It is not proved.

## Q4. Limitations as stated (Sec 6)
- v4: "One current limiting factor is that Quartet was designed with a specific (standard) data-type and compute architecture in mind. In future work, we plan to look into generalizing our approach to alternative formats, as well as larger-scale distributed model execution."
- v1 additionally said: "Certain aspects of our method rely on specialized operations, like stochastic rounding, which have hardware support for MXFP4, but may be lacking for other formats." v4 removes this sentence.

## Limitations the paper does not state (my assessment)
- effN and effD are fitted on 30M models in Table 3 and on 30–200M baselines. Whether they stay constant across N and D is assumed, not tested.
- The 7B evidence is only a training-dynamics curve relative to FP8 (Fig. 4c). No fitted effN or effD is given at 7B.
- The "FP4 is optimal" regions (Fig. 1b/c) depend on the BOPS speedup model or RTX 5090 speedups.
- The format is MXFP4 (E8M0 scales, 32-blocks), not NVFP4.
