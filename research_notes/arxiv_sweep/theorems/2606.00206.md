# 2606.00206 — Quantized Reasoning Models Think They Need to Think Longer, but They Do Not

Lotfi, Kirichenko, Li, Liu (FAIR at Meta). Published 2026-05-29, cs.LG. **Venue: preprint** (no comment/journal_ref; first pass found no acceptance). Line numbers refer to `scratchpad/papers/2606.00206/2606.00206.txt` (untrusted data, quoted only).

First pass (verify/batch_13.md) already confirmed several points: the 52% figure is one setting (Qwen-1.5B, AWQ 3-bit, MATH-500; 139/265); errors are labelled by a GPT-5 judge; Spearman ρ=0.92; high-KL tokens; density ratio 0.57 to 1.15; 2–4× top-20 inclusion at τ=80th percentile. This note goes deeper on scope, definitions and what the numbers actually average over.

## Nature of the evidence
**No theorem or lemma.** The only equation is the penalty, Eq.(1): z'_t(v) = z_t(v) − λ if v ∈ S, else z_t(v). The mechanism (Sec 6) borrows a logit-margin argument from Yayla & Kumar (2026), which targets classification nets under bit flips. The paper says "the same structural argument applies" to autoregressive models and then verifies it only through correlation. It states (l.1419–1420): "this argument provides intuition for one specific failure mode, not a complete causal explanation".

## Setup
- Models: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/14B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B, and QwQ-32B (RLVR-trained).
- PTQ: GPTQ and AWQ, W3/W4, g=128 (weight-only); FlatQuant W4A4KV4 and W8A8KV8.
- Benchmarks: GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME-120 (AIME-90 + AIME-2025), LiveCodeBench, GPQA-Diamond.
- Decoding: T=0.6, top-p=0.95.
- **Calibration differs by method** (App A.1, l.1565–1570):
  - GPTQ uses "model-generated reasoning traces on NuminaMath-1.5 … 128 sequences of length 2048".
  - AWQ uses Pile, 128×512.
  - FlatQuant uses WikiText2, 128×2048, with length 4096 for the 1.5B/7B models.
- The headline AWQ-3bit numbers therefore come from generic-text calibration, while the GPTQ numbers come from in-domain reasoning calibration.

## Q1. Exact numbers
- **52%** (Sec 4.2, l.609–614). The setting is DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on MATH-500.
  - BF16: "72 errors out of 500 questions, with only 19 attributed to overthinking (26%)".
  - "Under 3-bit AWQ, overthinking errors rise from 19 to 139, a 7.3× increase, making overthinking the dominant failure mode at 52% of all 265 errors."
  - GPTQ 3-bit: 19 → 39. FlatQuant: 19 → 64.
  - Definition of overthinking (l.601–603): "The model reaches the correct solution at some point in its chain of thought but does not commit to it as the final answer. Instead, the model opens new reasoning paths, excessively questions its own assumptions, or reverses a correct conclusion."
  - Labels come from a GPT-5 judge tuned to >95% agreement with human annotation on a subset. "Up to 52%" is a max over three methods in one model/benchmark cell, not across models.
  - Other numbers: the GSM8K analog is Fig 10 / B.5. The CoT-length/accuracy Spearman ρ = −0.73 is over 28 model-quantization pairs (l.400–401).
- **12–23%** (Sec 5, Fig 5, l.926–927). "Penalizing overthinking markers consistently reduces CoT length by 12% to 23% across all λ values".
  - This is **Qwen-1.5B only**, averaged over six quantization configurations and five benchmarks, with λ ∈ [0.5, 4.0]. So 12–23% is the range over λ, not over models.
  - Across five models (Table 1, best λ per configuration, most aggressive settings): "The penalty reduces CoT length by 4.1% to 28.0% on average across benchmarks."
  - The abstract and conclusion attach "12–23%" to "5 models, 3 quantization methods, 5 benchmarks", which overstates the scope.
  - Best case: Qwen-1.5B AWQ-3bit, +6.2% accuracy and −28.0% length.
  - BF16 models also shrink by 5.6–10.0%.
  - Table 1 reports "the best λ; we report the standard deviation w.r.t λ". No held-out λ selection is described, so the best-λ rows are selected on the evaluation benchmarks (optimistic).
- **58%** (l.1384–1388). "On AWQ 3-bit, overthinking errors drop from 139 to 58, a 58% reduction, while total errors decrease from 265 to 194."
  - FlatQuant W4A4KV4: 64 → 34 (47%), with total errors 168 → 143.
  - GSM8K, summed over the three extreme configurations: 344 → 237 (31%).
  - Again only Qwen-1.5B with GPT-5 judge labels. The Fig 3 caption pairs it with "improving accuracy by up to 14.2%".
- Ablations:
  - Low-KL-token penalty: CoT "+up to 41%", accuracy "−up to 9.5%".
  - Negative λ (boosting markers): CoT up to +445%, accuracy up to −34% (Fig 9).
  - Random tokens: negligible effect.
  - High-KL list: helps at low λ, hurts accuracy at high λ.
- Set S has 50 hand-curated markers ("Wait", "But", "Alternatively", "perhaps", "maybe", "however", "reconsider", "backtrack", "wrong", …). It deliberately excludes high-KL tokens such as "so".

## Q2. How token-level KL vs entropy is measured
- Sec 4.3 (l.729–738): "run both models on the same MATH-500 prompts under identical generation prefixes … At each decoding position t, we compute D_KL(p_t ‖ q_t) = Σ_v p_t(v) log p_t(v)/q_t(v), where p_t and q_t are the next-token distributions of the full-precision and quantized models".
  - The direction is **KL(FP ‖ quantized)**.
  - Each KL value is attached to "the token that the quantized model sampled at that position" and averaged per token type. Only tokens with ≥50 occurrences are kept.
  - Whose trajectory forms the prefix is not stated explicitly. The token attribution implies the prefixes are the quantized model's own samples, with FP teacher-forced on them. This is an inference.
- Scope: **one model (R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B), one quantizer (AWQ 3-bit), one benchmark (MATH-500)**.
- Entropy is the BF16 model's next-token Shannon entropy. The correlation is **Spearman ρ = 0.92 "across all positions"** (Fig 4c; Sec 6, l.1410–1414). Being rank-based, it supports a monotone relation but no functional form.
- Fig 7 / App B.2 (l.1659–1668): the rate at which at least one lexicon token is in the top-20 at H_t > τ vs H_t ≤ τ, with τ = the per-model 80th entropy percentile. Inclusion is ≈2–4× higher at high-entropy positions. This holds for R1-Qwen-1.5B and QwQ-32B in BF16, AWQ-3bit and GPTQ-3bit.
  - Note that this measures **top-20 inclusion**, including in BF16. It does not measure that quantized models *sample* markers more often at high-entropy positions.
  - The abstract's "at these positions quantized models disproportionately sample overthinking markers" is therefore an inference that combines two separate measurements (high-KL-token lists and Fig 7). It is not a direct conditional sampling-rate comparison.
- Density of high-KL vs low-KL tokens per CoT: 0.57 (BF16) → 1.15 (AWQ-3bit).

## Q3. Calibration-side or training-side fix?
**None proposed or tested.**
- The only fix is the decoding-time logit penalty.
- Related work (l.205–209) lists fine-tuning approaches for overthinking (Zhao et al. 2025 self-braking, early exit, shortest-of-N). It positions this paper as "mitigat[ing] it without any training, since PTQ itself is training-free."
- Calibration data is never ablated, even though methods use different calibration sets (above).
- Future work mentions only an entropy-adaptive λ and automatic per-model marker lists.
- The conclusion (l.1438–1442) suggests "Comparing token-level divergences between a reference model and its compressed variant could serve as a general diagnostic", i.e., a diagnostic, not a calibration objective.

## Limitations / future work (Sec 7, close paraphrase/quote)
- "Overthinking is not unique to quantized models… Quantization amplifies a failure mode already present in reasoning models."
- "Our KL divergence analysis is also not specific to quantization: any perturbation … from pruning, distillation, or low-rank approximation, may similarly affect uncertain positions."
- "Our penalty uses a fixed λ at every decoding step… adapting λ dynamically based on local entropy is a natural extension."
- "The overthinking markers list is manually curated for English reasoning models. Automatically constructing the list per model would make our approach more widely applicable."
- "our evaluation focuses on math, coding, and science benchmarks. Whether the same overthinking patterns arise in other reasoning and planning domains remains an open question."
- Sec 6: "not a complete causal explanation".

## Implications for the report
1. Line 323 says "토큰 KL이 고엔트로피 위치에서 최대이고 그곳에서 … 과표집". Change to: "위치별 KL(FP‖Q)이 FP 다음토큰 엔트로피와 Spearman ρ=0.92(R1-Qwen-1.5B, AWQ-3bit, MATH-500 한 설정)". Also note that the "high-entropy positions have markers in the top-20 2–4× more often" result (Fig 7, top-20 inclusion) does not directly measure conditional sampling rates. "Maximum" is not what the paper shows; it shows rank correlation.
2. "실패의 최대 52%" should add: one model, one benchmark, AWQ-3bit with **Pile calibration**, labelled by the GPT-5 judge. GPTQ-3bit gives 39 (with reasoning-trace calibration) and FlatQuant 64.
3. If the report cites 12–23%, scope it to "Qwen-1.5B, λ sweep". Across 5 models the range is 4.1–28.0% with best-λ selected on the test benchmarks.
4. Line 323 says "디코딩 측 로짓 페널티만 제안" (only a decoding-side logit penalty). This is correct, and it holds strictly: no calibration or training fix, and no calibration ablation.
5. **New, directly relevant to Idea 8's (a)/(b) design**: inside this paper, GPTQ-W3 is calibrated on self-generated NuminaMath reasoning traces and is far more robust than AWQ-W3 calibrated on Pile. On Qwen-1.5B MATH-500: 71.6% / 6.8K tokens vs 47.0% / 23.4K, and overthinking errors are 39 vs 139.
   - This is consistent with Idea 8's hypothesis but **confounded** by the algorithm (GPTQ vs AWQ).
   - Idea 8 should cite it as motivating evidence and should include the cross-design cell (AWQ with reasoning calibration, GPTQ with generic calibration), or else hold the algorithm fixed, as the plan already does.
6. Lemma 8.1 (KL ≈ (σ²/2)(1−‖p‖²)) is consistent with the observed ρ=0.92 because the relation is monotone. The paper used Shannon entropy and rank correlation, so it does not discriminate between collision entropy and Shannon entropy. Idea 8 could add a test of the functional form, e.g. KL vs 1−‖p‖² vs H on the same positions.
7. The "정답 미확정 비율(2606.00206 프로토콜)" in the experiment plan needs an LLM judge (GPT-5 prompt in Table 12 / App D) plus human validation. The overthinking category also includes "excessively questions its own assumptions", which is broader than "had the right answer and did not commit". The report should say which definition it uses.
