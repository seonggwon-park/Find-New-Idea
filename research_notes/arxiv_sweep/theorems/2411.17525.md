## 0. One-paragraph cold summary

The "linearity theorem" (Theorem 1, Sec. 3.3) is a **second-order Taylor expansion of perplexity around the pre-trained weights** under three assumptions: (A1) the pre-trained weights are a local minimizer of PPL (so the gradient term vanishes; the paper notes A1 can be dropped if the quantizer is *unbiased*), (A2) PPL is C³ near w*, and (A3) the **norm-scaled Hessian D*∇²φ(w*)D* is approximately block-diagonal with each diagonal block a scalar multiple of the identity**, Z_l = z_l·I. Under these, E[PPL(Ŵ)] ≈ PPL(W*) + Σ_l α_l t_l², where t_l² is the **relative Frobenius MSE** of layer l and α_l = z_l·M_{2,l}/2 is quantizer-independent. It is stated with "≈" and the qualifier "as long as t_1,…,t_L are small enough"; the only explicit remainder bound is in the *univariate* warm-up (App. D.2); the multivariate/block proof (App. D.4) has no quantitative error term. Assumption 3 is "validated" only by visual inspection of Hessian sub-blocks of OPT-125M (App. E). Empirical applicability on Llama 3.1 8B: predicted vs. real PPL agree for b > 3.0 bits and diverge below (Sec. 6.1, Fig. 1). The bit-allocation problem (Eq. 5) is a knapsack-style integer program; despite the abstract's "reduction to dynamic programming," the paper actually solves it with the **CP-SAT solver (Google OR-Tools)** (Sec. 5, "Solving the Problem"). The paper explicitly says the theorem "has no direct bearing on the data-aware layer-wise MSE minimization problems considered in … GPTQ and QuIP" (Sec. 2).

## 1. Exact statement of the linearity theorem

### 1.1 Notation (Sec. 3.1)
- W* = (W*_1,…,W*_L), W*_l ∈ R^{d_in^l × d_out^l}; R_l reshapes to a vector w_l ∈ R^{d_l}, d_l := d_in^l·d_out^l; d := Σ d_l.
- "Perplexity. Let ϕ : R^d → R be the perplexity function on R^d defined formally as ϕ(w) := PPL(R^{-1}(w)), (2) where PPL is the perplexity function operating in the space of W."
- D*_l := ‖W*_l‖_F · I_{d_l}; D* := Diag(D*_1,…,D*_L) ∈ R^{d×d}.

### 1.2 Assumptions (Sec. 3.2) — verbatim

> **Assumption 1 (Local optimality of the pre-trained weights).** The uncompressed model weights w⋆ are a local minimizer of perplexity ϕ.
>
> […] We emphasize that Assumption 1 is not needed if the compression mechanism used to compress each matrix W⋆_l is unbiased, i.e., if E[Ŵ_l] = W⋆_l for all l ∈ {1, …, L}. We will leverage this observation in Section 5.

> **Assumption 2 (Local smoothness of perplexity).** The perplexity function ϕ is three times continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of w⋆.

> **Assumption 3 (Regularity of pre-trained weights).** There exists a block-diagonal matrix Z = Diag(Z_1, …, Z_L), where Z_l = z_l I_{d_l}, z_l > 0 for all l ∈ {1, …, L}, such that
> D⋆ ∇²ϕ(R(W⋆)) D⋆ ≈ Z.

> "We validate that Assumption 3 holds on language models in Appendix E."

**Which Hessian approximation:** the *norm-rescaled* Hessian D*∇²φ D* (each layer's block scaled by ‖W*_l‖_F²) is approximated as block-diagonal **and isotropic within each block** (scalar times identity). This is stronger than "block-diagonal": it also assumes all directions inside a layer are equally curved after norm-scaling. No Fisher / GGN / Kronecker approximation is used; the object is the exact perplexity Hessian, approximated structurally.

**Independence / zero-mean:** There is **no explicit cross-layer independence assumption on the quantization errors**. Cross-layer cross-terms vanish because of the block-diagonality in Assumption 3 (Hessian structure), not because of independence of errors. There is **no zero-mean assumption**; the first-order term is killed by Assumption 1 (∇φ(w*) = 0). The paper notes an *alternative*: if the compressor is unbiased (E[Ŵ_l] = W*_l), Assumption 1 is unnecessary — that is the only place zero-mean error enters, and it is an "either/or" with local optimality.

### 1.3 Theorem 1 (Sec. 3.3) — verbatim

> **Theorem 1 (Linearity theorem).** Let the above assumptions hold. Given an arbitrary layer index l and an arbitrary (possibly stochastic) quantizer function Q_l, let Ŵ_l := Q_l(W⋆_l) be the compressed version of the layer weights W⋆_l. For each layer l, define the parameter t_l as the relative quantization error, i.e.:
>
>   t_l² = t_l²(W_l, Q_l) := E[‖Ŵ_l − W⋆_l‖_F²] / ‖W⋆_l‖_F²,   (3)
>
> Then, as long as t_1, …, t_L are small enough, the following linear approximation of the expected perplexity holds:
>
>   E[PPL(Ŵ)] ≈ PPL(W⋆) + Σ_{l=1}^{L} α_l t_l²,   (4)
>
> where expectation is taken w.r.t. the randomness in the compression process, and the terms α_l are layer specific constants that are independent of the compression process.

> "Discussion. … the coefficients α_l are 'universal,' in the sense that their values depend only on the layer weights, and not on the quantization function."

**What is linear in what:** expected *perplexity* (not loss, not KL) is linear in the per-layer **relative MSE t_l²** (squared relative Frobenius error), with layer constants α_l. In terms of the perturbation δ_l = Ŵ_l − W*_l, this is **quadratic** in δ (second-order Taylor); the first-order term is zero by A1.

### 1.4 Proof structure (App. D) and what α_l actually is
- D.1: "The proof follows from the material included in Section D.4."
- D.2 (univariate, pedagogical): with |φ'''| ≤ B3 and M3 := E|ξ|³ < ∞, the paper derives an explicit bound
  |E[φ(w*+t|w*|ξ)] − (φ(w*) + ½φ''(w*)t²|w*|²M2)| ≤ (1/6)|t|³|w*|³ B3 M3.
  **This is the only quantitative remainder bound in the paper**, and it is univariate.
- D.4 (multivariate block case = the actual proof): perturbation written as w* + D T ξ with T_l = t_l I, D_l = ‖w*_l‖ I, M_{2,l} := E‖ξ_l‖². Then
  "Let's assume that D∇²ϕ(w⋆)D is approximately block-diagonal, i.e., there exists a block-diagonal matrix Z = Diag(Z_1,…,Z_L) such that D∇²ϕ(w⋆)D ≈ Z. Moreover, assume that Z_l = z_l I_{d_l} for some z_l > 0 … Under this assumption, ⟨D∇²ϕ(w⋆)DTξ, Tξ⟩ ≈ ⟨ZTξ, Tξ⟩ = Σ_l t_l² z_l ‖ξ_l‖²  (17)"
  and E[φ(w*+DTξ)] ≈ φ(w*) + Σ_l α_l t_l², "where α_l := z_l M_{2,l}/2."
- So α_l = ½·z_l·E‖ξ_l‖² : half the per-layer isotropic curvature constant times the second moment of the normalized perturbation. With the normalization implied by (3) (M_{2,l}=1), α_l = z_l/2. No third-order remainder is tracked in D.3/D.4; the statement is "≈" throughout.
- **Leftover TODO in the text (App. C, footnote 1):** after "t̄_l is 'small enough'¹" the footnote reads "Add some comment on how small it is." — i.e., the applicability radius is never characterized analytically.

### 1.5 Empirical validation of the theorem and of Assumption 3
- Sec. 6.1 (Llama 3.1 8B, uniform HIGGS, grids on the PPL-vs-bitwidth Pareto frontier, 1 ≤ p ≤ 5, 9 ≤ n ≤ 4096): "We observe that the predicted perplexity is close to the real perplexity on relatively higher bitwidths (b > 3.0) and diverges on lower bitwidths, where the quantization error is higher. Thus, we can use the error model in realistic bitwidth ranges." Figure 1 caption: "Vertical line shows the limit of the theorem's applicability." Intro: "works remarkably well at predicting quantization error for different schemes in the 3-8 bit range".
- App. E (Assumption 3): on **OPT-125M only**; full Hessians infeasible ("768 × 768 = 589,824 parameters, leading to a Hessian with around 400 Billion entries"), so they compute the Hessian on subsets of t ∈ {25,150,300} parameters per layer (1800×1800 products) and report "a clear diagonal structure" visually (Figs. 4, 5). Conclusion: "we have fairly strong reasons to believe that Assumption 3 is satisfied for LLM's." No quantitative diagonal-dominance ratio, no test on Llama.
- App. C: the single-layer version (Eq. 11) and all-layer version (Eq. 13) were first found "through numerical experimentation", with α_l "computed via linear regression"; the theorem formalizes the empirical observation.

### 1.6 Scope restriction stated by the authors (Sec. 2)
> "We emphasize the fact that the linearity theorem has no direct bearing on the data-aware layer-wise MSE minimization problems considered in references such as GPTQ and QuIP, which are of the form min_{Ŵ_l∈Ω_l} ‖W⋆_l X − Ŵ_l X‖_F² … Here, we focus on the data-free case, and relate it to the quantization MSE over weights."

## 2. Per-layer MSE-optimal grids (Sec. 4; App. F)

**Gaussian assumption after Hadamard (Sec. 4.1):**
> "it is well-known (Ailon and Chazelle, 2009; Suresh et al., 2017; Chee et al., 2023) that multiplication of the layer weights with the Random Hadamard Transform (RHT) leads the weight distribution to closely match a Gaussian distribution, independently of the original weights."
> "Since, post-RHT, the weight distribution is approximately Gaussian, we obtain that, in this case, the layer error coefficients t_l will only depend on the chosen grid, and not on the original weights. (Please see the proof of this fact in Appendix F.) More specifically, t_l² approximately equals the per-dimension MSE of rounding the multivariate standard normal distribution to the grid G_n^p, which is constant given n and p, and independent of the original weights."
> "In this context, Theorem 1 implies that if weights are Hadamard-transformed, then MSE-optimized grids should be theoretically-optimal in terms of end-to-end model error, given a fixed bit budget."

**Algorithm 1 (RHT-VQ):** partition w ∈ R^D into D/g groups of size g (power of 2); s_i = ‖w_{i}‖₂; w†_{i} = RandomHadamardTransform(w_{i}/s_i, ξ) "▷ entries of w†_{i} are approx. from N(0,1)"; q†_{i} = RoundToNearest(w†_{i}, G_n^p) with p consecutive entries projected together; scales s = (s_1,…,s_{D/g})/√g.

**Algorithm 2 (HIGGS):** G_n^p = CLVQ(n, p) "(Pagès and Printems, 2003), computed once"; then RHT-VQ. "Section 4.1 described the exact quantity we need to optimize when choosing G_n^p: the expected MSE of rounding the multivariate Normal distribution to G_n^p. … it can be solved optimally by the Pagès and Printems (2003) algorithm arising in numerical PDEs. We use the same grid optimization procedure, as well as some pre-computed optimal grids. … the optimal grid only has to be computed once for any pair of n and p." (CLVQ = Competitive Learning Vector Quantization, borrowed from numerical-integration literature.) *Note:* "solved optimally" is the paper's wording; CLVQ is a stochastic k-means-type procedure for the Gaussian quantization problem.

**App. F ("Proof of Expected Error"):** chain of "≈": t_l² = Σ_i E‖ŵ_i − w_i‖² / Σ_i‖w_i‖² = Σ_i s_i² E‖q†_i − w†_i‖² / Σ_i‖w_i‖² ≈ Σ_i s_i² E_{w†∼N(0,1)}‖q†_i − w†_i‖² / … = Σ_i s_i²·g·t²(G_n^p)/Σ_i‖w_i‖² = t²(G_n^p). "That is, t_l² approximately equals the expected element-wise MSE of rounding the multivariate standard normal distribution to the grid G_n^p." The Gaussianity step is asserted, not bounded.

**Practical constraints (Sec. 4.3):** FLUTE lookup-table kernels support p ∈ {1,2}, b ∈ {2,3,4}; "p = 2 is always preferable to p = 1"; grid must fit shared memory, "total number of points in the grid 2^{k×p} can be at most ≈ 2^{10}"; "Increasing the grid dimension at fixed bitwidth reduces the expected error … This limitation creates a quantization error lower bound dictated by which dimensions we can use in practice." For 8-bit, "Constrained HIGGS" (CH8) restricts the grid to be uniform and optimizes its position/scale for Gaussian MSE.

**Grid comparison numbers (Table 3, Llama 3.1 8B, WikiText-2 PPL; FP16 = 5.607):**
- 3.25 bit: AF 8.056, NF 7.683, HQQ 7.317, HIGGS p=2 7.110, p=3 6.807, p=4 6.643; GPTQ (data-aware, 3.25) 7.133; HIGGS dyn data-free 6.388.
- ~4.02 bit: AF 6.194, NF 6.225, HIGGS p=1 6.142, p=2 6.015, p=3 5.981; GPTQ 6.238; HIGGS dyn data-free (4.00) 5.910.
- (Table 4, cleaner subset, FP16 5.606): 4.25 bit GPTQ 5.923, AWQ 5.905, HIGGS dyn data-free 5.831, HIGGS dyn (Wiki2-calibrated) 5.802; MMLU 5-shot 64.05 / 63.83 / 64.06 / 64.26 vs FP16 65.36.
- Intro: "for higher bitwidths, we observe that all methods produce results in the same accuracy range."

## 3. Dynamic-programming bit-allocation objective (Sec. 5)

**Setting:** "we wish to quantize each layer W⋆_l using one quantizer from a finite set of options {Q_1,…,Q_J}, each with its own error. Let j_l ∈ {1,…,J} denote the selection of the quantizer for layer l. Assume that quantizer Q_{j_l} corresponds to a specific bitwidth b_{j_l} and specific induced error t²_{l,j_l} from Eqn. (3)."

**Eq. (5), verbatim:**
> min_{j_1,…,j_L} Σ_{l=1}^{L} α_l · t²_{l,j_l}
> s.t. Σ_{l=1}^{L} b_{j_l} · d_l ≤ b_max · d
>      j_l ∈ {1,…,J} for all l ∈ {1,…,L}
> where b_max is the target bitwidth. Recall that d := Σ_l d_l.

**Inputs:**
- α_l: "Theorem 1 shows that the scaling coefficients α_l do not depend on the quantization method used. We use this fact to estimate these coefficients without using a real quantization method, and instead introduce a Gaussian noise insertion procedure (App. B.2)": G_l(W_l, t) := W_l + (t‖W_l‖_F/√(d_in d_out)) Σ_l, Σ_l i.i.d. N(0,1) (Eq. 9), so t_l²(W_l, G_l(·,t)) = t². **Algorithm 3:** for each layer l and J noise levels t_j, Δ_{l,j} = PPL(W*(l,t_j)) − PPL(W*); α_l = argmin_{α'} Σ_j (Δ_{l,j} − α' t_j²)² (least squares through origin). "We found that sampling J = 15 noise levels from linear theorem applicability range is enough." Calibration: "287k tokens from WikiText2 train set" (Sec. 6).
- "Data Free Dynamic Quantization": replace PPL on a calibration set by "KL-divergence between pretrained and quantized models on randomly sampled text tokens. We evaluate KL-divergence on 287k random tokens." (This is the only KL usage in the paper; there is **no KL ≈ ¼‖δ‖²-type formula anywhere**.)
- t²_{l,j}: "can be measured explicitly by quantizing W⋆_l with Q_j, creating a 'database' of per-layer errors, across supported bitwidths." (Measured, not taken from the Gaussian constant.)

**Solver — verbatim:**
> "Since the error function to be minimized is summable, Equation 5 can be expressed as a linear programming problem, which can be solved using already existing optimization libraries. Specifically, we use the CP-SAT solver from Google OR-Tools library (Perron and Didier, 2024), that can optimally solve this problem."
Abstract/intro wording: "obtained by reduction to dynamic programming" / "knapsack-style dynamic programming over the set of quantization choices at each layer". **In the body no DP recursion is written down**; the problem is a multiple-choice knapsack, solved as an integer program with CP-SAT. Note also that in the non-uniform experiments the options are restricted to FLUTE grids and CH8 (Sec. 6.2).

**Result (Fig. 3):** WikiText-2 PPL increase vs b_max for Llama 3.1 8B, with "Dotted lines represent Linear Model predictions."

## 4. Paper's own limitations / future work (Sec. 8 "Limitations", verbatim)

> "One direction for improvement is validating the approach across several model architecture types (e.g. Mixture-of-Experts). However, we believe our result should be generalizable, as the quantization approach used is model-independent. One other limitation is the requirement to use Hadamard transforms for weight incoherence, which may add runtime overheads in some cases. However, it is known (Chee et al., 2023) that these runtimes can be minimized, or that the corresponding matrices can even be eliminated by 'folding them into' the previous layer (Ashkboos et al., 2024). We aim to investigate this in future work, as further enhancements to existing kernels such as FLUTE (Guo et al., 2024)."

Other in-text caveats: theorem only "as long as t_l are small enough" (Thm 1), empirically b > 3.0 (Sec. 6.1); grid-dimension cap from shared memory (Sec. 4.3); "no direct bearing" on data-aware objectives (Sec. 2); Assumption 3 checked only on OPT-125M subsets (App. E).

## 5. What the report must change (`reports/LLM 양자화 연구 아이디어 10선.md`)

Current citation (line 38): "proxy에서 end-to-end 손실로 가는 다리는 1차(HIGGS linearity theorem, KL≈¼‖δ‖²)이거나 느슨한 Lipschitz 상한뿐이다 (… [HIGGS 2411.17525] [S])."

1. **Remove "KL≈¼‖δ‖²" from the HIGGS attribution.** No such formula exists in the paper. HIGGS states E[PPL] ≈ PPL(W*) + Σ_l α_l t_l² with t_l² the *relative* Frobenius MSE and α_l an *estimated* (regression) layer constant; the object is perplexity, not KL, and there is no universal constant like ¼. If the ¼ came from another source, cite that source.
2. **"1차" (first-order) is misleading.** The bridge is a *second-order Taylor expansion* (gradient term vanishes by local optimality / unbiasedness); PPL increase is linear in MSE, i.e., quadratic in the perturbation. Write: "2차 Taylor 전개(HIGGS Thm 1: ΔPPL ≈ Σ_l α_l t_l², t_l² = 상대 MSE)".
3. **State the assumptions when invoking it:** A1 local minimizer (or unbiased quantizer), A2 C³, A3 norm-scaled Hessian ≈ block-diagonal with scalar-identity blocks (isotropic per layer). No cross-layer error-independence or zero-mean assumption is used; cross terms vanish by A3. A3 was validated only by eyeballing OPT-125M Hessian sub-blocks (App. E). The multivariate proof has no remainder bound; only the univariate warm-up has one (O(t³)).
4. **Applicability range:** empirically b > 3.0 on Llama 3.1 8B (Sec. 6.1, Fig. 1); predictions diverge below. Any report idea relying on the linear model at ≤3 bits or for KV/activation quantization is extrapolating beyond what the paper tested (the paper is weights-only, data-free RTN-style).
5. **Don't lump HIGGS with QuIP/GPTQ as the same "MSE-proxy → loss" bridge:** the authors explicitly say the theorem "has no direct bearing" on the data-aware ‖W X − Ŵ X‖ objectives of GPTQ/QuIP. It bridges *weight-space* MSE to PPL, not activation-space proxies.
6. **Bit allocation:** if the report describes it as DP, note that Eq. (5) is a multiple-choice knapsack solved in practice with CP-SAT (OR-Tools); α_l requires calibration (PPL on 287k WikiText-2 train tokens, or KL on random tokens for the "data-free" variant) and t²_{l,j} is measured per layer and grid.
7. **Venue/tag:** upgrade [S] → [V]; cite as NAACL 2025 long (2025.naacl-long.543), not arXiv-only.
8. Line 321 (YAKV / pre-RoPE claim about "Hadamard 기반 HIGGS 양자화기"): HIGGS itself is a weight-quantization scheme; any KV-cache use is the citing paper's extension — no change to HIGGS facts needed, but keep the [U] on YAKV.
