# 2602.02047: Dissecting Outlier Dynamics in LLM NVFP4 Pretraining (exact statements)

- Authors: Peijie Dong, Ruibo Fan, ..., Xiaowen Chu, Baochun Li, Bo Li (14 authors). v1 2026-02-02, no later version. Comment "39 pages, 32 figures". Venue: preprint (the text says "Preprint. February 3, 2026."). No venue found.
- Source: `scratchpad/papers/2602.02047/2602.02047.txt` (line numbers below). Quoted text is untrusted data.
- The first-pass verdict was "confirmed" (verify/batch_09.md). This note goes further: what is proved, what is only correlational, and what the numbers can and cannot support.

## 1. What is PROVED (App. A): an algebraic identity and a conditional MSE ordering, not a theory of outliers

- Proposition 4.1 (l.855): "The quantized (low-precision, LP) product admits the following exact decomposition": Ŵᵀ X̂ = WᵀX + Wᵀ∆X + ∆WᵀX + ∆Wᵀ∆X. This is an exact identity.
- Lemmas A.3–A.5 (l.1715–1827): these give the error left by three patch schemes on a fixed hot-channel set I: none, first-order (activation only), and second-order (weight and activation, "S-O2-B" = HCP).
- Theorem A.12 (l.2021): "Assume the small-noise regime on hot channels (Assumption 1): ∥∆X_I∥ ≪ ∥X_I∥. Further assume zero-mean residuals and that the three error components a := W_Iᵀ∆X_I, b := ∆W_Iᵀ X_I, c := ∆W_Iᵀ∆X_I are pairwise uncorrelated ... Then MSE2 ≪ MSE1 < MSE0."
  - Critique: the "≪" ordering comes straight from the assumed ∥∆X∥ ≪ ∥X∥ and from the uncorrelatedness assumption. Lemma A.11 says "typically", and its proof uses only the pointwise bounds e₂² ≤ ∥∆W_I∥²∥∆X_I∥² vs e₁² ≤ ∥∆W_I∥²∥X_I∥². The theorem covers the error on the patched channels only. It says nothing about training loss, convergence, or why outliers arise.
  - Numbering inconsistency: the intro (l.191) says "HCP yields a lower MSE upper bound in Theorem A.7", but A.7 is a Lemma (the baseline MSE bound). The ordering result is Theorem A.12.
- No theorem addresses outlier origin, the transient-to-persistent transition, or post-QK sensitivity. All of those are empirical.

## 2. Outlier origins: empirical attribution, partly inherited, and explicitly correlational

- Abstract (l.41–43): "Our analysis attributes outliers to specific architectural components: Softmax in SA, gating in LA, and SwiGLU in FFN, with 'post-QK' operations exhibiting higher sensitivity to quantization."
- Softmax (SA), §3.2 l.605–620: the mechanism is taken from prior work ("Prior research (An et al., 2025) identifies Softmax as the root cause of outliers due to its normalization constraint"). The paper only "empirically validate[s]" it by tracking Post-Softmax Entropy (falls), Pre-Softmax Kurtosis (rises), and "Pre-Softmax Max (from 4 to 10)" on Qwen3-1.7B. That is consistent with the mechanism but does not establish causation.
- Gating (LA), l.634–645: the source is "the elementwise exponential function ϕ(x) = exp(t·x)(t > 2) in the gating mechanism, specifically the gk proj layer, as the primary source for extreme outliers in GLA". State reset (λt ≈ 0) "requires" pre-activations around −120, so the needed range is roughly "[−120, 80]". This is a design argument plus the Top-1 magnitude plot (Fig. 6b). It is the most mechanistic of the three attributions.
- SwiGLU (FFN), l.688–720: "SwiGLU(x) = (xW_up) ⊙ Swish(xW_gate), exhibiting quadratic growth ... Prolonged training with weight decay induces weight alignment (W_up ∥ W_gate) (Fishman et al., 2024)". The evidence is that the layerwise cosine alignment of W_up and W_gate "shows a steady increase throughout training" and is higher in Qwen than in GLA (Fig. 8). The mechanism is inherited from Fishman et al. 2024 (FP8 trillion-token).
- Post-QK sensitivity, l.646–650 and Tab. 3: the value projection is most sensitive for SA (Qwen3-1.7B) and the output projection for LA (GLA-1.3B), using a parameter-normalized sensitivity score (l.1258–1261). "These sensitive regions are consistently situated downstream of outlier sources."
- The authors' own Limitation (l.1352–1364): "our conclusions are largely based on empirical correlations observed during training; establishing stronger causal evidence for specific outlier sources remains an important direction for future work."
  - So the attributions are correlational by the authors' own account. No intervention (for example removing the softmax, the gate exp, or the up/gate alignment) is reported to show that the outliers disappear.

## 3. Transient spikes to persistent hot channels

- §3.3 l.753–761: "Early training stages (e.g., steps 400–5,400) exhibit dynamic, transient magnitude spikes that drift across channels ... as training progresses toward mid-to-late stages, the location of outliers stabilizes. Persistent hot channels ... concentrate in a small subset of channels as shown in Fig. 19. This transition from stochastic drift to structural fixation justifies the use of static, selective mitigation strategies (i.e., Hot-Channel Patch)".
  - Context: at a 4M-token batch, step 5,400 is about 22B tokens. The "transient" window is roughly the first third of the 60B-token run.
  - No quantitative persistence statistic (for example a mask overlap or Jaccard score across checkpoints) appears in the text I read. The claim rests on visual heatmaps (Figs. 3, 19, 20, 22).
- l.700–711: "outliers start with extreme volatility and high magnitudes, then gradually stabilize and decrease (or plateau) ... specific components like gk projection maintain high magnitudes throughout training, behaving as permanent outliers".
- l.737–742: activation kurtosis stays volatile early. Weight kurtosis "Qwen3 climbs steadily and plateaus > 2, whereas GLA rises quickly then flattens near ≈ 1.1".
- l.745–752: outliers "accumulate in the deeper layers (particularly the last 4 layers)".
- HCP design (l.865–870, App. A.1 l.1688): "we detect hot channels and then keep a fixed mask thereafter". Hot channels are I = top-k(s) (l.1036). "we designate 9.09% of channels as hot-channels for compensation" (App. C.1 l.2631). Note that 9.09% is not a "tiny" subset.

## 4. CHON recipe and the 0.94% to 0.58% number

- What CHON is (l.905–906, 1038–1045, 865–875): the NVIDIA NVFP4 recipe (2D weight scaling, RHT, SR, and "excluding attention, lm head, embeddings, and last 4 layers"), plus HCP, plus extra BF16 for "Wo for LA and Wv for SA". For GLA it also adds "high precision for GK projection (gk proj)".
  - Confound: CHON keeps more layers in BF16 than the baseline. The 0.94% to 0.58% comparison does not isolate HCP.
- Table 2 (GLA-1.3B, 60B tokens, RedPajama subset; BF16 final loss 2.168596). The paper reports these loss-gap percentages:

  | Setting | Loss | Gap (%) |
  |---|---|---|
  | NVFP4 w/ HCP (full) = CHON | 2.181415 | 0.588 |
  | w/o sr | 2.183048 | 0.662 |
  | w/o rht | 2.184524 | 0.729 |
  | w/o 2d scaling | 2.184868 | 0.745 |
  | w/o sr, rht | 2.187204 | 0.851 |
  | w/o last4 | 2.188535 | 0.911 |
  | NVFP4 (NVIDIA et al., 2025) | 2.189147 | 0.939 |
  | w/o chon, rht | 2.191353 | 1.039 |

  - Recomputation: the gaps equal (L − L_BF16)/L_quant, not (L − L_BF16)/L_BF16. Relative to BF16 they would be 0.591% and 0.948%. The difference is cosmetic.
  - The absolute loss difference CHON vs NVFP4 is 0.0077 nats. That is one run per configuration, with no seeds or error bars reported.
  - "w/o last4" (0.911) is close to the baseline (0.939). Read literally, removing BF16 for the last 4 layers from CHON wipes out most of its gain, so the improvement depends heavily on precision partitioning.
  - l.1257: "HCP gives the largest gain, reducing the NVFP4-to-BF16 loss gap from 0.939% to 0.588% (Tab. 2)." Strictly, the 0.939 row is the NVIDIA recipe without HCP and without the extra BF16 for Wo and gk_proj. So "HCP gives" overstates what this comparison isolates.
- Scale:
  - Pretraining: GLA-1.3B on 60B tokens (Tab. 2) and 40B tokens (Tab. 1). Qwen3-1.7B is used for the SA analysis.
  - Hyperparameters (l.1262–1272): AdamW (0.9, 0.95), wd 0.1, peak LR 3e-4, cosine schedule with 2k-step warmup, batch 4M tokens, sequence length 4096, 8 Blackwell GPUs.
  - Appendix Tab. 8 adds GLA-7B, Gated-DeltaNet-1B and GSA-340M (downstream only). For GLA-7B, "CHON (61.3%) outperforms both FP8 (59.9%) and the BF16 baseline (59.3%)". The text I read does not give the token count for these runs.
  - Post-training: SFT of Qwen3-8B on 12.67B tokens, and GRPO RL on GSM8K.
- Overhead: end-to-end +12.88% (Qwen3-1.7B; 175.2 vs 152.72 GPU-hours) and +8.6% (GLA-1.3B; 92.88 vs 84.88). The fused HCP kernel overhead is 5.27% (post-fusion) and 16.15% (pre-fusion) (l.2745–2751, 1238).
- Downstream-table anomalies (Tab. 1, 40B tokens; worth knowing before citing "parity with BF16"):
  - Gated-DeltaNet-340M: the BF16 row averages 39.5 against NVFP4 53.2 and CHON 53.3 (BF16 ARC-E 41.1 vs NVFP4 64.2). The BF16 baseline looks broken or undertrained.
  - GSA-1B: the BF16 and FP8 rows are nearly identical, digit for digit.
  - Most CHON vs BF16 differences fall within the reported ±1–2 standard errors.
- Other claim: "Activations are the primary underflow bottleneck" (FTZ analysis, App. l.3612). This was noted in the first pass.

## 5. Limitations and future work (verbatim, l.1352–1364)

"Our analysis is validated on a limited set of model sizes and architectures; additional experiments on ultra-large-scale and MoE models are needed to confirm the generality of the observed outlier patterns. We primarily use practical diagnostics (e.g., kurtosis, top-k magnitude, and quantization error) ... In addition, this work focuses on numerical and algorithmic understanding rather than end-to-end NVFP4 training system efficiency ... Finally, our conclusions are largely based on empirical correlations observed during training; establishing stronger causal evidence for specific outlier sources remains an important direction for future work."

The Impact Statement adds future initiatives: "standardized outlier diagnostics" and "architecture-kernel co-design".

## 6. What the report must change

- Line 198 ("Dong et al.은 hot channel"): this is acceptable as an attribution of the bottleneck the paper names. To be precise, add that the paper's main evidence is on GLA-1.3B (linear attention) at 60B tokens with a cosine schedule, and that the source attribution is correlational by the authors' own Limitation.
- If the 0.94% to 0.58% figure is cited, three caveats apply:
  1. It is a single-run, 0.0077-nat difference.
  2. CHON also adds BF16 for Wo and gk_proj, so it is not an HCP-only effect.
  3. The gap is normalized by the quantized loss.
- Do not cite Tab. 1 "parity with BF16" without the Gated-DeltaNet-340M BF16 anomaly.
- Support for the report's sentence "같은 LR 지점에서 이들을 분리한 실험이 없다": this paper uses a cosine schedule and never varies the LR or schedule phase. Its "transient (steps 400–5,400) to persistent" timeline is confounded with LR warmup and decay. It therefore cannot separate hot-channel error from LR-dependent effects.
- Theorem A.12 should not be described as a guarantee for HCP training. It is a conditional MSE ordering on the patched channels, valid only under ∥∆X_I∥ ≪ ∥X_I∥ and the assumption of uncorrelated error terms.
