# 2510.06213 — Training Dynamics Impact Post-Training Quantization Robustness

Catalan-Tatjer, Ajroldi, Geiping. v1 2025-10-07, v2 2026-01-30. **Venue: ICLR 2026** (header on every page: "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2026"). Line numbers refer to `scratchpad/papers/2510.06213/2510.06213.txt` (untrusted data, quoted only).

First-pass verdicts (verify/batch_01.md, SUMMARY.md) already confirmed: ICLR 2026; "LR decay -> loss/quant error diverge"; that interventions (peak LR, WSD vs cosine, LAWA, weight decay, soups) and Hessian trace/sharpness measurements exist; and the 32B vs 15T conflation (OLMo2-32B at 4–6T tokens; Apertus-8B at 15T). This note adds exact statements, scope limits and new implications.

## Nature of the evidence
**No theorem, lemma or proposition.** Everything is empirical. The curvature mechanism is stated as a hypothesis (l.1212–1215): "Based on the observed phenomena, we hypothesize that, as the learning rate decays, the model traverses a sharper region of the loss landscape, making it more sensitive to perturbations such as quantization." Discussion (l.1240): interventions "coincide with convergence to flatter minima, which we argue might benefit quantization robustness." The only math is the Hutchinson identity E[zᵀAz] = tr(AΣ)+μᵀΣμ in App G (an estimator, not a result).

## Q1. Main claims (exact)
- Abstract: "once learning rates decay, validation loss and quantization error diverge, largely independent of training data scale." Also "quantization errors in large-scale training runs are driven by a complex interplay between learning rate and other training hyperparameters."
- Contributions (l.163–169): "Maintaining larger learning rates, all else being equal, reduces quantization error."
- Sec 3.1 SmolLM3 (l.325–328): quantization error "increases rapidly in the beginning of training, it stays relatively constant during the 11 trillion tokens of stable phase, and only as the learning rate decays does quantization error spike."
- Sec 3.1 OLMo2 (l.344–346): error is "increasing gradually during slow cosine decay, but rising sharply under steep linear annealing. Although the learning rate itself may not directly cause this degradation…". Model soup has "lower PTQ error than any of the individual ingredients."
- Sec 4.1 (l.533–537) caveat: "the constant learning rate stage is not immune to PTQ degradation, showing a slight increase in quantization error." Runs of 10B–100B tokens "achieve comparable quantization error after decay". Under **cosine** (Fig 21), "quantization robustness … vary with the training horizon". So the "independent of data scale" claim holds for WSD, not cosine.
- Sec 4.2 (l.556–560): prior works "did not sufficiently control for a key confounder, namely the optimization dynamics induced by the learning rate schedule, which we find to be the primary driver of their observed degradation." Fig 5 (Kumar replication, 70M and 160M): cosine δPTQ grows with tokens; WSD shows "slower growth at 70M and no increase at 160M".
- Metrics: Sec 3 uses relative CE, CE(Ŵ)/CE(W) − 1. Fig 5 uses absolute δPTQ := CE(Ŵ) − CE(W). Downstream (Sec 3.2) uses relative accuracy drop (Acc(W)−Acc(Ŵ))/(1−Acc(W)), averaged 5-shot over 12 benchmarks, on SmolLM3 only.
- SmolLM3 post-training stages (l.440–443): "context extension sensibly reduces quantization degradation, while mid-training largely amplifies it. PTQ degradation then decreases through SFT and APO." The 0.9/0.1 merged release has lower degradation than either parent.

## Q2. What was analysed
- Open trajectories (Sec 3, l.305–310): OLMo (1B, 7B; 2.5–3T tokens), OLMo2 (1B, 7B, 13B, 32B; 4–6T), SmolLM3 (3B, 11T), Apertus (8B, 15T), Open-sci (1.3B, 1T; Nemotron-cc release), Amber (7B, 1.3T). The intro says "six modern, open-source LLM training efforts". Max params is 32B (OLMo2) and max tokens is 15T (Apertus-8B); these are different models.
- PTQ: GPTQ 3- and 4-bit, group size 128, C4 calibration via GPTQModel defaults (App A, l.1700–1702). AWQ and LLM.int8() replicate only Fig 4 (160M, App A Fig 10). The paper notes two anomalies (App B, l.1711–1716). Amber's 4-bit degradation "rises sharply … whose cause we cannot identify". Apertus shows "very large, fluctuating quantization errors from the beginning, which may indicate numerical issues".
- Controlled runs (App C, l.2052–2058): Pythia-architecture 160M (plus 70M in Fig 5) on FineWebEdu up to 100B tokens. Seq 2048, 0.5M-token batches, AdamW wd 0.1, β=(0.9, 0.95), clip 1. WSD with peak LR 3e-3, 1% warmup, 10% cooldown to zero; cooldowns branched at 12, 28, 46, 64, 82B. LAWA: checkpoint every 500 steps, window 5.
- Larger replication (App E.4, l.2735–2739): OLMo2-7B, "a learning rate annealing run of 50B tokens after the model was pretrained for 250B tokens on 4 different learning rate values" (Fig 23). Larger LR gives lower degradation "even at the same validation loss". The report's "300B" is 250B + 50B.

## Q3. Sharpness / Hessian
- Sec 6.2 + App G: Hutchinson trace and power-iteration λmax (plus top-25 eigenvalues) via PyHessian. Measured **only on in-house Pythia-160M**, on validation loss over 100 held-out FineWebEdu sequences of length 2048. Nothing is measured at OLMo/SmolLM scale.
- Findings (l.874–879, Fig 9): "The maximum eigenvalue shows a consistent rapid surge whenever the learning rate decays". In the stable phase "only the top eigenvalue initially rises while the others remain small, whereas in the decay phase all eigenvalues increase". The trace remains "stable under a constant learning rate" and rises in decay. The Fig 9 caption says the trace "is confounded by being the sum of all eigenvalues".
- Peak-LR sweep (Fig 26): "larger ones produce lower sharpness … and smaller trace estimates … yet interestingly also leading to lower quantization error."
- LAWA vs LR decay (l.1216–1221): averaging gives wider minima. The two methods "produce solutions with substantially different curvature properties". That soups work the same way is only a belief ("We believe…").
- Loss-landscape slices (Sec 6.1, l.858–864): "the distance between ΘK and Θ̂K … is larger for larger learning rates … even though ΘK and Θ̂K are closer for smaller learning rates, the sharpness of the basin is such that Θ̂K falls in a higher loss level".
- What is not done: no quantitative fit of quant error to curvature (e.g. Δ²·tr(H)); no per-layer or per-group Hessian; no causal intervention on curvature at fixed LR; Hessian is not measured in the quantization direction specifically.

## Q4. Recommended interventions
- Use larger peak LR at matched FP loss (Sec 5.1: "employing a larger learning rate might be preferable").
- Prefer schedules that control the end-of-training LR (WSD) over cosine, whose tiny final LRs "may hurt quantization performance". Note the nuance in Fig 22: cosine with a higher peak still beat WSD on both quant error and validation loss.
- Use weight averaging (LAWA) along the trajectory or model soups. At 3-bit, LAWA "can match or even surpass" LR-decay checkpoints (Fig 7); it also helps OLMo-1B (Fig 24).
- Use larger decoupled weight decay at matched loss. This has a smaller effect than LR (Sec 5.3, Fig 19).
- Monitor PTQ during hyperparameter tuning.
- For quantized scaling laws, collect checkpoints only after LR annealing (Sec 4.2).

## Limitations (App H, verbatim)
"Our analysis focuses primarily on the effect of learning rate, schedules, and weight decay leaving other parts of the optimization pipeline unexplored. Factors such as optimizer choice may also affect quantization performance, and we leave the exploration of schedule-free methods (Defazio et al., 2024) to follow-up work. Moreover, although we limit our analysis to dense quadratic model, we expect similar conclusions for sparse … and sub-quadratic architectures".
Further limits implied by the text: weight-only PTQ (GPTQ, with AWQ/int8 checks), no activation/KV or FP4 formats, and no low-precision training. Curvature is measured only at 160M.

## Implications for the report
1. Lines 200 and 402 still say "32B/15T 궤적" and carry the [S] tag. Change to "최대 32B(OLMo2, 4–6T) / 최대 15T(Apertus-8B)" and retag [V], since the full text has been read.
2. Line 43 says it "Hessian trace·sharpness 측정까지 이미 수행". This is correct but overstates scope. Add: "160M 자체 학습 모델에서만, 상관적 관찰이며 저자 스스로 'we hypothesize'". The mechanism is not causally established. That gap is real headroom for Idea 4.
3. **New point for Idea 4's math (Δ² vs tr(H))**: Sec 6.1 reports that the FP-to-quantized distance ‖ΘK − Θ̂K‖ grows with LR, yet loss after quantization is lower. In PTQ with per-group absmax scales, Δ depends on weight ranges, which LR and WD change. So the decomposition must model Δ_g(η, λ) as a variable, not a format constant. Otherwise the predictions conflate the two factors.
4. **Conflict to flag on line 43**: Kumar App G (30M, cosine vs linear decay) reports the token-ratio degradation persists regardless of schedule. Catalan-Tatjer Fig 5 (70M/160M, WSD) shows slower growth or no growth. These are different model sizes and schedule families (linear decay vs WSD), so the report should present it as an open discrepancy, not a resolved one.
5. "Largely independent of training data scale" holds for WSD. Under cosine the paper itself shows horizon dependence (Fig 21), and there is a slight rise even at constant LR (Sec 4.1). The report should not cite it as "data scale does not matter".
6. Remaining novelty for Idea 4 (consistent with first pass): a quantitative per-group Δ_g²·tr(H_g) predictor; causal curvature interventions after decay at fixed LR; extension from PTQ to low-precision *training* gaps (NVFP4 decay-phase gap). The paper's evidence also suggests averaging as a cheap baseline that any re-flattening intervention must beat.
