## Q1. Approximation-error theorem — exact statement and assumptions

Problem (Eq. 1/3): min_{Q,L,R} ‖(Q + LR − W)Xᵀ‖²_F with Q ∈ 𝒬_Q^{n×d}, L ∈ 𝒬_L^{n×k}, R ∈ 𝒬_R^{k×d}. Alg. 1 alternates Q ← LDLQ(W − LR, Q_Q) and (L, R) ← LPLRFACTORIZE(W − Q, k, X, Q_L, Q_R, T_in), and **keeps the best iterate** ("if ‖(Q_{t+1}+L_{t+1}R_{t+1}−W)Xᵀ‖²_F < MinError").

Quantizer assumption used by the analysis (Sec. 3, Sec. 4, App. G.2): Q_Q, Q_L, Q_R "can refer to uniformly dithered scalar quantizers, as described in App. G.2". Definition (App. G.2, Eq. 59): B-bit uniform grid on [−R, R] with Δ = 2R/(2^B − 1); round up with probability r = (x − q_k)/Δ, else down (i.e. **stochastic rounding / non-subtractive dither**).
> "Lemma G.6. For scalar x with |x| ≤ R, denote the quantization error of uniformly dithered B–bit scalar quantizer as ϵ = Q_{R,B}(x) − x. Then, E[ϵ] = 0 and Var(ϵ) ≤ R²/(2^B − 1)²".
Dynamic range: "For convenience of analysis, it is assumed that the dynamic range of Q_Q, denoted as R, is chosen to be high enough, ensuring it remains unsaturated. Consequently, for a scalar input the quantization error from Q_Q has zero mean and bounded variance, given by Δ²/4 = R²/(2^{B_Q} − 1)²." (Sec. 4). For Q_L, Q_R the ranges are set explicitly (below); Lemmas C.1/C.2 bound the saturation probability, and the analysis assumes "if Q_L or Q_R gets saturated, a trivial output of L = 0, R = 0 is returned" (Sec. 4) — footnote 4: "In practice, it can be easily checked if Q_L was saturated, and if so, Alg. 1 is repeated with a fresh realization of the stochastic quantizer Q_R."

> **Theorem 4.1 (informal; formal = Theorem C.4, App. C.4).** "Given W ∈ R^{n×d} and X ∈ R^{m×d} with m ≤ d, let D be obtained from the LDL decomposition XᵀX = mH = (M + I)D(M + I)ᵀ, and λmax, λmin denote the max and min eigenvalues of H. Additionally, let Q ≜ LDLQ(W, Q_Q), where Q_Q has dynamic range R and bit-budget B_Q, the quantization error be η ≜ Q_Q(Q + (W − Q)M) − (Q + (W − Q)M), and σ1 ≥ … ≥ σk … be the singular values of X(W − Q)ᵀ."

Formal conditions (Theorem C.4 / Lemma C.3):
- **Rank:** (λmin^{1/2} / (4 m σ1 λmax^{3/2})) · Σ_{i>k} σi² ≤ k ≤ m.  (σi are singular values of the *residual after LDLQ*, X(W − Q)ᵀ — not of W or WXᵀ.)
- **Dynamic ranges:** R_L = 2σ1/(σk √(m λmin)), R_R = σ1.
- **ε:** "for some absolute constant C and arbitrarily small ϵ that satisfies 0 < ϵ ≤ 4mkλ²max λmin^{−1} σ1".
- **Bit budgets:** B_L ≥ log2( 4 (σ1²/σk) √(nk/ϵ · λmax/λmin) + 1 ); B_R ≥ max{B1, B2}, B1 := log2( 2σ1 √(kd/ϵ · λmax/λmin) + 1 ), B2 := log2( (4Cσ1/σk) √(8 Σ_i σi² / ϵ) (√d + √k + √log 2 ...) ) [B2 garbled in extraction; it contains a subgaussian-tail term from Vershynin Thm 4.4.5].
- **Implicit:** H positive definite (λmin > 0), m ≤ d (App. C.5: when H ≻ 0 rewrite with H^{1/2} so m = d).

Conclusion (formal, Theorem C.4):
E‖(Q + LR − W)Xᵀ‖²_F ≤ m Σ_{i>k} E λi(η D ηᵀ) + ϵ ≲ (4 m d λmax R² / (π (2^{B_Q} − 1)²)) · (1 − k/n)²-type factor + ϵ
(informal Thm 4.1 normalises by 1/(nm): (1/(nm)) E‖·‖² ≤ (1/n) Σ_{i>k} Eλi(ηDηᵀ) + ϵ ≲ (4 d λmax R² / (π (2^{B_Q} − 1)²)) (1 − k/(2n))² + ϵ [the exact exponent/argument of the (1 − k/·) factor is garbled; it is the Marchenko–Pastur tail integral]), "while utilizing an average budget of ½ log2( (kσ1³/(mϵσk)) (λmax/λmin) √(d/n) ) bits per parameter for the low-rank factors L and R, when n ≈ d. Here, the expectation is over the stochasticity of the quantizers."

Two distinct levels of rigor:
1. **"≤" part is a proven bound** (given the assumptions above): error ≤ tail eigenvalues (i > k) of the backbone LDLQ error's D-weighted Gram ηDηᵀ, plus ϵ from quantizing L, R. Proof bounds only the *first* outer iterate ("after the first iteration, the alternating steps … simply refine the estimates … and can only yield a smaller error"), which is valid because Alg. 1 returns the best iterate.
2. **"≲" part is a heuristic** (App. C.4): "As the exact error distribution for non-subtractively dithered quantization (which is commonly used in practice), is not fully understood, a simplification is made for easier analysis, assuming the quantizer is subtractively dithered. For subtractively dithered quantizers, if Schuchman's conditions are met, this results in a uniform error distribution … Therefore, assuming quantization errors are independent and identically distributed, for large dimensions d, the eigenvalues of (1/d) Σ_j η_j η_jᵀ are distributed according to the Marchenko–Pastur distribution". So the explicit closed form needs: subtractive dither (≠ the quantizer defined in G.2), i.i.d. errors (LDLQ feedback makes them dependent in general), and large-d MP asymptotics.

Comparison made by the paper (Sec. 4, Eq. 4): vanilla LDLQ (Chee et al., QuIP Thm 1) gives E‖(Q − W)Xᵀ‖² ≤ E Tr(ηDηᵀ) = Σ_{i=1}^n Eλi(ηDηᵀ); "Evidently, Alg. 1 yields a smaller error provided Σ_{i>k} Eλi(ηDηᵀ) < Σ_{i=1}^k Eλi(ηDηᵀ) − ϵ". I.e. the gain is "remove the top-k eigen-energy of the backbone's quantization error at the price of ϵ"; it is a comparison of upper bounds, not a separation.

**Theory–experiment gap (important):** every experiment uses QuIP#'s **E8 lattice** quantizer for Q (2 bits, BlockLDLQ) and also for L, R at 4 bits, plus RHT incoherence processing (Sec. 5 "Baselines"; App. E). The theorem is for **scalar uniformly dithered (stochastic) quantizers with a fixed dynamic range** and plain LDLQ. No result in the paper covers deterministic nearest-point lattice rounding.

Supporting lemmas: Lemma 4.2 / B.1 (closed-form global optimum of rank-constrained regression min_{rank ≤ k} ‖(Z − Y)… X‖ via two SVDs; used for initialization of LPLRFACTORIZE); Lemma C.1/C.2 (dynamic ranges of Q_R / Q_L that keep saturation probability low); Lemma C.3 (error of LPLRFACTORIZE: E‖(LR − A)Xᵀ‖² ≤ Σ_{i>k} σi²(XAᵀ) + ϵ under the same rank/range/bit conditions); App. G (Eckart–Young–Mirsky, Vershynin Thm 4.4.5, Loewner ordering, σmin perturbation, dither variance).

## Q2. Bits/param regime and numbers

Claim (abstract): "outperforms existing post-training LLM compression techniques in the regime of less than 2.5 bits per parameter." Conclusion/Sec. 5.4: "primarily motivated with the goal of closing the gap with respect to uncompressed models in the 2 to 2.5 bits per parameter regime". Budgets tested: 2.08–2.4 bits/param (Sec. 5.1: "Per-parameter bit budgets range from 2.1 (e.g., rank-64 factors in 4-bit precision) to 2.4 bits"). B_Q = 2 throughout.

Baselines actually compared: **only QuIP#** (with and without its own rank-64 BF16 Q+LR, "No FT", i.e. RHT fine-tuning omitted "for the sake of direct comparison") in zero-shot; in Sec. 5.2 a *reduced* QuIP# fine-tuning ("not as extensive as in [36] … only … end-to-end fine-tuning … sequence length of 512, as opposed to 4096 … As such, the QuIP# numbers reported here are different from [36]"); in Sec. 5.3 LoftQ and LQ-LoRA numbers taken from their papers. AQLM is cited in related work only; no AQLM/QTIP/PV-Tuning comparison. So "outperforms existing PTQ techniques" = outperforms (weakened) QuIP#.

Caveats: zero-shot accuracies are noisy (the paper itself: "not as directly indicative of quantization performance as the perplexity results"); the r256 2.4b rows win against a 2.0b QuIP# — at matched 2.4b the comparison is only against QuIP#'s own r64 BF16 factors. Gaps to FP16 remain large at 7B (6.19 vs 5.12) and LLaMA-3 (8.22 vs 5.54).

## Limitations (App. H "Limitations and Further Discussions", and Sec. 5.4)
- "Its effectiveness largely depends on the presence of an inherent approximate low-rank structure".
- Cost: "it requires slightly more computational resources" (34 / 59 / ~90 GPU-hours as above).
- "Although CALDERA often achieves perplexities and accuracies similar to unquantized models, a gap remains, as shown in Tables 1 and 2. This indicates there is potential for enhancing quantization strategies. For example, the target rank (k) can be treated as a hyper-parameter, and adjusted across different layers … improved fine-tuning strategies such as those proposed by Liu et al. [23] … can be incorporated … Detailed investigations are left for future work."
- Sec. 5.4: "throughput improvement using custom kernels, paged attention, etc., is left for future work."
- Overclaim in App. H: "Theoretical guarantees show improved performance over existing benchmarks, when the matrix being compressed is inherently low-rank" — the theory only compares upper bounds with QuIP's LDLQ bound, under the dithered-scalar assumption.

2. `05_theory.md` l.105 "[recalled, unverified]" bound — correct it: the proved bound is E‖(Q+LR−W)Xᵀ‖²_F ≤ m Σ_{i>k} Eλi(ηDηᵀ) + ϵ, i.e. the **tail eigenvalues of the backbone LDLQ error ηDηᵀ** (not tail singular values of WXᵀ), plus ϵ from quantizing L, R with bit budgets B_L, B_R that grow like log(1/ϵ) and with λmax/λmin, σ1/σk. The "∝ Δ²·dims" closed form is a separate Marchenko–Pastur heuristic that assumes *subtractive* dither and i.i.d. errors. The recalled assumption "stochastic (unbiased) quantization with a known dynamic range, not the deterministic nearest/LDLQ rounding used in practice" is CORRECT in spirit, but sharpen: the theorem *does* use LDLQ (with a dithered scalar quantizer); what it does not cover is the **E8 lattice** quantizer used in all experiments. Requires λmin(H) > 0, rank condition k ≥ (λmin^{1/2}/(4mσ1λmax^{3/2})) Σ_{i>k}σi², k ≤ m. Upgrade verdict from (B) to verified.
3. `05_theory.md` l.110 ("None of the papers states a lower bound") — consistent: CALDERA's Eq. 4 comparison is between upper bounds only.
4. `02_ptq_weight_quantization.md` l.268/313: ID 2405.18886 now verified (drop "[ID from memory]"); venue NeurIPS 2024 confirmed by arXiv comment.
