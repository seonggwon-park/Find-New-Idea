## 1. Unbiasedness statement — what is proved vs what is implemented
- EDEN background (Eq. 1, l.430-450): S = <x,x> / <RHT(x,ω), Q(RHT(x,ω))>, Q_EDEN = S·Q(RHT(x,ω)); "the EDEN authors show that ... lim_{d→∞} E_ω RHT^{-1}(Q_EDEN(x,ω),ω) = x, i.e., Q_EDEN yields unbiased estimates in rotated space. In practice, this sequence converges fast enough to be unbiased with RHT performed in groups as small as d = 64."
- Stated assumptions (l.510-516): "the quantizer Q needs to satisfy a number of properties for EDEN to be unbiased, such as i) sign-symmetry and ii) Q(x) ≠ 0. These properties hold for non-under-flowing floating point quantization ... Based on Theorem 2.1 of Vargaftik et al. (2022) and the properties of stochastic rounding, the following hold:"
- **Corollary 3.1 (l.537-541):** "For all x ∈ R^d and scale s ≠ 0, we have: x̂ = Q_MS-EDEN(x, ω_RHT, ω_SR, s), E_{ω_RHT, ω_SR} RHT^{-1}(x̂, ω_RHT) = x." No proof is given in the paper (no "Proof" anywhere); it is asserted as a consequence of EDEN Thm 2.1 + SR unbiasedness on the FP8 scales.
- **Gap between the corollary and its own premise:** the corollary is stated as exact, for all d, while the paper's own restatement of EDEN for RHT is only a d→∞ limit. Appendix A (l.1535-1548) concedes this: "Even though EDEN (Vargaftik et al., 2022) only guarantees unbiasedness in the d → ∞ limit and requires rotations to be sampled independently, in practice we make a number of compromises": (1) d = 128; (2) "identical rotations for every rotation group within a tensor for every micro-batch"; (3) no SR on underflowing FP8 scales (claimed negligible, ≥~32000x below the max scale). Additionally the correction S is computed per 16-element NVFP4 group, not per rotation group; the justification is an informal "two-level" argument (rotate in 16, mix in 128), not a proof. Clipping is allowed in Q_RTN (s = 0.93·6·16/17 is clipping).
- So: unbiasedness of the implemented Quartet II is **empirically verified, not proved**: Fig. 9 (App. A) shows the relative error of the B-sample average gradient decays ~1/B on Llama-3.2-1B, attention block 0; NVIDIA and TetraJet-v2 also decay ~1/B (unbiased), NVIDIA+4/6 plateaus (biased).
- No variance bound or convergence theorem for training is given; the "analytic" support for "better gradient estimation across all GEMMs" is Gaussian MSE (Table 1) plus Fig. 1 GEMM-level error plots.

## 2. Error comparison vs element-wise SR (the "9.8 vs 17.5" question)
- Table 1 (l.340-412; MSE ×1e-3 over N(0,1)): RTN 1x16 9.0; RTN+4/6 1x16 7.6; RTN 16x16 12.4; RTN+4/6 16x16 12.4; SR 1x16 23.5; SR+4/6 1x16 17.5; **MS-EDEN 1x16 9.4**.
- **There is no 9.8 in the paper.** MS-EDEN is 9.4. The ">2x lower than SR" claim is 23.5/9.4 = 2.5x against plain SR. Against SR+4/6 (17.5) it is 1.86x, and the paper itself excludes SR+4/6 from backward comparisons because it is biased (l.588-593). The correct unbiased-vs-unbiased pair is 9.4 vs 23.5. Note also MS-EDEN (9.4) is slightly *worse* than biased RTN (9.0): unbiasedness costs ~4% MSE instead of ~160%.
- Text (l.566-575): "SR achieves unbiasedness at the cost of approximately 2.5x increase in MSE over RTN. At the same time, MS-EDEN shows much smaller error increase, improving by more than 2x over SR ... (c) stochastic rounding for the 8-bit scales introduces variance an order of magnitude smaller than 4-bit quantization itself." (c) is asserted, not measured separately in a table.
- Price: MS-EDEN needs RHT along the inner GEMM dim and re-quantization of W and X in the backward pass (l.549-554).

## 3. Fig. 4 loss gaps and speedups
- Fig. 4 (l.849-866): fully-NVFP4 C4 validation loss increase vs BF16 for N = 30M/50M/100M/200M Llama-2-like, D/N ∈ {25,50,100,200,400,800} (l.680-684), baselines NVIDIA, 4/6, TetraJet-v2. **Plot only; no table of values in the paper.** Y axis spans 0.02–0.12. Only text claim: "Quartet II improves consistently w.r.t. both isolated ablations and prior schemes, by at least 20% in terms of loss" (l.866-867). Per-point gaps cannot be quoted from the text; any specific Fig. 4 number in the report would be a read-off from the plot. Seeds/CI for Fig. 4 not stated.
- Nanochat, Table 5 (App. C, l.1660-1745), val BPB increase over BF16:
  - 560M / 11B tok: BF16 0.7693; NVIDIA 1.56%; 4/6 1.51%; TetraJet-v2 1.72%; Quartet II 1.26% (CI ±0.08%). Downstream CORE: BF16 23.5, NVIDIA 21.3, 4/6 20.9, TJ-v2 22.5, QII 21.9 (±0.6) — Quartet II is not best on CORE.
  - 1.9B / 38B tok: BF16 0.6925; NVIDIA 1.92%; 4/6 1.76%; TJ-v2 1.72%; Quartet II 1.44% (±0.02%). Relative gap reduction 16% (vs TJ-v2) to 25% (vs NVIDIA) — the "15-25%" of l.890-892.
- Speedups (Sec 7, l.1175-1195; Fig. 6): linear-layer fwd+bwd vs BF16, mean ± 2std of 10 runs. RTX 5090 (theoretical 8x): ">4x ... for large sizes, and still more than 4x even for small sizes"; abstract's "up to 4.2x" is this RTX 5090 linear-layer number. B200 (theoretical 4x): "smaller matrix sizes are entirely dominated by the quantization overhead, and we see actual speedups only starting at 3B, and get up to 2.5x for 22B". End-to-end (App. D.2, l.1845-1850): single RTX 5090, nanochat depth 26 (1.1B), batch 4: BF16 28 ktok/s vs NVFP4 52 ktok/s = 1.85x; "about 60% of the time is spent on operations untouched by the FP4 training recipe". No end-to-end B200 number.

## Paper's own limitations (Sec 8, l.1158-1173, close quote)
"Even though we see the main contribution of this paper in the application of advanced debiasing techniques to the backward pass ... the experimental verification focused on a relatively narrower problem of pre-training LLMs in the NVFP4 format, which currently is only supported on a single generation of NVIDIA accelerators. From basic statistical properties (i.e., measurably lower MSE on Gaussian source), we expect the approach to generalize to other problems and model architectures too, but doing that might require additional hyper-parameter tuning, such as randomized rotation temporal and spacial granularity." Also (l.577-580): "Micro-scaling groups have to be subdivisions of rotation groups ... rotations and scale corrections have to be applied on the inner dimension of multiplied tensors."
