## 0. What kind of paper this is

A systems/kernel paper. There is **no theorem, lemma, proposition, or proof anywhere** (grep for theorem/lemma/proposition/corollary/proof returns only a reference-list hit). The only "exactness" statement is an algebraic identity in Appendix D: "The scale relocation P (S_V ⊙ V_q) = (P ⊙ S_V) V_q and the online state rescaling are algebraically exact up to finite-precision rounding; approximation is introduced by FP8 quantization of the content cache and fused probability blocks." Everything about quantization sensitivity is **empirical, on one model (LongCat-Flash-Thinking), one format (FP8 E4M3), and one scale granularity (per-token)**.

The three components (Section 3): (3.1) RoPE-Aware Per-Token KV Quantization; (3.2) Quantized PV Computation Pipeline Reconstruction (scale fusion P' = P ⊙ S_V, block-wise dynamic P quantization with BlockN = 64, implicit dequantization in the online-softmax state); (3.3) End-to-End Dataflow Optimization (fused Q-quant / K-append / fetch-dequant kernels, 64→128 content tiling, in-kernel V transposition).

## 1. Q1 — Exact claim that the decoupled RoPE part needs high precision, and the evidence

### 1a. The claim, as stated

Section 2 ("Challenges of MLA Quantization"), bullet list — this is an **assertion in the preliminaries, not a derived result**:
> "• Compressed Latent Vector c_KV ∈ R^{d_c}: A dense, low-dimensional vector containing contextual content information.
> • Decoupled RoPE k^R ∈ R^{d_h^R}: A position-sensitive vector that requires high precision to effectively preserve positional information.
> The functional heterogeneity between these components introduces numerical heterogeneity, leading to differences in quantization sensitivity—an issue not extensively explored in prior work."

Introduction, contribution 2 (the paper itself frames it as empirical):
> "Building on a comprehensive analysis of MLA KV cache numerical and quantization errors, we empirically identify, to the best of our knowledge for the first time, the divergent quantization sensitivity between the latent content and decoupled RoPE components in the MLA KV cache. We propose RoPE-Aware Per-Token Quantization, along with supporting system optimizations, to ensure the preservation of the critical RoPE part from both algorithmic and system-level perspectives."

Section 3.1.1 ("Heterogeneous Quantization Sensitivity") — the design decision:
> "To address these issues, we propose a RoPE-aware strategy that applies FP8 quantization exclusively to the content components, while retaining the RoPE components in BF16 to preserve positional robustness. Although this quantization scheme is straightforward, it is firmly grounded in analysis and plays a pivotal role in preserving accuracy while optimizing KV cache compression rates."

Appendix A (Limitations) restates it as "higher precision", Appendix B as "incompatible with a uniform FP8 data path":
> "Specifically, MLA's decoupled RoPE component imposes heterogeneous precision requirements that are incompatible with a uniform FP8 data path. SnapMLA addresses this challenge by preserving the RoPE component in higher precision while quantizing the latent content path."

### 1b. Evidence item 1 — value distribution and token-wise MSE (Section 3.1.1, Figure 3, LongCat-Flash-Thinking)

> "To this end, we conduct an extensive analysis of the content and RoPE components of the MLA KV cache, based on LongCat-Flash-Thinking [7]. Our empirical findings uncover a pronounced divergence in the distributional characteristics of these two parts. As shown in Figure 3a, the RoPE component spans a significantly wider dynamic range (reaching ±10^3), exhibiting distinct outlier tails, while the content component is tightly concentrated around zero (within ±10^1), highlighting a stark contrast in their statistical behavior.
> This scale mismatch renders uniform quantization strategies ineffective. Specifically, FP8 quantization leads to an order-of-magnitude increase in Mean Squared Error (MSE) for the RoPE component (as shown in Figure 3b), highlighting their heightened sensitivity to quantization."

Figure 3 caption: "Analysis of the numerical value distribution and quantization error comparison for the content and RoPE components of MLA KV cache in LongCat-Flash-Thinking." Panel (b) axis labels in the extraction: "Token-Wise FP8 Quantiation Error", legend "With RoPE (Combined)" vs "Without RoPE (Content Only)", y = "MSE (Log Scale)", x = "Token ID (Sequence Index)" 0–35000. So (b) compares the per-token MSE of quantizing [content; RoPE] jointly vs content alone; the exact MSE values are not in the text.

Critical reading: this is an **absmax-dynamic-range argument**. With one E4M3 scale per token (max representable 448; Algorithm 1 uses σ = max/448.0), a ±10^3 outlier tail in the 64 RoPE dims sets the scale and crushes the resolution of everything else on that token. That is a property of **per-token scaling**, not a demonstration that positional information is intrinsically fragile. Nothing in the paper tests RoPE under finer group scaling (e.g., 16/32-element groups), which is exactly what FlashMLA's FP8/FP4 formats use.

Settings: "we performed a layer-wise numerical analysis using real inference data from LongCat-Flash-Thinking. We compare the attention outputs from SnapMLA with the BF16 ground truth, evaluating the discrepancies using three metrics: Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Cosine Difference (1 − cosine similarity), and Relative L2 Error, across all layers." Result (Appendix G, "Analysis"):
> "First, including RoPE term in the quantization process (Config A) results in a substantial increase in error metrics, particularly in the deeper layers. This "error explosion" empirically demonstrates the sensitivity of positional embeddings, underscoring the necessity of our RoPE-aware quantization. Second, as highlighted in the zoomed-in insets, coarse-grained quantization strategies (Config B&C) struggle to capture the dynamic range variations across tokens. Even the block-wise strategy (Config D) exhibits slightly higher degradation compared to our approach. In contrast, our method, which utilizes RoPE-Aware Per-token quantization, consistently delivers the lowest error rates across all metrics, maintaining numerical accuracy on par with the BF16 baseline."

Numeric magnitudes exist only as log-scale axes in the figure (RMSE axis ticks 10^-4..10^-2; cosine distance 10^-5..10^-1; relative L2 10^-2..10^-1); no numbers in prose.

Appendix J ("Evidence for Design Choices") says this is the strongest evidence they have:
> "RoPE-Aware Per-Token Quantization Appendix G provides the most direct evidence for this design choice. ... The results show that quantizing the RoPE component or using coarser content granularity leads to larger attention-output errors, supporting the choice of retaining the RoPE part in higher precision while applying per-token FP8 quantization to the content part."

### 1d. What is NOT in the paper (for Q1)

- No theorem or error-propagation analysis of why RoPE is sensitive (no use of the rotation structure, no bound through the logits).
- **No benchmark-level (Table 1) ablation of a RoPE-quantized variant.** Table 1 only compares BF16 FlashMLA vs SnapMLA. The "RoPE must stay high precision" claim is supported by kernel-output error metrics (Figure 5) and per-token MSE (Figure 3b), never by a downstream accuracy number.
- Only FP8 E4M3; only per-token (or coarser) scaling; only LongCat-Flash-Thinking for the sensitivity analysis.
- The authors explicitly disclaim factorial ablation (Section 4.3 and Appendix J, quoted in §3 below).

## 2. Q2 — Quantization granularity for the latent, and the numbers

### 2a. Format and granularity

- Format: Section 4.1: "For the FP8 experiments, we adopt the E4M3 format for efficient KV cache and MLA computation." Appendix A: "SnapMLA employs FP8 E4M3 quantization for the content portion of the MLA KV cache while preserving the RoPE component at higher precision."
- Granularity for the latent c_KV: **per-token (one scale per token over the whole content vector)**. Algorithm 1: "Require: Cached K = [K_c, K_r] ∈ R^{N×d} in HBM, with K_c ∈ R^{N×d_c} (FP8) and K_r ∈ R^{N×d_r}; in absorbed MLA, the PV value operand reuses the latent content cache, denoted V_q ≡ K_c. Require: Fp8 scaling factors σ_q ∈ R (for q_c) and σ_K ∈ R^{N×1} (for K_c) in HBM. Require: σ_q and σ_K are pre-injected into q_r and K_r." Query content gets a single scalar scale σ_q. The scale's storage dtype is not stated.
- Dimensions are never written as numbers; they are implied by Section 3.1.2: FlashMLA "partitioning the QK GEMM reduction dimension into nine thread groups (each spanning 64 dimensions) ... the first eight groups (content components) use FP8 and require dequantization, while the ninth group (RoPE component) retains BF16 precision." I.e. 8×64 = 512 content dims FP8 + 64 RoPE dims BF16 (= DeepSeek d_c = 512, d_h^R = 64).
- Bytes/token: **not stated**. My arithmetic: 512 B (E4M3) + 128 B (BF16 RoPE) + scale (4 B if FP32) ≈ 644 B/token vs 1152 B/token BF16 → ≈1.79× smaller. Compute cost ratio stated in Appendix H: "sixteen FP8 tiles (content term) and one BF16 tile (RoPE term), with the equivalent computational cost in BF16 units reduced from 17 to 16/2 + 1 = 9", Eq. (14): Peak_effective = 148 × 17/9 ≈ 279.6 TFLOPS.
- RoPE handling, Eq. (6), Section 3.1.2: Q_R ← Q_R / S^{Q_c}, K_R ← K_R / S^{K_c} — "we pre-scale the RoPE terms by the inverse of the content quantization scale ... effectively "disguising" the high-precision RoPE segments as scaled quantized values." RoPE stays BF16 in memory and in the GEMM.
- Why per-token (Section 3.1.1, "Decoding-Centric Quantization Granularity for Accuracy Preservation") — the stated reasons are **engineering**, not accuracy:
  > "While approaches like FA3 [14] demonstrate effective FP8 quantization during the prefill stage through block-wise quantization, this method encounters limitations in autoregressive decoding. In the decoding stage, tokens are generated sequentially, leading to "page tail" tokens within incomplete KV quantization blocks. This requires complex buffer management, which renders block-level quantization inefficient. To address this, we adopt per-token quantization granularity, offering two primary advantages: • Instant quantization, which enables immediate quantization of newly generated KV tokens and eliminates the need for "tail buffer" management. • Framework compatibility, which allows processing all tokens with unified logic, thereby simplifying integration into inference frameworks (e.g., vLLM, SGLang)."
  Note "per-block" in this paper (Appendix C, Eq. 10) means B×B tiles across tokens AND channels ("e.g., 128 × 128 or 64 × 64"), not per-token sub-groups along the channel axis. The paper never evaluates channel-group scaling (FlashMLA-style 16/32-element groups) at all.
- Downstream consequence of per-token latent scales (Section 2 / 3.2.1): "V inherits per-token quantization scales from the latent cache (c_KV). These scales are aligned with the GEMM's reduction dimension, resulting in a fundamental architectural conflict. Consequently, the conventional post-GEMM dequantization paradigm fails to work as intended." Fix: S_V fused into P, P' = P ⊙ S_V, then "block-wise dynamic quantization to P' ... The block size is set to the PV GEMM kernel's tiling parameter (BlockN = 64)"; Algorithm 1 line 7: σ_p^cur = m_cur / 448.0, p'_j = Quant(p_j, σ_p^cur). Appendix E: WG1's inverted execution order (P1V1 before P0V0) had to be abandoned for a monotonic order because rescaling an already-quantized FP8 P block by σ_P1/σ_P0 "disrupts its value distribution, leading to irreversible precision loss."

## 3. Limitations and future work — paper's own words

Appendix A ("Limitations"), verbatim:
> "SnapMLA is optimized for MLA decoding on NVIDIA Hopper-class GPUs, leveraging FP8 Tensor Cores, WGMMA, TMA, and Hopper-specific memory layouts. While these hardware-aware design principles are generally applicable, the reported kernel-level speedups are specific to this execution substrate and would require architecture-specific adaptations for earlier GPUs or alternative accelerators.
> In addition, SnapMLA employs FP8 E4M3 quantization for the content portion of the MLA KV cache while preserving the RoPE component at higher precision. Our numerical analyses and benchmark results validate this mixed-precision strategy for the evaluated decoding workloads. Exploring more aggressive precision formats, alternative calibration policies, or substantially longer context lengths remains a promising direction for future investigation."

Appendix J, verbatim (ablation disclaimer):
> "SnapMLA is designed and evaluated as an integrated decoding pipeline rather than as a collection of independently removable optimizations. ... As a result, removing one component can produce an invalid kernel, a substantially different data path, or an unfair comparison rather than a clean ablation point. We therefore interpret the current evaluation as evidence for the integrated co-design, not as a complete factorial ablation of every kernel subcomponent."

Section 4.1, footnote 2: "Due to certain restrictions, the specific type of the Hopper series GPU used cannot be disclosed".

## 4. Implications for the report (`reports/LLM 양자화 연구 아이디어 10선.md`, line 351)

Current text: "SnapMLA는 위치 부분을 고정밀로 두라고 주장 ([2602.10718] [S]) — 상반된다." (contrasted with FlashMLA's FP8/FP4 formats that quantize the 64 RoPE dims).

1. Tag [S] → [V]; the paper exists and the claim is real, but it must be qualified: SnapMLA keeps the 64 RoPE dims in **BF16** and quantizes only the 512-dim latent to **FP8 E4M3 with one scale per token**. The RoPE claim is **empirical** (Fig. 3: RoPE range ±10^3 with outlier tails vs content ±10^1; token-wise MSE "order-of-magnitude" higher when RoPE is included; Fig. 5 / Table 3 Config A: layer-wise attention-output "error explosion" at 32k on LongCat-Flash-Thinking). There is **no theorem and no benchmark-level ablation** of a RoPE-quantized variant; the authors themselves say the evaluation is "not a complete factorial ablation".
2. "상반된다" overstates the conflict. SnapMLA's evidence is confined to per-token (and coarser) scaling, where a single scale per token is set by the RoPE outlier tail; FlashMLA's formats use 32-element (e8m0) / 16-element (e4m3) channel groups, which give the RoPE dims their own scales. The two sources test different granularities, so neither settles whether RoPE needs high precision under fine-grained scaling. Rewrite as: granularity-confounded; open question.
3. Line 352 ("MLA 양자화 민감도 데이터는 … 뿐이다") should add SnapMLA Table 1 as FP8 latent-quantization accuracy data on DeepSeek-V3.1 and LongCat-Flash-Thinking (e.g., AIME-25 87.92→85.42, GPQA-Diamond 84.15→82.57 on V3.1; LCB 79.40→79.74 on LongCat), noting that even FP8-on-latent-only shows 1.5–2.5-point drops on some reasoning benchmarks without CIs — relevant as a ceiling for any sub-8-bit latent proposal.
4. For the report's own idea (weighted-MSE transform coding of the latent folded into W_UK/W_UV/W_O): SnapMLA is consistent with the sweep note "no theory of latent quantization under weight absorption" — it offers none. Two hardware constraints from SnapMLA bear on the idea: (a) per-token latent scales sit along the PV reduction dimension, forcing the P ⊙ S_V scale-fusion trick (Section 3.2) — any new per-token scheme inherits this; (b) non-uniform bit allocation across latent directions (reverse water-filling) would break the uniform FP8 WGMMA data path that SnapMLA relies on; the report should state that per-direction bit allocation needs either a grouped-format kernel or must be reduced to a uniform-bit transform + per-group scales.
5. SnapMLA's Config B/C/D results (per-tensor static/dynamic clearly worse; per-block "slightly" worse than per-token, Appendix G) are the only within-paper granularity evidence for the latent; the report can cite them as supporting per-token or finer scales for c_KV, but only at FP8 and only as kernel-output error, not accuracy.
