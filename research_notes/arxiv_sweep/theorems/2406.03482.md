## 1. Q1 — Exact estimator (Definition 3.1, Eq. (3)–(4), Section 3)

> "Definition 3.1 (QJL and inner product estimator). For any positive integers d, m, let S ∈ R^{m×d} be a JL transform matrix, i.e., entries of S are i.i.d. samples from the zero mean and unit variance Normal distribution. The QJL is a mapping function H_S : R^d → {−1, +1}^m defined as:
>   H_S(k) := sign(Sk) for any k ∈ R^d.   (3)
> Furthermore, for any pair of vectors k, q ∈ R^d the estimator for their inner product ⟨q, k⟩ based on the aforementioned quantizer is defined as:
>   Prod_QJL(q, k) := (√(π/2) / m) · ‖k‖₂ · ⟨Sq, H_S(k)⟩.   (4)"

Reconstructed exactly: Prod_QJL(q,k) = (√(π/2)/m) · ‖k‖₂ · Σ_{i=1}^m (s_iᵀq) · sign(s_iᵀk), s_i the rows of S.

So the constant is √(π/2) (≈ 1.2533), divided by m, multiplied by the key norm ‖k‖₂. The query q is projected by the SAME S but NOT quantized ("asymmetric"). The paper explains why asymmetry is necessary (Section 3, after Def. 3.1):
> "if we applied QJL to both vectors q and k in Equation (4), we would obtain an unbiased estimator for the angle between these vectors, as shown in [6]. However, to estimate the inner product one needs to apply the cosine function on top of the angle estimator, which results in a biased estimation. Thus, to achieve an unbiased inner product estimator, it is necessary to asymmetrically apply quantization to the JL transform of only one of the vectors q and k."

Algorithm 1 (QJL Key Cache Quantizer) restates it operationally:
> "1: Draw a random sketch S ∈ R^{m×d} with i.i.d. entries S_{i,j} ∼ N(0,1) as per Definition 3.1
> 3: Compute k̃_i ← sign(Sk_i) and ν_i ← ‖k_i‖₂
> 4: store the quantized vector k̃_i and the key norm ν_i in the cache
> 6: Compute inner product estimators q̃K(j) ← (√(π/2)/m) · ν_j · ⟨Sq_n, k̃_j⟩ for every j ∈ [n]
> 7: S̃core ← softmax(q̃K)"
(Line 6 in the paper has a typo "ν_i" where ν_j is meant.)

### Lemma 3.2 (unbiasedness), Section 3
> "Lemma 3.2 (Inner product estimator Prod_QJL is unbiased). For any vectors q, k ∈ R^d the expected value of the estimator Prod_QJL(q, k) defined in Equation (4) is: E_S[Prod_QJL(q,k)] = ⟨q, k⟩, where the expectation is over the randomness of the JL matrix S in Definition 3.1."

Proof mechanism (paper, Section 3): decompose q = (⟨q,k⟩/‖k‖²)k + q^{⊥k}; then x := s₁ᵀk ~ N(0,‖k‖²) and y := s₁ᵀq^{⊥k} are independent (Fact 3.3, Gaussian with ⟨q^{⊥k},k⟩=0), so E[y·sign(x)] = 0, and E[Prod_QJL] = √(π/2)·(⟨q,k⟩/‖k‖₂)·E|x| = ⟨q,k⟩ because E|x| = ‖k‖₂·√(2/π) (Fact 3.4: E|x|^ℓ = σ^ℓ · 2^{ℓ/2} Γ((ℓ+1)/2)/√π). That is where √(π/2) comes from: it cancels E|N(0,1)| = √(2/π).

The "folded Gaussian" structure makes the unbiasedness exact for every fixed (q,k), with NO assumption on q, k beyond being in R^d (k ≠ 0 implicitly).

## 2. Q2 — Distortion bound (Lemma 3.5) and per-coordinate moments (Eq. (5))

> "Lemma 3.5 (Distortion of inner product estimator Prod_QJL). For any vectors q, k ∈ R^d if the estimator Prod_QJL(q,k) is defined as in Equation (4) for QJL with dimension m ≥ (4/3) · ((1+ε)/ε²) · log(2/δ), then:
>   Pr_S[ |Prod_QJL(q,k) − ⟨q,k⟩| > ε‖q‖₂‖k‖₂ ] ≤ δ,
> where the probability is over the randomness of the JL matrix S in Definition 3.1."
(The text extraction renders 4/3 as "43"; the proof's exponent 3/4 confirms 4/3.)

Proof ingredients (Section 3), quoted:
> "the above is indeed the average of m i.i.d. estimators defined as z_i := √(π/2) · ‖k‖₂ · s_iᵀq · sign(s_iᵀk) for i ∈ [m]. Let us now calculate the ℓ-th moment of z_i using Fact 3.4:
>   E|z_i|^ℓ = (π/2)^{ℓ/2} · ‖k‖₂^ℓ · E|s_iᵀq|^ℓ = (√π · ‖k‖₂‖q‖₂)^ℓ · Γ((ℓ+1)/2)/√π,   (5)"
> "E|z_i|^ℓ = E|z_i|² · (√π‖k‖₂‖q‖₂)^{ℓ−2} · Γ((ℓ+1)/2)/Γ(3/2) ≤ E|z_i|² · ((2/3)‖k‖₂‖q‖₂)^{ℓ−2} · ℓ!/2"
> "by invoking a proper version of the Bernstein inequality, for instance Corollary 2.11 from [5], we have the following:
>   Pr_S[ |Prod_QJL(q,k) − ⟨q,k⟩| > t ] ≤ 2 exp( −(3/4) · m t² / (‖k‖₂²‖q‖₂² + ‖k‖₂‖q‖₂ · t) ).
> If we set t = ε‖q‖₂‖k‖₂ the above simplifies to: ... ≤ 2 exp( −(3/4) · mε²/(1+ε) ). Therefore if m ≥ (4/3)·((1+ε)/ε²)·log(2/δ) the error bound follows."

Then the paper's interpretive claim (Section 3, after Lemma 3.5):
> "Note that the distortion bound in Lemma 3.5 has remarkably small constants, even smaller than those of the original unquintized JL transform. This indicates that quantizing one of the vectors to just a single sign bit does not result in any loss of accuracy."
This is a statement about tail-bound constants, not about variance; it is not accompanied by a side-by-side JL bound in the paper.

### Dependence on m
Additive error scales as ‖q‖‖k‖/√m: m = Θ(ε⁻² log(1/δ)) for relative-to-‖q‖‖k‖ error ε. Nothing about d appears (the sketch dimension is independent of the head dimension d). The bound is sub-exponential (Bernstein), not sub-Gaussian, because z_i = (√(π/2)‖k‖)·(Gaussian)·(sign) has Gaussian tails but the variance proxy used is the second moment.

### Variance — NOT stated in the paper, derived here from Eq. (5) with ℓ = 2
E[z_i²] = (π/2)·‖k‖²·‖q‖² (Eq. (5) at ℓ=2: (√π‖k‖‖q‖)²·Γ(3/2)/√π = π‖k‖²‖q‖²·(1/2) ). With E[z_i] = ⟨q,k⟩ (Lemma 3.2):
  Var[Prod_QJL(q,k)] = (1/m) · [ (π/2)‖q‖²‖k‖² − ⟨q,k⟩² ] = (‖q‖²‖k‖²/m) · (π/2 − cos²θ_{qk}).
Compare the unquantized JL estimator (1/m)⟨Sq,Sk⟩ (Isserlis): Var = (‖q‖²‖k‖²/m)·(1 + cos²θ).
Ratio QJL/JL = (π/2 − cos²θ)/(1 + cos²θ): equals π/2 ≈ 1.571 only when q ⊥ k; equals (π/2−1)/2 ≈ 0.285 when q ∥ k; crosses 1 at |cos θ| = √((π/2−1)/2) ≈ 0.534. So the sign quantization costs at most a factor π/2 in variance (orthogonal case) and is strictly BETTER than unquantized JL for well-aligned pairs. This is my derivation from the paper's Eq. (5); the paper never writes the variance explicitly.

Key consequence for the report (not in the paper): the logit noise is heteroscedastic across cached tokens j: Var[q̃K(j)] = (‖q‖²‖k_j‖²/m)(π/2 − cos²θ_j). Noise variance grows with ‖k_j‖² and shrinks for keys aligned with the query.

### Caveat on the Bernstein constant (my check, not a paper statement)
With variance proxy v = E[z_i²] = (π/2)‖q‖²‖k‖² and c = (2/3)‖q‖‖k‖, the standard Bernstein form 2exp(−mt²/(2(v+ct))) gives 2exp(−mt²/(π‖q‖²‖k‖² + (4/3)‖q‖‖k‖t)), which is WEAKER than the paper's displayed 2exp(−(3/4)mt²/(‖q‖²‖k‖² + ‖q‖‖k‖t)) because π > 4/3. The extraction is garbled, so I could not reconcile the exact 3/4; treat the order m = Θ(ε⁻² log(1/δ)) as solid and the constant 4/3 as the paper's claim, not independently verified.

## 3. Theorem 3.6 — attention-score distortion (Section 3.1)

> "Theorem 3.6 (Distortion bound on QJL key cache quantizer). For any sequence of key tokens k₁, … k_n ∈ R^d and any integer m, Algorithm 1 stores binary vectors k̃₁, … k̃_n ∈ {−1,+1}^m along with scalar values ν₁, … ν_n in the cache. If the key embeddings have bounded norm max_{i∈[n]} ‖k_i‖₂ ≤ r and m ≥ 2r²ε⁻² log n, then for any query embedding q_n ∈ R^d with bounded norm ‖q_n‖₂ ≤ r the output of the procedure EstimateScores(q_n) satisfies the following with probability 1 − 1/poly(n) simultaneously for all i ∈ [n]:
>   |S̃core(i) − Score(i)| ≤ 3ε · Score(i),
> where Score is the vector of attention scores defined in Equation (2)."

Proof (quoted, Section 3.1): "by Lemma 3.5 we have the following with probability at least 1 − 1/n^{3/(2+2ε)}: |q̃K(j) − ⟨q_n,k_j⟩| ≤ (ε/r²)·‖q_n‖₂‖k_j‖₂ ≤ ε … By union bound, the above inequality holds simultaneously for all j ∈ [n] with high probability in n. Thus after applying the softmax function in line 7 of Algorithm 1 we get that with high probability in n: S̃core(i) ∈ e^{±2ε} · Score(i) ∈ (1 ± 3ε) · Score(i)."

Interpretation stated by the paper (Section 3.1):
> "This theorem shows that if the query and key embeddings have constant norms, as is common in practical scenarios, we can quantize each key embedding such that only m ≈ ε⁻² log n bits are needed to store each key token. This is independent of the embedding dimension of the tokens and scales only logarithmically with the sequence length."

Caveats (mine):
- The displayed probability 1 − n^{−3/(2+2ε)} follows from Lemma 3.5 with ε' = ε/r² and m = 2r²ε⁻² log n ONLY if r = 1. For general r the same computation gives failure probability 2·n^{−3/(2r²+2ε)}, which is "1 − 1/poly(n)" only for constant r and is vacuous for the norm magnitudes seen in real key caches. To get an r-free polynomial exponent one needs m = Θ(r⁴ε⁻² log n), i.e. m ∝ (‖q‖‖k‖)²/ε², which is the right scaling for an ADDITIVE logit error ε. The paper itself acknowledges the norm sensitivity in Section 4.1: "As demonstrated in Theorem 3.6, the distortion on the attention scores is directly proportional to the norms of the embeddings."
- The bound is a high-probability multiplicative sandwich per score; it is not a statement about E[S̃core] (no bias analysis after softmax). The "unbiased" property is pre-softmax only.
- e^{2ε} ≤ 1+3ε requires ε ≲ 0.47.
- Score(i) is the full softmax over n keys (Eq. (2)); the theorem silently takes ε < 1 relative to logits, not relative to logit spread, and does not involve the softmax temperature / √d scaling.

## 4. Q3 — Assumptions, exactly

1. Projection: S ∈ R^{m×d} with i.i.d. N(0,1) entries (Definition 3.1; Alg. 1 line 1). The theory requires i.i.d. Gaussian rows (Fact 3.3 independence argument). The IMPLEMENTATION uses something else:
> "we first generate a random JL matrix S with i.i.d. Gaussian entries and then orthogonalize its rows using QR decomposition. We then use this orthogonalized matrix in our QJL quantizer, as described in Algorithm 1." (Section 4.1, "Orthogonalized JL transform")
No theorem covers the orthogonalized S; rows are then dependent and the unbiasedness proof (which uses i.i.d. s_i) does not go through verbatim (for m ≤ d orthogonal rows the marginal of each row is still rotationally symmetric so E is unaffected, but the paper does not say this).
2. Norms stored: YES. ν_i = ‖k_i‖₂ is stored in full precision per key token (Alg. 1 lines 3–4). "Zero overhead" refers to not storing per-group (zero point, scale); the per-token norm is the only scalar. Query norm is not needed.
3. Same S for q and k, S shared across all tokens of a head ("Draw a random sketch S" once; data-oblivious; "requires no tuning").
4. For Theorem 3.6: ‖k_i‖₂ ≤ r for all i, ‖q_n‖₂ ≤ r, and m ≥ 2r²ε⁻² log n.
5. Values: NOT QJL. "We quantize the value cache using a standard quantization method, i.e., normalizing each token's entries and then rounding each entry to a few-bit integer representation" (Section 3.2) — per-token, following KIVI/KVQuant.
6. Outliers: "we quantize the outliers using an independent instance of our QJL quantizer but with a lower compression rate, utilizing more bits to accurately represent each outlier coordinate." (Section 4.1). The exact split (how many channels, how many bits) and the m/d ratio that yields "3 bits per FPN" are NOT given in the text; "we choose proper hyper-parameters of KIVI and QJL so that their bits per FPN become 3" (Section 4.2).
7. No assumption on the distribution of q, k (worst-case over fixed vectors; randomness is only in S).

## 6. Limitations / future work — the paper's own words

The paper has no limitations section. The only forward-looking or self-qualifying statements are:
- "We plan to complete implementation in the CUDA for future work, which will further accelerate our algorithm." (Section 4)
- "As demonstrated in Theorem 3.6, the distortion on the attention scores is directly proportional to the norms of the embeddings. Therefore, capturing these outlier coordinates is essential … By identifying and isolating these outlier channels, we can reduce the norm of the key embeddings and, consequently, significantly decrease the final distortion." (Section 4.1)
- "We observed that orthogonalizing the rows of the JL matrix S in Definition 3.1 almost always improves the performance of our QJL quantizer." (Section 4.1) — no explanation offered.
- "Across all datasets, our method's accuracy is generally comparable to the baseline, with slight variations." (Section 4.2)
Not discussed anywhere: softmax-induced bias, variance after softmax, heteroscedasticity across tokens, effect of the stored-norm precision, dependence on head dimension in practice, perplexity, long-generation drift, batch/latency at scale, or why KVQuant beats QJL on MultiQA-en.

## 7. What the report must change (line 52 of `reports/LLM 양자화 연구 아이디어 10선.md`)

Current text: "QJL은 '비편향 내적 추정량, 최소 왜곡'을 증명했지만 ([QJL 2406.03482, AAAI 2025] [V]) … D'Alberto의 노트 … 'QJL이 K의 내적 분산을 π/2배 키우고 softmax가 Jensen으로 증폭한다'는 정성적 주장".

1. Be precise about WHAT is unbiased: Lemma 3.2 proves E_S[Prod_QJL(q,k)] = ⟨q,k⟩ for the pre-softmax inner product only; Theorem 3.6 gives a high-probability (1±3ε) multiplicative bound on each softmax score, not unbiasedness of the score. Cite "Lemma 3.2 / Lemma 3.5 / Theorem 3.6" rather than "증명했지만".
2. "최소 왜곡" should become the exact bound: |Prod_QJL − ⟨q,k⟩| ≤ ε‖q‖‖k‖ w.p. ≥ 1−δ when m ≥ (4/3)(1+ε)ε⁻² log(2/δ) (Lemma 3.5); attention scores within (1±3ε) when m ≥ 2r²ε⁻² log n with ‖q‖,‖k_i‖ ≤ r (Theorem 3.6).
3. The "π/2배" statement attributed to D'Alberto is only the orthogonal-pair ratio. From the paper's own Eq. (5), Var[Prod_QJL] = (‖q‖²‖k‖²/m)(π/2 − cos²θ) vs (‖q‖²‖k‖²/m)(1 + cos²θ) for unquantized JL; the ratio is π/2 at θ = 90° and < 1 for |cos θ| > 0.534. The report should state the general formula (and that the paper does not write it) instead of the "π/2배" shorthand.
4. The report's hypothesis (token-heterogeneous logit-noise variance biases the softmax) is DIRECTLY supported by the paper's Eq. (5): Var[q̃K(j)] ∝ ‖k_j‖²(π/2 − cos²θ_j)/m varies per cached token j. The paper never notices this; the gap claim ("토큰 간 분산 이질성이 만드는 체계적 편향 … 없다") is correct and can be sharpened: QJL's own moment formula already gives the per-token variance that the proposed second-order correction needs.
5. Add the theory/practice gap: implementation uses QR-orthogonalized S, separate higher-bit QJL for ~4 outlier channels, per-token FP norm ν_i, and standard per-token value quantization; none of these are covered by the theorems. "Zero overhead" = no per-group scale/zero, but one scalar per token is stored.
6. Theorem 3.6's displayed probability holds only for r = 1; for the real norm magnitudes the m ∝ r² condition is too weak (needs r⁴ for an r-free exponent). If the report leans on "m ≈ ε⁻² log n bits per token independent of d", it must add "assuming O(1) norms", which is exactly what the outlier-channel split is trying to buy.
7. The statement about vLLM excluding QJL and "5개 이상 독립 그룹의 합의" is NOT from this paper; keep its [V] tag only if it was verified against the vLLM source, and do not attribute it to 2406.03482.
