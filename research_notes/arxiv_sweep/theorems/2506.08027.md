## 1. PROVED vs CLAIMED vs HYPOTHESISED
- PROVED: nothing (no theorem/lemma). The only deductive statement is the tautology that ceil guarantees no overflow.
- Algorithm 1 (L647-660): "Xfloat ← amax/destmax; expXfloat ← log2(Xfloat); expXint ← ceil(expXfloat) ▷ round-up; X ← clamp(expXint, −127, 127); X ← X + 127; return 2^X".
- Rationale (L663-671): "rounding up X ensures that after scaling no V/X value exceeds the maximum magnitude representable in the destination format. Conversely, rounding down, as in OCP v1 suggestion, will lead to scaled values overflowing the representable range, introducing more quantization noise. We hypothesize this aspect leading to MXFP8 training issues when using OCP v1 conversion scheme as shown in Figure 5."
- Overflow handling (Sec 3.3 L642-644, App. A.2): RN-ties-to-even and "saturating conversion mode ... magnitudes exceeding FP8-maximum are clamped to respective maximum representative magnitude". So "overflowing" = clipping of the block's largest elements to destmax (448 for E4M3, 57344 for E5M2), not Inf/NaN.
- App. A.1 (L1021-1050): Algorithm 1 is "a simplification" because device log2 rounds; emulation works on the bit pattern: "For all other values that are not powers of 2, round-up to the closest representable UE8M0 value." and "We find round-up to be the best choice for pre-training with MX-formats" (they mention round-down, RN, truncation as options, but Fig. 5 only shows OCP vs ours).

## 4. Theoretical reasoning for why round-down fails (my derivation; NOT in the paper)
- OCP/Rouhani et al. 2310.10537 Algorithm 1 (verified in its text, L194-196): "shared_exp ← ⌊log2(max_i |V_i|)⌋ − emax_elem; X ← 2^shared_exp", with clamping of normals on overflow; the MX paper adds "the specification allows for other implementation-defined conversion recipes".
- Since destmax = 1.75·2^emax for E4M3 and E5M2, ceil(log2(amax/destmax)) equals the OCP exponent or OCP+1; they differ only when the mantissa m of amax lies in (1.75, 2). In those blocks OCP clips amax (and any other element above 1.75·2^emax·X) with relative error up to 1 − 1.75/2 = 12.5%, versus the E4M3 RN bound 2^-4 = 6.25%. Under a log-uniform mantissa that is log2(2/1.75) = 19.3% of blocks; for Gaussian B=32 blocks 16.9% (Monte Carlo, scratchpad/mx_round.py). Elsewhere the two rules are identical. Round-up costs one binade of headroom at the bottom, which E4M3's 17.8 binades absorb.
- For MXFP4 (E2M1, destmax 6 = 1.5·2^2) the OCP rule clips whenever M/X ∈ (6, 8): 41.5% of blocks under log-uniform, 29.6% for Gaussian B=32. Yet the Gaussian MSE/σ² is 0.0132 for OCP vs 0.0133 for ceil (Monte Carlo, 2×10^5 blocks): MSE does NOT distinguish them. A pure floor(log2(M/6)) rule (always clipping, M/X ∈ [6,12)) gives 0.0413; that rule is not what OCP specifies.
- So the paper's mechanism (clipping of the block maximum, a biased error on the largest-magnitude elements) is plausible but untested; an MSE argument cannot carry it.

Mishra et al.의 발산 관찰과 정합" must be withdrawn or re-scoped to a hypothetical pure-floor rule. What survives: OCP clips the block maximum in ~17% (FP8) / ~30% (FP4) of Gaussian blocks, with error concentrated on the largest elements; the question is whether that bias, not MSE, drives the instability. Experiment 3 (L183) should compare ceil against the actual OCP rule (not a pure-floor rule). Because the two rules differ only in the clipping band, that two-arm comparison already isolates the clipping effect; log the per-step fraction of clipped blocks and the clipped mass per tensor type.
