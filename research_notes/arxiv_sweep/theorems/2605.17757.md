## 0. Bottom line for the report

1. **What is proved.** One exact algebraic identity (Eq. 1), one trivial lemma (Lemma 1, Hadamard conjugation equalizes a diagonal), and **Theorem 1**, which says: *if* you (a) replace the exact causal logit error by a surrogate in which the position-dependent query second moment C_j is swapped for the global C_Q = (1/N)ΣqᵀQ, (b) treat the quantization-residual covariance E_K as a *frozen* matrix independent of the rotation, and (c) *assume* E_K is diagonal in the rotated (ambient) basis with its entries already sorted ascending in the same index order as the eigenvalues of C_Q sorted descending, *then* R_k = U_Q (eigenbasis of C_Q) minimizes tr(R_kᵀ C_Q R_k E_K) over orthogonal R_k. The proof is Birkhoff polytope + rearrangement inequality. Under assumption (c) the result is close to tautological; the paper's own App. A.4 states that after R_K = U_Q the residual is in fact dominated by the high-eigenvalue channels ("E_K has a few very large diagonal entries and many tiny ones"), which is the *opposite* ordering from assumption (c) and is why Hadamard and bit-reversal are added on top. The paper's Limitations paragraph concedes it is "not yet a full proof of optimality for the complete autoregressive decoding process".
2. **The "logit error weighted by query energy λ_j" statement** is App. A.4, informal: with R_K = U_Q, R_Kᵀ C_Q R_K = Λ_Q, "λ_j = ‖Q u_j‖²₂ measures how much total query energy lies along u_j. Quantization noise on channel j then propagates to the attention logits with weight λ_j". It is a statement about Frobenius-norm logit-error *energy* (second moment summed over all query/key pairs), not about the mean of softmax outputs.
3. **MLA / latents: zero treatment.** "MLA", "latent", "DeepSeek", "RoPE", "rotary" never appear in the body; the only hit for "latent" is reference [CARE] in the bibliography. All models evaluated are GQA (Qwen3-4B/8B/32B, GLM-4.7-FP8). The paper never says whether R_K is applied before or after RoPE; R_V is folded into projection weights, R_K is applied online.
4. **Per-token heteroscedastic variance: not modeled.** Quantization is per-token, per-group min–max asymmetric INT2 with per-token percentile clipping (τ_t = quantile_ρ of |k̃_{t,c}| over channels, ρ_K = 0.96, ρ_V = 0.92). The theory sums the residual over tokens into one matrix E_K; there is no token-indexed variance, no noise model, no expectation, no mean/variance split. The word "variance" is used only for channel variance / eigenvalue spread.
5. **Softmax bias: not analyzed.** The only occurrences of "bias" are two "✗ biased" labels on Hadamard-rotated V activations in the Figure 1 schematic. "unbiased" appears once, in the Limitations paragraph as future work ("proving when the calibration estimator is unbiased"). Softmax enters the theory only through the score-weighted value target C_S = (1/N) VᵀSᵀSV (Eq. 2), which is a deterministic weighting, "heuristically define[d]".

## 1. Setup (Section 2 "Preliminaries and Motivation")

Row-vector, single-head notation: Q, K, V ∈ R^{T×d}, "S = softmax_row(QKᵀ/√d) ∈ R^{T×T}, and the attention output is O = SV". Quantizer: "Q(x) = s · clip(⌊x/s⌉, −τ, τ)"; "Given rotation matrix R [9], we denote the reconstruction of quantized K and V as K̂ = Q(KR)Rᵀ and V̂ = Q(VR)Rᵀ." Footnote 1: "Row-wise is used for our theory. Empirically, quantization is applied to the head dimension with block-size 128, 64 or 32."

**Eq. (1)** (exact identity, the "query-covariance weighting"):
> "For keys K, the downstream logit distortion is ‖QKᵀ − QK̂ᵀ‖²_F = tr((K − K̂) QᵀQ (K − K̂)ᵀ), (1) which is controlled by the query covariance QᵀQ rather than by KᵀK alone."

**Eq. (2)** (exact identity, value side):
> "For values V with quantized V̂ = Q(V), the downstream output distortion is ‖SV − SV̂‖²_F = tr((V − V̂)ᵀ SᵀS (V − V̂)), (2) which depends on how attention scores weight the value rows."

Both are plain algebra (Frobenius norm of a product). Note Eq. (1) uses the full (non-causal) QKᵀ; the causal version appears only in App. A.7.

## 2. Method (Section 3 "Algorithm Design: Offline Calibration and Justification")

> "Query-aware key target covariance. For a query row q, with the attention-aware key target covariance QᵀQ introduced in equation (1), its empirical estimator is C_Q = (1/N) Σ_{n=1}^N q_nᵀ q_n; we apply eigen decomposition as in App. A.2, C_Q = U_Q Λ_Q U_Qᵀ, and define the rotation as R_k := U_Q."
> "Score-aware value target covariance. For the attention matrix S = softmax_row(QKᵀ/√d), we **heuristically** define the target covariance in equation (2): C_S = (1/N) VᵀSᵀSV. We then compute the eigendecomposition ... C_S = U_S Λ_S U_Sᵀ, and define the raw rotation as R_v := U_S."
> "OSCAR rotations. ... R_K = U_Q H_Had P_br, R_V = U_S H_Had P_br. (3) Here R_k and R_v are the covariance base rotations, while H_Had further improves quantization geometry by redistributing channel energy, and P_br [21] interleaves large- and small-variance channels so that adjacent channels have similar dynamic range."

P_br (App. A.4): "Let σ be the descending-eigenvalue permutation ... and let β be the bit-reversal permutation on {0,...,d−1}. The permutation matrix P_K places the eigenvector with the k-th largest eigenvalue at position β(k)". For d = 128: "top-1 ↦ 0, top-2 ↦ 64, ...". With G_K = d = 128 "the permutation P_K would be a no-op".

Per-head vs per-layer: Algorithm 1 (App. B) line 4: "for each layer and KV head do ... C_K ← (1/N) Σ_n q_nᵀ q_n; C_S ← (1/N) VᵀSᵀSV; U_Q ← EigVec(C_K), U_S ← EigVec(C_S); R_K ← U_Q H_Had P_br, R_V ← U_S H_Had P_br; c_K, c_S ← CalibrateClip(KR_K, VR_V)". In the worked example (App. A.4) the "production importance metric" is "Q-side query covariance, averaged within each GQA group of 4 query heads and then across all 8 kv-heads", C_Q = (1/H_kv) Σ_h (1/(T·g)) Σ_{i∈G_h} Q_iᵀQ_i — i.e. **a head-sum of QᵀQ over the GQA group** (and, in that example, a single rotation shared by all kv-heads of the layer, "the shared rotation R_K = U_Q H_Had P_K"). The paper is mildly inconsistent on whether the rotation is per-KV-head or per-layer.

Quantizer and clipping (App. A.5): "token-wise percentile clipping separately to keys and values. Given clip ratios ρ_K, ρ_V ∈ (0,1], define τ_t^{(K)} = quantile_{ρ_K}({|k̃_{t,c}|}_{c=1}^d)"; "typical values ρ_K = 0.96 and ρ_V = 0.92"; "asymmetric INT2 quantization, optionally with groups along the channel dimension ... q_max = 3 ... dynamic min–max scale and zero point for each token and group". Section 5.1: clip thresholds are "calibration-derived per-layer".

Serving (Section 4): "the first S_0 tokens, which behave as attention sinks, and the most recent W tokens" stay BF16; main results use S = 64, R = 256. "OSCAR then optimize value rotation by absorbing R_V into the model's projection weights". Keys: "kt⁺ = Q₂⁺(clip(k_t R_K, τ_t^{(K)}))" computed online in a fused Triton kernel. Effective BPE at 128K context: 2.28 (Table 2), 2.38 (Table 1, with BF16 scale/zero at group 128 and BF16 initial tokens).

## 3. Theorem 1 — exact statement and assumptions (Section 3, proof App. A.6)

Lead-in: "Optimality of targets under ambient error assumption. Below we propose that under the diagonal residual assumption, our heuristic targets C_Q ... and C_S ... achieve the lowest frozen-error surrogate."

> "**Theorem 1** (Optimality of the simple spectral variants under ambient-basis diagonal residuals). Consider the frozen-error surrogate objectives for the key and value base rotations: L̃_K(R_k) = tr(R_kᵀ C_Q R_k E_K), R_kᵀR_k = I_d and L̃_V(R_v) = tr(R_vᵀ C_S R_v E_V), R_vᵀR_v = I_d. Assume that the frozen (independent of input and rotation) residual covariances are diagonal in the ambient basis:
> E_K = Σ_{j=1}^N (Q(k_j R_k) − k_j R_k)ᵀ (Q(k_j R_k) − k_j R_k) = diag(µ_1, ..., µ_d), µ_1 ≤ ··· ≤ µ_d,
> E_V = Σ_{j=1}^N (Q(v_j R_v) − v_j R_v)ᵀ (Q(v_j R_v) − v_j R_v) = diag(η_1, ..., η_d), η_1 ≤ ··· ≤ η_d.
> Then, on the calibration dataset: R_k = U_Q and R_v = U_S are minimizers of L̃_K(R_k) and L̃_V(R_v)."

Proof (App. A.6, verbatim core): C_Q = U_Q Λ_Q U_Qᵀ with "λ_1 ≥ ··· ≥ λ_d ≥ 0"; "Here E_K is treated as a fixed frozen residual covariance matrix. Let R_k = U_Q Z, ZᵀZ = I_d. Substituting into the objective gives L̃_K(R_k) = tr(Zᵀ Λ_Q Z E_K) ... L̃_K(R_k) = Σ_{i=1}^d Σ_{j=1}^d µ_i λ_j z_{ji}². (4) Since Z is orthogonal, the matrix W = (w_{ji}) with w_{ji} = z_{ji}² is doubly stochastic. Hence Eq. (4) is a linear function over the Birkhoff polytope, and its minimum is attained at a permutation matrix. ... By the rearrangement inequality, since λ_1 ≥ ··· ≥ λ_d and µ_1 ≤ ··· ≤ µ_d, the minimum is achieved by pairing the largest λ_j with the smallest µ_i. This is achieved by the identity permutation. Therefore, one minimizer is Z = I_d, and hence R_k = U_Q. The value-side proof is identical."

Critical reading:
- Assumption (c) couples the index ordering of E_K to that of Λ_Q. E_K is defined in *rotated* coordinates (e_j = Q(k_j R_k) − k_j R_k), so "ambient basis" = post-rotation channel basis. The assumption that channel 1 (the top query-energy direction) has the *smallest* residual is exactly what makes identity optimal. App. A.4 says the real post-U_Q residual is the reverse: "After R_K = U_Q, the rotated key channels inherit this imbalance, with a few channels having much larger variance than the rest. A per-group min–max quantizer ... Hence E_K has a few very large diagonal entries and many tiny ones, and tr(E_K) is dominated by the worst groups." So the theorem does not describe the regime the method actually operates in; the H_Had and P_br factors are motivated by the failure of that regime and are not covered by Theorem 1 at all.
- "Frozen" means E_K is held fixed while R_k is varied, although the paper itself notes "(ii) it shapes E_K, because the quantizer's per-token, per-group min–max scale (Sec. A.5) depends on the channelwise distribution of K̃" (App. A.4).
- The theorem is stated "on the calibration dataset" — a finite-sample statement, no generalization claim.

## 4. Surrogate derivation (App. A.7 "Justification of Surrogate Objectives in Theorem 1")

> "For the key branch, the empirical logit distortion on the calibration set is ℓ_K(R_k) = Σ_{i=1}^N Σ_{j=1}^i (q_i (k_j − k̂_j)ᵀ)², where k̂_j = Q(k_j R_k)R_kᵀ. Writing the rotated quantization residual as e_j^{(K)}(R_k) := Q(k_j R_k) − k_j R_k, we have k̂_j − k_j = e_j^{(K)}(R_k) R_kᵀ, so that ℓ_K(R_k) = Σ_{j=1}^N e_j^{(K)}(R_k) R_kᵀ C_j R_k e_j^{(K)}(R_k)ᵀ, C_j := Σ_{i=1}^j q_iᵀ q_i. To obtain a tractable objective, we approximate the position-dependent matrices C_j by the global query target covariance C_Q = (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^N q_iᵀ q_i. Defining the frozen residual covariance (where R_k is fixed) E_K := Σ_{j=1}^N e_j^{(K)}(R_k)ᵀ e_j^{(K)}(R_k), the key-side frozen-error objective becomes, up to the constant normalization factor 1/N, L̃_K(R_k) = tr(R_kᵀ C_Q R_k E_K)."
> "Under a similar approximation, the value-side downstream error is **heuristically, imitating that of key targets**, represented by the frozen-error surrogate L̃_V(R_v) = tr(R_vᵀ C_S R_v E_V)."

Notes: (i) the causal double sum Σ_i Σ_{j≤i} means key j is seen by queries i ≥ j, yet C_j is written as Σ_{i=1}^{j} — an apparent index slip in the paper; either way C_j is immediately replaced by the global C_Q, which discards the token-position dependence of the weighting (late keys are attended by fewer queries). (ii) The value surrogate has no derivation; the attention matrix S is the *unquantized* one, so the S-weighting is fixed, not propagated through softmax.

## 5. Lemma 1 and the "λ_j weighting" paragraph (App. A.4)

> "Setting R_K = U_Q diagonalizes the importance metric: R_Kᵀ C_Q R_K = Λ_Q. Geometrically, the j-th channel of K̃ = KU_Q is the projection of each key onto the j-th query principal direction u_j, and λ_j = ‖Qu_j‖²₂ measures how much total query energy lies along u_j. Quantization noise on channel j then propagates to the attention logits with weight λ_j: large-λ channels are exactly those whose error is most amplified, while small-λ channels can absorb error almost for free."

> "**Lemma 1** (Diagonal equalization under Hadamard conjugation). Let Λ ∈ R^{d×d} be diagonal and H_Had the normalized Walsh–Hadamard matrix. Then (H_Hadᵀ Λ H_Had)_{ii} = (1/d) tr(Λ), i = 1, ..., d." Proof: every entry has magnitude 1/√d.

Empirical aside (App. A.4): U_Q does **not** diagonalize KᵀK: "top-8 eigenvector self-alignment ... ranges from 0.05 to 0.15 across the 36 layers — essentially indistinguishable from the random alignment 1/√d ≈ 0.09 expected at d = 128" (Qwen3-8B, 2000 calibration tokens). Spectrum example (Qwen3-4B-Thinking layer 10): "λ_1...8 ≈ (162.5, 85.5, 35.6, 17.4, 11.6, 7.5, 6.1, 5.0), λ_121...128 ≈ (0.24, 0.22, 0.22, 0.20, 0.18, 0.05, 0.027, 0.009)".

## 7. Limitations / future work (App. D "Discussion", verbatim)

> "Limitations. Our theoretical analysis explains why the proposed covariance-target rotations are optimal under a frozen-error surrogate with explicit assumptions (Appendix A), but it is not yet a full proof of optimality for the complete autoregressive decoding process. Tightening this gap—for example, by proving when the calibration estimator is unbiased or optimal for the true attention objective—is an important direction for future work. We also focus on INT2 KV-cache quantization and do not explore other numerical formats or mixed-format designs, such as NVFP4, different weight precisions, or weight/KV precision combinations, which may create different accuracy–systems trade-offs. Similarly, OSCAR uses a fixed bit-width for quantized history tokens and does not study per-token bit allocation or thought-adaptive token precision, which recent work such as ThinKV suggests may be useful for long reasoning traces [64]. On the systems side, our implementation targets SGLang-style paged serving on H100 GPUs; additional tuning is needed to understand the best kernel design on other hardware such as B200. Finally, OSCAR is orthogonal to channel-wise or mixed-precision methods: applying attention-aware/Hadamard rotations inside channel-wise schemes may further improve accuracy, but combining these ideas while preserving an efficient serving layout remains open."

Replace "인접" with a precise statement: OSCAR minimizes the second moment tr(E_K QᵀQ) of the logit error and never separates mean from variance; Idea 1's object (E[softmax(ŝ)] − softmax(s) under token-varying σ_j²) is absent. Optionally add OSCAR as *supporting evidence* for Idea 1's per-token-key prediction: OSCAR uses per-token min–max scales + per-token percentile clipping and, without BF16 sink (S=64) + recent (R=256) protection, scores 0.00 on all five tasks (Table 5, (0,0) row) — consistent with sink tokens being the failure point of per-token key quantization, though OSCAR gives no mechanism.
2. **Idea 10, line 353**, four corrections: (a) OSCAR's models are all **GQA** (Qwen3, GLM-4.7-FP8), not MHA; write "MHA/GQA".
