## Definitions (Sec. 2.1, Eq. 1)
> "H_t := − Σ_{j=1}^{V} p_{t,j} log p_{t,j}, where (p_{t,1},...,p_{t,V}) = p_t = π_θ(·|q, o_<t) = Softmax(z_t / T)."

Shannon entropy in nats of the next-token distribution at position t (not a property of the sampled token). Relevant for the report's off-policy framing, verbatim:
> "In off-policy settings, sequences are generated by a rollout policy π_φ while the training policy is π_θ, with φ ≠ θ. The entropy is still calculated using π_θ, as defined in Equation (1), to measure the uncertainty of the training policy in the given sequence."

## Q1. Fraction of high-entropy "fork" tokens, exact threshold, and what changes during RLVR

### Fraction and threshold (Sec. 3, "Entropy Pattern 1", Fig. 2(a))
Setup: Qwen3-8B (post-trained reasoning model, thinking mode), AIME'24 + AIME'25 queries, decoding T = 1.0, "over 10^6 response tokens".
> "Specifically, the entropy of over half the tokens (approximately 50.64%) is below 10^−2, while only 20% of tokens have entropy greater than 0.672."

Threshold definition (Sec. 3, text beside Eq. 5):
> "h_threshold = 0.672 is the entropy threshold used to distinguish forking tokens from the other tokens, and is estimated by calculating the 80th percentile among the sampled 10^6 tokens above"

**Critical reading.** "20%" is not a discovered fraction; it is the 80th percentile by construction. The substantive empirical finding is the *shape*: ~50.64% of positions have H < 0.01 nats, and the 80th percentile is 0.672 nats (for Qwen3-8B on AIME, T=1). The 0.672 number is model- and dataset-specific; the paper itself never reuses it during RL training (see below: training uses a per-batch percentile threshold τ_ρ^B, not 0.672).

Qualitative content (Sec. 3, "Entropy Pattern 2", Fig. 2(b)/(c), token frequency > 100): highest-avg-entropy tokens are connectors ("wait", "however", "unless", "thus", "also", "since", "because", "suppose", "assume", "given", "define"); lowest are "word suffixes, source code fragments, or mathematical expression components".

Controlled decoding experiment (Sec. 3, Eq. 5, Fig. 3): Qwen3-8B, max length 28,672, separate temperatures T_high (H_t > 0.672) vs T_low. Text claim: "lowering T_high significantly degrades performance compared to lowering T_low. In contrast, increasing T_high results in substantially better performance than increasing T_low, which can even cause LLMs generating nonsensical outputs." Figure 3 values legible in the extracted text: averaged AIME'24/'25 scores in the range 62.71–72.22 along the T_high curve, collapsing to 20.14 and 0.14 on the T_low curve at high temperature; the exact temperature-to-score mapping is not recoverable from the text dump (temperature axis: 0.0, 0.05, 0.3, 0.6, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, symlog).

## Q2. Exact results for training on top-20% entropy tokens

### Method (Sec. 5.1, Eq. 6)
DAPO objective with the advantage multiplied by the indicator I[H_t^i ≥ τ_ρ^B] and the token normaliser replaced by Σ_i Σ_t I[H_t^i ≥ τ_ρ^B]. "ρ ∈ (0,1] is a predefined ratio specifying the top proportion of high-entropy tokens to be selected within a batch, and τ_ρ^B is the corresponding entropy threshold within the batch B". So the mask is a **per-(micro-)batch top-ρ quantile of the training policy's entropy**, not the fixed 0.672 threshold.

### Setup (Sec. 5.2)
verl + DAPO recipe; DAPO-Math-17K; ε_high = 0.28, ε_low = 0.2; max response length 20480, overlong cache 4096; train batch 512, mini-batch 32 (16 gradient steps/batch); lr 1e-6, no warmup/scheduling; **no KL loss, no entropy loss**; ρ = 20% for main results; base (not instruct) Qwen3-32B/14B/8B. Eval: 6 math benchmarks, zero-shot, 16 samples at T = 1.0, Acc@16 and Len@16. Number of seeds is not stated.

### Ablations / extensions (Sec. 5.3–5.4; figures only, no tables)
- ρ sweep (Fig. 7): 8B "remains relatively consistent across ... 10%, 20%, and 50%"; for 14B/32B "reducing ρ from 20% to 10% leads to a slight drop in performance, while increasing it sharply to 100% results in a notable decline." No numbers in text.
- Bottom-80% only (Fig. 7(a)(b), 8B): "substantial decline in performance". No numbers in text.
- Overall entropy (Fig. 7(b)(d)(f)): ρ ∈ {10%, 50%, 100%} and bottom-80% all give "persistently lower overall entropy" than ρ = 20% before convergence. This is the authors' exploration explanation; it is correlational.
- Scaling (Fig. 8): gain over vanilla grows 8B → 14B → 32B (the Table 2 AIME deltas).
- OOD LiveCodeBench v5 (Fig. 9, 32B, smoothed window 10, avg@16 roughly 24–28): top-10% and top-20% "significantly outperform" vanilla; no final numbers in text.
- Longer context (Fig. 10): continuing the 32B forking-token run with max length 29,696 lifts AIME'24 63.54 → 68.12.
- Llama-3.1-8B after cold-start SFT on 10k Qwen3-32B-generated solutions (Fig. 11): forking-token DAPO "still surpasses vanilla DAPO" but authors say "we believe the results on Llama-3.1-8B are less convincing".

### Confounders the paper does not control
- Response length rises by +1,378 to +2,876 tokens on average under the 20% mask (Table 2); accuracy gains on AIME coincide with ~25–60% longer responses. Length is not held fixed.
- Seeds / variance not reported for Table 2; AIME has 30 problems, so avg@16 on AIME'24/'25 still has sizeable variance.
- All RL results are DAPO-specific (clip-higher ε_high = 0.28, no KL, no entropy bonus). The authors' own Discussion 3 argues clip-higher already preferentially raises high-entropy tokens, so the 20% mask interacts with that choice.

## Paper's own limitations / future work (Sec. 8, verbatim)
> "Limitations We believe there is still room for improvement in our work. First, our experiments could be extended to models beyond the Qwen family. Although we attempted to evaluate our approach on LLaMA models, they struggled to achieve meaningful performance on the AIME benchmarks. Additionally, the scope of our dataset could be expanded to encompass domains beyond mathematics, such as programming or more complex tasks like ARC-AGI (Chollet et al., 2025; Chollet, 2019). Furthermore, our findings are based on specific experimental settings, and it is possible that the observations and conclusions presented in this paper may not generalize to all RLVR scenarios. For instance, in a different RLVR setting, the effective proportion of 20% observed in our experiments may need to be adjusted to a different value to achieve optimal results."

> "Future Directions Future directions involve developing new RLVR algorithms to better leverage high-entropy minority tokens and exploring how these insights can enhance not only RLVR but also other approaches, such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT), distillation, inference, and multi-modal training."

## Implications for the report (`reports/LLM 양자화 연구 아이디어 10선.md`, line 235)
Current sentence: "RLVR의 이득이 20% 고엔트로피 fork 토큰에서 온다는 결과도 있다 ([Beyond the 80/20 Rule 2506.01939, NeurIPS 2025] [V])."

1. **Scope the claim.** It is an empirical DAPO result on Qwen3 base models with math data only. The *gain* appears on 14B (+5.21/+4.79 AIME'24/'25) and 32B (+7.71/+11.04); on 8B it is parity (+0.53 avg; AMC'23 −0.63). "Comes from" should read "matched or exceeded full-token DAPO when gradients were restricted to the top-20% entropy tokens per batch, while bottom-80%-only training collapsed."
2. **Do not cite it for KL.** The paper never measures token KL during RLVR and trains with no KL loss. The report's adjacent sentence about KL concentrating on high-entropy positions must rest on 2606.00206 alone; 2506.01939 supports only (a) the entropy distribution shape (50.64% of positions < 0.01 nats; 80th percentile = 0.672 nats on Qwen3-8B/AIME/T=1) and (b) "RLVR primarily changes the entropy of already-high-entropy tokens" (Table 1 overlap 86.67% at step 1360; Fig. 4).
3. **Threshold semantics.** The 0.672 nats value is a Shannon-entropy 80th percentile of Qwen3-8B outputs; the training-time mask uses a per-batch quantile of the *trainer* policy's entropy (Eq. 6), not a fixed threshold. If the report's idea selects positions by collision entropy (1 − ‖p‖₂²) the report should say that Shannon and collision entropy rank positions differently in general and that 2506.01939 used Shannon.
4. **Useful hook the report currently misses.** Sec. 2.1 explicitly defines entropy under off-policy rollout (π_φ rollout, π_θ trainer) using the trainer policy. That is the same setting as the quantized-rollout mismatch the report discusses, so it is a legitimate bridge — but the paper does not study rollout–trainer mismatch, so the report's claim "no work connects the two" stands.
5. **Length confound.** If the report leans on the AIME gains, note that forking-token training lengthened responses by 25–60%, which the paper does not control for.
6. Keep the [V] tag; the venue "NeurIPS 2025" is confirmed by the arXiv comment field and the paper's first page ("Accepted to NeurIPS 2025").
