## Q1. Shrinkage bias: exact statement and assumptions (Sec 3.1)

### Definition
- t = |x|/s is the normalized magnitude.
- b_G(P) = E_{t∼P}[ρ_G(t) − t], where ρ_G is RTNE onto the codebook G.
- "A value b_G(P) < 0 indicates Shrinkage Bias."

### Assumptions
- (i) "Assuming no clipping (t ≤ max(G))".
- (ii) Only **interior** levels q_i, i ∈ {1, …, n−1}.
- (iii) "If the density inside this bin is locally uniform".

### Eq. 2
E[ρ_G(t) − t | t ∈ B_i] = (ℓ_i − r_i)/2 = (2q_i − q_{i−1} − q_{i+1})/4

- Results:
  - q=2 gives −0.125 (footnote 2: bin (1.75, 2.5), ℓ = 0.25, r = 0.5).
  - q=4 gives "the same asymmetry at double the scale", i.e. −0.25.
  - Uniform grids give ℓ_i = r_i, i.e. 0.
- The paper separates this "grid-geometry source" from "distribution-induced quantization error inside a symmetric bin".
- This is **a derivation, not a theorem**. It is bin-conditional only. No distribution-level b_G(P) is computed: there is no Gaussian or Laplace assumption anywhere in the paper.

### What the derivation omits (my check)
Under absmax scaling, the bin of the **top level** is one-sided, (5, 6]. Under the same locally-uniform assumption, its error is **+0.5**: an *expansion*, not a shrinkage. The interior-only restriction hides this.

My Monte Carlo (`scratchpad/sim/perbin.py`): i.i.d. N(0,1) blocks of 16, FP32 absmax scale to 6, RTN. Per-bin mean error and contribution to energy-weighted bias Σ(q−t)t/Σt²:

| Level | Mean error | Uniform-density prediction | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| q=0 | −0.125 | −0.125 | −0.00016 |
| q=2 | −0.112 | −0.125 | −0.0050 |
| q=4 | −0.164 | −0.25 | −0.0139 |
| q=6 (non-max elements) | **+0.545** | +0.5 | **+0.0165** |
| others | ≈ 0 to +0.03 | 0 | small |

- Net: 1 − α ≈ **+0.0033**, so α_A ≈ 0.9967. This matches the report's own figure of 0.9968.
- For E1M2 under the same setting: 1 − α ≈ −0.0008, a slight expansion.
- So the net E2M1 shrinkage is a small residual of large terms that nearly cancel.
- The locally-uniform formula overstates the q=4 bin by about 50% for Gaussian blocks.
- The sign of b_G(P) for a real tensor is therefore distribution- and scale-dependent. It does not follow from Eq. 2 alone.
- Comparing my runs:
  - Same sim, Laplace and Student-t3 blocks: α(E2M1) ≈ 0.997–0.9975, α(E1M2) ≈ 0.9994–1.0003.
  - Gaussian SQNR: 20.5 dB (E2M1) vs 21.4 dB (E1M2).
  - Laplace and t3 SQNR favours E2M1 (20.8 vs 19.9 dB and 21.1 vs 19.0 dB).
- One earlier script run gave α = 0.9959 for Gaussian. Three seeds of a cleaner script give 0.9967–0.9968. I use the latter.

## Q2. "RHT amplifies the bias": exact wording and evidence

### Wording
- Abstract: "this bias accumulates multiplicatively across layers and is amplified by the Random Hadamard Transform (RHT)".
- Sec 3.2: "RHT transforms the tensor from being dynamic-range-limited to local-resolution-limited."
- Sec 3.2: "By shifting tensor mass from extreme tails into mid-magnitude regions, RHT inadvertently forces data into E2M1's most asymmetric rounding bins, exacerbating quantization noise (∆SQNR < 0)."
- Key Finding 2: "applying RHT exacerbates this decay under E2M1 by shifting tensor mass into the most biased asymmetric bins".

### Evidence
The evidence is entirely tensor-level **MSE and entropy** metrics on real MLP tensors:
- B_eff, the bucket-entropy ratio (Eq. 6).
- ∆SQNR = 10 log10(NMSE_I/NMSE_H16) (Eq. 7).
- Figure 2, Figs 4–7, App. A.

Example (Sec 5.2, linear_fc2/fwd_x):

| Grid | Before RHT | After RHT |
|---|---|---|
| E2M1 | 21.90 dB | 20.00 dB |
| E1M2 | 19.94 dB | 23.19 dB |

- E1M2's B_eff rises from 0.56 to 0.97.
- linear_fc1/fwd_x is nearly neutral: ∆SQNR −0.008 dB (E1M2) and +0.007 dB (E2M1).

### Critical point
- **No measurement of the signed bias or of α before and after RHT.**
- A drop in SQNR is an MSE statement. It is equally explained by plain resolution mismatch: E2M1's coarse steps at 2–6 against a post-RHT near-Gaussian with absmax scaling. That explanation does not need a bias mechanism.
- So "RHT amplifies the bias" is **motivated and argued qualitatively, not demonstrated**.
- My sim gives weak support only: α(E2M1) moves from ≈0.997 for heavy-tailed blocks to ≈0.9967 for Gaussian. That is a change of about 0.0003–0.0008 in 1 − α, a small effect.

## Q4. Is the effect of bin uniformity isolated?

**Only partially, and not end-to-end.**
- Sec 5.1 says the comparison "focuses on the **joint effect** of FP4 grid choice and RHT scope".
- Table 1 changes two factors together:
  - grid: E2M1 → E1M2
  - RHT scope: bwd_dw → all three GEMMs
- Held fixed: block 1×16, FP32 single-level scale (not NVFP4's E4M3 scale), RHT block 16, SR on dY only, no 2D weight scaling.
- No end-to-end run is reported with E1M2 + bwd_dw-only RHT against the E2M1 reference.
  - Table 2 has the E1M2 bwd_dw-only number (1.88721), but no E2M1 loss on the same table/run.
- No E2M1 full-RHT run with max_fpx = 6 is reported numerically.
  - Section B says only "FPROP RHT is detrimental; DGRAD RHT raises LM loss at later stages", with no numbers.
- Grid geometry is cleanly isolated only at the single-tensor and single-GEMM SQNR level (Sec 5.2, App. A).
- Uniformity is also confounded with level count: the range-restricted uniform E2M1 (max 2) has fewer levels. No 8-level uniform grid emulated on E2M1 hardware is tested.
- The E2M1 reference was tuned by one-factor ablations at Dense 1.5B for 200B tokens (Section B, Table 3), which reduces concerns about a weak baseline. The UFP4 side's tuning is not described in the same way.

## Limitations stated by the paper
There is no dedicated limitations section. Closest statements:
- Sec 5.3: "both FP4 recipes still incur a measurable BF16 gap".
- Sec 5.4: "a residual gap to BF16 remains".
- Sec 5.5 / Conclusion: "Our recommendation is narrow: E2M1 should remain available for raw outlier-heavy tensors and inference workloads".
- Sec 5.5: "In these tested recipes, E2M1 range restriction is therefore not a satisfactory substitute for native E1M2/INT4 support."
- Sec 4: "Scale hierarchy design remains orthogonal" (asserted, not tested).
- Future work is a hardware recommendation: "Future training accelerators should support E1M2/INT4-style uniform 4-bit grids as first-class training data elements"; Ascend 960/HiFloat4 is named as a candidate platform.
- The paper also states, in Section B, that its SR finding differs from TetraJet-v2.

## What the report must change

### Line 162
"정량 이론이 없다" is already flagged by the first pass. Go further:
- The paper's quantitative content is the bin-conditional Eq. 2 under locally-uniform density for interior levels, plus the GEMM projection identity (Eq. 3–5).
- It has no distribution-level bias formula and no measured α.
- "RHT amplifies the bias" is supported only by ∆SQNR/B_eff (MSE-type) evidence, not by a signed-bias measurement.
- "자동 리뷰는 효과 분리가 안 됐다" can now be grounded in the paper itself:
  - Sec 5.1: "joint effect of FP4 grid choice and RHT scope".
  - Table 1 changes grid and RHT scope together.
  - FP32 single-level scales are used, not NVFP4 E4M3.

### Line 176 (Idea 3 math)
- The report attributes shrinkage to a decreasing density inside the big [4,6] cell. That is the "distribution-induced" mechanism, which UFP4 explicitly distinguishes from its own "grid-geometry" mechanism: asymmetric bins at q = 2 and 4 under locally-uniform density.
- The report should say which mechanism it quantifies, or that its −0.66% / 0.9968 number contains both.
- It should add that the top bin (5, 6] contributes a large positive error (about +0.5 per element), which mostly cancels the q = 2 and q = 4 shrinkage. The net 1 − α ≈ 0.0033 is a residual.
- This makes the report's own idea (a Gaussian closed form) a genuine extension of UFP4, because UFP4 has none.

### Line 198
"UFP4는 shrinkage bias" as its claimed bottleneck stays confirmed. Add that UFP4's experiments use FP32 block scales, so it is not a comparison against the NVFP4 recipe.
