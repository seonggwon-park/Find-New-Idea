## Status: nothing is proven
No theorems. Eq. 5 is an empirical fit; the log2 G term is justified only by the boundary condition G = 1 ⇒ δ = 0.

## Q1. Functional form and constants (Sec 3, Eqs. 1–5; Table 1)
- Full loss (Eq. 4): `L(N,D,G) = A/N^α + B/D^β + E + δ_p(N,D,G)`. The Chinchilla part is fitted with **α = β tied** (App. C/E: "we also set α = β").
  - Fitting: Huber loss on log-losses with L-BFGS.
  - Constants: **E = 1.9279, A = 237.7042, B = 596.2490, α = β = 0.3022**.
  - The Chinchilla fit uses models of "145M to 2.8B" plus the official OLMo-2 7B and 13B releases (App. C). It reports MSE 0.0014 and R² 0.982.
- Quantization error (Eq. 5): **`δ_p(N,D,G) = k · D^γD · (log2 G)^γG / N^γN`**. The text says "We incorporate a logarithmic term for G, as G = 1 (no quantization) yields δp = 0." (G = 1 means one element per scale, not "no quantization". The justification is heuristic.)
- Table 1 fitted values:

| target | k | γN | γD | γG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| δ_W4A4 | 0.1582 | 0.2186 | 0.0745 | 0.7779 |
| δ_W4A16 (weights only) | 0.2522 | 0.3589 | 0.1610 | 0.3533 |
| δ_W16A4 (activations only) | 0.1004 | 0.1816 | 0.0331 | 0.9812 |
| δ_W4A4, FC2 input 8-bit | 0.3519 | 0.2637 | 0.0964 | 0.3407 |
| δ_W16A4, FC2 input 8-bit | 0.1273 | 0.2347 | 0.0827 | 0.4491 |

- **Internal inconsistencies (present in both arXiv and PMLR):**
  - The text gives γG(W16A4) as 0.9821, but Table 1 says 0.9812.
  - The text gives γG(W16A4, FC2 8-bit) as 0.4471, but Table 1 says 0.4491.
  - Sec 4.2 defines the ground truth as "lossbf16 − lossW4A4", which has the wrong sign.
  - PMLR App. D swaps the symbols ("model size (D), number of training tokens (N)").
- **Units:** N and D appear to be raw counts. A sanity check supports this: 145M / 20B / G = 128 gives δ ≈ 0.069, against the Fig. 3 range "[0.6, 0.65]", which is evidently 0.06–0.065. The paper does not state the units explicitly, and it never says what G value is used for "per-token/channel" in log2 G.
- **Fit data:** Eq. 5 for W4A4 is fitted to **80 W4A4 runs**, i.e. 4 N × 4 D × 5 G.
  - Validation: 973M models at 100B and 200B tokens, said to be "consistently accurate". This is about 1.6× N and 2× D beyond the fit range.
  - Relative prediction error, Table 2 arXiv: W4A4 4.7% (against 8.5% for the baseline laws with one curve per G); W4A16 5.2% (against 19.3%).
  - Ablation without D (Table 4 arXiv / Table 6 PMLR): W4A4 8.6%, W4A16 13.8%.
- **Implied sensitivities** (my arithmetic from Table 1; the paper's empirical averages are in parentheses):
  - D from 10B to 100B: W4A4 ×1.19 (paper: +22%); W4A16 ×1.45 (+43%); W16A4 ×1.08 (+12%).
  - N from 74M to 594M: W4A4 ×0.63 (−34%).
  - G from 16 to 8, out of range for this paper: W4A4 ×0.80; W4A16 ×0.90; W16A4 ×0.75.
- Contrast with Ouyang (2411.17691, post-training quantization): Ouyang's token exponent is ≈0.53, against **γD = 0.0745** for W4A4 quantization-aware training (QAT). QAT error grows with D far more slowly than PTQ degradation does.
- **Version differences:** the PMLR version keeps every constant and the 268/276K numbers. It adds:
  - App. D: 2- and 3-bit **weight-only** runs (PMLR Table 4). These are reported as following "the same predictable trends", but no constants are fitted.
  - App. I, Table 7: a random-Hadamard-rotation ablation on the FC2 input.
  - Renumbered tables.

## Q2. Scale of the 268 experiments (Sec 4.1)
- "Llama3-style" models on OLMo2-Mix-1124.
  - **N ∈ {74, 145, 297, 595}M** parameters.
  - **D ∈ {10, 20, 50, 100}B** tokens.
  - Group size **G ∈ {32, 64, 128, 256, per-token/channel}**.
  - Validation: **973M at 100B and 200B**.
- "These 268 QAT experiments on A100 GPUs consumed 276K GPU-hours in total."
- **Precisions:** W4A4, W4A16, W16A4, plus FC2-8-bit variants. Per-tensor quantization is excluded.
- **Quantizers:** AbsMax for weights; AbsMax for activations when G < 256, LAC when G ≥ 256.
- **Number format:** INT4 throughout. FP4 is "matched" for group-wise quantization, and the INT4-fitted law "accurately predicts" the FP4 trend (Fig. 13).
- **Training:** OLMo2 hyperparameters. The quantization error stays within about 0.06–0.065 across LR from 5e-4 to 4e-3 (Fig. 3).
- **Loss metric:** smoothed training loss.
- **Accounting:** the paper does not break down how the 268 runs are distributed. 80 per precision × 3 precisions = 240, plus the FC2 runs and the validation runs.
- **Largest fit point:** 595M parameters / 100B tokens, which the authors themselves name as a limitation.

## Q3. FC2-input outlier finding and the mixed-precision recommendation (Sec 4.3–4.4)
- **Decomposition:** `δW4A4 ≈ k(δW16A4 + δW4A16)` with an "observed coefficient" of **0.906** (Fig. 6).
- **Ratio R = δW16A4/δW4A16** (activation error over weight error):
  - R > 1 for every tested D/N and G.
  - At G = 32, R falls from **1.67 at D/N = 100 to 1.20 at D/N = 1000**.
  - At D/N = 1000, R rises from **1.20 at G = 32 to 1.62 at G = 256**.
  - The paper concludes: "as D/N increases, the main source of quantization error shifts from activations to weights. However, δW16A4 remains larger than δW4A16 even at high D/N (G = 32)".
- **Kurtosis:** "QAT effectively reduces outliers in the input activations of the QKV Proj, O Proj, and FC1 Proj layers", but "even though QAT lowers the kurtosis of the FC2 Proj input from 123 to 89, this value is still significantly higher than in other layers".
  - Attribution: "The main reason for this high kurtosis is that the FC2 Proj input comes from the output of the SwiGLU module." This is asserted with a citation; no ablation tests the cause.
- **Fix:** "quantizing the FC2 Proj input to 8-bit ... 8-bit quantization provides an upper bound on the improvements possible."
  - "with W4A4 QAT, 8-bit FC2 lowers quantization error by 20.5% for G = 32 and by 42.9% for G = 256."
  - γG(W16A4) drops from 0.9812 to 0.4471 (0.4491 in the table).
  - With 8-bit FC2, "R ranging from 0.85 to 1.10 for D/N [100] and 1000, and for group sizes G = 32 and G = 256" (Fig. 8b, 595M / 100B).
- **Recommendation:** "special treatment of the FC2 Proj input—through mixed-precision quantization or targeted outlier suppression—is crucial for maximizing low-bit QAT performance. Once the FC2 Proj input bottleneck is removed, further improvements to W4A4 QAT should focus on jointly optimizing both weight and activation quantization errors... a shift in QAT development from mainly activation-focused methods to approaches that balance both error sources."
- **Abstract claim:** "with more training data, weight quantization error eventually exceeds activation quantization error". Without the FC2 fix, R > 1 in every tested setting, so the crossover is an extrapolation. It is observed (R down to 0.85) only **with** the FC2-8-bit fix.
- **PMLR Table 7** (145M, 20B tokens, quantization error at G = per-channel / 256 / 128 / 64 / 32):

| FC2 input treatment | per-ch | 256 | 128 | 64 | 32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.084 | 0.071 | 0.065 | 0.055 | 0.047 |
| random Hadamard rotation | 0.053 | 0.049 | 0.046 | 0.044 | 0.040 |
| 8-bit | 0.048 | 0.042 | 0.040 | 0.039 | 0.035 |

  - Text: "8-bit quantization of the FC2 input consistently outperforms the rotation, yielding an additional loss reduction of 0.005 to 0.007."
- **Other appendix claim:** 4-bit beats 8-bit QAT because the "EPM for W4A4 is consistently above 0.5". Kumar's 8-bit optimum is attributed to per-tensor activation quantization (0.24 degradation with AbsMax per-tensor, Fig. 14b).

## Limitations / future work (own statements, App. B)
- "we do not conduct experiments on the MoE architecture. Since MoE models contain more weight parameters but similar activation sizes, they may exhibit a different ratio of weight to activation quantization error".
- "our analysis mainly centers on W4A4 ... ternary ... investigating unified scaling laws for these settings is also valuable."
- "the largest training compute ... is to train a 595M parameter model trained over 100B tokens. Intuitively, the accuracy of scaling law extrapolation would be further improved by increasing both the model size and the number of training tokens."
- Future work on fully quantized training: "4-bit FQT remains a challenge. Our work also lays the groundwork for future research on 4-bit FQT."
- **Unstated by the paper (my critique):**
  - QAT here quantizes the forward pass only, with a straight-through estimator presumably; backward-pass quantization is out of scope.
  - INT4 AbsMax with no rotation in the main law, so the "FC2 bottleneck" is specific to rotation-free INT4. PMLR Table 7 shows Hadamard closes most of it.
  - The largest D/N ratio is about 1350 (74M at 100B), still far from frontier over-training; for example, 8B at 15T is about 1900.

## What the report must change
1. **Report line 208, Idea 4 Prediction 3** ("그룹 크기를 16→8로 줄여 Δ²를 4분의 1로 하면 B가 약 4분의 1", i.e. "cutting group size from 16 to 8 quarters Δ², so B drops to about a quarter"). The only empirical granularity law with fitted constants contradicts the quartering: the error depends on G as (log2 G)^γG.
   - Extrapolated to G from 16 to 8, Table 1 gives a 10% (weights only), 25% (activations only) or 20% (W4A4) reduction, not 75%. G = 8 and 16 lie outside the fitted range of 32–256, and the setting is INT4 AbsMax QAT rather than NVFP4.
   - Restate the prediction as "B ∝ measured E[Δ_block²]" and cite Chen Table 1 as the prior expectation (~0.8×), as discover/idea4_citations.md:111 already recommends.
2. If the report adds a sentence on quantization error growing with D, cite Chen with **γD = 0.0745 for W4A4 QAT** against Ouyang's ≈0.53 for PTQ. This is the gap between the PTQ and QAT regimes, and Idea 4's NVFP4-training setting is closer to the QAT one.
3. In research_notes/01_pretraining_low_precision.md:181, "logarithmic in group size G" should become "a power of log2 G: (log2 G)^0.7779 for W4A4". Venue "ICML 2026" is confirmed (PMLR v306).
