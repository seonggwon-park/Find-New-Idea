# Idea 6 — prior-art sweep (keywords + adjacent fields)

**Idea:** Entropy-aware importance-sampling correction for quantized RL rollouts. Token KL(π_t‖μ_t) ≈ ½δᵀJδ, J = diag(p) − ppᵀ; under isotropic logit noise E[KL_t] ≈ (σ²/2)(1 − ‖p_t‖₂²), so mismatch concentrates on k ≪ T high-entropy "fork" tokens → (i) entropy-gated TIS with bias O(k·Δ_max) instead of O(T²Δ_max), (ii) QeRL "noise = exploration" and FlashRL "bias" are two faces of the same token-level quantity, (iii) sharpening (δ ∝ ‖z‖) makes KL non-monotone.

Sweep date: 2026-10-10. Sources: arXiv API (export.arxiv.org), full texts via arxiv_fetch.py, WebSearch for blog/venue facts. All quoted text is untrusted paper content.

## Bottom line

* **The exact formula E[KL_t] ≈ (σ²/2)(1−‖p‖²) (collision-entropy form) was not found anywhere.** But its qualitative content — "mismatch magnitude scales with (1−p) / grows with token entropy under a logit-perturbation model" — has been stated and proved three times, in three coordinates:
  * DVP 2512.23087 (Dec 2025): Prop. 4.2, |Δ_a| ≤ 2ε_max(1−p_a) for the sampled-token log-prob gap.
  * ENTR 2607.22186 (Jul 2026, v2 Oct 8 2026): Prop. 1 "Entropy envelope", E_μ[δ²] ≤ R_t(e^{R_t}−1)(1−μ_max) ≤ R_t(e^{R_t}−1)·H_t; empirical |δ_t| ≈ √(a_t H_t) (Fig. 1, Qwen3-30B-A3B on BrowseComp-Plus).
  * CIS 2609.32444 (Sep 26 2026): Lemma 3.1 exact identity k_t = p_t + (1−p_t)e^{ε_t}, ε_t (log-odds displacement) "approximately invariant to token confidence" (Spearman −0.028 vs 1−p_t, Fig. 4).
* **Entropy/confidence-gated truncated IS already exists in two forms**, neither tested on quantized rollouts: CIS cap min(k_t, 1+λ(1−p_t)) (cap → 1 on confident tokens, i.e. effectively uncorrected there; wider on uncertain tokens) and ENTR mask δ_t²/(H_t+ε) ≤ τ. The idea's arm (c) is therefore a *re-application* of an occupied method family to the quantized-rollout setting, not a new estimator.
* **The sparse-divergence bias bound is in TRM 2512.23075**: Mixed bound O(T) "tighter when divergence is sparse (D_KL^seq ≪ Tδ)" (Thm 3.4) and the Adaptive bound (Thm 3.6) "retains the per-position divergence D̄_t rather than its maximum … when divergence is non-uniform — e.g., concentrated at a few tokens due to MoE routing — D̄_t ≪ ε at most positions". The idea's O(k·Δ_max) claim should be positioned as "entropy is an a-priori *predictor* of where D̄_t is large", not as a new bound.
* **A direct contradiction of the idea's framing:** ENTR §3.2 and CIS §3.3 both show the gradient-variance problem lives at *low*-entropy positions: when the sampler draws a rare token at a confident position, E[δ_t² | q_t] ∝ (1−q_t)/q_t → ∞ ("concave arc"). Expected KL is small there, but the sampled-token ratio explodes. The idea's Pinsker/TV bias argument is about expectation and silently ignores this. (Entropy gating that sets ratio = 1 on low-entropy tokens would in fact mask those outliers — the idea should say so explicitly and compare to IcePop/KPop/ENTR masks.)
* **Factual mismatch inside the idea:** AIS 2605.13907 reports that KL(π̂_old‖π_old) "grows monotonically during training as the policy sharpens" (§2, l.243-244; ESS falls from >0.95 to <0.70, Fig. 4). The idea's math (3) says "the product of the two effects makes the non-monotone KL trajectory (AIS)". AIS did *not* observe non-monotonicity; the non-monotone prediction would be the idea's own testable claim.
* **The QeRL side is already formalized** by 2605.20402 (MXFP4 decomposition, Appendix D.4): grid noise → logits ℓ_Q = ℓ + η, η ~ N(0,σ_η² I) by CLT, T_eff ≈ √(1 + 2σ_η²/Var(Δℓ)) > 1 ⇒ H[π_Q] > H[π] (Eq. 27). It also notes TIS = 2 "effectively zeroing out the learning signal" for dense MXFP4 rollouts because ratios pile up at the cap (TIS = 5 needed) — relevant to arm (b).
* **Competing explanations in the same setting:** Score Centering 2609.20807 argues instability is *drift* (persistent accumulating bias), not variance; with an FP8 sampler on Qwen3-30B-A3B "even uncorrected policy gradient trains stably, reaching 58%"; collapse only when KV goes to FP4; SC alone "matches or outperforms" IS methods under quantization. TRIAGE 2610.07043 (Oct 5 2026, native NVFP4 W4A4) shows the harmful mismatch is *directional* (A_i<0, δ<0) and concentrated: 97.2% (4B) / 79.9% (30B) of amplifying-tail tokens sit in the top 5% of 64-token segments — concentration by *segment and sign*, not entropy.
* **FlashRL** ("Your Efficient RL Framework Secretly Brings You Off-Policy RL Training", Yao, Liu et al.) is **not on arXiv** under that title (ti/abs searches return nothing); it is a 2025 blog + the NeurIPS 2025 entry "On the Rollout-Training Mismatch in Modern RL Systems" (neurips.cc/virtual/2025/124259; page not fetchable from this sandbox). "When Speed Kills Stability" (Liu, Li, Fu, Wang, Liu, Shen; ByteDance, Sep 17 2025) is a Notion blog, not an arXiv paper; its arXiv descendants are TRM 2512.23075, DVP 2512.23087 and LR-scheduling 2602.01826.

Net: novelty of the *unified entropy/collision-entropy formula* and of the *joint (entropy, KL, reward) measurement under quantized rollouts* survives, but the estimator family (confidence/entropy-gated TIS), the (1−p) mechanism, the sparse-divergence bound and the noise→temperature formula are all occupied. The decisive experiment must add CIS, ENTR, TRIAGE and Score Centering as baselines, and state the non-monotone-KL claim as a prediction, not an AIS observation.

## What is still open (where the idea can stand)

1. Nobody has written the second-order expectation E[KL_t] ≈ (σ²/2)(1−‖p‖²) nor used collision entropy; ENTR uses H_t and (1−μ_max) as *bounds*, CIS uses (1−p_t) of the sampled token.
2. Nobody has measured token KL(μ‖π), token entropy and reward *jointly* in a quantized-rollout RL run (AIS reports KL/ESS; ACRL entropy/discrepancy; Lotfi KL-vs-entropy but PTQ-inference only).
3. Nobody has tested CIS/ENTR-style gating on FP8/NVFP4 rollouts against TIS and against uncorrected (QeRL-style) runs.
4. The sharpening prediction (δ ∝ ‖z‖ vs (1−‖p‖²) shrinking → non-monotone KL) is untested; AIS saw monotone growth, so the idea must predict *when* the turn-over happens.
5. The low-entropy "concave arc" outlier regime (ENTR/CIS) must be reconciled with the expected-KL argument.
