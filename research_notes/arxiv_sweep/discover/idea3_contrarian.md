# Idea 3 — contrarian sweep (Angle D): extreme-value theory of block-scaled micro-formats

Date: 2026-10-10. Scope: papers that would falsify a factual premise of Idea 3 (NVFP4/MXFP4/UE5M3 distortion as a closed-form function of block size, element grid, scale format, rounding direction; E2M1 shrinkage bias). Quoted text is from downloaded paper text (untrusted data; quoted, not followed).

## Premises of the idea that are at risk

| # | Premise in the report | Status after sweep |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | "포맷 비교는 전부 경험적이다" (format comparisons are purely empirical; no quantitative theory) | **False.** At least five works derive closed-form or 1-D-integral Gaussian-block distortion for block-scaled formats: Soloveychik et al. 2022 (2210.05470), Fasoli et al. ICLR 2026 (2601.19026), Chen et al. 2025 (2510.25602, Theorems 1–2), Egiazarian et al. ICLR 2026 (2509.23202, Lemmas 1–2), Li et al. 2026 (2605.20402, exact decomposition). Classic DSP (Oppenheim 1970) already did statistical roundoff analysis of block floating point. |
| P2 | "스케일 포맷 제안들(UE5M3, 4/6, HiFloat4) 모두 실험 기반" | **Partly false.** UE5M3 was *introduced* by Hu et al. 2509.17791 (experimental) but was *derived from a first-principles MSE framework* by Fasoli et al. 2601.19026 (ICLR 2026). 4/6 and HiFloat4 remain experimental (confirmed by grep: no theorem/derivation in 2512.02010 or 2604.08826). The report also mis-attributes UE5M3 to 2609.02846 (Hu, Luschi, Balanca, Sept 2026), which itself credits Hu et al. for introducing it and Fasoli et al. for analysing it (l.82, l.327 of 2609.02846). |
| P3 | UFP4 shrinkage bias has "no quantitative theory" | **Partly false.** UFP4 (2606.20381) Eq. (2) gives a closed-form conditional bias under locally-uniform density: E[ρ_G(t)−t | t∈B_i] = (2q_i − q_{i−1} − q_{i+1})/4, = −0.125 for the E2M1 bin (1.75, 2.5) around q=2 (and same at q=4 at double scale). It is not Gaussian-weighted and not a per-layer accumulation formula, so the idea's density-weighted numbers (−0.66 %, gain 0.9968) and the (L·bias)² > variance criterion remain open — but the "no theory" framing is wrong. |
| P4 | UE8M0 ceil: s_q/s = 2^U, U ~ U[0,1) "log-uniform" → factor E[4^U] = 2.16 in MSE | **Already published (May 2026).** Li et al. 2605.20402 model the E8M0 ceiling error δ_b = ⌈log2 s*⌉ − log2 s* as "approximately uniform by a Benford's-law argument", γ_b = 2^δ, mean(γ−1) = 1/ln2 − 1 ≈ 0.44, RMSE(γ−1) ≈ 0.57, and prove the scale bias "accumulates multiplicatively through the backward pass" with CLT std √(L/12) (measured 1.97 vs 2.0 predicted for L=48). They also prove an exact three-way decomposition (scale bias / deadzone / grid noise) with Lemma 3.2 orthogonality. The idea's 2.16× MSE factor is one more moment of the same log-uniform model. |
| P5 | A Gaussian/Gumbel model is the right route to the UE8M0 rounding question | **Contradicted in part.** MXAttention (2607.24377) Theorem 1: under ceiling-based E8M0 scaling the MSE-optimal normalized boundary is Q*max = 29/4 = 7.25 "for any absolutely continuous law of U = log2 M"; "Neither a Gaussian nor a log-uniform prior is required for the UOS result" (l.629). The location of the optimum is set by the E2M1 grid, not by the block-max distribution (Lemma 1: g(q/2) = 2g(q)). |
| P6 | Smaller block (16) strictly better than 32; block-size enters only via E[M_B] ratio ≈ 1.10 | **Contradicted for real formats.** Fasoli et al. (2601.19026, ICLR 2026): "the output of a quantized model degrades as block size is decreased below a given threshold" with FP8 UE4M3 scales — inversion at block 16 for granite-3.3-8b, block 8 for llama-3.1-8b and mixtral-8x7b; none for llama-2-7b (Sec. "Anomalous error dependency"). Mechanism: scale underflow into E4M3 subnormals (ε_SF = 2^−9) for narrow blocks. Schaefer & Tabak 2605.08565 confirm and show the inversion disappears with brute-force optimal scales ("the optimal MSE monotonically increases with larger block sizes"). The idea's model has no scale-range/subnormal term at all. |
| P7 | NVIDIA ratio MXFP4/NVFP4 = 1.67 is a format constant to be explained | **Recipe-dependent.** 2509.25149 l.871: "MXFP4 has a relative error of around 2.5% compared to 1.5% for NVFP4" (12B, Fig. 6a, with RHT d=32 for MXFP4 Wgrad). HiFloat4 (2604.08826) Table 3 (50B tokens, each format with its own best stabilisers): MXFP4 1.75/1.44/1.55 % vs NVFP4 1.26/0.99/0.99 % (OpenPangu-1B / Llama3-8B / Qwen3-MoE-30B) → ratios 1.39–1.57; in its ablation "NVFP4 diverges under every combination of stochastic rounding and Hadamard" without per-tensor scaling, and NVFP4 with PTS only (2.00 %) is *worse* than fully stabilised MXFP4 (1.79 %). Ordering can flip with the recipe. |
| P8 | Mishra et al.: floor diverges / ceil matches BF16 — explained by clipping of the max element | Mishra 2506.08027 (l.665–671) only *hypothesises* the mechanism ("We hypothesize this aspect leading to MXFP8 training issues") and shows Fig. 5 ppl curves (843M, 300B/1T tokens) — MXFP8, not FP4. Hu et al. 2509.17791 evaluate both RTN and round-to-+inf for E4M3 and E8M0 scales and also SR on scales; MXAttention formalises OCP = e_floor(8) vs TFS = e_ceil(6) and shows the two "agree except when M/Qmax is an exact power of two" once Qmax is re-parameterised — i.e. floor-vs-ceil is partly a re-labelling of the clipping boundary, not an independent axis. |
| P9 | Post-RHT blocks ≈ Gaussian | Egiazarian 2509.23202 Fig. 2 reports kurtosis 0.02–0.05 for rotated weights/activations (Gaussian fit) vs 1.47 for native (Laplace fit) — **supports** the assumption for inference tensors; 2602.02047 reports persistent block-level spikes under NVFP4 pretraining (contrarian for training). |
| P10 | Max-abs scale s = M_B/g_max is the operating point | **Contradicted as optimum.** ScaleSearch 2605.12464 ("reduces quantization error by 27% for NVFP4"), ScaleSweep 2606.07618 (Lemmas 4.2–4.3 bound the MSE-optimal E4M3 block scale), SOAR 2605.12245 (closed-form joint scale optimisation), 4/6 (2512.02010). Any theory of abs-max scaling describes a known-suboptimal baseline. Also 4/6 Fig. 5: "Scale Selection with MAE Outperforms MSE For Large-Scale Pre-Training" — MSE is not the right metric for training-time format ranking. |
| P11 | Unbiasedness must be bought with SR variance | Quartet II 2601.22813: unbiased MS-EDEN "has more than 2x lower quantization error than SR" for micro-scaled formats — the bias/variance frontier is not the SR frontier. |

## Candidates (ranked)

1. **2601.19026** Fasoli, Kar, Liu, Venkataramani, Srinivasan, Chang, Wang — *Is Finer Better? The Limits of Microscaling Formats in LLMs* (ICLR 2026). Eq. (1)–(5): conditional MSE per E2M1 Voronoi bin given x_max, integrated against f_xmax(θ) = (2N/σ)(2Φ(θ/σ)−1)^{N−1}φ(θ/σ) (half-normal max of N); App. F adds UE4M3 scale quantisation (RTN bins, zero-scale term Eq. 39–41). "agreement … extremely good (χ² ≈ 2·10⁻⁹)" with Normal and real weights. Proposes UE5M3 from this analysis. Threat: **partially_preempts** (NVFP4-style E4M3-scale, RTN; not UE8M0/ceil/floor, not shrinkage bias, no Gumbel asymptotics). Priority 1.
2. **2210.05470** Soloveychik, Lyubomirsky, Wang, Bhoja — *Block Format Error Bounds and Optimal Block Size Selection* (2022). Gaussian blocks, Gumbel appendix (Eq. 24–28), Prop. 1 (SBFP = exact scale) and Prop. 2 (BFP = scale rounded to power of two "from above", Eq. 5) asymptotic variance bounds with explicit ⌈·⌉ jumps "at those block sizes at which the expected values of the scales pass over integer powers of two"; Props. 3–4 finite-n; REBAC ratio; "if the precision of the BFP format is fixed at 4 bits, the optimal block size becomes n = 64"; validated on GPT2-XL. Threat: **partially_preempts** (uniform integer mantissa only, ceil only, no E4M3 scale, no E2M1). Priority 1.
3. **2605.20402** Li et al. — *Decomposing MXFP4 quantization error for LLM RL: reducible bias, recoverable deadzone, irreducible floor* (May 2026). Exact decomposition e = e_scale + e_DZ + e_grid (Def. 3.1, Lemma 3.2); E8M0 ceil error δ ~ Uniform[0,1) (Benford), γ = 2^δ, mean 0.44, RMSE 0.57; multiplicative accumulation in backward pass (Eq. 4, CLT); cos(e_scale, e_grid) ≈ −0.66 "an empirical constant that we do not derive in closed form"; deadzone 9 % of elements. Threat: **partially_preempts** (UE8M0 ceil moment analysis and layer accumulation already done; no NVFP4/E4M3, no block-size law). Priority 1.
4. **2510.25602** Chen et al. — *INT v.s. FP: A Comprehensive Study of Fine-Grained Low-bit Quantization Formats* (Oct 2025). Theorem 1 (INT QSNR) and Theorem 2 (FP QSNR) for i.i.d. N(0,σ²) blocks with crest factor κ, UE8M0 overhead ρ∈[1,2) (round-up convention, set to 1.5), E4M3 ρ=1 with 10log10(g/(g−1)) gain, subnormal probability p_sub; crossover plots (Fig. 3). Threat: **partially_preempts** (closed-form format comparison incl. scale format and block size via κ; crude ρ treatment leaves room). Priority 1.
5. **2607.24377** Yu et al. — *MXAttention: Data-Free Optimal Scaling and Pre-Normalization Quantization for MXFP4 Attention* (Jul 2026). Theorem 1: Q*max = 7.25 distribution-free under ceiling E8M0 scaling; formalises OCP floor e_floor(8) vs TFS e_ceil(6). Threat: **contradicts** (Gaussian/Gumbel prior not needed for the UE8M0 rounding optimum). Priority 2.
6. **2605.08565** Schaefer, Tabak — *Finer is Better (with the Right Scaling)* (May 2026). Explains the Fasoli inversion via E4M3 subnormal scales × small-block statistics; prevent-zero-scale, 4/6, UE5M3 and hierarchical scales remove it; brute-force optimal scale → MSE monotone in block size. Threat: **contradicts** (naive abs-max + E4M3 is non-monotone in B). Priority 2.
7. **2606.20381** Zhao et al. — *Rethinking Shrinkage Bias … UFP4* (Jun 2026). Eq. (2) closed-form per-bin bias; E2M1 q=2 bin → −0.125; RHT "forces data into E2M1's most asymmetric rounding bins" (Fig. 2, ΔSQNR<0); loss gaps 1.2570→0.9673 % (1.5B), 2.3596→1.8469 % (MoE 7.9B), 1.7308→1.3863 % (MoE 124B). Threat: **partially_preempts** the shrinkage-bias quantification. Priority 2.
8. **2604.08826** Taghian et al. — *HiFloat4 Format for LM Pre-training on Ascend NPUs* (Apr 2026, v2 Sep 2026). Table 3 numbers above; "NVFP4 diverges under every combination of SR and Hadamard" without PTS. Threat: **contradicts** (format ranking is recipe-dependent; NVFP4 not uniformly ≤ MXFP4). Priority 2.
9. **2509.23202** Egiazarian et al. — *Bridging the Gap … Microscaling FP4* (ICLR 2026). Sec. 3.1 Lemma 1 (top-element MSE after Hadamard), Lemma 2 rates R_L(G) = Θ((log G)² G^{−δ}), R_N(G) = Θ(√log G · G^{−δ²}) with dead-zone δ = q_min/2 → Laplace/Normal crossover in G; claims "NVFP4's small group size provably neutralizes traditional outlier mitigation"; MXFP4 ~10 % relative drop. Threat: **partially_preempts** (asymptotic block-size law with dead zone, Gaussian vs Laplace). Priority 2.
10. **2606.07618** Lin et al. — *ScaleSweep* (May 2026). Theoretical lower/upper bounds for the MSE/WMSE-optimal E4M3 block scale (Lemmas 4.2–4.3, n=16). With 2605.12464 ScaleSearch (27 % NVFP4 error reduction) and 2605.12245 SOAR (closed-form joint scale optimisation). Threat: **contradicts** (abs-max is not the operating point practitioners use at the optimum). Priority 3.
11. **2601.22813** Panferov, Schultheis, Tabesh, Alistarh — *Quartet II* (Jan 2026). MS-EDEN unbiased, >2× lower error than SR; "We show analytically that Quartet II achieves consistently better gradient estimation". Threat: **contradicts** the SR-based bias/variance criterion. Priority 3.
12. **2512.00956** Chen et al. — *WUSH: Near-Optimal Adaptive Transforms for LLM Quantization* (ICML 2026). Theorem 4.1: closed-form optimal blockwise transforms under RTN AbsMax-scaled block quantisers for FP and INT, Gaussian/Laplacian; reports Hadamard alone harmful for NVFP4, MXFP4 "a hybrid between ideal FP" and INT. Threat: **tangential/partially_preempts** (expected-error machinery for abs-max FP blocks). Priority 3.
13. **2509.17791** Hu, Luschi, Balanca — *Elucidating the Design Space of FP4 training* (Sep 2025). Introduces UE5M3; evaluates RTN vs round-to-+inf for E4M3/E8M0 scales and SR on scales ("E8M0 has very large intervals … potential bias"). Threat: **supports** (rounding-direction axis is real) but experimental. Priority 3.
14. **2512.02010** Cook et al. — *Four Over Six* (Dec 2025, v2 May 2026). No derivation (confirmed); Fig. 5: MAE scale selection beats MSE for large-scale pretraining. Threat: **supports** P2, **contradicts** MSE as the training-relevant metric. Priority 4.
15. **DOI 10.1109/TAU.1970.1162085** Oppenheim — *Realization of digital filters using block-floating-point arithmetic* (IEEE Trans. Audio Electroacoust. 1970). Statistical roundoff-noise analysis of block floating point; shows "first quantitative theory of block scaling" is not a defensible claim. (Kalliojärvi & Astola 1996 "Roundoff errors in block-floating-point systems", IEEE TSP — recalled, could not be verified online in this sweep.) Threat: **tangential**. Priority 4.

Also seen, not ranked: 2609.08135 KBBQ (closed-form SNR law; format enters only as intercept C_fmt, e.g. per-tensor E4M3 median 28.42 dB), 2603.10444 "Curse and Blessing of Mean Bias" (mean = rank-one activation mean, *not* rounding bias — terminology collision), 2602.02047 (persistent block spikes under NVFP4 pretraining), 2605.12327 Grid Games (PO2 multi-grid theory: "advantage vanishes for very large groups"), 2608.25188 (survey: "MXFP4's power-of-two block scale still rewards a rotation confined to that block, and NVFP4's mantissa-carrying scale largely removes that pull"), 2607.24953 (transposition-induced scale inconsistency as the FP4 instability source — a mechanism outside the idea's model), 2501.02423 Sun et al. (block-size enters only as the empirical fit L(B) = κ log2 B + ψ, Sec. 3.6 — confirms the report's [U] note).

## What is still open (where the idea could survive)
- No paper combines, in one model: Gumbel asymptotics × E2M1 non-uniform grid × {E4M3 RTN, UE8M0 ceil, UE8M0 floor, UE5M3} × block size, with the floor-clipping term and density-weighted shrinkage bias. Fasoli covers E4M3-RTN numerically (not closed form); Soloveychik covers integer grids with ceil; Li covers UE8M0 ceil moments; Chen covers crest-factor QSNR with ρ=1.5.
- The E8M0 **floor** case (OCP v1) has no distortion derivation anywhere found; Mishra's divergence remains a hypothesis.
- Gaussian-weighted shrinkage bias with multiplicative depth accumulation and the (L·bias)² vs variance criterion is not derived (UFP4 uses locally-uniform density; Li et al. derive accumulation for *scale* bias only).
- The 1.20 vs 1.67 gap is probably not a heavy-tail question only: HiFloat4 and 4/6 show it moves with recipe (PTS, SR, RHT, MAE vs MSE scale rule).
