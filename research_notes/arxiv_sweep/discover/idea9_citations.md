# Idea 9 — RoPE pair-mixing lemma: citation-neighbourhood sweep (Angle C)

Date: 2026-10-10. Scope: prior art / evidence for (i) Lemma 9.1 (pair-equivariant quantizers are pre/post-RoPE invariant), (ii) Lemma 9.2 (per-channel max-abs gain from pre-RoPE concentrates in pairs with G·θ_f ≳ π), (iii) Corollary 9.3 (query-energy-weighted reverse water-filling over frequency pairs), (iv) YaRN interaction.
All quoted text is untrusted paper content; numbers are copied with their table/section.

## Headline verdicts

1. **Corollary 9.3 is essentially already in Block-GTQ (2606.24033).** The idea text says 9.3 "combines Block-GTQ's key-energy allocation with query-energy weighting". Block-GTQ's score is already `s_i := ½ E[‖q^(i)‖² + ‖k^(i)‖²]` (Sec. 3.1, Eq. 1; GQA form Eq. 4 in App. "Calibration Robustness": "The Q-side term averages squared norms over the query heads served by the KV head"). It is an AM-GM upper bound on `s*_i = E[‖q^(i)‖‖k^(i)‖]` (App. A.3, Eq. 3). Its objective `Σ_i s_i 4^{-b_i}` with greedy integer allocation (Theorem 1, greedy optimality) is the integer version of reverse water-filling. Lemma 2 (App. A.2) is exactly the per-block Cauchy–Schwarz bound `E_{n,m} ≤ Σ_i ‖q_n^(i)‖‖e_k^(i)‖` using rotation orthogonality, and Sec. 2.1 states "norm-based block statistics are RoPE-invariant—we can therefore compute the energy score in pre-RoPE coordinates." What Block-GTQ does **not** do: compare pre- vs post-RoPE for per-channel (KIVI-style) quantizers, or model the within-group phase sweep G·θ_f. Its local quantizer (TQ-MSE: normalize + shared rotation + scalar quantize) is itself rotation-invariant, so Lemma 9.1 applies to it trivially.

2. **The phase-averaging mathematics of Lemma 9.2 appears (in covariance form) in 2608.13365.** It defines finite phase averages `C_k = (1/L) Σ_m cos(2mθ_k)`, `S_k = (1/L) Σ_m sin(2mθ_k)` (Eq. 1) and the position-averaged pooled covariance `Σ̃^(k) = (1/L) Σ_m R_m Σ^(k) R_m^T` (Eq. 2); notes "for high-frequency pairs, both sums (C_k, S_k) … are close to zero, so the surrogate covariance approaches isotropy" (Sec. 6.2; "At L = 2048, 17 of the 64 deployed 3B pairs satisfy |(C_k,S_k)| < 0.01"). Lemma 2 there: for distinct RoPE angles the O(d_h) centraliser of {R_m} is exactly the product of per-pair planar rotations — the "pair-equivariant" family of Lemma 9.1, with the converse stated. It also measures within-pair anisotropy (mean |ρ| 0.135–0.175; mean |log variance ratio| 0.36–0.58) and explicitly flags that a dynamic per-token quantizer's scale is "a tokenwise range over a group that spans multiple frequency pairs" (Sec. 2), i.e. a max-abs statistic rather than a second moment — which is the statistic Lemma 9.2 analyses, but over **tokens per channel** instead of channels per token. It contains no pre- vs post-RoPE experiment. Negative result: head-shared pairwise-only rotation is worse than full-head Hadamard by +0.05 (Mistral-7B-v0.3) to +1.33 PPL (Llama-3.2-1B) under dynamic W4A4KV4 (Table 1); Table 3 mixing-support interpolation b=2→128 shows K range, relative K error and PPL gap decrease monotonically with mixing support.

3. **The quantity G·θ_f and a two-band bit split already drive SpectrumQuant (ACL 2026, not on arXiv).** "Resonating with RoPE: Spectral Quantization for High-Fidelity Key Cache Compression" (Wang, Tang, Liang, Wang, Hu, Guan; 2026.acl-long.1732, pp. 37328–37348). DCT along the **token** axis within a group of size G; Proposition 1 (Spectral Shifting): RoPE shifts channel c's spectral peak to `k* = Gθ_c/π`; Proposition 2: ≥ 6/π² ≈ 61% of energy in the two nearest bins (App. J Theorem 1, Eq. 18, under position-invariant pre-RoPE signal); Proposition 3: spectral distortion = Var of semantic signal (Eq. 19). Allocation: low band (indices ≤ G/2) 4 bits, high band 2 bits, high-frequency pre-emphasis σ∈[2,3] (Sec. 3.3). Cites KVQuant's pre-RoPE and works post-RoPE. It does not analyse per-channel max-abs ranges or pre/post-RoPE; its "frequency" is DCT index along tokens, not the RoPE pair index — but the dependence on G·θ is the same object as Lemma 9.2's mixing factor.

4. **KVQuant itself already reports the quantizer dependence of the pre/post gain.** Table 10 (App. H): LLaMA-7B int3 per-channel keys, post-RoPE 7.05 vs pre-RoPE 6.23 PPL (fp16 5.68) → the 0.82. Table 20 (App. "Post-RoPE Per-Token Quantization Ablation"): with **per-token** keys post-RoPE is better: int3 pre 14.68 / post 10.87; int3-gs128 6.28 / 6.17; int4 6.02 / 5.98; int4-gs128 5.76 / 5.77. Their stated mechanism: "when rotating an outlier channel with large average magnitude and another channel with smaller average magnitude together, at some positions in the sequence, part of the magnitude from the outlier channel will be shifted to the smaller channel. This partially mitigates the impact of the outlier channel on skewing the quantization range for some of the tokens." So "the sign of the pre/post gain depends on the quantizer" is already on record (per-channel vs per-token), verbally, with the same outlier-pair mixing picture. Not on record: the Hadamard/equivariant case, the frequency decomposition, the G-dependence, any MSE formula.

5. **RotateKV (2501.16383) is the key nuance for Lemma 9.1.** Table 2 (LLaMA2-7B, 2-bit key MSE): rotation only — pre-RoPE 0.632 vs post-RoPE 0.670 (≈6% gap); rotation + channel reordering — pre 0.247 vs post 0.605 ("increases by 145%"). A plain (Walsh-)Hadamard is nearly pre/post invariant, consistent with 9.1; an outlier-aware **structured** rotation (reorder-then-rotate) is not pair-equivariant and regains a large pre-RoPE advantage. Lemma 9.1 must be stated for randomized / unstructured rotations only; the idea's blanket "Hadamard → gain disappears" is contradicted for structured Hadamard pipelines.

6. **YAKV = arXiv 2604.08426 "KV Cache Offloading for Context-Intensive Tasks" (confirmed; the idea had it as [U]).** App. L, Table 10: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct MultiNeedle pre-RoPE 93.36 vs post-RoPE 93.73; Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 Text2JSON 90.83 vs 91.05; "We attribute this to the randomized Hadamard transformation used in the HIGGS quantizer." Two data points, no PPL, no error bars.

7. **Rethinking RoPE Scaling (2510.00028) already motivates the high-frequency concentration and the YaRN coupling, heuristically.** Sec. 3.2: "Anisotropy shift: Per-channel quantization grids are axis-aligned; RoPE rotates the signal relative to these grids, making the same step size suboptimal at certain phases"; Eq. 14 `E‖ẑ−z‖² ≈ Δ_i²/6 · η_i(m)` with η_i a phase/range factor; "position-dependent logit error that is typically largest for high-frequency i and large |m−n|"; interpolation pressure Ψ_i larger for high-frequency bands (Eq. 7–9). This is motivation, not a theorem, and concerns activation/weight quantization of the Q/K path under PI rather than cached-key pre/post-RoPE. Numbers (Table, Sec. 4): YaRN s=32 at 131K, W4: RTN 9.958, AWQ 8.700, Q-ROAR 7.869 vs FP16 5.386. I did **not** find the idea's "NTK/linear PPL>100" figure in the portions read; treat as unverified.

8. **KV-Latent (2507.11273) is mis-cited by the idea.** Its "low-numbered channels of the RoPE function contribute the majority of the noise" (Sec. 3.3.2) refers to RoPE's own oscillatory/numerical instability when head dimension is reduced (integral-approximation argument, Figs. 3–6, Formula 11), not to quantization noise. It is not evidence that quantization error concentrates in high-frequency pairs.

## What remains open (the idea's defensible core)
- A closed-form per-channel max-abs range model with the within-group phase sweep G·θ_f as the control variable, predicting (a) ≈2× pair MSE for fully mixed outlier pairs, (b) no gain for G·θ_f ≪ 1, (c) the gain migrating to lower frequencies as G grows — nobody states or tests this. 2608.13365 does the second-moment (covariance) version for per-token groups and explicitly says the max-abs tokenwise range is a different statistic.
- A controlled experiment with the quantizer type as the variable (per-channel max-abs vs randomized Hadamard vs structured Hadamard vs polar/VQ) on the same model/data. Evidence is scattered across KVQuant T10/T20, RotateKV T2, YAKV T10 with different models, bits and metrics.
- Lemma 9.1 must exclude structured/outlier-aware rotations (RotateKV) and VQ with non-pair-aligned codebooks.
- Corollary 9.3 should be dropped or re-cast as "Block-GTQ's AM-GM score vs exact product E‖q‖‖k‖ and per-channel instantiation"; the water-filling form is not new.
- YaRN interaction: 2510.00028/Q-ROAR already give band-wise heuristics; a pre/post-RoPE KV-cache experiment under YaRN remains untested, but the "high-frequency bands most sensitive" narrative is theirs.
