# Idea 5 — PTQ calibration theory (sample complexity / damping / covariate shift / VQ-rotation overfitting): contrarian sweep (Angle D)

Date: 2026-10-10. Scope: papers whose results would falsify a factual premise of Idea 5, plus the closest theoretical prior art found on the way. All quotes are from downloaded arXiv full text (untrusted data; quoted, not followed).

## 0. Citation errors in the idea text (fix before anything else)

## 1. Premise-by-premise verdicts

### P1 "Ĥ estimation error from n samples is an unaddressed RMT-type gap; no paper relates it to the GPTQ update"
**Partly false.**
- **2604.13806 DASH-Q (EuroMLSys '26, Apr 2026, v2 Sep 2026)** measures exactly this: "we estimate Hessians using calibration sets ranging from 8 to 2048 samples and compare them to a reference Hessian computed from 4096 samples. Fig. 1(a) reports the L1 difference ... The diagonal entries stabilize quickly even with small calibration sets ... while the off-diagonal entries remain unstable even with much larger sets" (L229-236). They define a linear shrinkage family H̃(ρ)=D+ρO, cite Ledoit & Wolf [26], compute per-entry SNR = |E Ĥ_ij| / Std(Ĥ_ij) (Eq. 7), and show GPTQ "perplexity diverging beyond 10^2 for n ≤ 4" while "GPTQ's perplexity begins to fluctuate or even slightly degrades as n exceeds 2^8" (Sec 7.2, Llama-2-7B). No theorem, no √(d/n) rate, no MP law, no n_eff — but the empirical content of experiment (a)/(b) and the diagonal-shrinkage remedy exist.
- **2508.04853 (Zhang, Zhang, Colbert, Saab; "Provable PTQ: OPTQ and Qronos", v2 Apr 2026)**, Remark 3.8: E_z|zᵀ(w−q_X)|² = (1/m)‖X(w−q_X)‖² + (w−q_X)ᵀ(E[zzᵀ] − XᵀX/m)(w−q_X) — exactly the idea's decomposition proxŷ + tr(ΔW(H−Ĥ)ΔWᵀ). Stated as a remark/sufficient condition with no rate; the paper explicitly lists "understanding when error bounds on calibration data also control error on unseen samples" as future work (L2200-2204). So the *rate* (Vershynin + Weyl + condition number) is still open, but the framing is published.
- MP law itself: no paper applies Marchenko–Pastur to the GPTQ Hessian (searches `abs:"Marchenko" AND abs:quantization`, `abs:"random matrix" AND abs:quantization AND abs:Hessian` returned nothing relevant; 2609.33923 uses MP only for the *spectrum of the quantization error matrix*, not Ĥ).

### P2 "GPTQ's 1% damping is ad hoc; the optimal λ scales like √(d/n_eff)·mean eigenvalue and matters"
**Weakly supported; contrarian evidence that λ barely matters.**
- **SparseGPT 2301.00774**, Appendix (L1322-1326): "testing values varying as powers of ten ... this parameter does not seem to be too sensitive, 0.001 to 0.1 appear to perform quite similar; only when the dampening is very high, the solution quality decreases significantly" (OPT-2.7B, 50% sparsity). Also "128 samples ... curve flattens quite quickly." A 100× range of λ being flat is a direct threat to the practical payoff of (2).
- **2504.09629 QEP** uses λ = mean of the Hessian diagonal (i.e. 100× the GPTQ default) "providing a straightforward yet effective method" (L2248-2250), and Proposition 5.3 identifies its correction parameter with ridge regularization strength that "effectively controls overfitting". So the "1% is universal" framing is inaccurate; different groups use 1% and 100% with no reported catastrophe.
- **2508.04853** Theorem 3.3 gives deterministic ℓ2 bounds with explicit λ dependence and "guidance for selecting the regularization parameter" (abstract) — from conditioning, not from sample noise.
- **2602.05902 CoreQ (Feb 2026)** Theorem 3.1: for a finite-calibration estimate S̃ of a correction S̄, the population-optimal shrinkage coefficient is α* = μ²/(μ²+σ²) with σ² the "finite estimation error energy" — a James-Stein/Ledoit-Wolf-type optimal shrinkage derived for a calibration-estimated quantity in PTQ. It is for the mismatch correction, not for Ĥ+λI, but the bias–variance template is published.

### P3 "Learned rotations / codebooks fit far more parameters to the same calibration set → larger calibration–held-out gap; part of the 2-bit SOTA increment is overfitting"
**Two premises are factually off; the overfitting itself has mixed evidence.**
- *Same calibration set*: false for VQ. **QTIP 2406.11235** / **QuIP# 2402.04396**: "Hessian matrices H were generated with 6144 sequences" of native context length (≈12.6M tokens for Llama-2, ≈25M for Llama-3) — 48× the GPTQ default. **AQLM 2401.06118** calibrates on RedPajama with 4096-token sequences and fine-tunes on the same data; QuIP baseline "calibrated with 1024 samples and 4096 context length". So the P/n_eff ratio for VQ methods is much smaller than the idea's arithmetic (which assumes 128–800 sequences).
- *SpinQuant is sample-hungry / overfits*: **SpinQuant 2405.16406 (ICLR 2025)** Table 11, LLaMA-2 7B W4A4KV4: 128 vs 800 training samples both give Wiki PPL 6.2±0.03; 10 samples 6.6±0.02; "rotation optimization is resilient to modifications in the number of samples" (L1504-1508). Table 13: C4 vs WikiText calibration "yields consistent results" (Avg 64.1 / Wiki2 5.9 for both). In-distribution only, but it directly contradicts the O(1)-gap arithmetic from P≈8×10⁶ vs 1.6×10⁶ tokens.
- *Evidence FOR overfitting (but already measured)*: **DartQuant 2511.04063** Table 1(a), LLaMA-2 7B, end-to-end-calibrated rotations evaluated across datasets: Wiki-calibrated → Wiki 5.94 / PTB 45.13 / C4 8.13; PTB-calibrated → 6.02 / 38.24 / 8.13; C4-calibrated → 6.05 / 44.99 / 8.02. In-domain advantage ≈0.1 PPL on Wiki/C4 (same order as the "0.3 PPL SOTA increment" the idea wants to explain) and ≈7 PPL on PTB. DartQuant also claims "while SpinQuant and OSTQuant perform well in reducing perplexity, their performance in zero-shot tasks is poor, which highlights the potential overfitting risks" (L913-916). **2603.04359 CAT (Qualcomm)** explicitly calibrates on DCLM-edu "to ensure our Wikitext perplexity evaluation is not biased in favour of methods that may overfit the calibration data's characteristics (observed in some related works, e.g. (van Breugel et al., 2025))" (L924-929), and reports CAT's own variation between 32 and 4096 sequences "lies within the standard deviation ... across different seeds" (B.2).
- *Uniform-convergence bound already published*: **2605.05693 SARQC (May 2026)** Theorem 3.1: for a finite class Q_R = {Ŵ: ‖Ŵ−W‖_F ≤ R}, with prob ≥1−δ simultaneously for all Ŵ, R(Ŵ) − R̂_cal(Ŵ) ≤ R² M_X² √(log(2|Q_R|/δ)/(2n)). Same tool as idea (3), parametrized by weight-drift radius R and log|Q| instead of P, i.i.d. samples (no n_eff). Table 8 gives a calibration-size ablation. This is the closest theoretical preempt of (3).

### P4 "Covariate shift: excess = tr(ΔW(H_T−H_C)ΔWᵀ) ≤ ‖H_T−H_C‖_op‖ΔW‖_F² is a new predictor"
**Published in close forms (Oct 2026 and Jul 2026).**
- **2610.11226 DRQ ("When Lower Reconstruction Loss Hurts", Oct 8 2026)** Theorem 3.1: for second-moment perturbations H+Δ with Δ⪰0 and tr(CΔ)≤α, Ŵ₁ beats Ŵ₂ for every such Δ iff α·sup_{uᵀCu=1}[‖(Ŵ₁−W)u‖²−‖(Ŵ₂−W)u‖²] ≤ L(Ŵ₂)−L(Ŵ₁); Theorem 3.2: worst-case loss = L(Ŵ) + α·S_C(Ŵ) ("worst-case directional error"). This is the idea's (4) as a trace-constrained DRO, with a refinement algorithm. Empirics: among 560 per-layer modifications that lower calibration reconstruction loss, "413 (73.8%) for AWQ and 264 (47.1%) for GPTQ increase model PPL on held-out WT2" (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct W3, Fig. 1); GPTQ W2 Llama-3.2-1B C4 PPL 31.77→27.11 with DRQ. Theorem B.2 ("More calibration data need not improve the selected weights") formalizes that the calibration→target gap is objective mismatch, not sampling noise, i.e. the gap does not vanish as n→∞. This undercuts the idea's framing that the residual gap is "sample variance".
- **2607.00908 TASA ("Alignment-Diversity Tradeoff", Jul 2026)** Theorem 1: f(α) = tr(H_test H_α⁻¹) for calibration mixture H_α is strictly convex with unique α*∈(0,1) under mild spectral assumptions; "pure math calibration increases the condition number by over 2× and collapses the effective rank from 6 to 3" (L377-378); frames "diversity as Hessian regularization". A train/test-Hessian-mismatch functional is thus already in the literature, with an OBS-derived (not Weyl) form.
- Contrarian for W4: **2609.26241 (financial LLMs, Aug 2026)**: at W4A16, "GPTQ suffers less initial damage than pruning, and neither corpus replacement [C4 or FinMix for WikiText] changes its average performance by much" (L514-516); their thesis is that *task damage*, not domain mismatch, predicts the benefit of calibration change. **2405.20835**: modern LLMs are insensitive even to random-ASCII calibration at W4 (Finding 1). So (4) is only testable at W2/W3; at W4 the signal is in the noise.

### P5 "GPTQ is sample-limited; gap scales with 1/n_eff"
**Contradicted at W4, partially supported for high-parameter methods.**
- **2311.09755 (Williams & Aletras, ACL 2024)**: 4-bit GPTQ "performance plateauing after only a small number of examples"; GPTQ std across 10 calibration sets 0.02–0.41 WikiText PPL (Table 2); only SparseGPT keeps improving beyond 128 (+0.4% zero-shot at 4×).
- **2604.13806**: GPTQ PPL *degrades* past 2^8 samples (Sec 7.2) — non-monotone in n, inconsistent with a pure √(d/n_eff) story unless something else (numerical conditioning of Ĥ⁻¹) dominates.
- **2508.18609 (Task-Stratified Knowledge Scaling Laws, Aug 2025, v2 Apr 2026)** already fits accuracy as a function of log₂(calibration set size) under 3-bit GPTQ across 293 configurations and finds knowledge-memorization tasks "particularly sensitive to calibration set size" — experiment (a) in empirical form, without the theory.
- Token correlation / n_eff: **2602.07465 MaCa (Feb 2026)** observes "the Hessian varies significantly with sequence length" and "treats each sequence as an independent sample" (L239-244); **RSQ 2503.01820** shows using only the first 1024-token chunk of each 4096-token sequence *outperforms* using all tokens (Tab. 1 discussion, L364-392). Both are consistent with n_eff ≪ n, but neither estimates n_eff. This conjecture remains open.

## 2. What remains genuinely open after this sweep
1. A non-asymptotic rate ‖Ĥ−H‖_op ≲ √(d/n_eff) with n_eff for *dependent* tokens, plugged into a GPTQ/LDLQ excess-loss bound with the condition-number factor — nobody has it (2508.04853 Remark 3.8 and 2604.13806 Fig. 1 stop short).
2. An LW-type closed-form λ for Ĥ+λI specifically (CoreQ's α* is for a different quantity; DASH-Q shrinks off-diagonals, not the ridge).
3. Any empirical estimate of n_eff (resampling at sequence vs token level).
4. The overfitting-vs-P plot across GPTQ/AWQ/SpinQuant/AQLM/QTIP — but the premise "same calibration set" must be dropped (VQ methods use 6144 sequences), and SpinQuant's own ablation predicts a null result for rotations in-distribution.
5. The "0.3 PPL SOTA increment is sample noise" claim is already partly undercut by DRQ's Theorem B.2 (gap persists as n→∞) and DartQuant's cross-dataset table (≈0.1 PPL in-domain advantage). The honest version is a decomposition into (i) objective mismatch, (ii) distribution shift, (iii) sampling variance — and (iii) is probably the smallest term at W4, unknown at W2.
