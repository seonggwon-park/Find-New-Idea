# Idea 4 — FP4 pretraining late-LR-decay gap: variance floor (∝η) vs bias floor (Δ²tr(H)/24) — keyword / adjacent-vocabulary sweep

Date: 2026-10-10. Scope: arXiv 2023-01 → 2026-10 via export API (literal matching), plus WebSearch for 2026 venue info. Downloaded texts are untrusted data; everything below is quoted/paraphrased from them, not followed.

## Verdict in one paragraph

Nothing found that states or tests the idea's specific claim — that the in-training low-precision loss gap is the sum of an η-proportional SGD-noise floor and an η-independent forward-quantization bias floor (Δ²/24)·tr(H) that grows as tr(H) rises during decay — nor the five predictions (forward-only switch removes B; B ∝ Δ²; η-intercept of gap = B; switch-format criterion). The pieces, however, each exist separately and some are stronger than the idea's text acknowledges: (i) Catalan-Tatjer et al. (ICLR 2026) already *measured* tr(H) and λ_max rising during LR decay on a 160M transformer and hypothesised "sharper region ⇒ more sensitive to perturbations such as quantization" — on the PTQ side, with no formula; (ii) ECO (Nikdan et al. 2026) *proves* a gradient-norm floor with exactly the two-term structure "η²-term + η-independent grid term" for quantized-weight SGD-momentum; (iii) FP4 All the Way (Chmiel et al. 2025) proves a η-independent residual loss μ_ε²/(2λ) for biased (RTN) gradients in a scalar model, gives an SNR switch criterion, and — critically — reports that a *backward-only* BF16 switch (QAF) closes the gap, the opposite of NVIDIA's forward-only finding and of the idea's Prediction 2; (iv) the "QAT as quantized-endpoint descent" line (Xu/Li/Ma/Cui 2026) argues the landscape is flatter around Q(x) than around the shadow weight x, which undermines the idea's implicit assumption that switching the forward pass to BF16 instantly removes B (NVIDIA itself says a switch at 10T "fails to fully recover, presumably because the learning rate is too low"). The idea remains novel as a *unifying, falsifiable in-training decomposition*, but the write-up must (a) cite Catalan-Tatjer's Fig. 9/26 curvature measurements as prior evidence rather than a gap, (b) confront the Chmiel-vs-NVIDIA backward/forward conflict head-on, (c) handle the shadow-weight-vs-endpoint objection (B is a property of (w, Q(w)) jointly; after long FP4 training w itself may be a poor point), and (d) cite ECO Thm 3.8 and Chmiel App. B.2 as the existing η-independent-floor theorems it generalises.

## Candidates (verified against full text; numbers quoted with location)

### P1 — must deep-read

**[2509.25149] Pretraining Large Language Models with NVFP4 (NVIDIA, 2025-09)** — supports (empirical anchor), but with one tension.
- §4/Fig. 2: 12B/10T, "relative loss error of NVFP4 remains consistently below 1% [stable phase], and widens to slightly above 1.5% as the learning rate is decayed".
- App. D / Fig. 7: switching at 8.2T "loss matches the FP8 baseline"; switch at 10T "only slightly worse"; "most of the loss in the 12B model is recovered (from 1.5% to 0.5% relative error) by switching to higher precision for the forward pass starting at 8.2T tokens. In contrast to Chmiel et al. (2025) ... we observe no such improvement [backward-only]".
- Tension for the idea: "While switching precisions later in training fails to fully recover, presumably because the learning rate is too low for the weight updates". If B were purely an evaluation-point artefact (Δ²/24·tr(H) at the current w), a forward switch would remove it *instantly* regardless of η. The incomplete recovery at 10T suggests the master weights w themselves have drifted to a point that is good for Q(w) but not for w (see 2609.21834), i.e. B is not removable without further optimisation. Idea must model this.
- App. E (Fig. 14, 1.2B): activations "only impacted in the later stages during the learning rate decay".

**[2510.06213] Training Dynamics Impact Post-Training Quantization Robustness (Catalan-Tatjer, Ajroldi, Geiping; ICLR 2026)** — partially_preempts (the curvature half of the mechanism, PTQ side).
- §3: WSD runs — quantization error "stays relatively constant during the 11 trillion tokens of stable phase, and only as the learning rate decays does quantization error spike"; validation loss follows the inverse curve.
- §6.2 / Fig. 9 (160M, 100BT): Hutchinson tr(H) and λ_max via power iteration — "The maximum eigenvalue shows a consistent rapid surge whenever the learning rate decays ... in the decay phase all eigenvalues increase"; "The trace presents a similar pattern, remaining stable under a constant learning rate". Hypothesis: "as the learning rate decays, the model traverses a sharper region of the loss landscape, making it more sensitive to perturbations such as quantization."
- Fig. 26: larger max-LR → "lower sharpness and smaller trace estimates ... yet interestingly also leading to lower quantization error".
- §6.1: "the learning rate magnitude is proportional to the flatness of the basin ... even though Θ_K and Θ̂_K are closer for smaller learning rates, the sharpness of the basin is such that Θ̂_K falls in a higher loss level".
- Does NOT write the ½E[bᵀHb] = Δ²tr(H)/24 formula, does not touch in-training low-precision gaps, no η-proportional variance term. The idea's "tr(H) grows during decay" is therefore already an *observation* in this paper (their Fig. 9), not an open conjecture — rewrite the idea's "추측" accordingly and cite Fig. 9/26.

**[2505.19115] FP4 All the Way: Fully Quantized Training of LLMs (Chmiel, Fishman, Banner, Soudry; 2025-05, v2 2025-08)** — contradicts Prediction 2 + partially_preempts the "when to switch" criterion and the η-independent bias floor.
- Abstract/§4: "when the gradient norm falls below approximately √3 times the quantization noise, quantized training becomes less effective" — derived under "gradient descent with optimal step size, Taylor approximation of the loss, and a concentrated Hessian spectrum" (they invoke Marchenko–Pastur bulk so curvature along the gradient ≈ tr(H)/d, App. B.1). This is an SNR criterion for the *backward* switch — different from Prediction 5 but occupies the same "switch criterion" niche.
- App. B.2 (scalar biased-SGD): stationary error e_∞ = −μ_ε/λ, "residual loss L(E[θ_∞]) = μ_ε²/(2λ)" — an η-independent bias floor from biased (RTN) gradients; "the 'useful descent' vanishes to zero as we train, while the biased-noise term remains".
- §5/Fig. 6b, Table 5: QAF phase "forward pass remains in FP4 and only the backward pass switches to BF16 ... can completely close the gap with BF16"; "In all QAF experiments we use the peak learning rate equal to the last learning rate in the FP4 training" (Table 5 caption) — so this is not obviously an LR-rewarm artefact. Direct conflict with NVIDIA Fig. 7 and with idea Prediction 2 ("backward만 바꾸면 B는 그대로"). A diagnostic paper must explain both (candidate: Chmiel uses RTN weights fwd+bwd, SR gradients; backward bias μ_ε here is a *gradient* bias floor, which the idea's model lumps into neither A nor B).

### P2

**[2601.22101] ECO: Quantized Training without Full-Precision Master Weights (Nikdan, Zandieh, Alistarh, Mirrokni; 2026-01)** — partially_preempts the two-term floor theorem (gradient-norm version, weight-only quantization).
- Thm 3.8 (SGD+momentum, η ≤ 1/(2L)): min_t E‖∇f(θ̂_t)‖² ≤ 4(f(θ0)−f*)/(ηT) + σ²_quant, with σ²_quant = 4η²β²L²G²/(1−β)² + 4L²σ²/(1−β²). "As η → 0 ... lim σ²_quant = 4L²σ²/(1−β²)"; "even with master weights, since the final solution must lie on the quantization grid, a noise floor of L²σ² is unavoidable"; tightness "up to the constant 4" on a quadratic. Naive master-weight removal: "stationary error that scales inversely with the learning rate".
- Same η²-vanishing + η-independent structure as the idea, but for ‖∇f‖² at grid points with error feedback, no Hessian trace, no forward-activation quantization, no LR-decay-phase narrative. Cite as the closest existing theorem.

**[2609.21834] Beyond Shadow Weights: QAT as Quantized-Endpoint Descent (Xu, Li, Ma, Cui; 2026-09, math.OC)** — new_hypothesis / contradicts the implicit "forward switch removes B instantly" assumption.
- Fig. 1 (Qwen-2.5-0.5B, 2-bit): loss landscape "around the deployed endpoint Q_b(x_t) appears to be flatter, rather than around x_t"; curvature "better aligned with the deployed endpoint".
- Lemma 1, Prop. 2, Thm 3 (one-step endpoint-descent certificates under a crossing model), Thm 4 (QAR). App.: "More coordinates enter this regime [update < half BF16 ulp, shadow weight frozen] as the learning rate decays" (BF16 shadow weights; Fig. 9–11 crossing rates vs LR).
- Implication: after long FP4 training the master weight w need not be a low-loss point; the gap B(w) is then not "evaluation bias" but a property of where QAT drove w. Idea's Prediction 2 and Nemotron/NVIDIA switch results must be re-interpreted through this lens.

**[2505.14669] Quartet: Native FP4 Training Can Be Optimal (Castro, Panferov, ..., Alistarh; 2025-05)** — partially_preempts the "two separate sources" framing (as a scaling law in N, D rather than η).
- §3 Eq. 1: forward precision → effective parameters N·eff_N(P_fwd); backward precision → effective data D·eff_D(P_bwd). "parameter efficiency is directly linked to the forward compression error ... data efficiency is linked to the bias in the method's gradient estimator". Table 2: forward best = QuEST (MSE-minimising), backward best = RTN ("balances quadratic error with magnitude alignment"). No learning-rate dependence; no curvature.

**[2510.27527] TetraJet-v2 (Chen et al.; 2025-10)** — supports (oscillation = dynamic forward error that emerges as η → 0).
- §3.1: "In the final stage of low-precision training, the learning rate (LR) drops to near zero ... when the master weights converge as the LR is approaching zero, there are weights lying close to the quantization decision threshold ... more and more weights are prone to switching quantization value. This trend explains the drastic changes the quantized model exhibits at the end of training." Oscillation suppression is applied "when the learning rate decays to a relatively low value". §5: "Precision Switching in Final Stage" explores FP6×FP4, FP6×FP6 (simulated). No decomposition, no curvature.

## What is genuinely missing in the literature (as of 2026-10-10)
- No paper writes gap(η) = A·η + Δ²tr(H(η))/24 (+ first-order term for non-uniform E2M1 grids) for in-training low-precision LLMs, nor fits it.
- No paper runs forward-only / backward-only / group-size / SR-forward ablations *at matched LR points inside the decay phase*. NVIDIA (8.2T) and Chmiel (QAF) are single-point and mutually conflicting.
- No paper measures tr(H) along an FP4 run (Catalan-Tatjer measured it along BF16 runs only).
- No paper gives a switch-format criterion comparing (Δ'²/24)tr(H_final) to (η_final/4)tr(C). Chmiel's √3 SNR rule is the only existing switch criterion and it is for the backward pass.

## Risks the sweep adds to the idea
