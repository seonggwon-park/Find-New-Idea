# S10 prior-art check: tie-indifference theorem for quantized MoE route flips

Verdict: **survives_narrowed**. Novelty 2/5.

## What the candidate claims
When a quantized MoE router flips experts, the flip happens near a tie of a router whose scores roughly track expected loss. If that holds, then:
1. The first-order loss change of a flip has conditional mean about 0, so router statistics cannot predict its sign.
2. The route-mediated damage is second order: proportional to the sum over flips of g_tie^2 ||E_k - E_{k+1}||_H^2.
3. Gate-continuous blending removes the damage. Pinning the full-precision route recovers at most this second-order term.

## Premise check against 2608.11212 (Gu, 2026-07-14, single author, arXiv only)
- RMF about 0.31. Confirmed at line 18: "RMF ≈ 0.31". The process-replicated value is 0.313 ± 0.020. A pre-registered re-execution gave 0.231. The real int4 kernel gave RMF 0.194 [-0.111, 0.394] (line 398), and that interval includes 0.
- AUC 0.772 for detecting a flip, AUC 0.490 for predicting harm given a flip, P(harmful|flip) = 0.572. All three confirmed (lines 227-229, Result 2). The combined logistic sign-AUC is 0.520. An 80-dimensional all-layer router vector also scores at chance (line 699).
- "An empirical barrier, not a theorem." Confirmed verbatim at line 67.
- Normalization convention as a damage-magnitude moderator. Confirmed (lines 381-386). The sign of the effect is the problem: "The one clean single-variable effect of the convention is on the damage magnitude (routed ceiling ×7–10, False=more damage)". The naive g_tie^2 law predicts the opposite. With norm_topk_prob=True, the top-k weights are renormalized upward, which raises g_tie and should mean more damage. The toggled configurations are off-distribution, so this is a caveat, not a refutation. Still, the candidate cannot cite the moderator as support. **This is a premise problem.**
- 2608.11212 also offers its own competing explanation for the chance-level AUC: "the damage is majority nonlocal: a single-layer flip cannot carry a multi-layer harm" (line 236). It also reports P(harm>0 | no scoring-layer flip) = 0.55.
- Lemma 1 (line 646) already states that route jumps lie on tie surfaces and that the output stays continuous when the crossing experts carry vanishing weight. That is the qualitative half of consequence (3).
- 2606.19036 (Huu et al.) covers tie geometry only: thickening volumes (Theorems 4.2, 4.4 and 4.7), first hits being order 1 (Theorem 5.1), and occupation time (Theorem 5.3). It has no loss or sign analysis. Confirmed.
- 2606.05688 (VSRAQ) uses margin and rank-preservation losses (Lmargin, line 460). It has no theory of when a flip is harmful. Confirmed.

## Closest work and overlap
1. **Classical plug-in / margin-condition regret.** Hu, Kallus & Uehara, 2102.00479 (2021): "the regret of the policy it defines converges at a rate given by the exponentiation of the Q*-estimate's pointwise convergence rate". Hu, Kallus & Mao, 2011.03030 (2020) prove the same for contextual linear optimization via near-degeneracy. Earlier: Audibert & Tsybakov's plug-in classifiers. The candidate's Proposition is this argument with router logits as the Q-estimate and quantization noise as the estimation error: flips only happen where the true gap is no larger than the perturbation, so regret is of order sigma^(1+alpha). **The math core is known.** The new part is applying it to MoE routing.
2. **Score-function / Optimal Brain Damage identity.** For a calibrated language model, E_y[grad_z(-log p(y|x))] = 0. So the first-order expected NLL change of *any* perturbation is zero, and expected damage is KL-like, i.e. second order, about 1/2 (g ΔE)^T F (g ΔE). This needs no router calibration. Consequences (1) and (2) therefore do not single out the tie mechanism. Tie-indifference gives an O(m) first-order term *per x before averaging over y*. The Fisher identity gives a zero mean only *after averaging over y*. That distinction is the only one left to test.
3. **2609.12550** (Huang, 2026-09-11). Lemma B.1 and Theorem B.2 give the "first-order term vanishes, leading term is second order" expansion for quantized MoE expert blocks. They explicitly assume that no selected/unselected pair exchanges order. They leave E_flip as "a distinct route-support correction, not an ordinary Taylor remainder" (line ~1416). Same style of argument, with the flip term deliberately left open.
4. **2608.08853** (2026-08-09), on OLMoE: "the router's top-scored expert is the counterfactual-best vertex only 17.2% of the time, with router-utility Spearman 0.030" (lines 59-60, 242). This measures within-top-8 commitment, not the k/k+1 boundary, but it is strong evidence *against* a loss-calibrated router. An uninformative router also predicts sign AUC near 0.5, which competes with the tie-indifference explanation.
5. **2609.32821** (2026-09-26, merged MoE, also OLMoE). Routing drift predicts the NLL gain from restoring routes at chance (AUROC 0.47-0.52). Entering and leaving experts have mean output cosine of only 0.041-0.096. The mixture outputs, however, stay aligned (cosine 0.896-0.976). So the experts that swap are not similar: ||E_k - E_{k+1}|| is not small, and any second-order bound must lean on g_tie being small.
6. **SRA-MoE** (Nota AI, ICML 2026 workshop, abstract only; not found on arXiv): "many others induce negligible output discrepancy despite large routing changes". This is empirical, with no theory.
7. **2607.23390.** Theorem 23 ("transversal top-k events have certified mismatch windows") localizes route disagreement in time. It says nothing about the sign of the loss change.
8. **ReMoE** (2412.14711) introduced flip-rate metrics. Huu et al. and Lemma 1 of 2608.11212 already cover continuous gating that removes jumps.

## What remains
No paper derives the expected loss of a quantization-induced route flip as a function of margin, gate weight at the tie, expert-difference norm and router calibration, or tests that relation on real quantized MoE serving. The specific remaining contribution:
- The decomposition E[dL | flip, m] = a·m + 1/2 g_tie^2 ΔE^T H ΔE, plus measurement of whether the a·m slope is zero. A nonzero slope would mean the router is miscalibrated at the boundary.
- A test that separates the Fisher/score-identity explanation from tie-indifference. Tie-indifference predicts that the *teacher-free* expected KL of a flip is second order and that the first-order term vanishes per x. An uninformative router predicts a nonzero per-x first-order term whose sign is random.
- An explanation, or a refutation, of the norm_topk_prob "False = more damage ×7-10" moderator, which runs opposite to a naive g_tie^2 law.

## Significance
For a main-track reviewer this is weak as a theorem: the math is classical, and the calibration assumption is not true by construction. Unselected experts' logits get no expert-specific gradient, and 2608.08853 measures within-set router-utility Spearman at 0.03. It could be a solid section of an empirical paper, or a workshop paper, if the decomposition is measured on 2-3 MoEs with real low-bit kernels. The underlying effect is small: RMF on the real int4 kernel has a CI that includes 0, and total damage is 0.0757 nats.
