# X06 (A6): Quantized KL anchor = FP anchor + reward shaping βη. Prior-art hunt

Verdict: **survives_narrowed**. Novelty 3/5. Date of hunt: 2026-10-11. About 30 tool calls.

## Bottom line
I found no paper (2023 to Oct 2026) that analyzes how quantizing the **reference or teacher** model changes a
KL-regularized RL or on-policy-distillation (OPD) objective. The identity
KL(π‖π_q) = KL(π‖π_ref) + E_π[log π_ref − log π_q] is a single line of algebra. It is the standard
"KL-to-a-different-prior equals reward shaping" fact, so the identity itself contributes nothing new.
What would be new is applying it to quantized anchors and measuring the consequences.
Two framing claims in the hypothesis are weak or false as stated (see Premise problems). Fix them before building on the idea.

## Premise problems
1. **"Noise" is the wrong word.** η(x,y) = Σ_t[log π_ref(y_t) − log π_q(y_t)] is a *deterministic, systematic* function of the sequence.
   Its mean grows **linearly** in T (E_π[η] ≈ T·KL_tok(π_ref‖π_q) when π≈π_ref). The √T fluctuation is smaller.
   So the dominant effect is a **per-token penalty**, which acts as a length penalty, plus a drift of the optimum to π* ∝ π_q e^{r/β}.
   Zero-mean noise is the secondary effect. The √T law also assumes weakly correlated η_t, but quantization error is systematic
   across positions, so the true growth could lie anywhere between √T and T.
2. **"Comparable to a binary reward" depends on β.** For small perturbations Var(η_t) ≈ 2·KL_tok.
   W4: KL_tok ≈ 0.02 nats gives sd(η_t) ≈ 0.2. With T = 8k, sd(η) ≈ 18 nats and mean(η) ≈ 160 nats.
   At β = 1e-3 (the verl/Jet-RL/QuRL default), β·sd ≈ 0.018, about 4% of a Bernoulli(0.5) reward sd of 0.5. That is not comparable.
   At β = 0.04 (DeepSeekMath GRPO, old TRL default), β·sd ≈ 0.7, which is comparable. The claim must be stated as a function of β·√T·sd(η_t).
   KL_tok for W4 has to be measured; 0.02 is an assumption.
3. **The "exactly equivalent" claim holds for KL-in-reward (PPO-style) or the exact-KL objective, not for GRPO's k3-as-loss.**
   In k3-as-loss the anchor enters through the ratio π_q/π_θ, so the gradient change is not the additive reward βη.
   The author already marks this as unproven. It is a real gap in the claim, not a footnote, because most quantization-RL stacks
   (verl `use_kl_loss`) use exactly this form.
4. **Relevance is limited by practice.** Many recipes set β = 0: DAPO, 2609.22870, QeRL, CIS.
   In QLoRA/PEFT RLHF (TRL), the anchor is the quantized base with adapters disabled, which is the *same* model as the policy's initialization.
   There the anchor is self-consistent and η relative to the FP model is irrelevant.
   The idea matters only for (a) an FP or BF16 actor with a low-precision ref (Jet-RL FP8 ref, or offloaded INT8/INT4 refs), and (b) OPD with a quantized teacher.
5. "FP8 anchor is harmless" is consistent with Jet-RL training fine at FP8-ref and β = 1e-3. That is anecdotal: no ablation exists.
6. Gap-claim citations check out: the 2609.22870 quote is exact, and 2601.18150 addresses only the mismatch. One correction: Jet-RL (2601.14243) *does* quantize the reference to FP8 and keeps a KL loss. Cite it as a deployed instance of the setting, not as a stack that ignores the anchor.

## What overlaps
- The algebraic identity and the shifted optimum π* ∝ π_q e^{r/β} are textbook KL-regularized RL facts (the reference-choice literature, e.g. 2407.13709).
- The fact that quantized LLM distributions are flatter (higher entropy) is QeRL's finding (2510.11696 §3.2).
- The practice of an FP8 reference with KL in on-policy FP8 RL is in Jet-RL (2601.14243, l.1313).

## What remains (novel as far as I can find)
- A measured decomposition of anchor quantization into (i) a linear-in-T per-token penalty (a length bias) and (ii) a fluctuation term, both as functions of format (FP8/W8A8/W4/NVFP4) and β.
- Whether a W4 or NVFP4 anchor or teacher causes measurable length shortening, entropy drift, or reward-hacking toward tokens where π_q > π_ref.
- The k3-as-loss gradient under a perturbed anchor, either by derivation or by showing it is biased differently from KL-in-reward.
- OPD with a quantized teacher: the student provably converges to π_T^q, and the open question is how much downstream accuracy that costs per format.
- A cheap fix: correct the anchor log-probs with a calibrated per-token offset, or use an FP anchor only on high-|η_t| tokens.

## Sharpened hypothesis
With an FP actor and a quantized anchor, KL-regularized RL optimizes r − β·Σ_t[log π_ref − log π_q] on top of the FP objective.
Its leading effect is a **systematic per-token penalty β·KL_tok(π‖π_q)** that biases toward shorter, higher-entropy (π_q-like) outputs.
That effect scales as βT. The zero-mean part scales as β√T·sd(η_t) and is secondary.
Prediction: FP8 anchors (KL_tok ~1e-3) are harmless at any common β. W4/NVFP4 anchors are harmless at β = 1e-3 but measurably shorten CoT and raise entropy at β ≥ 0.01-0.04 on 8k-token RLVR.
For OPD (effective β = 1), a quantized teacher caps the student at the quantized teacher's accuracy.
Test: Qwen3-1.7B/4B GRPO with KL-in-reward and with k3-loss, ref ∈ {BF16, FP8, W4A16, NVFP4}, β ∈ {1e-3, 1e-2, 4e-2}. Measure response length, entropy, accuracy and the realized E[η]/T.

## Significance (main-track reviewer view)
Modest. The math is one line, and the realistic default (β = 1e-3, FP8 ref) is predicted to be harmless.
A reviewer will ask why anyone would quantize the anchor while keeping a BF16 actor.
Jet-RL is the answer: it does exactly that, for a 1.8× ref speedup.
The work becomes interesting only with a surprising measured effect: a W4 or NVFP4 anchor or teacher that causes length or entropy drift, or an OPD accuracy loss, that people are unknowingly incurring.
Otherwise it is a workshop note or an appendix section in an FP8/FP4-RL systems paper.
