# S07 prior-art check: dispatch vs combine quantization in expert-parallel MoE

Verdict: **survives_narrowed**. Novelty 2/5.

## The gap claim is partly false

The gap said "no work quantizes combine or analyzes the dispatch/combine asymmetry." That is false for the first half.

1. **Insights into DeepSeek-V3, arXiv 2505.09343 (May 2025, ISCA'25).**
   - l.781: "dispatch uses FP8 (1 byte), while combine uses BF16 (2 bytes)".
   - l.919-922: "While the combine stage still uses higher precision (e.g., BF16) due to accuracy requirements, we are actively testing FP8, custom precision formats (e.g., E5M6) and mixing FP8-BF16 for further reductions."
   - l.948-953: they validated LogFMT-nBit "by quantifying the output of the residual branch to simulate the combine stage in MoE models. When setting n = 8 ... LogFMT-8Bit shows superior training accuracy compared to E4M3 or E5M2. After increasing the n to 10 bits, we find it's similar to the BF16 combine stage."
   - l.963-966: they did not use LogFMT because of encode/decode overhead, "(50%~100%)" when fused with all-to-all.
   - So combine quantization was tested, and it was found to need about 10 bits. The setup was a 7B dense-model training simulation with no k-expert averaging. This is the opposite of S07's prediction that combine is more tolerant.
2. **DeepEP low-latency combine `use_logfmt=False` flag.** Third-party sources (DeepWiki API page, NVIDIA CloudAI DeepEP benchmark docs) show this flag. The current README (V2 ElasticBuffer) says "Combine inputs must be BF16". I could not read the source directly (GitHub raw returned 404 from the sandbox). So combine compression appears to have shipped in an open kernel at some point. Treat this as **unverified at source level**.
3. **NVIDIA Model-Optimizer PR #2660** (opened 2026-10-05, merged 2026-10-09). It adds NVFP4 fake-quant hooks `moe_dispatch_nvfp4` and `moe_combine_nvfp4` to vLLM expert-parallel serving. The combine hook quantizes "the expert output entering prepare_finalize.finalize". On Qwen3-30B-A3B GSM8K (100 questions), BF16 scored 0.99 and NVFP4 dispatch+combine scored 0.98. There is **no separate dispatch-vs-combine ablation**. This is the exact experimental tool S07 needs, now public, and a clear sign that NVIDIA is about to publish such numbers.
4. **torchao MXFP8 EP training tutorial.** Forward combine stays BF16; backward combine is MXFP8 (from the web search summary, page not opened).
5. **MegaScale-MoE, 2505.11432.** In FP8 training, "we replace BF16 TP reduce-scatter with FP8 all-to-all in forward propagation and perform reduction in FP32" (l.1375-1378), using per-token quantization. Quantizing the reduction-side stream is established.
6. **Tensor-parallel partial-sum compression** quantizes the reduction stream (independent per-rank errors that are summed) at about 4-5 bits for inference: 2411.07942 (Dong et al.), 2411.09510 (Hansen-Palmus et al.), and Flash Communication 2412.04964 / V2 2508.03760. This is the dense analogue of combine quantization.
7. **Dispatch-only sensitivity data**, FlashCommunication V2 2508.03760 Table 2: Qwen3-30B-A3B C4 perplexity is 9.65 at INT8, 9.66 at INT6, 9.70 at INT5, 9.88 at INT4, 10.61 at INT3 and 19.71 at INT2. They note "DeepSeek V3 ... only quantizes the dispatch volume" (l.385-386).
8. **Step-3, 2507.19427**, l.1561-1562: attention-FFN disaggregation sends "FP8 tokens ... and BF16 activation". The same asymmetric convention is used in AFD.

## Premise checks

- 2603.02731 l.233-235 is **confirmed verbatim**: "activations are quantized into MXFP4 immediately before the All-to-All (A2A) dispatch ... while the combine phase retains BF16 accumulation to preserve reduction accuracy." There is no ablation in that paper. It is a training paper.
- The DeepSeek-V3 report 2412.19437 is **confirmed** at l.1817-1824: "For both the forward and backward combine components, we retain them in BF16 to preserve training precision". Note that the motivation is training: backward combine carries activation gradients.
- **"Current practice may be inverted"**: the only direct published evidence (2505.09343) says combine needs more bits than FP8 at the same bit-width. That evidence is weak: it is a dense simulation, a training setting, and has no k-averaging. Even so, the inversion framing is currently contradicted rather than supported.
- 2610.03741 and AirMoE 2608.22932 do analyze combine-side aggregation noise with gate-weighted, independent per-expert errors. AirMoE eq. (10) gives MSE = c_l [Σ(|h_m|√p_m/η − g_m)² + σ²/η²] under an "uncorrelated normalized symbols across activated experts" assumption. This is wireless channel noise, with no dispatch comparison and no quantization.
- QUADS 2607.15810 has no dispatch/combine content. 2607.18745 is a single-matmul gauge paper. Both statements by the author are correct.

## What remains

- No paper gives a matched-bit, controlled comparison of dispatch-only vs combine-only quantization sensitivity for MoE inference.
- No paper gives the first-order decomposition into (a) gate averaging, k_eff = (Σg)²/Σg², (b) dispatch coherence ρ through expert Jacobians, and (c) the dynamic-range penalty of expert outputs, which have outliers or massive activations from down_proj.
- No paper does byte-constrained bit allocation between the two streams.
- No paper shows that per-expert-output fine-grained scales (MX/NVFP4) remove the outlier penalty that made DeepSeek keep BF16.

## Weaknesses a reviewer will raise

- The proposition is textbook first-order error propagation: independent errors add in power, coherent errors add in amplitude.
- k_eff ≈ 4-6 for top-8 means only 6-8 dB, about 1-1.3 bits, of combine headroom. That is easily erased by expert-output dynamic range, so the result is likely "combine ≈ dispatch − 1 bit at best".
- Combine error lands directly on the residual stream, while dispatch error is filtered by SwiGLU Jacobians (gain may be < 1).
- The ModelOpt tooling (Oct 2026) means NVIDIA can publish the ablation quickly.

## Significance

This is marginal for a NeurIPS/ICLR main track. It is a useful systems/numerics measurement, better suited to MLSys or a workshop, unless it yields a deployable combine-FP8/NVFP4 kernel with end-to-end decode speedups. In decode, combine bytes are 2× dispatch, so they are the larger target, and the result would need to come with a clean predictive law validated across DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3 and GPT-OSS.
