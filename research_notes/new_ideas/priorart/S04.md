# S04 prior-art audit: common-mode quantization error and self-consistency; lazy prompt-KV quantization

Verdict: **premise_false**. The narrowed "dithered votes" version is weak and partly anticipated. Novelty 2/5.

## The central premise fails
The gap claim says that prefix sharing (the vLLM/SGLang default for n>1) is what makes the quantization error common-mode, and that existing studies measure only per-sample degradation. Both parts of this are wrong.

1. **Prefix sharing does not cause common-mode error. Determinism does.** A deterministic KV quantizer applied to the same prompt gives the same quantized prompt KV whether or not the prefix is shared. Without prefix caching, each of the N requests re-prefills the prompt and gets bit-identical quantized KV, up to batch nondeterminism. 2609.04748 found that with caching disabled, "repeated execution was bit-identical in every configuration" (abstract).
2. **Under deployed (deterministic) quantization, no error is "idiosyncratic" in the sense the Proposition needs.** Weight quantization, activation quantization and on-the-fly quantization of generated-token KV are all deterministic functions of the context. The N samples are therefore i.i.d. draws from a single perturbed distribution p_Q(.|x), and majority@N converges to the mode of p_Q's answer distribution in every case. The c-versus-xi_s split only means something if the quantizer is deliberately randomized per sample: different rotation or Hadamard seeds per sample, or stochastic rounding. No deployed stack does this. So prediction (i)'s comparison point, "per-sample independent error at matched per-sample KL", is a counterfactual construction, not something per-sample studies measured or deployments run.
3. Because of (2), "the majority gap does not shrink with N" holds for **any** deterministic quantization, including W4 weights. It is not specific to the shared prompt KV. 2510.10964 (Kim et al., Oct 2025) already reports majority-vote parallel scaling (group size G) for GPTQ 4/8-bit reasoning models and for KV quantization at 2/4/8 bit, framed as memory–accuracy Pareto frontiers (Sec. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 10; lines 612–637 of the extracted text).

## The math core is already published
- The Proposition (majority@inf -> 1{pi > 1/2}, and the gap is a sign disagreement weighted by the problems near 1/2) is Lemma 1 / Theorem 2 of **Chen et al., "Are More LLM Calls All You Need?" (2403.02419, Mar 2024)**. Its query-difficulty indicator d_V(x) = max_{a != y} Pr[G(x)=a] - Pr[G(x)=y] (line 284) decides whether infinitely many votes give a correct answer. Theorem 2 (line 300) gives the easy/hard non-monotonicity. With correlated voters this is the Condorcet jury theorem, which goes back to Ladha (1992). The candidate's only addition is a Jensen/Taylor step: zero-mean per-sample noise enters E[pi(c+xi)] only at second order. That is textbook dithering.
- The Lemma (cross-sample logit-error correlation equals query alignment against the shared Delta K) is one line, q_s^T Delta K. It is correct but not a contribution.

## Other premise checks
- 2606.00206 (Lotfi et al., May 2026). The claim that it is per-sample is correct. It reports W4A4KV4 (FlatQuant), T=0.6, top-p 0.95, with pass@1/accuracy and KL by position (lines 229, 1615). There is no majority voting.
- 2610.00694 (Felicio, Sep 2026). The claim that it is per-sample is correct. Flip/TV is per position, and the sampled acceptance equals 1 - TV (line 995). There is no multi-sample aggregation.
- 2605.20315 Mix-Quant (May 2026). It does quantized prefill (FP4 W/A) followed by a high-precision decode. It argues that **prefill-side KV errors are attenuated**: "quantization errors on low-attention tokens are attenuated by their small attention weights... prefill quantization errors do not simply grow" (lines 333–338). This works against the S04 claim that prompt-KV error dominates.
- 2606.03458 KVarN (Jun 2026). In test-time scaling, KV error **accumulates in the generated-token cache during decoding** (lines 28, 52, 422–430). In reasoning workloads the dominant KV error is decode-side, and it is not early-concentrated.
- "Concentrated in early decode steps" has no supporting evidence in any paper I read. The plausible mechanism is that early tokens steer the trajectory, but that is a sensitivity claim, not an error-concentration claim.

## Significance problem for lazy prompt-KV quantization
In math-reasoning test-time scaling (AIME, MATH), prompts are about 100–500 tokens. Generations are 4k–32k tokens per sample, times N samples. The shared prompt KV is stored once and makes up well under 1% of KV memory, so quantizing it, lazily or not, saves almost nothing. The idea only matters for long-context prompts combined with parallel sampling (RAG or long-document QA with n>1), and that is not the regime the candidate motivates. Keeping the prompt KV in FP for the first k steps is also a trivial engineering policy, close to KIVI's FP residual window and Mix-Quant's phase-aware precision.

## What remains (narrowed)
The one non-trivial idea is **per-sample randomized quantization ("dithered votes")**. Give each of the N parallel samples an independent rotation or rounding seed, so that the vote sees the mixture distribution E_xi p_xi, whose answer-mode bias is second order, instead of the deterministic p_Q. This costs no memory if the seeds are applied only to the generated-token KV, or to Hadamard signs that are cheap to vary. The idea is partly anticipated by **Ex Uno Pluria (Nam et al., 2411.14860, NeurIPS 2024)**: training-free ensembles whose members come from one model through different low-precision roundings. That work studies classification ensembles of weight-rounded members, not LLM self-consistency, KV, or reasoning. I found no LLM paper that varies the quantizer per sample to debias majority voting.
