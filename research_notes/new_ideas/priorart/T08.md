# T08 prior-art and premise check: quantized-rollout TIS and termination-hazard length drift

**Verdict: premise_false. Novelty 2/5.**

## Candidate
A 4-bit sampler under-emits commit/termination tokens. Under the claim, the per-token ratio rho = pi/mu = 1/kappa exceeds C, so TIS truncates it. Termination is then under-reinforced by kappa*C, and length drifts upward. The size of the drift is predicted from the offline hazard ratio kappa. FP8 samplers show no drift.

## Fatal premise problem (2606.00206, Lotfi et al., 2026-05-29)
- Method (lines 729-737). The paper computes D_KL(p_t || q_t) between BF16 and AWQ-3bit under identical prefixes. It associates each KL value with the token the quantized model sampled, then averages per token.
- Table 10 (lines 3593-3596) lists the 50 lowest-KL tokens: "_Answer, </think>, <EOS>, ... boxed ...". The caption says "These mathematical and formatting tokens remain stable under quantization."
- So at the positions where a quantized sampler actually emits </think> or <EOS>, p is close to q and rho is about 1. Truncation at C=2 does not act on sampled termination tokens. The length inflation comes from branching positions where the sampler picks "Wait/But/Alternatively". Those are the high-KL tokens in Table 9. There rho < 1, the weight is not truncated, and IS down-weights it correctly.
- The hazard model conflates two different things:
  - an aggregate per-step hazard ratio kappa < 1, which comes mostly from the sampler reaching fewer "commit-ready" prefixes;
  - a per-token ratio rho > C on sampled EOS tokens.

  Token-level TIS acts only on the second, and the evidence says the second is about 1. The real residual mismatch is the prefix/state-occupancy shift. Token-level TIS ignores that shift for any C, so the predicted dependence on C (factor kappa*C) is unsupported.
- Sign is not fixed by kappa. The bias term is -E_mu[(rho-C)_+ A grad log pi]. Its sign follows the sign of A at the terminating prefixes, which is endogenous to training. 2609.20807 (lines 416-422) makes the same point: drift pulls toward the sampler after prefixes with positive expected advantage and away after negative ones.
- Minor point. 4-bit weight-only PTQ does lengthen CoT modestly: on MATH-500 with R1-Distill-1.5B, BF16 gives 5.2K tokens, GPTQ-4 gives 6.2K, AWQ-4 gives 6.3K and FQ W4A4KV4 gives 8.0K (Fig. 2). The text, however, calls 4-bit weight-only "mild ... CoT lengths remaining close to the BF16 baseline" (l.387-388). The strong effects are at 3-bit and W4A4KV4.
- The premise "Spearman rho=-0.73 between CoT length and accuracy over 28 pairs" is slightly misquoted. The correlation is between accuracy *degradation* and CoT length *increase* (l.400-401). The 12-23% figure is correct (l.155-156, l.926).

## What overlaps (mechanism already published)
- **2609.20807 Score Centering (Marek & Ryabinin, 2026-09-17)**, Eq. 3 and l.403-410. The PG update under training-inference mismatch (TIM) is drift + signal, where "Drift is an artifact of TIM that acts as distillation toward the sampler". l.462-465 adds that IS cancels drift exactly, "clip or mask large importance ratios ... reintroducing drift". Combined with "quantized samplers think longer" (2606.00206), this already predicts the *direction* of the effect: the learner is pulled toward the sampler's longer behaviour. The gap claim that 2609.20807 "predicts neither its direction nor an observable" is therefore half wrong: it predicts the direction (toward the sampler) but names no length observable. The paper has no mention of response length (grep found 0 hits).
- **2609.20511 "When EOS Tokens Disagree" (Yang et al., 2026-09-17)**. Termination-token mismatch suppresses stopping and causes length inflation in OPD. Prop. 1 (App. C, l.1150-1185) proves that a local sampled-token update misses a nonnegative stopping-logit term p(s)C(s) relative to the trajectory-consistent update. The result is "relative continuation bias", meaning length growth. This is the same structural result: a per-token correction that ignores prefix/future mismatch biases toward continuation. Their setting is teacher-student OPD, not a quantized sampler with TIS.
- **2605.20402 MXFP4 RL decomposition (Li et al., 2026-05-19)**, App. G l.1839-1846. After RL, MXFP4 W4A4 produces "many more unparseable or non-terminating responses". "The long-CoT gap is therefore driven by trajectory divergence and non-termination ... importance-sampling-side fixes cannot close it because reweighting tokens cannot undo the fact that the explored trajectories differ." This is an empirical observation of 4-bit RL non-termination, plus the claim that IS-side fixes cannot close the gap. In that setup the sampler and learner share the same QDQ path, so it is not a TIS mismatch.
- **2601.18150 FP8-RL**: premise verified. For Qwen3-8B, "Response length grows consistently in both configurations" (l.266). For the 30B MoE, "Reward and response length curves also overlap significantly" (l.302). Both are FP8 W8A8 with token-level TIS.
- **2610.07043 TRIAGE (NVFP4 RL, 2026-10-05)**. A directional analysis (sign of the gap x sign of the advantage) under native NVFP4 with TIS at C=2. It finds an imbalance toward negative-advantage/negative-gap tokens. It does not discuss length or termination. This is closely related to "direction" but has no length prediction.
- 2602.13953 QuRL (TIS bias at long horizons, l.455-462), 2605.13907 AIS, 2604.07853 QaRL (divergence grows with response length, l.443), 2609.32444 CIS, 2607.22186 ENTR, 2607.15810 QUADS, 2610.07767 TRACE. All are correction methods. None predicts length direction from an offline sampler statistic. TRACE reports that vanilla QAT under FP4 gives *shorter* responses late in training (l.1790-1791), which counts as weak counter-evidence to a universal upward drift.

## What remains
A *measured, quantitative* link is still open: an offline sampler statistic that predicts the sign and magnitude of RL length drift under quantized rollouts. Candidate statistics are the sampler's offline length inflation and the occupancy shift of commit-ready prefixes. No paper tests that this link holds under TIS, fails to vanish with larger C, and vanishes with score centering or with sequence-level/prefix-corrected IS. The specific "TIS truncation of termination tokens" mechanism should be dropped.

## Sharpened hypothesis (if pursued)
Under <=4-bit rollouts with token-level TIS, the learner's length drifts toward the sampler's offline length bias, not specifically upward. The drift magnitude scales with the sampler's offline CoT inflation (from a 2606.00206-style measurement) and is insensitive to C in {2, 5, inf}. It is removed by score centering (2609.20807) or by prefix-level (sequence/segment) IS. If so, length drift is a cheap observable of TIM drift. The falsifier is a strong dependence on C, which would revive T08's original truncation mechanism.

## Significance
As stated, a main-track reviewer would object. The core mechanism rests on a per-token ratio premise that the cited PTQ data contradicts (termination tokens are among the lowest-KL tokens). The sound remainder, "drift = distillation toward sampler, visible as length", is a corollary of 2609.20807 plus 2606.00206. The sharpened version is a solid empirical/diagnostic workshop paper. It reaches main-track level only with a quantitative offline->online prediction across several bitwidths and models, and a clean C-sweep and score-centering ablation.
