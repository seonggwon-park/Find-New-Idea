# P03 prior-art check: worst-case gap between Haar-rotated GPTQ and WaterSIC

**Verdict: survives_narrowed (novelty 3/5).** Nobody has published a worst-case bound for this gap. However, the stated asymptotic rate is wrong, and the upper-bound half follows almost entirely from QuIP Lemma 2.

## Premise checks (against the downloaded full text)
- **2605.13768 (Ordentlich & Polyanskiy, HRQMM II, v2, 8 Jun 2026), lines 1594-1597.** The paper says: "It is an interesting open
  problem to estimate worst possible gap (over possible spectra λj ≥ ϵ) between GPTQ with rotation and WaterSIC". **Verified.**
  - Eq. 33 (l.1551-1559) is a ratio of expectations. The expectation itself is exact (Cauchy-Binet, footnote 3), but O&P only say the quantities "concentrate quickly"; they do not prove it.
  - O&P also state that pivots "smoothly decrease from the arithmetic mean to harmonic mean".
  - The abstract (l.32) gives "within 0.1 bit (depending on the layer type)" on Llama-3-8B.
  - Fig. 5 caption (l.1438) says singular-ΣX layers "we skip". These skipped layers are exactly the regime this candidate targets.
  - l.1484 credits "[17, Lemma 2]" (QuIP) with an earlier estimate "for a restricted class of ΣX".
- **2608.25188 (survey), l.4248-4250.** "the authors leave the worst-case gap between rotated GPTQ and WaterSIC open". **Verified.**
- **2603.04956 (WaterSIC).** The paper proves that unrotated GPTQ can have an arbitrarily large gap. This is the AM-GM term of Eq. 11, l.619. It does not treat the rotated case.
  - The paper uses dead-feature erasure (τ = 1e-3·median) and δ = 1e-4 instead of 10% damping (l.1033-1040, l.2233). So the reference covariance also gets regularized.
- **Numbers, re-run (Eq. 33 with n = 200, plus a Haar Monte-Carlo with 20 draws).**
  - Two-point worst case:
    - 0.045 at ε = 1e-1 (MC 0.048). Matches.
    - 0.466 at ε = 1e-2 (MC 0.471). Matches.
    - 1.381 at ε = 1e-3, f = 0.70 (MC 1.385 ± 0.016). **Not 1.23.** The claimed value is an under-maximization.
  - Log-uniform spectrum: 0.006 / 0.074 / 0.284. Matches.
  - Eq. 33 tracks Haar MC to within about 0.01 bit at n = 200.

## Math error in the hypothesis (premise problem)
Take the n→∞ limit of Eq. 33 for the two-point spectrum {ε (fraction f), 1 (fraction 1-f)}:
- For t < 1-f, the pivots are r(t) = (1-f-t)/(1-t).
- So AM(r) → (1-f) + f ln f + O(ε). Near f → 1 this behaves like (1-f)²/2, **not** (1-f)·const.
- Optimizing f gives 1-f = 2/ln(1/ε), and

  sup gap = ½log2(1/ε) − **log2** ln(1/ε) + ½log2(2/e²) + o(1).

  Checked numerically: at ε = 1e-4 / 1e-6 / 1e-8, the limit formula minus ½log2(1/ε) + log2 ln(1/ε) equals -0.885 / -0.906 / -0.915. This tends to -0.943.
- The candidate's −½log2 log(1/ε) is the rate of the **unrotated PCA-basis (Specht-ratio) gap**. That is the classical Specht 1960 max AM/GM on [ε,1], and here it equals 1.53 / 2.87 / 4.32 / 7.35 / 10.47 bits at ε = 1e-2…1e-8.
- So the correct statement is sharper: rotation saves ½log2 ln(1/ε) + O(1) bits in the worst case, but no more.

## Closest prior art
1. **QuIP, 2307.13304 (Chee et al., NeurIPS 2023), Lemma 2 (l.303-309).** For μ-incoherent H, tr(D) ≤ (μ²/n)(tr H^{1/2})².
   - Haar conjugation gives μ = polylog(n) (l.412, l.468).
   - Hence gap ≤ ½log2 μ² + ½log2(AM(√λ)²/GM(λ)). Over [ε,1] the second term is ½log2(1/ε) − log2 ln(1/ε) + O(1). **This is already the correct rate as an upper bound, up to the n-dependent ½log2 μ² term.**
   - It is much tighter than the candidate's PCA/Newton upper bound.
   - Asymptotically, the true Haar gap is only about ½ bit below the μ-free QuIP functional.
2. **2605.13768 (O&P II).** Supplies the open problem, Eq. 33 and footnote 3 (the exact E|Σ^(k)| = e_k/C(n,k) identity), and the AM→HM pivot ramp.
3. **2610.09212 (CurveTQ, 6 Oct 2026).** Contains:
   - Prop. 1, the transform-coding-gain floor;
   - App. A.3, a 2×2 example where the rotated gap is 0.10 bit;
   - Prop. 2, "the substitution hypothesis is not a theorem": neither of native-weighted vs rotated-Euclidean dominates for all H;
   - a "ramp property … checked numerically, not proved" (l.1399).

   CurveTQ does not give a worst-case-over-spectrum bound.
4. **2011.14626 (Hegland 2020).** Volume-sampling expected Gaussian-elimination error is a ratio of elementary symmetric polynomials. This is a tool for proving Eq. 33's validity or for setting up the extremal problem.
5. **2603.04956 (WaterSIC).** Unbounded gap without rotation; rotation-invariance of WaterSIC.
6. **2609.14706 (WaterKron) and 2608.25188 (survey).** No worst-case analysis of the rotated gap.

## Searches run (22 arXiv API, 2 WebSearch, plus the homepage PDFs of O&P I/II)
No paper bounds sup over spectra of the rotated-GPTQ vs WaterSIC (or vs waterfilling) gap. Neither does any paper:
- analyze Haar-rotated Cholesky pivots via elementary symmetric means / finite free compression in a quantization context; or
- tie damping to a cap on that gap.

Adjacent-field queries returned nothing relevant: MIMO V-BLAST/DFE with random unitary precoding, GMD, and free-probability Schur complements.

## What remains (narrowed contribution)
1. **Exact asymptotics of the Haar-rotated worst case:** ½log2(1/ε) − log2 ln(1/ε) + ½log2(2/e²) + o(1).
   - Lower bound: two-point spectra.
   - Matching upper bound: needs a μ-free, expectation-level version of QuIP Lemma 2, or Schur-concavity of the Eq. 33 functional. This would remove QuIP's log n slack.
   - Rigor needs (a) Eq. 33 → Haar pivots, via finite free compression → free compression, or concentration of log-determinants of Haar compressions, and (b) two-point extremality.
2. **The rotation dividend in the worst case:** exactly ½log2 ln(1/ε) + O(1) bits relative to the PCA basis (Specht).
3. **Damping as a cap.** The correct parameter is ε_eff = (λ_min+δ)/(λ_max+δ), not δ/mean(λ). With GPTQ's δ = 0.01·mean and LLM λ_max/mean of 1e2-1e3, ε_eff is about 1e-4 to 1e-5, i.e. a 2.5-4 bit worst-case cap. Note too that damping changes the objective.
4. **Empirics:** measure the gap on the singular or near-singular layers that O&P skipped (Fig. 5 squares) and on layers with few calibration tokens.

## Significance
For a main-track reviewer this is a modest but clean theory note. It resolves an explicitly posed open problem from the people who posed it. The headline "unbounded" part is nearly immediate from QuIP Lemma 2 plus Specht, so the value lies in:
- the sharp constant, ½ bit below the QuIP functional;
- the log-log rotation dividend;
- turning it into a diagnostic for which real layers break the "rotation ≈ waterfilling" folklore.

On its own it is unlikely to clear NeurIPS/ICLR. It is plausible as a section of a larger paper, or as an ISIT/TMLR-style note. The caveat that the margin is in entropy-coded rate (it does not transfer to fixed-rate kernels) remains.
