# T02 prior-art check: magnitude-sorted token blocking for block-scaled Wgrad GEMMs in FP4 RL

**Verdict: survives_narrowed. Novelty 3/5 (2.5 if judged strictly).**
The math core is already published, on the channel axis for PTQ: sorting by second moment and then forming contiguous groups is optimal for the block-max bound. The phenomenon is also published: a few token rows dominate the gradient norm in Wgrad. What remains unpublished is applying the permutation to the token/reduction axis of the weight-gradient GEMM during training. That includes per-step dynamic sorting under SR and RTN, a sort-versus-RHT crossover result, and the FP4-backward RL setting. I found no paper, blog or code that permutes tokens before block-scaled Wgrad quantization.

## Premise checks
- 2509.25149 uses a 16-point RHT on Wgrad inputs: **true** (line 568).
- 2610.00053 abstract wording: **true** (lines 33-34).
- 2601.22813 Table 1 reports SR 23.5e-3 and RTN 9.0e-3, on N(0,1) only: **true**.
- 2605.20402 handles the deadzone only on the forward/rollout path: **true** (lines 564-566, 1468-1471).
- "RL magnitude heterogeneity is documented by 2506.01939": **overstated**. That paper documents *entropy* heterogeneity (20% high-entropy forking tokens, lines 28-32, 108-118), not per-token gradient norms. The gradient-magnitude link through (1-p_t) is the author's inference, valid only at the logits.
- "ReQAT documents critical low-entropy tokens": **true, but in deployment QAT**, not the RL backward. It is arguably useful: such tokens have p~1, so tiny gradients that a block shared with forking tokens would zero under RTN.
- "No paper uses the permutation invariance of the reduction axis in training": **holds** in my search. But the phenomenon (Xi et al. 2023) and the lemma (PermuQuant Prop. 3.2) are both published, so the gap is narrow.
- "The sort key is free from the loss": **false at inner layers**. Backpropagation through causal attention sends gradient into prompt and context tokens that have no loss term, and the separable model `|delta_ti| = a_t c_i` is only approximate. The key is still nearly free from another source: the row-wise (Dgrad) quantization of dY already computes per-token block amaxes, and the leverage score `||delta_t|| ||x_t||` is cheap.
- A hidden issue in the math core: the bound uses the global `max_t x_tk^2`. The permutation is shared with X, whose token-axis groups are also block-quantized. X has its own token outliers (massive activations at BOS and delimiters). Sorting by delta can therefore raise X's block error, and the bound hides that.

## What overlaps
- The exchange-argument optimality of sorted contiguous grouping under block-max scaling, plus a `C log(2g)` approximation guarantee (PermuQuant, Prop. 3.2 and Thm. 3.3).
- Token-row sparsity of the Wgrad output gradient, and the leverage-score key (Xi et al. 2023).
- Token-axis Hadamard for gradients (HALO; NVIDIA RHT on Wgrad).
- The diagnosis that one outlier sets a block's scale so small Wgrad entries underflow (2610.00053).
- Permutation as a grouping lever placed beside gradient row sparsity, without the link being made (2608.25188).

## What remains
1. A **dynamic, per-step token-axis permutation** of both Wgrad operands before 1x16/1x32 block quantization in training, with the GEMM exactly invariant. Not found anywhere.
2. **SR-variance analysis and a sort-versus-RHT crossover** as a function of the tail index of token magnitudes. PermuQuant compares sorting only against other permutations, and composes sorting with Hadamard empirically, without a crossover result. A bound that covers both operands (a joint key on `||delta_t|| ||x_t||`) would also be new.
3. **Composition of sort and RHT**: sort first, then a within-block Hadamard. After sorting, each block's magnitudes are homogeneous, so the RHT `sqrt(2 ln n)` crest penalty applies to the block rms rather than the global rms.
4. **RL-specific evidence**: measured per-token gradient-norm tails at inner layers in GRPO/DAPO, and the end-to-end gap in native NVFP4/MXFP4 RL (TRIAGE-like setups). Also deadzone protection of small-gradient low-entropy tokens.
5. A **systems angle**: piggyback on the MoE token-permute and the row-to-column transpose-quantize kernels that already exist (2603.02731, 2610.00053), so the gather costs nothing extra.

## Sharpened hypothesis
In FP4-backward RL fine-tuning (native NVFP4 or MXFP4 with SR on gradients), take a per-step token permutation shared by dY and X. Sort it by the leverage score `s_t = ||dY_t|| ||X_t||`, reusing the amaxes from the row-wise Dgrad quantization, and fuse it into the existing transpose-quantize kernel. Then:
- (a) it lowers Wgrad MSE and raises Wgrad cosine compared with the 16/32-point RHT whenever the token-score distribution has tail index alpha < ~2, a crossover predicted by a bound that accounts for both operands;
- (b) sort followed by RHT dominates either alone;
- (c) it narrows the BF16 gap in reward and accuracy at equal throughput.

Credit PermuQuant (sorting optimality) and Xi et al. 2023 (token sparsity and the leverage key). Claim as new: the token/reduction axis, the SR and dynamic setting, the crossover theorem, and the RL measurements.

## Significance (main-track reviewer view)
Borderline. A reviewer will call it "PermuQuant's sorting lemma moved to the token axis, plus Xi et al.'s leverage score". It clears the bar only with: (i) a non-trivial crossover or joint-operand theorem; (ii) measured heavy tails of inner-layer token gradients in RL; (iii) an end-to-end FP4-RL gain over RHT+SR at negligible kernel overhead. Without these it is a workshop or systems-track paper.
