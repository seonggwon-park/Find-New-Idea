# T06 prior-art check: reusing rotations in unbiased FP4 backward estimators (fixed or shared Hadamard signs)

**Verdict: premise_false.** A narrow residue survives. Novelty 2/5.

## Premise checks (against the paper text)

1. **"Quartet II reuses one rotation across micro-batches": FALSE.**
   - Source: 2601.22813 (v2, updated 2026-06-01), App. A, l.1541-1545: "We apply identical rotations for every rotation group within a tensor for every micro-batch to reformulate the rotation as simple GEMM. **I.e., we re-randomize rotations per-tensor per-micro-batch.**"
   - The rotation is shared across the groups within one tensor. It is redrawn for every micro-batch, so there is no frozen rotation across steps.
   - Two other parts of the gap claim do hold:
     - There is no proof of Corollary 3.1 anywhere in the text (a grep for "proof" finds nothing).
     - Unbiasedness is checked only in Fig. 9: Llama-3.2-1B, attention block 0, error decaying about as 1/B.
   - Note that Fig. 9 averages over fresh ω. It never tests a fixed ω.

2. **"Fixed signs over the whole run make the error on the slowly moving weight operand a persistent bias": FALSE for the recipes cited.**
   - In NVIDIA NVFP4 (2509.25149) and in 2610.00053, the RHT is applied only to Wgrad. Its operands are dY and X, which are fresh data every micro-batch, not W.
   - 2610.00053, l.621-683:
     - Dgrad uses SR on dY. W is "not transformed".
     - The Wgrad operands are a deterministic RTN of the column-facing dY together with the transformed X.
     - "A fixed D ... does not inherit a probabilistic concentration guarantee ... We neither claim that guarantee nor compare alternative sign vectors."
   - In NVIDIA/TE and Tseng (2502.20586), unbiasedness comes from stochastic rounding (SR), not from the rotation. For any fixed orthogonal R, E_SR[R^T SR(Rx)] = x (before saturation). A frozen sign vector therefore adds no bias at all.
   - In 2610.00053's Wgrad path everything is RTN. That path is biased whatever the sign policy, and redrawing signs alone does not fix it, because RTN shrinkage is biased in expectation over R too (see 2606.20381). Only an EDEN-corrected RTN is unbiased over R.

3. **"Redrawing every step removes a measurable loss floor at negligible cost": already tested, with a negative result.**
   - Source: 2509.25149 App. E.4.3 and Fig. 13, 12B model, 4T tokens. After 3.4T tokens, training switched to one of three policies: "seed per instance" (a new sign vector for every transform), "single fixed seed", or no signs.
   - Result: "We observe lower model quality in the absence of random sign vectors and **no improvements from inducing randomness at every transform instance**."
   - On the 1.2B model there was no difference between any of the strategies.
   - Section 4.2 adds: "Our studies show no measurable impact from increasing the number of random sign vectors."

| 2606.20381, UFP4 (2026-06-18) | RTN on E2M1 is biased (shrinkage), and RHT amplifies it, so the bias is not a rotation-reuse effect. |

The mathematical core is standard: SGD with a biased gradient converges to a neighbourhood whose size scales with ||b||² (Ajalloeian & Stich 2020, and the work that followed). A constant bias drifts as O(Tη), against O(√T η) for zero-mean noise. In a quadratic model the floor is ½ bᵀH⁻¹b. All three are textbook results.

## What remains

The bias mechanism needs **both** of the following:
- an estimator whose unbiasedness comes only from the rotation (EDEN or MS-EDEN with RTN), and
- a rotation that is frozen across steps on a slowly varying operand (W in Dgrad).

No published recipe does this today. Quartet II redraws per micro-batch, and the recipes with fixed signs use SR or rotate only Wgrad.

A natural *design question* is left over. Caching the rotated, RTN-quantized backward weight across micro-batches or steps would save the "re-quantize W in the backward pass" cost that Quartet II names (l.~555). With an EDEN-type estimator, that cache produces a deterministic bias correlated from step to step. Quantifying it is a modest, hypothetical-systems contribution.

A second leftover is the sub-claim that sharing one rotation across the G groups of a tensor correlates their errors, so the variance does not fall as 1/G. It is still untested. It affects variance only, not bias, and Fig. 9 already shows that averaging over micro-batches gives the 1/B decay.

## Sharpened hypothesis

For EDEN-type RTN backward estimators (MS-EDEN), suppose the rotated and quantized weight in the backward pass is reused for K micro-batches or steps, which removes the backward re-quantization of W.

- This introduces a bias b_K. Its correlation from step to step is about 1 while ηK ≪ Δ, the scale over which W changes.
- The resulting loss floor is about ½ b_Kᵀ H⁻¹ b_K, and it grows with K.
- There is a crossover K* at which the throughput saved by the cache is outweighed by the excess loss. The test is to measure K* against redrawing per micro-batch.
- The prediction is that SR-based recipes (NVIDIA/TE) show no such effect at any K, which is consistent with NVIDIA's Fig. 13 ablation.

Measuring the correlation between groups when they share one rotation (variance that does not fall as 1/G) is a secondary diagnostic.

## Significance

The hypothesis as stated would be rejected by a main-track reviewer:
- Its central empirical claim was tested at 12B / 4T tokens and came out negative.
- The Quartet II premise misreads the appendix.
- Its mathematics is textbook biased-SGD.

The narrowed caching question is at most a workshop or appendix item, unless it opens a sizeable throughput win.
