# S03 prior-art check: fan-out-aware precision

**Verdict: survives_narrowed. Novelty 2/5.**

Most of the core is already published:
- RDKV covers the weighted reverse water-filling rule, with per-token weights that add up over every reader within one request.
- AdaptCache and EVICPRESS cover per-entry compression rates for prefix caches, weighted by how often an entry is reused across requests.
- vLLM's own documentation contradicts prediction (2).

What remains is narrower: precision for a shared prefix when the samples' outputs are combined (self-consistency, best-of-N), and downgrading under load in order of remaining fan-out.

## Closest work (all read in full text)

### RDKV, 2605.08317 (2026-05-08)
- **Prop. 3.1** (txt l.211-219): `w_t := sum_tau a_{tau,t}`, "the total attention that token t receives across all query positions".
- **Prop. 3.2:** channel weights for K are `||Q[:,c]|| ||K[:,c]||`.
- **Thm 3.3** (l.271-280): `b_u* = [log2(ln2 * w_u * sigma_u / lambda)]_+`, "a reverse water-filling".
- **Lemma A.2:** the high-rate approximation by Bennett.
- **Overlap:** a token's weight is the sum of what all its readers see, and bits follow log2(weight). This is S03's theorem within one request.
- **Differences:**
  - RDKV uses RMS distortion with linear weights, so bits are log2 of the weight, not 1/2 log2.
  - RDKV has no cross-request multiplicity c_j.
  - RDKV is applied once after prefill.

### AdaptCache, 2509.00105 (SOSP'25 BigMem workshop)
- l.107-112: "Compression ratio and device placement need to be aware of the content and the reuse frequency."
- l.180-185: `Utility(i) = Freq(i) * (alpha * Quality(i,M_i,R_i) - delay)`, chosen per KV entry by greedy marginal utility.
- **Overlap:** frequency-weighted, per-entry lossy compression for shared or reused prefixes. This is claim (1) in empirical systems form.

### EVICPRESS, 2512.14946
- l.705: `Util(method, ratio, device) = (alpha * quality - TTFT) * frequency`.
- The utility is optimized jointly over compression and eviction across all contexts and storage tiers.
- It applies "conservative compression to contexts that are sensitive" (l.34).

### vLLM documentation (docs/features/quantization/quantized_kvcache.md, l.45)
- Quote: "Some attention layer types (e.g. sliding-window) are more sensitive to KV-cache quantization. The `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers` flag leaves the specified layers at the model's native dtype".
- The example is `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers sliding_window`.
- This is the opposite of claim (2).

### Other papers checked
- **Mix-Quant, 2605.20315:** see the premise checks.
- **Block-GTQ, 2606.24033:** l.2877-2879: "multi-GPU and batched serving are open". Confirmed.
- **ReadKV, 2610.11245:** progressive per-query fetch within one request. Confirmed.
- **DPS, 2609.34380:** switches *weight* precision under KV pressure, with a threshold-with-hysteresis policy. It has no per-request KV value rule. Confirmed.
- **MorphServe, 2506.02006:**
  - It *does* have a sensitivity-based rule (LTS/LRS/MDS) for which weight layers to swap (l.553-616).
  - l.879: "KVResizer does not quantize or compress existing KV caches". It has no per-request or per-KV rule.
  - The premise is half true.
- **Joint Encoding of KV Blocks, 2601.03067:** fuses similar blocks across requests and gives a rate-distortion analysis with a Poisson model. It does not allocate bits by fan-out.
- **RateQuant, 2605.06675:** per-head reverse water-filling with calibrated D(b). It does not consider sharing.
- **CommonKV, 2508.16134:** cross-layer SVD sharing. It has no precision rule (not contradicted).

## Premise checks

1. **Mix-Quant is not KV-cache bit allocation.**
   - It runs W4A4 NVFP4 *compute* in prefill and BF16 in decode. The prefill workers write the KV "in the precision expected by the high-precision decode engine" (l.429-433).
   - Mix-Quant already explains its own robustness (l.326-339): "quantization errors on low-attention tokens are attenuated by their small attention weights".
   - It also explains decode sensitivity (l.880): "token-level errors can propagate through subsequent autoregressive steps".
   - So "nobody has resolved this" is false.
   - A fan-out view would actually predict that prefill KV, which every decode step reads, has *high* value. It therefore does not explain Mix-Quant's prefill redundancy.
   - Decode sensitivity comes from discrete token feedback, which a first-order linearization leaves out.
2. **The lemma's lower bound is false in general.**
   - `tr(G Sigma)` with `Sigma = sigma^2 I` equals `sigma^2 ||sum_r a_r J_r||_F^2`.
   - The upper bound `(sum_r ||a_r J_r||)^2` holds by the triangle inequality.
   - The lower bound `sum_r ||a_r J_r||^2` needs `<J_r, J_s>_F >= 0` for all pairs. Counterexample: `J_2 = -J_1` gives 0.
3. **Prefix sharing across N requests is not "coherent" under the stated loss.**
   - The N requests write N separate outputs, so the summed loss is `sum_r tr(J_r^T J_r Sigma)`. That is a sum of Grams, not the Gram of a sum.
   - The +1/2 log2 N follows from multiplicity alone, which is AdaptCache-style frequency weighting.
   - Coherence appears only when the outputs are *aggregated*, for example a vote or average over samples.
4. **Claim (2), SWA vs global, contradicts the hypothesis's own lemma.**
   - With attention weights about 1/W for SWA and about 1/T for global, the incoherent weight is `sum a^2`, giving about 1/W for SWA and 1/T for global.
   - So SWA KV should get *more* bits, not fewer. The T_rem/W ratio counts reads and ignores the attention weights spreading thinner over a longer context.
   - Practice (vLLM) agrees that SWA layers are more sensitive. SWA KV is also a small share of the bytes, so protecting it is cheap.
5. **Claim (3), downgrading requests near completion first:** I found no paper that does this. It follows from remaining-read weighting, but it is a scheduling heuristic, not a theorem.

## What remains

- **(a) Aggregation-coupled objectives.** Under self-consistency or best-of-N with a shared prefix, the error in the shared prefix is common-mode across samples and is not averaged out by the vote.
  - If the aggregate is the mean over N samples, the prefix tensor's weight relative to one suffix is about N^2, which gives about log2 N extra bits (not 1/2 log2 N).
  - The right metric is the probability that the vote flips, not the summed MSE.
  - No paper found that covers this.
- **(b) Dynamic downgrade order inside HBM** using remaining expected reads, weighted by attention (T_rem times predicted attention mass), with a closed-form rule and tests against DPS-style weight downgrading. Not found.
- **(c) Concurrent radix-tree sharing in HBM** with a closed-form multiplicity term. This differs from AdaptCache/EVICPRESS, which are about DRAM/SSD tiers with empirical profiling. It is incremental.

## Significance (main-track reviewer)

- As stated, a reviewer would see the theorem as RDKV plus a multiplicity factor, and claim (1) as AdaptCache/EVICPRESS with a formula.
- Claim (2) would likely be refuted by the authors' own experiments.
- Direction (a) could interest reviewers if it shows a measurable accuracy gain at equal bytes on self-consistency or best-of-N (for example AIME maj@16), together with a correct coherent-error analysis. Even then it reads more like a systems/workshop result than a main-track paper.
