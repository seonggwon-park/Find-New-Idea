# T10 prior-art check: SR-variance-corrected Adam second moment for FP4 backward passes

Verdict: **survives_narrowed**. Novelty 2/5. Significance: low as stated.

## Bottom line
I found no paper that subtracts an element-wise, computed stochastic-rounding (SR) variance from Adam's v in quantized training.
The mechanism itself is published. DP-Adam (2304.11208) and DP-AdamBC (2312.14334) subtract a known noise variance from v and clamp the result.
The DP literature also has published evidence against the approach:
- 2507.01129 (Ganesh et al., PPML 2025) argues that "aiming for unbiased estimates of second moments of gradients in adaptive optimizers is misguided" (abstract, line 16). It also says "an unbiased estimate of the preconditioner is unlikely to be the right goal" (line 904-906).
- 2511.07843 (DP-AdamW) finds that bias correction "consistently decreases accuracy" with AdamW (line 44, 80).

What is new here is the noise source and the element-wise exact variance. The idea that noise-inflated v shrinks Adam's step is the DP-AdamBC thesis transplanted to a new noise source.

## Closest work (read)
- 2304.11208 Tang & Lecuyer, "DP-Adam: Correcting DP Bias in Adam's Second Moment Estimation" (2023-04). This is the same correction, using a scalar DP noise variance.
- 2312.14334 Tang, Shpilevskiy, Lecuyer, "DP-AdamBC" (2023-12). It has a scalar bias Phi (line 284: Phi ~ 2.441e-8). It shows that E[g~]/sqrt(E[g~^2]) = E[g]/sqrt(V[g]+E[g]^2+Phi) (line 317), so "DP-Adam is DP-SGDM" when Phi dominates (lines 347-381). This is the T10 thesis with a global noise level.
- 2507.01129 Ganesh et al., "On Design Principles for Private Adaptive Optimizers" (2025-07). Bias correction (Alg. 7) "can be negative, and hence we need to take the positive part" (line 762-763). Its takeaway (line 904): a quadratic problem with added gradient noise has the same gradient distribution as a different noiseless data distribution, so the noisy-v preconditioner is arguably the right one. The same argument applies directly to SR noise.
- 2511.07843 DP-AdamW (2025-11, ICML'25 workshop). Its BC variant hurts with decoupled weight decay.
- 2502.20566 Ozkara, Yu, Park, "Stochastic Rounding for LLM Training: Theory and Practice" (2025-02).
  - Prop. 1 (lines 395-418): SR noise added to gradients *decreases* time-correlation (cor_SR = rho^2/(1+rho^2+Delta^2) < rho^2/(1+rho^2)) and so stabilizes Adam at high LR.
  - Their Adam+SR convergence bounds (Thms 2-3) put SR on the weight update and not in the gradient; they do not touch v.
  - Implication for T10: removing SR noise from v may remove a stabilizer.
- 2510.21314 Tang, Li, Zou (ICLR 2026). It analyses Adam with quantized gradients, weights and both moments. Gradient error delta_t *does* enter v (Appendix lemma, line 3645: v_t = beta2(v_{t-1}+theta) + (grad + delta_t)^2). It is modelled as a bounded relative error (q_G, Assumption 3.1), not as zero-mean variance. It offers no correction.
- 2505.19115 Chmiel et al., FP4 All the Way. The sqrt(3) threshold (Sec 4.1, lines 406-450) is derived for plain GD with the optimal step under isotropic E[ee^T]=sigma_q^2 I. AdamW is used only empirically (line 564). It has no preconditioner analysis.
- 2511.00874 Liu et al. Lemma 3 (lines 722-754): the SR variance term decays as 1/b. Theorem 1 is for SGD (line 865), while experiments also use Adam (Table 1, line 913).
- Checked and not overlapping:
  - 2601.22813 (Quartet II): unbiased gradient estimator, not the optimizer.
  - 2509.17791 (FP4 design space).
  - 2607.04422 (Full-Stack FP4): reshapes v for NVFP4 *storage*.
  - 2610.12444 (ZIP-SR, NeurIPS 2026): SR in preconditioner space for 4-bit *state* storage.
  - 2607.10611 (M+Adam): multiplicative updates for low-precision weights.
  - 2606.20381 (UFP4 shrinkage bias): RHT scope and SR on dY only.
  - 1-bit Adam (2102.02888): freezes v because compression noise corrupts it. This is an old analogue of the "noise corrupts v" observation.

## Premise check
1. 2505.19115 gives a sqrt(3) threshold and does not touch the preconditioner: **TRUE** (GD-only derivation, isotropic noise).
2. 2511.00874 is SR x batch size for SGD only: **mostly true**. The theory is SGD-only, but the experiments use Adam.
3. 2510.21314 covers storage quantization and not gradient-noise inflation: **OVERSTATED**. That paper explicitly puts gradient quantization delta inside v and carries q_G through the bounds. It treats it as worst-case relative error, not as variance, and proposes no correction.
4. The DP analogue uses a single global known noise level: **TRUE** for 2304.11208 and 2312.14334 (Phi is a scalar).
5. Math core problems:
   - With a mean-reduced loss, the SR variance of dW = X^T dY is a sum over B*T tokens of per-token variance times 1/B^2. It therefore also scales as 1/B, which is exactly what 2511.00874 Lemma 3 says. The hypothesis writes v ~ mu^2 + sigma_d^2/B + sigma_q^2 with no 1/B on sigma_q^2.
   - With the 1/B included, the inflation factor in the noise-dominated regime is about 1 + sigma_q,ps^2/sigma_d,ps^2, which does not depend on B. So "small batch" matters only through the mu^2 term.
   - Per-token dY variance across tokens is typically far larger than the FP4 rounding variance, so the factor is plausibly 1.0x-1.1x.
   - A *uniform* inflation is equivalent to a LR rescale and is absorbed by LR tuning. Only heterogeneity of the ratio across coordinates or tensors can matter.
   - "Exact" holds only for a specific recipe:
     - Standard NVFP4 recipes apply RHT to the wgrad operands before SR (2509.25149, 2606.20381), so the variance must be computed in the rotated basis.
     - If both operands are SR-quantized, cross terms appear and 3 GEMMs are needed, not 1.
     - The extra GEMM is the same shape as wgrad. That is about +1/3 of backward GEMM FLOPs, which is not negligible.
     - A Hutchinson sketch does not give per-element variances of a matrix-valued dW cheaply.
   - The max(v - sigma_q^2, eps') clamp reintroduces bias (see 2507.01129 lines 1075-1093).

## What remains
- No one has measured the element-wise ratio sigma_q^2 / v under a real NVFP4 recipe (RHT + SR on dY).
- No one has tested whether the *heterogeneity* of that ratio across tensors explains the FP4-BF16 gap or any LR-optimum shift beyond what a scalar LR change fixes.

## Sharpened hypothesis
Under a standard NVFP4 recipe (RHT on wgrad, SR on dY), the per-tensor SR-to-data noise ratio rho_l = E[sigma_q^2]/E[v - sigma_q^2] is heterogeneous across layer types (e.g. embeddings/LM-head and late MLP projections). That heterogeneity, not its mean, produces a per-tensor effective-LR distortion.

Kill switches, in order:
- (a) If median rho_l < 0.05 at the target batch size, stop.
- (b) If a per-tensor scalar LR multiplier (1+rho_l)^{1/2} recovers as much of the gap as the element-wise subtraction, the element-wise "exact variance" contribution is dead.
- (c) Compare against the DP-literature controls: no correction, clamp-BC, and the "noise is part of the problem" null (2507.01129).
