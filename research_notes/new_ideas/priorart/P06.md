# P06 prior-art audit: Second-moment linearity theorem (rotation-seed variance is O(2^-b), mean is O(4^-b))

Verdict: **survives_narrowed**. Novelty: **2/5**.

## Bottom line
No paper I found applies a first-order (gradient-term) variance analysis to quantization rotation or dither seeds. None derives Std_seed proportional to 2^-b against a mean proportional to 4^-b, or the resulting crossover bit-width. The math core itself is not new, though.
- Var[g^T delta] = sigma^2 ||g||^2 is the delta method.
- The best-of-N corollary, E max = sigma ||a|| sqrt(2 log N), with a correlation-rho term for a second direction, is stated and proved as Theorem 1 of arXiv 2606.10929 (June 2026). That paper covers random weight-space search around pretrained LLM weights, not quantization.
- Best-of-N rotation-seed selection that generalizes from validation to test perplexity is already published empirically in 2609.21450 (Sep 2026). That paper gives a different mechanism: outlier interference.

What remains is a quantization-specific instantiation plus the bit-width scaling law and crossover prediction. That is an analysis contribution, not a theory contribution.

## Premise problems
1. **SpinQuant's spread is the wrong evidence.** The 13- and 6-point spreads are W4A4. There the mean degradation is large and is dominated by activation-outlier effects. P06's own theory says Std/mean grows like 2^b, so the first-order term should dominate only at high bit-width. At W4A4 the spread is better explained by rotation-dependence of the second-order quantity, i.e. t_l itself varies with R through max/outlier interactions. 2609.21450 formalizes exactly that. Cite SpinQuant as motivation only, not as confirmation.
2. **The realized error is not "a fixed-norm error rotated by Haar".** δ = Rᵀ e(RW) depends on R. By stabilizer symmetry, E[δ] is proportional to W (MMSE-scale shrinkage) unless EDEN-style unbiased rescaling is used (2609.08759). This adds a mean first-order term c⟨g,W⟩ with c = O(4^-b). That term competes with ½tr(HΣ) and can be either sign. The covariance is isotropic only on W⊥. The theorem must include both points.
3. **The randomized-Hadamard case is not covered by Prop. 2.15 of 2608.25188.** That result gives marginal sub-Gaussianity of Rw, not E[δδᵀ] ≈ (t²‖W‖²/d)I. With only random signs, the variance constant depends on the Hadamard structure. A separate (easy) lemma is needed.
4. **Mixed origin of ||g_eval||.** Even at a population minimizer, a finite evaluation set has ||g_eval|| ~ sqrt(tr Σ_g / n). So a component of seed variance always exists for that specific eval set, and it does not transfer (cos(g_cal, g_eval) ≈ 0 for that part). This strengthens "quantized beats BF16 is noise" but weakens the seed-selection story. Both effects must be separated: population gradient (post-training mismatch) vs. eval-sampling gradient.
5. **"Quantized beats BF16 at 6-8 bits" is weakly documented.** It appears as ~±0.3% recovery in 2411.02355, often within benchmark standard error. The crossover claim needs a careful paired-bootstrap design, or it collapses into evaluation noise.

## What remains (narrowed contribution)
- Std_seed[ΔL] ≈ 2^-b · C(g, W) vs mean ≈ 4^-b · C'(H, W), the crossover b* = log2(C'/C) per model and task, and an empirical test across b = 3..8 with many seeds.
- Decomposition of ||g_eval|| into a population term (post-training mismatch, base model on a task) and an eval-sample term (∝ 1/√n). This gives a prediction for when seed selection transfers, cos(g_cal, g_eval), which is measurable by backprop on BF16. 2609.21450 never measures it.
- Weight-only regime (where the first-order term should dominate), as opposed to W4A4.

## Significance (main-track reviewer view)
The theorem is a one-line delta-method computation. The best-of-N corollary is published (2606.10929 Thm 1). Seed selection is published (2609.21450). A NeurIPS/ICLR reviewer would see this as weak theory. It survives only as a careful empirical-analysis paper that (a) verifies the 2^-b vs 4^-b scaling across many seeds, bit-widths and models, (b) predicts b* and transfer from measured gradients, and (c) debunks "quantization improves the model" claims with paired statistics. Expect borderline main-track at best. It is more natural for TMLR or a workshop unless the predictive validation is striking.
