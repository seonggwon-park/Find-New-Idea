# X07 (A7): precision gating with conformal risk control. Prior-art audit

**Verdict: PREEMPTED. Novelty 2/5.**

The core is distribution-free, label-free risk control of disagreement with the full model, using a cheap per-request or per-token confidence score to decide when to run the expensive path. That core was published in CALM (2022) and in Fast-yet-Safe (2024), with layer depth as the cheap axis. Redline (2609.33887, Sep 2026) applies the same LTT reference-relative risk machinery directly to weight quantization against bf16, and to lossy speculative acceptance. WitCert itself lists conformal calibration of its gate threshold as future work, and its same-author companion (2608.15810) already pairs exact fallback with conformal extrapolation. Swapping "fewer layers" for "fewer bits" does not make a main-track contribution.

## Closest work (full text read)

### 1. CALM, Schuster et al., 2207.07061 (NeurIPS 2022 oral). The mechanism is preempted.
- It calibrates per-token local exit decisions so that global sequence constraints hold (l.153–155). It defines **textual consistency** with the full model, E[D(Y_early, Y_full)] ≤ δ, and notes this "is achievable using only unlabeled calibration data" (l.163–172, Def. 1 l.491). If D is the 0/1 mismatch, this is exactly X07's "fraction of answers differing from the FP model ≤ α".
- Confidence measures: the **softmax response** (top-2 margin, l.460), the hidden-state saturation measure, and an exit classifier. These are X07's "token margin" score.
- Proposition 1 (LTT for CALM, l.533): any FWER-controlling procedure gives a consistent LLM_early with probability ≥ 1−ε. LTT does **not need monotonicity** (l.505–508). This makes X07's "new lemma (a)" unnecessary.
- The difference is only the cheap axis: fewer layers in CALM, fewer bits in X07. CALM even names quantization as a complementary technique (Related Work).

### 2. Fast yet Safe, Jazbec et al., 2405.20915 (NeurIPS 2024). The CRC version is preempted.
- **Consistency risk** (Eq. 8, l.229–234) is computed with unlabeled data by replacing y with the full model's ŷ_L.
- Prop. 1 (l.315–317) gives E[R(λ̂_CRC)] ≤ ε under **monotone risk** rather than monotone loss. The "main technical insight is that risk control can be relaxed to assume monotone risks" (l.319–323). This is the monotonicity issue X07 lists as "new lemma (a)", already solved for the analogous mid-generation (token-level) exit.
- It applies the method to LLM language modelling (it improves on CALM) and to soft speculative decoding (BiLD, §5.5, l.804–807).

### 3. Redline, Lee et al., 2609.33887 (27 Sep 2026). The quantization instantiation is preempted at the configuration level.
- "Redline keeps the reference-relative risk ... within a user-chosen budget with high probability and deploys the fastest configuration that passes ... It also applies without modification to the acceptance rule of speculative decoding and to weight quantization" (abstract).
- §5.4 (l.756–764): it tests int8, nf4 and fp4 against bf16. "all three low-precision formats are valid at α = 0.10, and the deployed format, nf4, reduces weight memory by 2.81×."
- It uses LTT with Holm correction, so it needs no monotonicity (l.855–859). It cites CALM as "the closest prior use in decoding."
- What is not in Redline: a per-request gate. Its configuration is static. But a gate threshold λ is just another grid axis for Redline. Redline's framework already covers it, even though Redline does not run that experiment.

### 4. WitCert, 2607.28699, and its companion 2608.15810 (Metask Lab, Jul–Aug 2026). They explicitly flag the idea.
- WitCert l.142–143: "calibrating the gate threshold τ conformally on live traffic is attractive future work." The authors **name X07 as their future work**.
- WitCert l.135–142 gives the argument against it: split conformal is marginal, not per-request, and "the silent needle collapses ... occur exactly where a calibration set drawn from typical traffic stops being representative".
- 2608.15810 has an exact-fallback gate (fallback rate 0.30 → 0.14 at matched risk, abstract and l.21). It adds anytime-valid (Ville) admission and an **exchangeable order-statistic (conformal) bound** over 80 serving histories: Pr(E[TV|h_new] > µ_conf) ≤ 1/(N+1) + α (l.810–816, Lean-checked). The loss there is served-output TV, not answer disagreement, and the gated action is the KV write, not the whole request. So it differs from X07 in the object, but it has the same structure: a quantized path, an FP fallback, and a distribution-free calibration of the gate.

### 5. Other cascade and routing guarantees (not quantization-specific)
- Conformal Cascade, 2607.25018: a K-tier small→large LLM cascade with a conformal set-size deferral rule and coverage ≥ 1−Kα (Thm 2, l.235). It is restricted to multiple choice. Its loss is correctness against a gold label, not FP agreement.
- CR², 2605.12001: a device-edge router whose CRC calibration controls the "marginal false-acceptance risk under the full-information utility reference."
- Other routers: CRC-Router 2609.30714, RACER 2603.06616, LEC 2512.01556, C3PO 2511.07396, Conformal Arbitrage 2506.00911.
- Hierarchical group-conditional CRC, 2607.24562, §E8 (l.1441–1457): CRC stays valid at int8 and int4 when calibrated at the deployed precision. Participation falls 0.294 → 0.058 for Gemma at int4.
- 2608.01460 (EMNLP 2026): quantization as "configuration shift" for CP. Weighted CP fails under it (l.856). This works against X07's planned weighted-conformal extension (b).

## Premise check
- "Tier-A bound vacuous by orders of magnitude at 1–2 bits": **TRUE.** WitCert l.366: "1–2 bit schemes are vacuous by many orders of magnitude". l.2369: "vacuous for 2–4 bit schemes (1289× loose at 4-bit)".
- "Probabilistic certificate covers non-adaptive queries only": **TRUE, but it applies to Tier B (sub-Gaussian) only.** WitCert l.390–399 says Tier A "is adaptive-safe ... carries no adaptivity caveat". The gap claim conflates the two tiers.
- "2606.01850 only benchmarks": **TRUE** from the abstract. It is an empirical study of conformal coverage under compression.
- "Queries H1–H4 found nothing on risk-controlled precision cascades": **MISLEADING.** The queries missed CALM, Fast-yet-Safe and Redline because those use the words "early-exit", "consistency" and "operating point".
- **Premise problem 1, the WitCert residual-norm score:** WitCert Fig. 1 (l.367–369) says that at sign-1bit the bound's ROC "tracks the diagonal, i.e. with the current witness its per-step score carries no signal." In the regime where X07 claims an advantage (1–2 bits), one of its three candidate scores is known to carry no signal.
- **Premise problem 2, "the guarantee still holds at 1–2 bits":** this is true and trivial. CRC validity does not depend on the score. Utility is the real question. At 1–2 bits, whole-answer disagreement with FP in free generation is close to 1, so any α well below that base rate forces escalation of almost all requests. The 1–2 bit regime is where the method is least useful, not where it shines.
- **Math core:** lemma (a) is either trivial or already handled by prior work. For request-level escalation the per-sample loss 1[not escalated ∧ differs] is monotone in nested escalation sets. For mid-generation escalation, Fast-yet-Safe's monotone-risk relaxation applies, and LTT (CALM, Redline) needs no monotonicity. Extension (b) is weighted CP (Tibshirani 2019) and weighted CRC, and 2608.01460 shows it fails for quantization configuration shift.

## What remains (thin)
1. An empirical systems question that has not been measured: which cheap scores from the quantized pass predict disagreement with FP, at token level and at answer level, at W4/W3/W2? Candidates are margin, entropy, quantized-vs-FP logit residual proxies, and Hadamard-domain outlier statistics. What is the escalation-rate versus α frontier compared with a static Redline-selected format? This is a measurement contribution, not a theorem.
2. Cost accounting specific to precision escalation. Unlike early exit, escalating mid-generation must recompute or dequantize the KV cache for the prefix. The cost depends on whether the FP weights are resident, for example nested or any-precision weights (Any-Precision LLM 2402.10517, MatGPTQ 2602.03537, MoBiQuant 2602.20191). This is an engineering contribution.
3. A per-request conditional or anytime version that answers WitCert's objection. This means risk control that stays valid under traffic drift, using online CRC or e-processes. But 2608.15810 already occupies the anytime-valid version for compressed serving state.

## Significance (main-track reviewer view)
A NeurIPS or ICLR reviewer would cite CALM and Fast-yet-Safe within one paragraph and call the work "CRC/LTT applied to a new cheap/expensive pair." Redline (Sep 2026) already shows LTT-controlled reference-relative risk for weight quantization. The quantization-side score analysis might fit a workshop or a systems venue (MLSys) if it shows a large escalation-rate gain over static Redline selection at matched α. It is not main-track as a theory contribution.

## Sharpened hypothesis (if pursued)
"At matched label-free risk α of disagreement with FP (controlled with LTT or CRC, as in CALM and Fast-yet-Safe), a per-request W4→FP16 gate driven by a quantization-specific score achieves a lower average bit-cost than the best static configuration chosen by Redline. The score would be, for example, the gap between the quantized top-2 margin and a cheap estimate of the logit perturbation from the scales actually stored. The method uses any-precision nested weights so escalation needs no second weight copy, and KV prefix re-quantization cost is counted. The gain should vanish below 3 bits, where no cheap score separates disagreeing requests."

Its claimed novelty is limited to the score design and the empirical frontier. The guarantee is borrowed and should be presented that way.
