## Overlap, with quotes

### RDKV (2605.08317), Sec. 3.1
RDKV already treats eviction as the zero-rate end of a rate-distortion bit allocation and derives a closed-form threshold.
- Line 29: "we cast KV cache compression as a rate-distortion problem, under which eviction and quantization are two end-points of …"
- Prop. 3.1 (line 211): evicting token t gives a TV distance of a_{τ,t}. The weight is w_t = Σ_τ a_{τ,t}.
- Objective, Eq. 5: ΔD = Σ_u w_u σ_u 2^{-b_u} subject to Σ b_u ≤ B. This uses Bennett's high-rate approximation.
- Theorem 3.3 (line 271): b*_u = [log2(ln2 · w_u σ_u / λ)]_+. The paper states: "The water level λ/ln 2 induces a phase transition: units with w_u σ_u < λ/ln 2 receive b*_u = 0" (line 280).
- Line 282: "eviction and quantization are not separate actions but two regimes of one allocation curve, selected jointly by the budget B".
- The allocation runs "separately for each layer-head pair (ℓ,h)" (line 442). In practice it is solved as an MCKP over {0, 2, 4, 8, 16} bits.

Consequence for S08: put S08's power law w_i = C i^{-α} into Theorem 3.3. The kept count is then n* = (C σ ln2/λ)^{1/α}, and λ is set so the budget binds. That gives a closed form directly. Keep-all happens iff w_N σ ln2 ≥ λ.
- S08's single-bit-width model (n·b = B, one width for every kept token) is a restricted subcase of RDKV's per-token allocation. RDKV's optimum weakly dominates it.
- S08's α_c is therefore a parametric corollary of RDKV's Theorem 3.3 plus a uniform-width constraint.
- The S08 distortion model has a different form: eviction error enters squared as τ(n)², and quantization error scales as Σp_i²·4^{-b}, not RDKV's linear w·2^{-b}. That only changes constants and exponents, not the structure.

### TailorKV (2505.19586), Sec. 3–4
- Defines a per-head "sparse error" E = 1 − Σ top-k attention mass (Eq. 5). That is exactly S08's tail mass τ(n).
- Line 468: layers "with sparse distributions … are more sensitive to quantization. In contrast, quantizing the dense layer … incurs no performance loss."
- Classifies each layer as "quantization-friendly" (all tokens, 1-bit) or "sparsity-friendly" (top-k at high precision). It does this offline, using a "dense preference score P = n_q − Σ top-k Â" (Eq. 7) and a threshold.
- This is S08's decision rule (heavy tail → keep all at low precision; light tail → keep few at high precision), with the measured tail mass used directly instead of a fitted α. The only differences are that it works per layer, offloads instead of evicting, and uses an empirical threshold.

### ARKV (2603.08727), Sec. III-C/D
- Per-layer "OQ ratio" ρ_ℓ is built from attention entropy, variance and kurtosis (Eqs. 4–7), where "entropy, variance, and kurtosis provide a compact characterization of attention concentration". The resulting split is Original / Quantized / Evicted under a byte budget (Eq. 1).
- This is a heuristic statistics-to-policy map of the same kind S08 proposes.

### BreadthKV (2610.05685), Sec. 5.2 and App. B (PACT)
- Lines 582–590: "Per-head online allocation did not help … letting each head pick its own width lowers attention error by 20–42% … but end to end the per-head version scored below a fixed 4-bit width on the dev set and was indistinguishable from fixed widths on the test sets (1 of 18 comparisons significant)."
- Lines 592–593: "attention error at a single step predicts accuracy poorly."
- The abstract says bit-width is chosen by 60-problem end-to-end calibration "since offline attention error does not predict it reliably."
- This directly undercuts S08's empirical claim that "a head-wise policy predicted from α beats uniform policies", at least for long-CoT decoding. S08's α rule is also an offline attention-error proxy.

### MoE-nD (2604.17695), Sec. 5.4
- Already routes per-layer eviction ratio together with K/V bits.
- Line 1118: "Per-layer eviction routing, not per-layer quantization routing, is the loadbearing novel contribution" (Δevict +15.0 pts; Δquant −2.1 pts).

### 2605.25085 (Wyner-Ziv)
- Proves and measures power-law distortion for eviction: sliding window w = Θ(ε^{-1/α}), and sink-plus-recent D = D* + O(k^{-2α}) (Corollary 4.10).
- Measured α_TV ≈ 0.44 (natural language) and 0.38 (code). This is a different quantity from S08's α: it is truncation sensitivity, not rank-sorted attention weights.
- Lines 55–60 state explicitly that it does not combine this with quantization: "This axis is orthogonal to the one we study".
- So the S08 ingredient "eviction distortion ~ n^{2−2α}" is published in an analogous form. The joint closed form is not.

### Classical precedent (adjacent field)
Mallat & Falzon, "Analysis of Low Bit Rate Image Transform Coding", IEEE TSP 1998 (not on arXiv).
- Low-rate transform coding of coefficients with power-law decay: how many significant coefficients to keep versus how finely to quantize them.
- Result: D(R) ~ R^{1−2α'}, determined by the decay exponent.
- The S08 Lambert-W optimisation is the same textbook calculation with attention weights in place of wavelet coefficients.

Model-level problems:
1. τ(n) ~ C n^{1−α}/(α−1) requires α > 1. If the measured head tails have α ≤ 1, which is plausible for "dense" heads (TailorKV's quantization-friendly layers), the tail sum depends on N and the formula breaks down in the regime that matters most.
2. The 4^{-b} high-rate model fails at 2–3 bits. SemKV (2608.28911) reports a sharp "quality cliff" at 2.322 code bits. BreadthKV's best width jumps between 2, 3 and 4 bits.
3. Hardware widths are discrete ({2, 3, 4, 8}), so the "Lambert-W α_c" reduces to comparing 3–4 discrete options. The continuous closed form adds little operationally.
4. Treating eviction error and quantization error as independent ignores softmax renormalisation, which RDKV handles in Prop. 3.1.

## What remains
1. A parametric explanation: a measured per-head rank-attention exponent α (with finite-N correction) predicts which regime wins at a given byte ratio B/N.
   - The candidate regimes are evict-at-16-bit, BreadthKV's uniform low-bit plus eviction, and keep-all-low-bit.
   - The test is whether this explains why 2412.12706 and BreadthKV favour breadth while MoE-nD finds per-layer eviction routing to be load-bearing.
   - 2607.08032 lists a "predictive compression scaling law … closed form" as an open problem (line 1802).
2. A quantitative statement of how much a uniform-width policy (deployable, one kernel) loses relative to RDKV's per-token water-filling, as a function of α. This is a gap ratio analogous to RateQuant's Theorem 3, but for the eviction/precision axis.

A method that claims head-wise α-routing "beats uniform" faces three problems:
- RDKV is a stronger baseline that already allocates per head and per token.
- TailorKV and ARKV are prior heuristics for the same decision.
- BreadthKV reports that per-head width selection gives no end-to-end gain.

## Significance (main-track reviewer view)
Low as currently framed.
- The theory is a one-line corollary of RDKV's Theorem 3.3 and of classical low-rate transform-coding analysis.
- The empirical policy is preempted by TailorKV, ARKV and MoE-nD, and contradicted by BreadthKV's PACT negative result.
- A diagnostic paper could interest a workshop or TMLR audience if it shows the α law predicts the winning regime across models and tasks out of sample, including the long-CoT case where BreadthKV found offline proxies unreliable.
