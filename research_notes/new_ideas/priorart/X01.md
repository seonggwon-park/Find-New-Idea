# X01 prior-art check: "Residual blocks become Wiener denoisers; error subspace sets PTQ robustness"

**Verdict: survives_narrowed. Novelty 2/5.**

The method part ("a GPTQ objective weighted by the Wiener gain") is already published in 2610.07522, Theorem 1. The Wiener / optimal-denoiser algebra is textbook (Wiener 1949; Alain & Bengio 2014 on the optimal DAE: r(x) - x ~ sigma^2 grad log p). What is left is a narrow, falsifiable mechanistic claim about *pretrained* LLM blocks: the PTQ counteraction of 2609.11716 is concentrated in low-variance / off-manifold directions, and it fails for error along the top-PC / massive-activation directions. No paper I read tests that.

## Premise check
1. "2609.11716 gives no mechanism": **partly false.** App. E.6 attributes counteraction to the block's response to the input error (99.9% of the cumulative negative interaction), which is a Jacobian-level mechanism. What is missing is *why* that response is negative and *in which directions*. l.1395-1397 (aggregate statistics) and l.1789 ("Clarifying this mechanism may inspire future PTQ methods") are quoted accurately. l.1398-1400 already names training stochasticity as the open hypothesis.
2. "2609.32060: G^2 linear in depth, i.e. no cancellation; nothing explains the tension": **misleading.** The linear-G^2 law is the theory for untuned residual scales. Their GPT-2 measurements have a negative depth exponent (attenuation), and they directly tested counteraction in MLPs and CNNs, finding none. The two papers do not contradict each other: one is LMs, the other is image MLPs and CNNs. So the "tension" is an architecture/training difference, and could even serve as a test case.
3. "2610.07522 is only a magnitude-decreasing ridge": **false.** Its Thm. 1 is a generalized-Wiener-filter pretransform plus rounding in metric G.
4. Math core: the linear-Gaussian result (optimal residual = Wiener, J_f = -tau^2(Sigma+tau^2 I)^-1) is standard. **It is internally fragile for depth.** Composing L such maps shrinks legitimate low-variance signal by prod_l lambda_i/(lambda_i+tau^2), which contradicts the growing hidden norms and preserved features in LLMs. A credible model needs tangent/normal (manifold) structure: contraction only normal to the data manifold, as in contractive / denoising-AE theory, not a global Wiener map. Also, in pre-norm (RMSNorm) blocks the Jacobian is exactly blind to errors along h itself, by scale invariance. In LLMs that direction is dominated by the massive / top-PC channels. So "no cancellation along top PCs" has a trivial normalization explanation that must be controlled for before it can be credited to Wiener behaviour.

## What remains (narrowed contribution)
An empirical spectral decomposition of PTQ counteraction in pretrained LLMs: kappa_i = -<P_i dh, P_i du>/||P_i dh||^2 per eigenband of the hidden-state covariance (and per massive-activation channel), with the RMSNorm radial direction projected out. It tests: (a) kappa is concentrated in low-variance bands; (b) kappa ~ 0 for error in the top-PC / massive subspace beyond the radial direction; (c) along Pythia/OLMo checkpoints the per-band kappa tracks a fitted tau_eff^2 (e.g. from the block's symmetric Jacobian spectrum). A method only counts if it differs from 2610.07522, for example by *re-allocating* bits or rotations so that quantization error lands in the self-cancelling subspace (error shaping into the low-variance bands), rather than Wiener-pretransforming the weights.

## Significance
For a main-track reviewer, the mechanism paper alone is a modest follow-up to a one-month-old paper. It is publishable only if the subspace prediction holds cleanly across families *and* yields an error-shaping PTQ gain over 2610.07522 / QEP / GPTAQ at W3/W2. Tarawneh's null result in residual MLPs under QAT is a ready-made counterexample that reviewers will cite against the tau^2 claim.
