# X04 prior-art check: quantized generators vs zero-shot detection and KGW watermarks

**Verdict: survives_narrowed. Novelty 2/5.** The math is textbook and the two directional claims are already known as entropy effects. What is left is an empirical measurement nobody has published (detector AUROC on text from quantized generators, with the scorer at FP) plus a simple correction. The expected effect at W4 is small.

## Papers read in full text, with overlaps

1. **Fast-DetectGPT 2310.05130** (ICLR 2024). App. B, l.1014: "The sample mean µ̃ in Eq. 4 represents the cross-entropy of distribution qφ(x̃|x) and pθ(x̃|x)". l.349–362: with the same model for sampling and scoring, the numerator is Likelihood + Entropy (−µ̃). l.1786: the black-box drop is attributed to "potential mismatch between the scoring model Neo-2.7 and the source models".
   - Overlap: the claimed "exact identity" is cross-entropy = H + KL applied to the paper's own µ̃. Generator/scorer mismatch is already the black-box surrogate setting. Precision mismatch is one instance of it, and a mild one.
2. **Binoculars 2401.12070.** The score is log-PPL over cross-PPL. It already builds the cross-entropy between two models into the statistic.
   - The hypothesis says Binoculars' shift is "exactly ΔH − KL". That is false as stated. Binoculars is a ratio, and its denominator depends on the text prefix. The per-token identity holds only for the expected numerator, conditional on a shared prefix.
3. **KGW 2301.10226.** Thm 4.2 (l.439–460) and Lemma E.1 (l.3299+) give a *lower bound* on E|s|_G that is linear in average *spike* entropy (l.513–520: the bound is 142.2 against an empirical 159.5).
   - Overlap: "more entropy gives more green tokens" is Kirchenbauer's own result. The claimed premise that the expected green fraction "rises monotonically with entropy" overstates it: only the bound is monotone, and it is monotone in spike entropy, not Shannon entropy.
4. **Bahri & Wieting 2508.13131** ("Improving Detection of Watermarked LMs", TMLR 01/2026). l.44–51: "watermark performance improves with more available entropy". They combine watermark detectors with Binoculars, likelihood and RoBERTa detectors, and the combination helps most at low entropy (l.995–1003).
   - Overlap: the "the two families respond differently to entropy" framing.
   - It also cuts against the hypothesis. In their Fig. 1 (l.831), "LLh also improves with entropy". Across prompts, the likelihood detector did *not* move opposite to the watermark. Their setting differs (the negatives are Mistral text, not human text), but "opposite directions" is not a safe general claim.
5. **Gloaguen et al. 2502.10525** and **PRO 2510.23891** quantize *weight-distilled* watermarked models.
   - 2502.10525 l.836–844: "nearly all tested schemes are durable to quantization ... even at 4 bits".
   - PRO Table 1 (l.697–752): Gu et al. KGW drops from AUC 0.991 / TPR 0.956 to 0.982 / 0.910 under 4-bit GPTQ, with the PPL column going from 5.0 to 5.5.
   - Quantization raised PPL there, but detection fell slightly, which is the *opposite* of "strengthens watermark". The mechanism is different: it damages the distilled bias rather than applying a generation-time logit processor. Even so, this is the only published quantization × watermark data, and it does not support the claimed direction.
6. **2605.14240** (paraphrase-attack paper), App. A.0.2, l.619–642: "we quantized HuggingFace 'tiiuae/falcon-7b' ... The typical degradation effect was about 2% ... This is unexpected". The quantized models are falcon-7b and falcon-7b-instruct, which are the **Binoculars scoring models (the detector)**. The generators were GPT-4o, Gemini and others (l.194–195, l.636–642).
   - **Premise error:** this is the reverse of X04's setting (quantized scorer, FP or API generator). It is not evidence about quantized generators.
7. **Alignment Imprint 2604.16923** (Apr 2026). This is a martingale-CLT TNR theorem (Thm 1, l.461) for Fast-DetectGPT-type statistics in which the generator (aligned) and the scorer/reference (base) differ, and it uses "entropy contraction induced by alignment" (l.433). It is the closest *theory-of-mismatch* framework: a source/scorer distribution shift analysed through entropy, for an alignment shift instead of a quantization shift. It does not mention quantization.
8. **Supporting facts for the premise "quantization raises entropy":**
   - 2609.07901 (Sep 2026), l.24–28 and l.248–250: Qwen3-8B AWQ-int4 raises decision-token entropy by +0.091 bits (CI [0.020, 0.163]). But lexical diversity falls (TTR −0.011), semantic collision rises by 2.6pp, and there is no content effect at 14B or 32B.
   - 2405.00632: quantized LLMs show higher predictive entropy on multiple-choice (MCQ) answers (l.714–717).
   - 2606.00206: per-position KL(FP‖Q) against BF16 next-token entropy gives Spearman ρ = 0.92 (Fig. 4c, l.722 and l.1412). **Verified.**
   - Conclusion: entropy does rise, but the rise is small and depends on model scale.

## Factual premise check
- The identity E_q[log p] − E_p[log p] = H(p) − H(q) − KL(q‖p) is correct, per token and conditional on the prefix. It is a one-line rewrite of the cross-entropy µ̃ in Fast-DetectGPT App. B.
  - The shift is negative whenever H(q) + KL > H(p), so detection weakens through the KL term even with *no* entropy change. Framing it as "worse when entropy rises" undersells the KL term.
  - At the sequence level, prefixes are drawn from q, so the summed expectation is taken along q's trajectory. That is still exact as a sum of per-position terms, but those terms cannot be computed from FP rollouts alone.
- The "exact" claim for Binoculars is **false** (ratio statistic; see item 2).
- KGW monotonicity is a lower bound in spike entropy, not an exact monotone in Shannon entropy (item 3). KGW detection never uses the FP model, so the "detector scores with FP" framing does not apply. The claim reduces to "the quantized generator has higher spike entropy".
- The "closest hit" in 2605.14240 is about a quantized *detector* (item 6).
- The only quantization × watermark numbers (PRO, distilled watermarks) go *down*, not up.

## What remains
1. A controlled measurement of the AUROC and TPR@1%FPR of Fast-DetectGPT, Binoculars and likelihood/LRR detectors on text from W8/W4/W3/W2 (GPTQ, AWQ, NF4, GGUF k-quants) and KV-quantized generators, scored by the FP model. This has not been published anywhere.
2. The same measurement for generation-time KGW, Unigram and Aaronson on quantized generators, with the observed spike entropy reported.
3. A precision-marginalized µ̃/σ̃ correction (score against a mixture over likely deployment precisions). This is a straightforward instance of distribution-aligned surrogates (Glimpse, DALD, ImBD), but it has not been applied to precision.

## Significance (main-track view)
Weak. For a decent W4 model, per-token KL is roughly 1e-2 to 5e-2 nats. The human–machine gap in white-box Fast-DetectGPT is several σ̃, and cross-family surrogate mismatch (which detectors already tolerate) is far larger. So the expected W4 degradation is close to the noise floor. The 2605.14240 detector-side ~2% figure and PRO's 0.991 → 0.982 are consistent with this. The result becomes interesting only if ≤3-bit or KV-cache quantization causes large AUROC drops, or if an adversary deliberately picks a quantizer to maximise KL at high-entropy positions (a "quantization as evasion attack" framing). The identity itself would not count as a contribution at NeurIPS or ICLR. Better venues are a Findings or workshop paper, or a section of a larger robustness study.

## Sharpened hypothesis
When an FP model scores text produced by its own k-bit quantization, the expected per-token drop in the Fast-DetectGPT numerator equals the cross-entropy gap H(q̃, p) − H(p). Empirically, this gap is dominated by KL at high-entropy positions (ρ ≈ 0.92 per 2606.00206). The resulting AUROC loss is below 1 point at W8/W4 but becomes material at ≤3-bit, aggressive KV-cache quantization, or with an adversarially chosen quantizer. Generation-time KGW green rates track the quantized model's *spike* entropy, but the gain is too small to matter. A precision-marginalized µ̃ recovers most of the low-bit loss. The direction of the watermark effect must be measured, not assumed: weight-distilled watermarks *lose* AUC under GPTQ (PRO Table 1).
