# S02 prior-art check: winner's curse in quantized-key selection (empirical-Bayes ranking)

Verdict: **survives_narrowed**. Novelty 2/5.

Every ingredient has been published. The ranking rule exp(m + v/2) is published for KV eviction. Empirical-Bayes shrinkage for ranking under heteroscedastic noise is a classical published result. The sigma^2/2 Jensen correction for quantized keys is published. What remains is applying them together to top-k selection from noisy quantized-key scores. No paper found does that. The question is whether the effect is large enough to matter.

## Premise checks

1. RaBitQCache l.23 and l.113: confirmed as quoted. The paper promotes unbiasedness and has no selection-bias analysis. Its error bound (Hoeffding, l.2081-2190) is not used to adjust the ranking.
2. Fathom: confirmed. The variance only sets bit depth, and ranking is by the estimate.
3. BreadthKV 2610.05685 l.26-27 and l.436-437: "Even with exact knowledge of future attention, it loses about 13% of the future attention mass at 10% retention", and l.434 calls this "a floor that no scorer can remove". The number is correct, **but the inference is a misreading.** The 13% is an oracle floor: the mass outside the best 10% set across the next 256 queries. It contains no selection error. It cannot support "selection errors cost mass directly"; if anything it shows the reachable mass is capped no matter how good the scorer is.
4. "FP8 lightning-style indexers": DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (2609.19969, l.1002-1003) states "DeepSeek-V4 already uses quantization-aware training (QAT) for FP4 indexer queries and keys". A QAT-trained indexer's quantized score *is* the learned ranking function. It is not a noisy estimate of a well-defined s_i with a known sigma_i. The model applies cleanly only to post-hoc quantized proxies (Quest-type bounds, RaBitQCache, self-indexing, H2O on quantized K), not to DSA-style learned indexers. The V3.2 FP8 claim is supported only by secondary sources (lmsys and vLLM blogs). With FP8 logit noise of about 2^-4 relative, sigma^2/(tau^2+sigma^2) is about 1e-3, so the effect would be negligible there.
5. **The direction of the effect is regime-dependent, not a uniform winner's curse.** Under the candidate's own posterior, (m_i + v_i/2) - s_hat_i = sigma_i^2/(tau^2+sigma_i^2) * (mu - s_hat_i + tau^2/2). High-noise tokens are penalized relative to naive ranking only when s_hat_i > mu + tau^2/2; below that point they are *promoted*. Example: tau = 3 with a 10% budget gives a threshold of about mu + 3.84 < mu + 4.5, so naive ranking *under*-selects noisy tokens at the margin. The headline claim "ranking by s_hat over-selects high-noise tokens" therefore holds only for small budgets or flat heads (z_k > tau/2 in z-units).
6. The theorem is trivial: linearity of expectation plus the lognormal mean. Corollary 2 is the standard Gaussian posterior-mean (Tweedie) shrinkage. The independence assumption also fails for shared-rotation schemes: RaBitQ and Hadamard use one random rotation for all tokens, so errors are correlated across tokens. A Gaussian prior on logits over-shrinks the heavy upper tail, which is exactly where the selected tokens sit. Henderson-Newton and Gu-Koenker avoid Gaussian priors for this reason.

## What remains

- Applying posterior-expected-mass ranking with empirical-Bayes shrinkage to selection from *post-hoc quantized* key proxies (1-2 bit RaBitQ or sign sketches, low-bit Quest, quantized-K H2O). sigma_i is known from stored per-token scales and norms.
- The regime analysis is the more interesting result: a crossover at s_hat = mu + tau^2/2 decides whether noise should be penalized or rewarded. The candidate's framing missed this.
- An empirical measurement of whether swap-induced mass loss is non-negligible at 1-2 bits, compared with the oracle floor.

## Significance (main-track reviewer view)

A reviewer would see a two-line theorem that combines Expected Attention's MGF ranking (2025) with classical empirical-Bayes ranking (2014-2020). The likely gain is small: the 10% oracle already loses 13% of the mass, and noise only reorders tokens near the boundary. It matters only at 1-2 bits, where the heteroscedastic sigma_i is large. Without a demonstrated recall or accuracy gain of about 1 point or more at matched budget on RULER or LongBench with 1-bit proxies, it reads as a workshop note. With such a gain, and with an NPMLE or heavy-tailed prior plus the crossover result, it could be a solid short paper.
